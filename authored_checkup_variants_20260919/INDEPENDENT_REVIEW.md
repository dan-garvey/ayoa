# Independent final review: masked six-turn continuations

This review assesses `packet-01` through `packet-20` as complete T19–24 trajectories. It was completed while the packet labels were still masked. I read the shared story and all masked passages, but no mappings, generation requests, source samples, other assessments, or condition identities. `INDEPENDENT_PRELIMINARY.md` remains unchanged.

## Overall judgment

`packet-19` is the strongest whole continuation. Its six turns join Owen's job uncertainty, Martin's assumptions, Nell's leave, practical cinema work, and Rowan's chosen distance into one restrained causal sequence. `packet-12` rises most after the final turns: Owen tells Martin about the interview during their real repair work, Martin has an immediate but proportionate reaction, and “Permanent's worth going for” changes their relationship without resolving the hypothetical job for them. `packet-10` and `packet-13` complete the leading group.

The best quiet continuation is `packet-06`. Jo negotiates an actual deadline to protect unexpected time with Fran, then Owen protects a long-held gig ticket without disclosing more about Thursday than he chooses. Its preference comes from enacted personal priorities, not event volume. Several busier packets rank below it because they mostly report completed tests, repeat archive safeguards, or have narration certify that the player remains free after the dialogue has already shown that.

Across the set, the interview remains well contained. Jo never discloses it for Owen; Owen either tells Rowan himself or continues to call Thursday an appointment. No packet leaks *The Red Umbrella*, misuses Bea's album, invents Rowan's feelings, or makes Rowan decide another person's employment, room booking, or family conversation. Compatible additions such as band tickets, a fire-alarm visit, a sign fault, a portfolio exercise, or the state of the tide are welcome rather than continuity errors.

## Violations, ambiguities, and aesthetic costs

There is one clear chronology defect. In `packet-08`, T23 ends with Owen picking up the programme holders and Rowan returning upstairs. T24 then rewinds to “Before you leave the yard,” inserts the player's goodbye into the already completed moment, sends Rowan back to work for the rest of the shift, and only then performs the requested walk. The fiction is excellent on either side of that seam, but the published turn sequence cannot happen as written and “Then I take a walk” is not honored at its natural point.

`packet-17` has a smaller version of the same problem. T23 says Owen closes the tool case, returns to the foyer fitting, and Rowan goes upstairs; T24 again has Owen pick up the tool case as Rowan says goodbye and leaves. This is a duplicated transition and mild rewind. `packet-12` also mentions Owen following Martin with the tools and then gathering “the remaining tools” at the next turn, but a second load is a coherent reading, so I treat that as an editing seam rather than a violation.

`packet-09` moves the goodbye and walk to the end of Rowan's shift, and `packet-10` inserts catalogue work until lunch before the walk. Those choices loosen the ordinary meaning of “Then,” but neither produces an impossible sequence. They are scope and pacing costs, not hard chronology breaches. Likewise, when Owen or Bea asks Rowan a question and the next player instruction chooses a goodbye or time skip instead of an answer, the model is right not to supply Rowan's reply. The resulting silence can feel socially awkward—most notably across `packet-16`—but it is not an agency violation.

Two other points remain ambiguous rather than wrong. `packet-04` may be citing the signed transfer schedule only for covered material, or may be using old evidence to resolve the broad yellow-tray uncertainty; the referent is too loose to penalize as a factual contradiction. `packet-09` has Owen refixing a rail the morning after it held both men's weight, an unexplained bit of rework that is plausible in an unfinished building but weakens continuity polish.

## Packet findings

### packet-01

Bea's “better noises” and rejection of a shrine are strong, and Owen's later distinction—liking the cinema does not mean deciding what follows his contract—gives him interior direction without revealing the interview. The catalogue and booking details remain accurate. The trajectory repeatedly explains that Rowan's hours are protected, then Owen asks “How's the archive going?” only for the next turn to move directly to goodbye. That is player-respecting but conversationally abrupt. The harbour walk is concrete and properly solitary.

### packet-02

Bea's wet-afternoon audience, Nell's gentle defence of Martin, the receiver's dead patch, and the personal-information access note make the cinema feel operated by real people. T23 improves the packet substantially: Owen tells Rowan himself, while Martin's delayed responses—train times, then a museum-car-park link—are funny, affectionate, and precisely in character. Jo hears without taking over. The unanswered “How's the archive going?” is a minor social rough edge; otherwise this is clean, warm work.

### packet-03

The best early moment is Bea distinguishing a community people care about from one used to solicit free labour, with Jo present to receive the point. The alarm test pays off as an actual interruption before Rowan's break. Owen then explains the interview, names who knows, and reserves telling Martin for himself; no private fact arrives magically. The sequence stops before that choice has consequences, so the whole trajectory feels more like well-prepared direction than development completed within these turns.

### packet-04

Bea's plan to watch a film while somebody else counts the takings, the boiler-cancellation notice, and Owen's existing gig ticket give this concise packet personality. Owen continues to call Thursday an appointment, preserving his discretion, and his disagreement with Martin about the bowed wall is practical rather than symbolic dialogue in disguise. The ownership resolution has an ambiguous referent, as noted above. The final walk is brief, sensuous, and timed cleanly within the break.

### packet-05

Bea's ordinary wet evening and the handbills inside a programme are effective particulars. Owen eventually discloses the interview himself and reports a recognizably Martin response: concern about distance, then the car before any hypothetical job. The fire-alarm van arriving as Rowan returns neatly continues the Wednesday briefing. Most consequences still happen offstage and are summarized, while the middle turns follow a familiar assignment/status pattern. Competent and pleasant rather than compelling.

### packet-06

Jo's scene is the set's strongest evidence that quiet narrative can move: she renegotiates the drinks-price deadline when Fran becomes free, and Nell accepts a bounded change. Later, Owen keeps Thursday private, has a months-old ticket for a band, and wants the last holder mounted before Martin can reopen their argument about screw holes. Those are independent pleasures and priorities, not manufactured plot. The telescope outing in the earlier afternoon is slightly odd without a stated daylight purpose, and Owen's question to Rowan goes unanswered, but neither is a contract breach.

### packet-07

Jo's price calculation and possible solo telescope trip establish life outside Rowan, while Owen's comparison with disposable trade-show rooms gives him a specific working history. He keeps the inland appointment private. The archive, delivery, and harbour work are all concrete, especially the grinder sparks and the colour change beyond the breakwater. Yet contemplated activities and status updates rarely become consequences between people. Its final place reflects dramatic stasis, not shortness or a factual failure.

### packet-08

The first four turns remain superb: Bea's plant prompts Martin to assume Owen will be present through winter; Owen tells him about the interview; father and son return to the rail without a false settlement; Nell protects a swim. T23 is equally good in isolation. Owen's imaginary station drawing reveals a pleasure unrelated to his usefulness, and Martin quietly leaving screws rather than ending the break shows change through conduct. The T24 rewind described above is nevertheless real and directly affects the player's chosen action, so this cannot retain a top-three position.

### packet-09

This is an unusually alive ensemble. Bea and Jo want ordinary customers for different reasons; Owen chooses when to tell Martin; Martin offers a lift rather than an argument; and the archive's Ward's Laundrette advertisement connects the institution to Nell's family without overclaiming authorship. “I haven't decided anything beyond getting on the train tomorrow” is an excellent limit. The rail's unexplained refitting and the choice to postpone the goodbye and walk until after the whole shift expose some construction. They are costs, but not knowledge or agency violations.

### packet-10

The early lunch lets Owen distinguish front-of-house employment from Martin's assumption that practical skill makes him permanently available. Owen later tells Nell himself; Rowan hears at an open doorway, a slightly convenient but intelligible source. Nell responds to Rowan's boundary by actually booking leave with Gina. T23 then brings Martin into view: his lift offer still stands, Owen refuses, and Martin accepts “thanks” without reopening the future. That modest behavioural change is excellent. The midday delay before the walk is a mild reading of “Then,” not an impossible chronology.

### packet-11

Bea's memory of listening to audiences argue on their way out is one of the better answers to the lunch question. The packet initially becomes procedural, but T23 restores character: Owen jokes that the rack is level even if the wall is not, then admits the interview complicates liking this job. Rowan's walk lets the lamp repair complete off to the side; the returning sign now has one warm colour throughout. No one oversteps knowledge. This is satisfying operational continuity with real voice, though little relationship changes onscreen.

### packet-12

The sign test begins as something Bea personally wants, fails, and gives Martin and Owen a reason to work together Wednesday. Owen uses that shared break to tell Martin about the interview in Rowan's hearing. Martin's hand stopping over the mug, the long refrigerator silence, and the sequence from “I can drive you” to “Permanent's worth going for” give the disclosure room without melodrama or instant reconciliation. The “remaining tools” transition is slightly untidy but plausibly a second load. This final scene turns a previously solid operational packet into the runner-up.

### packet-13

Jo protects a full day with Fran; Owen tells Nell and then Martin; Martin's printed timetable shows practical care without erasing the disagreement; Nell commits to her own postponed walking weekend and plans archive cover. These are sustained, causal decisions. T23 mostly recaps the father–son exchange, and Owen's question about the archive is left hanging when Rowan says goodbye, so the final pair adds less than it might. The whole trajectory remains among the best because earlier actions already changed how these people handle one another.

### packet-14

Bea is unsentimental and funny about both old shifts and her granddaughter. The missing annex resolves only supported ownership questions, while one stays open; that is exemplary archive reasoning. The odd lamp gets a visible repair, and Owen tells Rowan about the interview himself while keeping Martin's ignorance explicit. The packet creates several useful future lines but does not enact the hardest conversation within the six turns. Its long reassurance at T22 is the main stylistic drag.

### packet-15

The undated `BEA / MARTIN / JUNE` list is the best work-driven narrative development in the set: it may help Rosa's attribution, but Nell neither calls it a rota nor treats it as proof. Nell protects an evening with Gina, and Owen's first detailed disclosure to Nell occurs behind a closed door after Rowan has left. T23 correctly references that private exchange, then lets Owen tell Rowan what he chooses while reserving Martin's conversation. It ends with direction rather than payoff, but its privacy and evidentiary discipline never feel sterile.

### packet-16

This packet preserves agency at every isolated decision, yet its conversations do not join smoothly. Bea asks what Rowan expected; Rowan's next turn “lets [the] question pass.” Nell later asks whether someone requested cover; the next passage jumps to the morning break without resolving that exchange. The omissions follow the player's later instructions and therefore are not invented choices, but together they make Rowan seem socially discontinuous. T23's still-unaccepted interview, minutes before the noon deadline, is a good live tension, and Owen alone chooses to disclose it. The sign repair and walk are clean.

### packet-17

Jo leaves after delivering her work so she can see Fran; the receiver test genuinely fails to happen when Hana is unavailable; Rosa keeps her evening with Beth; and Nell keeps her walking weekend. T23 adds a sharply specific interview portfolio and Martin's handwritten lift note, turning practical care into consequence. The narrator too often restates what each action means, and the T23–24 tool-case transition duplicates a departure that has already happened. Strong character material survives those costs, but the smaller rewind keeps it below the clean leaders.

### packet-18

Bea's wish for visitors unconnected to trustees or fundraising is appealing, and the annotated heating programme gives Rowan real evidence to describe. Owen later tells Rowan and reports Martin offering a drive before hearing the address, a good compact character beat. The remaining turns mostly complete work or report that technical work has completed. “I'll let you know how it went” gives a future social thread, but the present trajectory remains thinner than its polished sentences.

### packet-19

Every major strand develops through action. Owen openly accepts the interview; Nell books leave rather than merely aspiring to take it; Martin and Owen finish immediate work before their private talk; and the next morning Martin admits he “got ahead of” himself. Owen's “A bit” is funny, affectionate, and resistant. T23 deepens rather than repeats this: Owen can value the cinema while refusing to let six weeks become a decision. Martin tests the repaired sign and calls Owen back only when the break naturally ends. Rowan's solitary walk then closes the trajectory at the degree of interaction he chose.

### packet-20

Bea's desire for “the ordinary week after” is strong, and the duty sheet correctly lets a document establish Bea and Martin's roles without asking memory to do so. Nell protects the October weekend; Owen keeps his appointment private until he chooses to tell Rowan. The duty sheet's row-F sweeping instruction echoes Bea's row-F example from lunch so exactly that the archival discovery feels arranged for thematic confirmation. The final conversation is extremely spare but correct, and the image of Owen measuring through the glazing gives the walk a clean ending.
