At half past twelve, you go down to the foyer. Bea is not there.

Jo has spread supplier lists and a calculator across one end of a cafe table. Her lunch is a bowl of rice pudding with a conspicuous layer of brown sugar stirred through it. Owen sits opposite her, his tool bag under the chair; he has spent the morning fitting a display rail beside the front doors. Nell brings her lunch out of the office, and you eat with the three of them.

Jo circles a figure on one price sheet.

“If the dairy confirms this by three, I’ll finish the menu costs today. If they don’t, it waits until Thursday.”

“I thought you were doing it tomorrow,” Nell says.

“Fran and I are both off tomorrow. That almost never happens, and I’m not turning it into a day of supplier calls. You’ll still have my cafe hours this afternoon.”

Nell considers the calendar beside Jo’s papers. “Thursday is fine for the prices.”

Jo adds another spoonful of sugar to the rice pudding and returns to the list.

Partway through lunch, Owen takes out his phone.

“I sent the shift change,” he tells Nell. “Thursday to Friday, same hours.”

Nell checks her work messages. “I have it. Friday, ten until four. Confirmed.”

“And I replied to the museum. I’m taking the interview on Thursday.”

He says it to Nell, not to you, but the small table leaves no private edge to the conversation. Jo continues folding a supplier sheet along its crease; the news does not surprise her.

Nell sets down her phone. “All right. Thanks for telling me.”

After a moment she asks, “Does Martin know?”

“No.” Owen looks towards the empty chair at the end of the table. “I’m going round tonight. I’d rather tell him before the interview than explain afterwards why I kept it quiet.”

Neither Nell nor Jo tells him what Martin’s answer will be. The confirmed Friday shift goes onto Nell’s rota, and Owen pockets his phone. Martin remains elsewhere, still without the information Owen has decided to give him.
