On Wednesday morning, you find Nell in the office with the paper diary open beside her computer. The pencilled auditorium hold from yesterday has been replaced with a firm entry.

You ask what she wants you to catalogue next and whether anything practical has changed.

“Programmes again this morning,” she says. “Take the box marked 1987 to 1990. The transfer schedule covers the programme collection, but check that the contents match the label. Start with a box-level record, then make separate entries for anything heavily annotated or in poor condition.”

She points to a second grey case on her list.

“Leave that one for now. It includes old payroll sheets and home addresses. The trust owns the papers, but I’m still setting the access note for personal information.”

Nell closes the diary.

“Upstairs arrangements are unchanged. Keep the loan cupboard locked, and put unresolved ownership in the yellow tray.”

The hearing-assistance test went ahead the previous evening. Nell explains that reception initially dropped out near the back corner of the auditorium; Dev adjusted the transmitter, and Hana repeated the walk through the seats without finding another gap. The receivers are now charging in the office for opening weekend.

“The sign lit as well,” she adds. “Martin was right about one lamp being a colder colour. It works, but I’ve reported it to the restoration company.”

The only change that affects the building this morning is a cafe delivery due through the service yard at eleven.

“Jo will have the side door open while they unload,” Nell says. “It still needs locking again as soon as they’re finished.”

She gives you the shelf reference for the programme box. The archive room remains available for your assigned work, with no exhibition materials or unrecorded loans added overnight.
