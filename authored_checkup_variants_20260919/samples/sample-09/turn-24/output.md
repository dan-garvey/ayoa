The rest of your shift passes in box C-seven. Among the distributor letters are requests from a hospital social club, a youth theatre and a tenants’ association asking for particular films or different screening times. Replies survive for some and not for others. You keep each reply with its request and record the gaps without supplying an explanation.

At half past three, you secure the box, save the catalogue and lock the workroom.

Owen is packing his drill into its case when you come downstairs.

“I’ll see you tomorrow,” you tell him.

“Friday,” he says. “Interview tomorrow.”

He closes the case and returns to gathering his tools.

You leave by the side door and walk down Harbour Street alone. The afternoon rain has cleared, leaving the paving dark and the gutters busy. At the inner harbour, the tide is low enough to expose weed on the lower steps and a band of black mud beneath the quay wall.

Two fishing boats are unloading grey crates under a cloud of gulls. Farther along, the boatyard’s open shed frames the pale hull of a vessel raised on supports. Hammering carries across the water, irregular and much louder than it seems from the far side.

You follow the harbour road past stacked lobster pots, a shuttered summer kiosk and a bench facing the moorings. The air smells of diesel, salt and wet rope. Workers from the packing depot cross towards the bus stop in coats pulled over their uniforms; none of them turns towards the cinema road.

Beyond the inner harbour, the wall curves towards open water. Waves strike its seaward face and send brief veils of spray above the stone. You continue along it by yourself, with Lowhaven behind you and the repaired Picture House hidden among the roofs.
