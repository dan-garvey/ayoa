“I’ll see you tomorrow,” you say.

“Friday, actually. Tomorrow’s the appointment.” Owen sets down his mug. “I’m not in here.”

You leave him with the unmounted programme holder and go out alone.

Harbour Street slopes towards the inner harbour. The morning is overcast but dry, with a wind that pulls at the hem of your coat. Fishing boats sit low against the quay, their ropes creaking through rust-streaked rings. One crew unloads grey crates beneath the circling gulls while somebody on the next boat hoses scales from the deck.

You follow the water past stacked lobster pots and a locked summer kiosk. Across the harbour, machinery knocks intermittently inside the boatyard. A forklift reverses somewhere behind the food-packing depot, its warning tone carrying over the roofs.

Near the harbour mouth, the wind sharpens and wrinkles the water between the breakwaters. You turn there and retrace the quay. The walk takes the rest of your break, and you return to Harbour Street on your own.
