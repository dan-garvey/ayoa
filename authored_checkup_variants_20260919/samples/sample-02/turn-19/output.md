Downstairs, Bea is at the cafe table with a blue accounts folder, a calculator and an open lunch box. A cheese sandwich sits beside two pickled walnuts. Nell is in the office speaking on the telephone.

You bring your lunch to the table and sit opposite Bea.

“What do you enjoy about having the cinema open again?”

Bea considers this while closing the calculator cover.

“I like that we’re buying cups now instead of roof felt. For two years, most of the money went on things people will only notice if they fail.”

She looks towards the front windows.

“I wanted the sign restored. It cost more than I’d planned to approve, and I still wanted it. Martin’s testing the timer tonight.”

She takes another bite of her sandwich.

“And I like Rosa putting names beside the old programme boards. They were always described as if the cinema painted them by itself.”

Bea pushes the accounts folder a little farther from her lunch.

“My granddaughter is coming for one of the opening-weekend screenings. We send each other dreadful films on secondhand DVDs, so she’s disappointed that our first programme looks respectable. I bought two seats in the middle row.”

Her finger rests on the closed calculator.

“I’m watching that film with her. Somebody else can count the box-office money.”
