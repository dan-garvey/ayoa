Bea finishes her sandwich, initials the two invoices Nell has brought her and returns to the boatyard. Jo goes back to comparing supplier prices. When lunch breaks up, you return upstairs.

The programme box occupies the afternoon. One weekly sheet advertises three films but gives four sets of show times; a pencilled correction in the margin resolves the extra screening. Another has been folded around a bundle of smaller handbills for a Saturday children’s matinee. You record the relationship before returning them to the same bundle.

The dated run becomes clearer as you work. There are duplicates for several summer weeks, then a gap of nearly a month. You note the missing dates without assuming the programmes were ever present. Old admission prices, local shop advertisements and handwritten alterations go into the descriptions only where they distinguish one item from another.

By four o’clock, the agreed end of your shift, the transfer papers have their first complete group of catalogue records and the programme box has a dated inventory. You save the final entry, place a marker at your stopping point and close the box.
