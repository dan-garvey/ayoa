# packet-17

## Turn 19

Player: At lunch, if Bea is present, I ask what she enjoys about having the cinema open again. If she is not here, I eat with whoever is around.

At half past twelve, you lock the archive workroom and go downstairs with your lunch.

Bea isn’t there. Jo occupies one end of the joined tables with a mug of black coffee, a calculator and three supplier price lists. Owen has been fitting a programme rack beside the entrance; his tape measure and screwdriver rest on a folded dust sheet. Nell comes out of the office carrying the paper diary and sits with the rest of you.

Once everyone has started eating, Owen speaks to her.

“I sent the Thursday shift change. And I replied to the museum this morning. I’m taking the interview.”

Jo continues with her lunch. Until now, Nell has known only about the inland appointment.

“All right,” Nell says. “I’ve got your message.”

She opens the diary and moves his six-hour shift from Thursday to Friday.

“Friday, ten until four. Confirmed. I’ll include it when I send the updated rota.”

“Thanks.”

“I hope the interview goes well.” Nell closes the diary. “Does Martin know?”

Owen looks towards the auditorium, though his father isn’t in the building. “Not yet. I’m going to tell him myself. I want to catch him on his own rather than announce it across a table.”

“I’ll leave that with you,” Nell says.

Nothing more is asked of him. The museum has offered only an interview, and nobody treats it as a job already won.

Jo finishes a calculation in the margin of her price sheet, then closes the folder.

“I’ll have the menu costs to you by half one,” she tells Nell. “After that, I’m leaving. Fran’s actually free this afternoon, and I’m not spending it comparing wholesale cheese.”

“Half one is fine.”

“If a supplier changes something after that, it waits until tomorrow.”

Nell nods. “Go and see Fran.”

The conversation moves on to the programme rack and whether its lower edge needs another fixing. You finish your lunch while Owen and Nell compare the fitting to the drawing he made that morning. His Thursday interview is accepted, his cinema shift is formally moved, and the conversation he still has to choose his way through is with Martin.

## Turn 20

Player: I let the conversation finish and go back to the catalogue. I work until the end of my agreed shift.

The lunch conversation ends without being prolonged. Owen returns to the programme rack, Jo gathers her supplier sheets, and Nell takes the amended rota back into the office.

You unlock the archive workroom and resume with the programme box.

Through the afternoon, you complete its box-level description and work through the dated bundles inside. You record gaps without filling them by assumption, note duplicate sheets separately and distinguish printed show times from later pencil alterations. A loose page that cannot be connected confidently to either neighbouring bundle goes into a temporary sleeve with its original position recorded.

The transfer schedule supports the trust’s ownership of the programme files. Where a handwritten inventory makes broader claims about photographs and staff papers not present in the box, you describe the claim without extending it to material you haven’t examined.

At half past one, the side door closes downstairs. Jo has delivered her finished cost sheet and left for her afternoon with Fran. After that, the building settles again.

You stop at half past three, the finish time agreed for today. The entry in progress is complete. The loose page, its sleeve and every programme bundle return to their recorded positions; the box goes back onto its marked shelf. You save the catalogue, close the laptop and confirm that the loan cupboard is still locked.

Downstairs, you enter your finishing time on the hours sheet. Jo’s menu figures lie on Nell’s desk, and Owen’s amended shift has been written into the rota.

Nell looks over the hours sheet. “Same start tomorrow. We’ll deal with the yellow-tray questions first, then you can continue that programme run.”

She returns to her work, leaving your first shift finished at the time agreed.

## Turn 21

Player: The following morning I check with Nell for my next archive assignment and any practical changes I need to know about.

On Wednesday morning, you find Nell in the office with the paper diary open and the yellow query slips laid beside it.

“Yellow tray first,” she says. “The unsigned collection list stays in the transfer file, but it doesn’t establish ownership. Keep the loose programme page in its temporary sleeve at the position where you found it. Its sequence remains unconfirmed.”

Your next assignment is to finish the programme box begun yesterday, then start the adjacent box if time allows. Nell has not assigned any loaned material for today, so the cupboard remains locked.

“There are two practical updates,” she says. “Dev couldn’t get Hana for last night, so his provisional auditorium hold expired at noon. No receiver test took place. He’ll have to arrange another date.”

She takes a printed email from beneath the diary.

“Rosa checked Monday’s photographs yesterday morning. They’re usable, and she kept her evening with Beth. She has two unresolved painter attributions for the captions. If you happen to find evidence while cataloguing the programmes already assigned to you, flag it. Don’t abandon your box to hunt for it.”

Nell writes the two names on a query slip and attaches it to the programme-box reference rather than making it a separate urgent task.

She then turns the office calendar towards you. The dates 9–11 October are blocked out in ink.

“One advance notice. I’m away that weekend with Gina. We’ve postponed it twice, and we aren’t moving it again. Before then, I’ll arrange who can issue the loan-cupboard key. Until that’s settled, don’t plan restricted-loan work for those dates.”

She hands you the resolved yellow slips and the new attribution query. Your assignment remains the programme catalogue, with the additional names to notice only if the evidence appears in the material already in front of you.

## Turn 22

Player: I say I am not available for additional front-of-house shifts; I want to keep the hours outside my archive contract for myself.

“I’m not available for additional front-of-house shifts,” you say. “I want to keep the hours outside my archive contract for myself.”

Nell nods once. “Understood. Your contract is for thirty hours of cataloguing. I won’t add you to the front-of-house rota or treat living upstairs as being on call.”

She taps the October dates on the calendar.

“And this wasn’t a request for you to cover me. I’ll arrange a trustee to hold the cupboard key, or restricted-loan work will pause while I’m away.”

Nothing is added to your rota. Nell returns the calendar to its place, and you take the programme-box references upstairs to continue the assignment she has given you.
