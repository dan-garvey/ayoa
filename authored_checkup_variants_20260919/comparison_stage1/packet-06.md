# packet-06

## Turn 19

Player: At lunch, if Bea is present, I ask what she enjoys about having the cinema open again. If she is not here, I eat with whoever is around.

At half past twelve, Bea isn’t in the cinema. Nell and Jo are at one of the cafe tables, and you take the remaining chair with your lunch.

Jo has supplier sheets spread beside her plate. Figures have been written and crossed out along the margins. A small tub of rice pudding waits near her elbow under a thick layer of brown sugar.

“The cheese has gone up again,” she says.

Nell looks across at the figures. “Are you keeping the toastie?”

“For the first month. I’ll see what actually sells before I change it.”

Jo turns to another sheet, then caps her pen.

“I’m leaving at two. Fran’s late shift was cancelled, so we’ve got the afternoon together. I’ll finish the drinks prices tomorrow morning.”

“You said I’d have them today.”

“I know. The food prices are done. You can have the rest by ten tomorrow.”

Nell checks the page Jo has completed. “Ten is fine.”

“If the cloud lifts, we’re taking the telescope beyond the west breakwater. It’s been sitting in the van for weeks.”

The rain has weakened to a mist against the foyer glass. Jo eats her rice pudding while Nell finishes a sandwich, and the three of you remain at the table until lunch is over. Then Jo gathers her supplier sheets and carries them back behind the counter. Nell takes her mug into the office.

## Turn 20

Player: I let the conversation finish and go back to the catalogue. I work until the end of my agreed shift.

You return upstairs and continue with the programme box.

The afternoon’s run begins in January 1985. Most weeks have a single folded sheet, but Easter has three near-identical versions with different matinee times. One programme has been folded around an old bus timetable; another carries a thumbprint in blue paint beside a pencilled change from 7.15 to 7.30. You record the gaps and duplicates without trying to reconstruct the missing weeks.

At two, the side door closes below as Jo leaves. The rain clears soon afterwards, and pale light reaches across the worktable.

You complete the box-level record, then begin a second group covering late 1985 into 1986. Its paper band says **AUTUMN — INCOMPLETE**, which proves accurate: November stops midway through the month and resumes with a Christmas programme advertising two children’s matinees.

At four, the end of your agreed shift, you finish the entry already open. The programmes go back in their existing order, and you save the afternoon’s records before returning the boxes to the shelves.

## Turn 21

Player: The following morning I check with Nell for my next archive assignment and any practical changes I need to know about.

On Wednesday morning, you find Nell in the office updating the staff rota.

“What should I take next in the archive, and are there any practical changes I need to know about?”

“I’ve checked yesterday’s records. Finish the programme box you started, then take the next one on the shelf—the run from 1986 to 1988. Keep those at box level for now. I don’t need a separate entry for every duplicate.”

She returns the unsigned collection list from the yellow tray to its transfer file, with its status still marked as unconfirmed.

“Dev confirmed the auditorium before yesterday’s deadline. He and Hana completed the receiver test last night, so that booking is finished.”

Nell makes one alteration to the rota in ink.

“Owen’s inland appointment is definite now. He sent the shift change last night, and I’ve confirmed him for Friday instead of tomorrow.”

She closes the rota and gives you the reference sheet for the next programme box.

## Turn 22

Player: I say I am not available for additional front-of-house shifts; I want to keep the hours outside my archive contract for myself.

“I’m not available for additional front-of-house shifts,” you say. “I want to keep the hours outside my archive contract for myself.”

Nell looks up from the rota.

“Understood. I wasn’t asking you to cover Owen. His shift has moved rather than opened up, and your hours are archive hours.”

She sets the rota aside.
