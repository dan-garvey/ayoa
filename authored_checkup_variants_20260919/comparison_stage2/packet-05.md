# packet-05

## Turn 23

Player: If Owen is around during a break, I ask how his week is going. If he is not, I take my break without looking for him.

At quarter past ten, you come downstairs for a break. Owen is alone at one of the cafe tables with a mug of tea and the cinema’s new card reader in front of him. Its instruction leaflet is spread open, but he has put the machine aside.

You ask how his week is going.

“Busy. Better than Monday, though.” He turns the mug between his hands. “I’ve got an interview tomorrow for a permanent exhibition-technician job at a museum inland. I accepted it this morning.”

He glances towards the office, where Nell is still working.

“I told Dad last night. He asked how far away it was, then whether I’d checked the car before the drive. We haven’t discussed what happens if they offer me anything. They haven’t offered me anything, so that can wait.”

He takes a drink of tea.

“How’s the archive going?”

## Turn 24

Player: If we have been talking, I tell Owen I will see him tomorrow. Then I take a walk along the harbour on my own.

“I’ll see you tomorrow,” you tell Owen.

“Maybe. I’m not on shift, though—I moved it to Friday for the interview.”

He draws the card reader back towards him as you leave the table.

Outside, the rain has stopped. You go down Harbour Street and turn along the inner harbour, where the pavement runs beside a stone quay darkened by the morning weather. Fishing boats lie against their fenders below, decks crowded with stacked pots, blue rope and covered tubs. Diesel hangs in the air with salt and wet timber.

The tide is high enough to slap against the harbour steps. Across the basin, somebody hoses down a deck while two others sort a net over a waist-high rail. Farther inland, a refrigerated lorry reverses towards the food-packing depot, its warning signal carrying over the roofs.

You follow the quay as far as the view opens towards the breakwater. Beyond it, the sea is a flat grey under a brighter strip of sky. Wind presses at your charcoal coat and shakes water from the railings.

The circuit brings you back towards Harbour Street before eleven. A service van has stopped outside the Picture House, and a technician is unloading a case of fire-alarm testing equipment.
