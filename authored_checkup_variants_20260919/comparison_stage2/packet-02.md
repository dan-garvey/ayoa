# packet-02

## Turn 23

Player: If Owen is around during a break, I ask how his week is going. If he is not, I take my break without looking for him.

During your morning break, you lock the archive room and go downstairs. Jo’s delivery has been unloaded, and the side door is shut again. Boxes of dry goods stand inside the kitchen with supplier labels still attached.

Owen is at a cafe table with a mug of tea. A screwdriver and several clear programme holders lie beside him, but he has set the work aside while he drinks.

You ask, “How’s your week going?”

“It’s changed a bit since Monday.” He turns the mug between his hands. “I’ve got an interview tomorrow for a permanent exhibition-technician job at a museum inland.”

He says *interview* carefully, without treating it as an offer.

“I replied this morning. Nell’s confirmed that I’ll work Friday instead of Thursday. I told my dad on the phone last night, too.”

Owen takes another drink.

“He didn’t say much at the time. Ten minutes later he sent me the train times. This morning I got a link to the museum car park.”

Jo passes through the foyer carrying an invoice. She hears this but leaves the conversation to him.

“So that’s tomorrow,” Owen says. “The rest of the week is mostly getting these holders level before anyone puts programmes in them.” He nods towards the clear stands, then looks across at you. “How’s the archive going?”

## Turn 24

Player: If we have been talking, I tell Owen I will see him tomorrow. Then I take a walk along the harbour on my own.

“I’ll see you tomorrow,” you tell Owen.

“Friday, probably,” he says. “I’m at the museum tomorrow.”

He sets his mug aside and turns back to the programme holders. You leave him to the work.

Outside, you walk down Harbour Street alone. The pavement is still damp, and the wind carries salt, diesel and the sharper smell of fish from the quay. At the inner harbour, the tide has fallen far enough to expose a band of dark weed along the stone walls.

Two fishing boats are tied alongside, their decks crowded with coiled rope and stacked pots. A man in orange waterproof trousers hoses scales towards a drain while another checks plastic crates against a clipboard. Across the water, intermittent hammering comes from the boatyard sheds.

You follow the quay past a shuttered excursion kiosk and a row of benches facing the moorings. Gulls wait on the harbour rail, lifting aside only when you come close. Nobody joins you or draws you into conversation.

The circuit takes just over ten minutes. You return by the same wet street, let yourself in through the cinema’s side door and reach the archive room before your break is over. Downstairs, Owen has resumed fixing the clear holders to the foyer wall.
