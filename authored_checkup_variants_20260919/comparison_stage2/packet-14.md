# packet-14

## Turn 23

Player: If Owen is around during a break, I ask how his week is going. If he is not, I take my break without looking for him.

At eleven, you stop at the end of a catalogue entry and come downstairs for a break.

The sign contractor has gone. A folded ladder lies ready to be collected beside the front doors, and the replacement lamp sits indistinguishably among the others above the glass. Owen is at the cafe counter with a mug of tea and half an apple. His tape measure and drill are on the programme stand, but he is not using them.

You ask how his week is going.

“More complicated than it was yesterday.” He turns his phone over beside the mug. “I accepted an interview this morning. Permanent exhibition-technician job at a museum inland. Eleven tomorrow.”

He takes another bite of the apple.

“It’s only an interview. There isn’t a job to decide about unless they offer it. Nell knows now, and Jo knew when the email arrived.”

Owen looks towards the front doors rather than the office.

“My dad doesn’t. Martin, I mean. I need to tell him myself.”

## Turn 24

Player: If we have been talking, I tell Owen I will see him tomorrow. Then I take a walk along the harbour on my own.

“I’ll see you tomorrow,” you tell Owen.

“Friday,” he says. “I’m not here tomorrow. Nell moved the shift.”

He takes his mug back towards the kitchen, and you leave through the side door without asking anything further.

The lane brings you onto Harbour Street, then down to the inner harbour. The earlier cloud has broken apart, but the wind off the water catches at your charcoal coat and pushes loose hair across your forehead.

The tide is low enough to expose weed-darkened steps beneath the quay. Two fishing boats lie against their fenders while a crew unloads grey crates into a waiting van. Diesel, salt and wet rope carry across the road. On the opposite side of the water, sparks flash briefly inside one of the boatyard sheds.

You follow the harbour wall. Summer signs remain outside the cafes, though only one has its tables out. A refrigerated lorry turns inland towards the food-packing depot. Beyond the harbour mouth, the sea is a flat steel colour against the breakwater.

Near the end of the quay, a fingerpost marks the coast path in one direction and the old east pier in the other. You turn back before either route leaves the harbour, keeping the walk within your break.

When you return through the service yard, the ladder has been collected. Owen’s drill starts again in the foyer as you climb towards the archive.
