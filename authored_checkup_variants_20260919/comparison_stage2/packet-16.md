# packet-16

## Turn 23

Player: If Owen is around during a break, I ask how his week is going. If he is not, I take my break without looking for him.

During the morning break, the sign contractor’s tower stands outside the closed front doors. Martin is on the pavement with him, looking up at the unlit section while they compare two lamps.

Owen is inside at one of the cafe tables, his work gloves beside a mug. He has been unpacking fittings for the foyer shelves, but for the moment he is looking at his phone.

“How’s your week going?” you ask.

He turns the phone face down. “The work here’s fine. Tomorrow’s more complicated.”

After a pause, he says, “I’ve got an interview at a museum inland. Permanent exhibition technician. They build their displays in-house, so it’s close to what I used to do on trade shows, only without living out of a bag.”

Through the glass, Martin points towards the cinema sign. Owen watches him for a moment.

“I moved my shift to Friday. Nell doesn’t know why yet, and Dad doesn’t know I applied.” He looks back at the phone. “I have to accept the interview by noon. It’s only an interview, but I do want to go.”

## Turn 24

Player: If we have been talking, I tell Owen I will see him tomorrow. Then I take a walk along the harbour on my own.

“I’ll see you tomorrow,” you tell Owen.

“I won’t be here tomorrow,” he says. “That’s the shift I moved. Friday.”

He turns his phone face up again as you leave him at the table.

With the tower blocking the front entrance, you go out through the side door and follow the lane down to the inner harbour. The rain has passed, leaving the paving dark and the iron rings along the quay slick with water.

Fishing boats sit below street level on the falling tide. Their hulls knock softly against rubber fenders while gulls wait on the warehouse roofs. Farther around the basin, work carries from the boatyard: a power saw starting, metal struck twice, then the intermittent whine of a drill.

You walk past stacked lobster pots and a shuttered excursion kiosk. A delivery van idles outside the food-packing depot, its rear doors open while two workers move insulated crates onto a trolley. Nobody pays you particular attention.

Near the harbour mouth, a weathered fingerpost marks the coast path towards the eastern cliffs. You follow the quay as far as the first section of sea wall, where the water beyond the breakwater is darker and rougher than the sheltered basin, then turn back along the moorings.

Your circuit brings you to Harbour Street again, with the cinema one road behind the water.
