# packet-17

## Turn 23

Player: If Owen is around during a break, I ask how his week is going. If he is not, I take my break without looking for him.

At eleven, you come downstairs for a break. Owen is seated near the front windows with his tools packed into a closed case. The programme rack is fixed to the wall now, level despite the slope of the old plaster.

“How’s your week going?” you ask.

“Less uncertain than it was.” He nods towards the rack. “That’s finally finished. The museum interview is at eleven tomorrow, and they’ve sent the details. Portfolio first, then a short practical exercise.”

Beside his mug lies a folded piece of paper covered in Martin’s square handwriting. Owen turns it so you can read the brief note:

**I CAN PICK YOU UP AT 9.15. ROADWORKS ON THE INLAND ROAD. —DAD**

“I told him about the interview last night,” Owen says. He gives no account of the conversation itself. “He put that through my door this morning.”

Owen takes out his phone and sends a reply accepting the lift. Whatever Martin hoped the cinema job would become, he now knows Owen is interviewing elsewhere, and he has chosen to help him reach it.

“I’m going through my portfolio tonight,” Owen says. “I’ve got plenty of installation work to show them. I need to make sure they see more than somebody who can repair a crooked display stand.”

He finishes his tea and closes the tool case. When the break ends, he returns to the foyer fitting, and you go back upstairs to the catalogue.

## Turn 24

Player: If we have been talking, I tell Owen I will see him tomorrow. Then I take a walk along the harbour on my own.

“I’ll see you tomorrow,” you tell Owen.

“Friday, probably,” he says. “I’m not in tomorrow. Dad’s collecting me for the interview.”

He picks up his tool case, and you leave him to the programme rack.

Harbour Street slopes directly towards the water. You follow it down alone, past a newsagent and a closed amusement arcade, until the buildings give way to the inner quay.

The rain has stopped. Puddles hold broken pieces of sky, and a stiff wind carries the smells of diesel, wet rope and seaweed between the warehouses. Fishing boats rise and fall against their fenders. At one berth, two deckhands unload grey crates onto a waiting trolley while the boat’s engine idles beneath their shouted measurements.

You follow the harbour wall towards the breakwater. Across the water, workers move between the open sheds of the boatyard. Welding light flares briefly inside one doorway. Farther out, beyond the harbour mouth, the sea is the colour of slate.

There are other people along the quay—a delivery driver checking paperwork, two visitors studying a tide board, a woman exercising an elderly dog—but nobody joins you or asks for your attention.

At the end of the inner harbour, you turn back. The Picture House sign appears above the roofs as you climb Harbour Street, restored lettering still unlit in the daylight. You return before the end of your break and go back upstairs to the catalogue.
