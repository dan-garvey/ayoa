# packet-01

## Turn 23

Player: If Owen is around during a break, I ask how his week is going. If he is not, I take my break without looking for him.

At quarter past eleven, you finish the catalogue entry in front of you and go downstairs for your break.

Nell’s office door is closed. In the foyer, Owen sits at a cafe table with a mug of tea and a redrawn plan of the auditorium. His tools are packed beneath the chair, and the numbered seat labels are stacked to one side. He is on break as well.

You ask how his week is going.

“Not bad. I’ve spent most of this morning checking seat numbers against three different plans. None of them agrees with the actual seats.”

His new drawing is precise, with each row measured and numbered in small block figures.

“Thursday’s shift has been moved, so that’s sorted. Mostly I’m trying to get through the reopening without Dad volunteering me for every job that involves wood or a screwdriver.”

He takes a drink of tea.

“I do like working here. More than I expected, probably. That doesn’t mean I’ve decided what I’m doing after the temporary job ends, but Dad’s already talking as if I have.”

Owen turns his pencil between his fingers, then looks towards the staff staircase.

“How’s the archive going?”

## Turn 24

Player: If we have been talking, I tell Owen I will see him tomorrow. Then I take a walk along the harbour on my own.

“See you tomorrow,” you tell Owen.

“I’m not in tomorrow,” he says. “Friday.”

He returns to the auditorium plan without explaining what he will be doing instead. You leave him to his break and step out onto Harbour Street.

The morning rain has stopped, but the pavement remains dark and shining. You walk downhill towards the inner harbour alone. Fishing boats pull against their mooring ropes below the quay, their decks crowded with tubs, coiled lines and orange floats. Across the water, metalwork rings from the boatyard in short, irregular bursts.

The tide is high enough to lift the smaller boats level with the harbour wall. A crew unloads covered crates from a blue-hulled trawler while gulls wait on the roof of the fish shed. Farther along, the harbour opens towards a strip of restless grey sea.

You follow the quay past the chandlery and the locked excursion kiosk, then turn back before the road reaches the outer wall. Nobody stops you. By the time you return to the cinema, Owen has cleared his mug and taken the seat plan with him.

Nell’s office door remains shut for her rota calls. You go back upstairs before the end of your break.
