# packet-04

## Turn 19

Player: At lunch, if Bea is present, I ask what she enjoys about having the cinema open again. If she is not here, I eat with whoever is around.

Downstairs, Bea is at the cafe table with a blue accounts folder, a calculator and an open lunch box. A cheese sandwich sits beside two pickled walnuts. Nell is in the office speaking on the telephone.

You bring your lunch to the table and sit opposite Bea.

“What do you enjoy about having the cinema open again?”

Bea considers this while closing the calculator cover.

“I like that we’re buying cups now instead of roof felt. For two years, most of the money went on things people will only notice if they fail.”

She looks towards the front windows.

“I wanted the sign restored. It cost more than I’d planned to approve, and I still wanted it. Martin’s testing the timer tonight.”

She takes another bite of her sandwich.

“And I like Rosa putting names beside the old programme boards. They were always described as if the cinema painted them by itself.”

Bea pushes the accounts folder a little farther from her lunch.

“My granddaughter is coming for one of the opening-weekend screenings. We send each other dreadful films on secondhand DVDs, so she’s disappointed that our first programme looks respectable. I bought two seats in the middle row.”

Her finger rests on the closed calculator.

“I’m watching that film with her. Somebody else can count the box-office money.”

## Turn 20

Player: I let the conversation finish and go back to the catalogue. I work until the end of my agreed shift.

Lunch winds down over the remaining sandwiches. Bea reopens her accounts folder, finishes one calculation and carries the papers into Nell’s office.

You return upstairs to the programme box.

The afternoon’s work is a near-complete run of weekly sheets from 1985. Two weeks are missing. Several others survive in duplicate, with one copy kept clean and the other altered at the box office: show times crossed through, ticket prices changed, and one children’s matinee marked **SOLD OUT** in thick blue pencil.

A tea ring obscures part of a local printer’s address. Another sheet has been folded around a smaller notice announcing a cancelled performance after a boiler failure. You describe the notice separately while preserving its place inside the programme.

Nell reviews the queries in the yellow tray during the afternoon. The unsigned collection list remains part of the transfer documentation, but the signed schedule is cited as the evidence of ownership. She initials the query slip and leaves you to update the entry.

By the agreed end of your shift, the transfer box has a completed file-level record and the programme box has its first dated sequence entered in the catalogue. You save the work, return the box to its shelf and record your finishing time downstairs.

## Turn 21

Player: The following morning I check with Nell for my next archive assignment and any practical changes I need to know about.

On Wednesday morning, you find Nell in the office with the paper diary and staff rota open beside her. Owen’s Thursday shift has been moved to Friday and initialled by Nell. Dev’s provisional auditorium booking from Tuesday evening is marked **COMPLETED**.

You ask Nell for your next archive assignment and whether anything practical has changed.

“Carry on with the programme boxes today. Take P-three next—the label says 1986 to 1988, but check the actual range before you use it. Start with a box-level record, then separate out any clear dated runs.”

She turns the staff rota towards herself.

“Martin and Owen are fitting the foyer display frames this morning. They’ll have tools and packing across the front entrance, so use the side door until lunch. There’ll be some drilling below you.”

Nell marks the programme box on the archive list.

“Dev and Hana finished the receiver test last night, so the auditorium is free. Owen’s shift change is confirmed. Nothing there alters your hours.”

She gives you the box reference slip.

“If P-three contains anything that doesn’t look like a programme or related insert, keep it in place and add it to the query tray. I’ll check the tray before the end of the day.”

## Turn 22

Player: I say I am not available for additional front-of-house shifts; I want to keep the hours outside my archive contract for myself.

“I’m not available for additional front-of-house shifts,” you say. “I want to keep the hours outside my archive contract for myself.”

Nell looks up from the rota.

“All right. You’re here for the archive, and that’s where your thirty hours stay. I won’t add you to front of house.”

She closes the rota and leaves the programme-box reference beside you.

## Turn 23

Player: If Owen is around during a break, I ask how his week is going. If he is not, I take my break without looking for him.

At midmorning, the drilling stops. You come downstairs for your break and find Owen at one of the cafe tables with a mug of tea and a spirit level beside it. Two black poster frames lean against the foyer wall. Martin is in the service yard cutting narrow wooden packing strips.

“How’s your week going?” you ask.

“Busy. Tomorrow’s appointment is on, and Nell’s moved my shift to Friday. That part’s sorted.”

Owen glances towards the nearest frame.

“The wall bows out in the middle. Dad wants to pack the bottom corner; I think the top fixing needs moving. We’re trying both before either of us puts another hole in the plaster.”

He drinks some tea.

“I’ve told Nell I still finish at four on Friday. There’s a band playing that night and I’ve already got the ticket.”

He sets down the mug. “How’s it going upstairs?”

## Turn 24

Player: If we have been talking, I tell Owen I will see him tomorrow. Then I take a walk along the harbour on my own.

“I’ll see you tomorrow,” you tell Owen.

“Friday,” he says. “I’m not here tomorrow.”

You leave by the side door while Martin and Owen return to the display frame.

Harbour Street slopes towards the inner harbour. The rain has passed, leaving the paving dark and the gutters running. You walk beside the quay on your own, past coils of green rope, stacked fish crates and a row of boats tugging against their lines.

Two crewmen rinse the deck of a trawler with a hose. Farther along, a forklift carries shrink-wrapped pallets towards the packing depot. Diesel hangs in the air beneath the cleaner smell of salt and wet stone.

The route curves around the harbour office. Beyond the wall, the sea is iron-grey, with white water breaking against the outer rocks. You follow the railings as far as the end of the inner quay, then turn back.

The walk takes the rest of your break. As you return through the cinema’s service yard, the drilling begins again inside.
