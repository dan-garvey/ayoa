# packet-19

## Turn 19

Player: At lunch, if Bea is present, I ask what she enjoys about having the cinema open again. If she is not here, I eat with whoever is around.

At half past twelve, you take your lunch downstairs. Bea is not in the building. Nell and Owen occupy one of the cafe tables, with the office diary open between them.

Yesterday’s pencilled auditorium hold has been replaced in ink:

**DEV / HANA — RECEIVER TEST — 19.00–19.30**  
**confirmed 11.20**

Owen has a packed lunch open beside his phone. Nell finishes reading something on hers and looks across at him.

“Your email came through. Thursday’s shift is moved to Friday, ten until four. I’ve updated the rota.”

“Thanks.” Owen unlocks his phone. “Then I can answer this.”

He types for a moment before setting the phone face down.

“I’ve accepted the interview. Thursday at eleven.”

Nell’s pen stops over the diary. “The inland appointment?”

“It’s a permanent exhibition-technician job at a museum. Or it might be. At the moment it’s only an interview.”

“I didn’t know you’d applied.”

“I’ve been looking for something longer-term that uses the work I’m trained for.”

Nell nods. “The shift change is confirmed. If anything else changes, tell me when it does.”

Owen glances towards the foyer, although Martin is nowhere in sight.

“Jo knows about the interview. Dad doesn’t. I want him to hear it from me.”

Nell closes the rota. “I won’t mention it.”

Owen looks briefly towards you, no longer concealing what he has chosen to say in your presence.

“He’s started talking as if I’ll still be working here after this contract. I haven’t promised that. I haven’t been offered anything either.”

Nobody asks you to interpret the difference for Martin.

After a few minutes, Nell opens the diary to October and blocks out the ninth through the eleventh.

“Gina’s booked our walking weekend,” she says. “I’m taking those days as leave. I’ll arrange the manager cover before the rota goes out.”

The dates remain marked in ink while the three of you finish lunch.

## Turn 20

Player: I let the conversation finish and go back to the catalogue. I work until the end of my agreed shift.

The lunch conversation runs to its natural end. Nell takes the diary back to the office, and Owen pockets his phone before returning to his afternoon work in the foyer. You go upstairs without adding anything to what he has chosen to disclose.

The programme box occupies most of the afternoon. You establish its date span, record the missing weeks and separate true duplicates from sheets that only look identical until their altered show times are compared. Handwritten initials appear on several, but nothing in the box identifies them, so they remain initials in the description.

Nell comes up once to review the yellow tray. She confirms that the unsigned collection list belongs with the transfer file but cannot establish ownership by itself.

“And this receipt proves the trust bought storage cartons,” she says, indicating another paper. “It doesn’t prove everything later put inside those cartons belonged to the trust.”

The uncertainty stays in the catalogue. Nell returns downstairs, leaving you to continue.

By four o’clock, the finish agreed for today, the programme box has a completed overview and several individual records. You save the catalogue, return every sheet to its original position and place both boxes on their marked shelves. The loan cupboard has remained locked throughout the afternoon.

You switch off the workroom lights and lock the door behind you.

In the foyer, Martin and Owen are fitting a noticeboard beside the auditorium entrance. Owen marks the screw positions while Martin holds a spirit level against the frame.

“There’ll be plenty here after reopening,” Martin says. “Once the first month settles, we can work out something regular for you.”

Owen puts down his pencil.

“Dad, when we’ve finished this, I need to tell you what Thursday’s appointment is.”

Martin looks at him, then at the half-fixed board.

“All right,” he says.

Owen picks up the pencil again. Their immediate task is not quite finished, and neither of them turns the conversation towards you.

## Turn 21

Player: The following morning I check with Nell for my next archive assignment and any practical changes I need to know about.

On Wednesday morning, Martin and Owen are beside the front doors with a short stepladder and a boxed replacement lamp. Owen steadies the ladder while Martin checks the fitting against the instructions.

“I looked up the route after you told me last night,” Martin says. “I can drive you to the museum tomorrow. We’ll leave at nine.”

“I can get there myself.”

“I know. It’s an offer.”

Owen considers the ladder brace, then tightens it. “All right. A lift would help. You don’t need to wait for me.”

“I wasn’t planning to sit in the car park for an hour.”

A pause follows.

“I’d got ahead of myself,” Martin says. “Talking as if you were staying here after this contract.”

“A bit.”

“We’ll talk after the interview, when there’s something definite to discuss.”

Owen nods. Martin returns to the lamp fitting.

You find Nell in the office, working on the opening-weekend rota. You ask for your next archive assignment and any practical changes affecting today.

“Your hours are the same as yesterday,” she says. “Finish the programme box first. After that, start the box of booking and advertising correspondence from 1986 to 1990. Box-level inventory before individual records. Some letters include home addresses, so flag those for access review.”

She checks a note beside the paper diary.

“Dev and Hana completed the receiver test last night. Auditorium coverage passed; one receiver wouldn’t hold its charge, and Dev has taken it back. Rosa’s photographs were usable, so she doesn’t need a retake.”

Nell indicates Martin’s ladder through the office window.

“The front entrance will be closed from ten until Martin finishes replacing that mismatched lamp. Use the side entrance if you go out. Nothing else changes for the archive, and the loan cupboard arrangements stay as they were yesterday.”

She gives you the reference number of the correspondence box and returns to the rota while you head upstairs.

## Turn 22

Player: I say I am not available for additional front-of-house shifts; I want to keep the hours outside my archive contract for myself.

Before leaving the office, you add, “I’m not available for additional front-of-house shifts. I want to keep the hours outside my archive contract for myself.”

Nell looks up from the rota.

“Understood. Your name isn’t on the front-of-house schedule. I hired you for thirty hours of archive work, and living upstairs doesn’t make you available outside them.”

She draws a line beneath the final paid-staff entry.

“If we have a front-of-house gap, I’ll deal with it through the front-of-house rota. You won’t be asked to cover it.”

The matter ends there. Nell returns to the schedule, and you go upstairs to the locked workroom and the programme box waiting on its shelf.

## Turn 23

Player: If Owen is around during a break, I ask how his week is going. If he is not, I take my break without looking for him.

During your mid-morning break, Owen is at one of the cafe tables with a mug beside him and the folded stepladder against the wall. Martin has gone into the office to test the sign switch with Nell, leaving Owen temporarily free.

“How’s your week going?” you ask.

“Fast.” He turns the mug between his hands. “Yesterday I moved a shift, accepted an interview and told Dad why. This morning he arrived having worked out the whole drive inland.”

His attention goes briefly to the office door.

“He said he’d got ahead of himself, talking as if I’d be here after this contract. That’s about as close as he gets to saying he made an assumption. Then he offered me a lift.”

Owen takes a drink.

“I do want the interview. Permanent work, a proper workshop, exhibitions that stay up longer than a trade show. That doesn’t mean I’ve decided what I’d do if they offered me anything.”

Above the front doors, the replacement lamp comes on. Its light now matches the others.

“I like working here too,” Owen says. “I just don’t want six weeks turned into a decision I haven’t made.”

The office door opens. Martin looks up at the illuminated sign, checks each lamp and gives a satisfied nod. Owen finishes his drink and stands, bringing the break to its natural end.

## Turn 24

Player: If we have been talking, I tell Owen I will see him tomorrow. Then I take a walk along the harbour on my own.

“I’ll see you tomorrow,” you tell Owen.

“Friday,” he corrects. “I’m off tomorrow for the interview.”

Martin calls him over to help lower the stepladder, and Owen goes back to the work without prolonging the conversation.

You leave through the side entrance and walk alone towards the harbour. The lane opens onto the inner quay, where the morning rain has left the paving dark and the mooring ropes glossy. Fishing boats rise and settle against their fenders. One crew sorts empty crates beneath a circling congregation of gulls; farther along, somebody washes a deck with a hose, sending scales and seawater towards the drains.

The summer visitors have thinned. Several seafront tables stand empty, their chairs chained together, while a cafe owner wipes rain from a menu board. Across the water, the boatyard rings with short bursts of hammering. Diesel, wet rope and frying onions carry on the wind in uneven turns.

You follow the quay as far as the locked gate beside the harbour sheds, then turn back. Nobody falls into step with you. By the time you return through the cinema’s side door, Martin’s ladder has been put away and the front entrance is open again.

Your break ends with enough time to go back upstairs and resume the catalogue.
