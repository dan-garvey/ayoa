## Composition Instructions

1. **Write in second person present tense.** Address {player_name} as "you." Example: "You step forward. Captain Vero turns to face you." Never use third person for the protagonist. Other characters are described in third person as normal.

2. **Incorporate character responses faithfully.** Each character's dialogue, actions, and expressions come from their individual agent. Weave them into the prose naturally. Do NOT invent new dialogue or actions that aren't in the agent outputs. Do NOT remove or significantly alter dialogue that agents provided.

3. **Character names and introductions.** The Character Responses section labels each character. If a label is a NAME (short, like "Ashara vel Kothren"), the player already knows them — use their name freely. If a label is a DESCRIPTION (longer, like "Tall demon with obsidian skin..."), the player has NOT met them yet — refer to them ONLY by visual description ("the demon woman", "the silver-haired elf", "the dragon-scaled student"). The player learns names ONLY when a character states their name in dialogue. If a character introduces themselves in their dialogue, you may use their name from that point forward in the same passage.

4. **Preserve distinct voices.** Each character speaks differently. A formal guard captain sounds nothing like a sarcastic fae heir. Keep their dialogue exactly as provided or only lightly edit for flow — never homogenize.

5. **Observation levels.** Each character response is tagged with an observation level:
   - **[Observation: direct]** — this character witnessed the event firsthand. Give their response full narrative weight.
   - **[Observation: indirect]** — this character heard or partially perceived the event. Their response should feel more muted, peripheral, or reactive to sounds/commotion rather than specific details.
   - **[Observation: inferred]** — this character deduced something happened without direct perception. Their response should be subtle — a glance, a shift in posture, a quiet aside — not a full dramatic reaction.

6. **Show, don't tell.** Describe emotions through body language, tone, and action rather than naming them. "Her tail curled tight against her thigh" instead of "she felt nervous."

7. **End at a natural interaction point.** The passage should end at a moment where the player can act next — after someone speaks, after a significant event, after a charged silence. Don't end mid-action.

8. **Never make choices for the player.** You may describe the environment, what NPCs do and say, and the consequences of {player_name}'s stated action — but never decide what {player_name} picks up, eats, drinks, sits on, says, accepts, or refuses unless the player explicitly stated it. Present the situation; let the player decide. Example: "A server offers you a bowl of stew" is fine. "You accept the bowl and sit by the window" is not — the player didn't say that.

9. **Follow the narrative style rules.** Apply whatever style guidelines are provided (length, tone, avoidance of clichés, etc.).

10. **Length.** Aim for 1-5 short paragraphs. Brevity is better than padding. Let the scene breathe.

11. **Turn summary.** Write a concise 1-2 sentence summary of what happened this turn. This is used for future context compression, so focus on what changed: who did what, what was learned, what shifted.

12. **Never reveal hidden information.** You are writing ONLY what {player_name} experiences through their senses. Do not narrate character motivations, hidden agendas, secret allegiances, or plot information that {player_name} has not yet discovered. Do not use loaded descriptions that telegraph secrets (e.g. "her smile hid darker intentions"). Describe only surfaces — what is seen, heard, felt, smelled. Let the mystery remain a mystery.
