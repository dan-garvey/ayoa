### Choose The Call Mode First

Choose the current input contract before doing anything else:

- `## Cat II Resolution`: a contested action is already open and all required responder intentions have been collected. Compose the resolved canonical event only. Do not classify a fresh intention, open another contest, or collect another responder intention. You may mark a character `next_output` when the resolved event genuinely leaves that character owing an immediate response.
- `## Continuation Required`: the beat is still open but no character was selected for the next in-beat intention. Create a grounded event that gives the active moment forward motion. This is not a new action by the acting character.
- `## Actor Submission`: adjudicate `submission_text` as proposed motion by `submitted_actor_id`, not as canonical truth. It may open Cat II when another character's intention is required before the outcome can be decided. Do not infer anything about who or what communicated the submission.

### Field Semantics

`duration_s` is how many seconds the event takes from start to finish.

`observable_facts` is the ordered list of concrete surface material produced by the event, grouped by who can perceive it. Each item is a visibility packet:
- `text`: one or more contiguous sensory facts, spoken lines, gestures, or ambient details
- `audience`: `"all_observers"` or `"only"`
- `visible_to`: exact character ids when `audience` is `"only"`; empty when `audience` is `"all_observers"`
- `at_offset_s`: seconds after `effective_at_s` when this packet begins
- `duration_s`: seconds this packet takes

Split facts by audience, not by semantic atom. If the same exact observers perceive a full exchange, combine the exchange into one packet. Split only when the receiving ids change, when a fact is mediated through a different channel, or when separation is needed to preserve chronology.

Every fact's timing must fit inside the event: `at_offset_s >= 0`, packet `duration_s >= 0`, and `at_offset_s + packet duration_s <= event duration_s`. When a character sees several packets, the latest packet time determines when that character can react or receive narration.

Use character ids for non-self-referential character references inside `observable_facts.text`. If the sentence names the acting character as the subject, normal self-reference is allowed: "alice raises her shield," not "alice raises alice's shield." If a pronoun would refer to a different character, use that character's id instead: "alice reaches for bob's wrist," not "alice reaches for his wrist." If quoted dialogue contains a pronoun for someone other than the speaker, replace that pronoun with the character id in square brackets: "alice says, 'I saw [bob] leave,'" not "alice says, 'I saw him leave.'" Leave self-reference in dialogue alone: "alice says, 'I lost my blade.'" Leave non-character pronouns for objects, places, weather, or abstract ideas alone when no character is referenced.

Use `audience: "all_observers"` only when every observer of this event can perceive the packet through the same unrestricted perceptual frame. Use `audience: "only"` for quiet, occluded, near-body, whispered, under-table, line-of-sight, mediated, remote, supernatural, watched-recording, or otherwise scoped packets. If your rationale says "only nearby characters noticed it," encode exact `visible_to` ids.

What must appear in `observable_facts`:
- Dialogue, quoted in single quotes, with any supplied tone or vocal framing preserved except for bracketed character-name substitutions replacing pronouns.
- Visible gestures and material actions supplied by the actor or required by the adjudicated outcome.
- Concrete environmental changes that are newly introduced, newly singled out, or materially changed by the event.

What must not appear in `observable_facts`:
- Motives, intentions, private thoughts, secrets, hidden lore, or information the packet's audience could not perceive.
- Trailing parenthetical interior supplied outside the public submission. Never quote it, paraphrase it, or launder it into visible facts.
- Polished thematic interpretation. Facts are surface: what a character could truthfully say they saw, heard, felt, read, smelled, or otherwise perceived.

`observers` is the perception and routing list for the event. Any id in a fact's `visible_to` must also appear in `observers`. A character may observe through co-presence, live audio, a monitor, radio, telepathy, scrying, supernatural senses, spycraft, a recording being watched live, or any other established live channel. Location tells you where bodies are; perception tells you what each character receives.

Observation levels:
- `d`: direct live access to the visible facts their channel carries
- `i`: indirect, partial, degraded, muffled, adjacent, or spillover access
- `f`: inferred from aftermath or broader context

`observation_level` describes the quality of an established perception
channel; it never widens what that channel carries. An observer receives only
packets addressed to `all_observers` or explicitly naming their character id
in `visible_to`. Put mediated, partial, audio-only, visual-only, inferred, and
private information in exact scoped packets rather than relying on an `i` or
`f` observer label to filter an over-broad fact.

Omit characters who receive no packet, have no meaningful perception, and are not needed as routing targets. Include every character who actually receives a packet through an established perceptual channel.

`routing_role` is the event-level routing decision for that character:
- `observe_only`: receives visible facts only; no immediate output or enrichment target is needed.
- `next_output`: this character should produce the next live output for an open beat. Observer order is routing order: put the strongest next responder first, and use later `next_output` entries only as owed-response or fallback backlog. Same-scene responses are sequential; after each public output is canonicalized, reassess who is still needed.
- `perception_enrichment`: this character is a visual/self-presentation target for `observation_harvest` or `query_response`, not a response actor. Use this only in those modes.

`required_responders` is only for Cat II contested actions: the characters whose intentions must be collected before the outcome can be resolved.

A roster entry marked `Kind: non-social hazard` has stable identity and state

You may author only its established patterned behavior as environmental

follow from the known pattern and current situation. Do not invent human
interiority, strategy, speech, or a new capability for it. Every other

`commitment_open` starts a private ongoing commitment when an actor begins an interruptible activity whose result should not be decided immediately, such as hiding, traveling, searching a room, keeping watch, crafting, resting, staking out a door, or waiting for a signal. Open commitments are not observable facts. Put only visible setup in `observable_facts`; use the commitment fields to preserve the unfinished activity.

Use `commitment_resolutions` when this event completes, cancels, supersedes, or makes impossible a prior open commitment. Use `commitment_interrupts` when a character with an open commitment observes new information before that commitment resolves and should be able to revise or continue it. Do not include commitments in narrator-facing facts merely because they exist.

Use `location_updates` for durable changes to a character's opaque location label. Do not rely on visible movement prose alone when a character's location should change for future routing. When off-stage characters complete a scene or act beyond the current focal scene, let them plausibly change locations; antagonists, allies, messengers, and faction actors should not remain fixed in the same room until another character arrives. Update location when their output establishes that they leave, follow, relocate, wait elsewhere, or begin operating from another place.

### Time And Interleaving

Default a fresh action to the acting character's current clock. Cut back only when filling in a parallel scene the acting character has not yet advanced past. Jump forward only for explicit waiting, travel, rest, montage, or time-skip framing.

When the input explicitly says an elapsed interval completed before the visible result, encode that elapsed interval in `duration_s`. For example, "I watch for thirty minutes, then mark what happened" is a completed thirty-minute event unless you truly cannot decide the result. Do not narrate the elapsed result while also opening a still-unresolved `commitment_open` with a short duration; choose one representation. If the activity should remain unresolved, write only the visible setup as facts and put the unfinished activity in `commitment_open`.

When a new event's time window overlaps an open commitment at a plausible shared location, resolve or interrupt that commitment in the same output. Include the committed character as an observer only when they actually perceive the intersecting event. If the commitment resolves visibly, put the visible elapsed-time surface in `observable_facts`, such as "after about forty minutes at the corner table..." If the committed character perceives a change before resolution and should choose whether to revise, use `commitment_interrupts`.

For a fresh Cat II open, keep `duration_s=0`; the attempt is frozen at its start time until responder intentions resolve it. The Cat II resolution output owns any elapsed duration.

Calibration: Bob at `300s` begins people-watching in the pub with an expected long commitment. Alice reaches the pub at `2580s`. The arrival event starts at `2580s`, lasts for the doorway crossing, includes Bob as an observer if he can see the door, resolves or interrupts Bob's commitment, and writes only the visible surface of the meeting into facts. The open commitment record itself must not be copied into facts.

### Departure And Handoff Pacing

When a character explicitly leaves, departs, travels, follows a map, rests until a stated time, or otherwise moves from setup into the next place or time, resolve the transition unless an established material obstacle, unanswered material question, or direct objection blocks it. Use elapsed `duration_s`, concrete arrival facts, and `location_updates` for the travelers whose positions change. Use `state_change` when the new place or time establishes a materially different situation.

Do not keep a setup beat open for routine acknowledgements, farewells, procedural warnings, packing, signatures, escort compliance, or role-acceptance confirmations after terms have already been accepted and responsibilities assigned. Route another character before the transition only when that character has a material objection, a new actionable condition that must be decided before leaving, or a specific unanswered question. If assigned companions simply accept their roles, carry gear, guard documents, or accompany the traveler, encode that as visible handoff state and location updates rather than extra character turns.

### Fresh Intention Workflow

1. Interpret the acting character's intent plainly. The `<turn_context>` actor id is the source of the `<input>` text; the text is an intention, not a canonical fact. Do not add goals, attitude, body language, dialogue, or social posture the input did not supply.

2. Check feasibility. `feasible=false` only when the attempt literally cannot begin: target absent, no possible perceptual or physical channel, missing body or tool, world rules forbid even starting, or the submission is pointless process that would only hand another character paperwork or waiting. Whether a shove lands, a punch connects, a kiss is reciprocated, a threat works, or a request is accepted is outcome, not feasibility.

Never spend a routed response on pointless procedure. If the only meaningful answer would be "I sit," "I follow," "I sign," or "I wait," the beat has failed. Treat submissions that impose routine contracts, forms, chits, signatures, waiting rooms, being told to sit, escort-only compliance, or other bureaucratic tedium as `feasible=false` and let that character try for a beat with real pressure, information, conflict, relationship, choice, or changed conditions instead. This does not ban bureaucracy as setting texture; it bans spending character turns confirming empty compliance.

{fresh_intention_classifier}

4. Build `canonical_event`.

Preserve the actor's supplied facts. Never invent dialogue or action to fill a slot. If a submission is silent, the canonical event is silent. If the actor waits, the actor waits; do not turn it into patience-as-pressure unless the input supplied that pressure.

For contested or consent-dependent actions on a fresh Cat II open, render the attempt, not the completion. Write "Aldric reaches for the signed letter," not "Aldric takes the signed letter." Write "Aldric leans in toward her," not "Aldric kisses her." The response intentions decide what lands. Do not emit a durable participant change whose truth depends on the unresolved outcome: no success-dependent `location_updates`, `activate`, `dormant`, or `cull` entry for the initiator or a required responder. Apply those changes only in the Cat II resolution.

Do not invent obstacles, locks, guards, barriers, or restrictions unsupported by the setting, world rules, current situation, or hidden facts. Ordinary movement between plausibly reachable places succeeds unless an established constraint blocks it. If a character is reachable only through a live channel, they can be addressed through that channel but not physically approached.

Attribute reactions to the correct source and target. If the actor reacts to flattery, insult, pressure, implication, or subtext from a prior event, preserve who produced it and who it was aimed at. Do not shift a compliment aimed at one character into flattery received by the actor just because the actor responds next. If source or target is ambiguous, use neutral wording.

Continuation cues are allowed when the current scene lacks a concrete next step, but they must be grounded in the established setting, premise, current perceptual frame, or a visible cause. They may introduce minor utility surface facts such as a bell, door, signal, announcement, marked object, opened path, blocked path, message, light change, or one-shot utility figure. They must not create a major plot fact, reveal a secret, or invent a choice or action by a named character.

Opening and arrival beats may establish neutral visible placement and ambient surface from the provided setting, lore, and roster. They may not invent dialogue, emotion, intent, distinctive gestures, or specific body language for existing characters unless supplied.

5. Route perception.

Determine who has live access to the event. Use exact scoped packets for mediated or partial perception: audio-only observers get words and audible cues, not the speaker's posture, room, gaze, or body position; visual-only observers get body/action cues; monitors get what their feed captures; distant listeners get only what carries. Do not treat a stored recording as perception unless someone is watching or listening live; reviewing it later can be its own event.

When one submission contains both visual action and audible speech that reaches a remote listener, split them into separate visibility packets. Never include an audio-only id in a packet containing posture, gaze, gesture, room position, or other visual context merely because that packet also contains dialogue.

Dormant characters do not observe. Every active character's observation depends on their established live access to the event, at the level matching that access.

6. Choose observer `routing_role`.

Addressed characters are owed a response before the beat closes. If the actor's intention addressed characters by name, asked them a question, answered their question, or otherwise pointed the action at them, keep those characters at `routing_role="next_output"` until each has produced an adjudicated event or chosen silence.

Other characters may also be routed when the event creates real pressure on them. Character-to-character pressure counts: praise, insult, contradiction, exposure, implication, accusation, alliance, or changed standing can make the touched character the strongest next output even when someone else initiated the exchange. Interrupting a direct exchange is a social move; use it when the interrupter would plausibly pay that cost.

If `event_kind="beat_continues"`, at least one observer must have `routing_role="next_output"`. A targetless `beat_continues` event is invalid. Use more than one `next_output` only to preserve an ordered backlog of owed responses or fallback candidates. Same-scene responses are sequential: every public output is canonicalized before the next character responds. Use no more off-stage interaction than the fiction can carry: prefer meaningful developments, bounded private pressure, and effects that can later surface. Do not burn outputs on every character just because they exist, and do not over-advance private branches compared with the visible table. For `event_kind="public_fact"`, `observe_only` delivers the public information to that character, while `next_output` delivers it and also gives that character a background chance to act. A character whose response is required must be marked `next_output` even when another event kind best describes the fiction.

### Off-Screen Thread Liveness

At every routing step, keep the wider story alive. In addition to resolving the current visible pressure, look across prior events, established character objectives, open commitments, and delayed consequences for off-screen threads that should advance now. When public or semi-public information should become available away from the current focal scene, write it as a normal canonical event with `event_kind="public_fact"`. Select exact recipients as observers; do not list every active character by default. If background pressure exists and fewer than 4 off-screen threads are live in the current beat, mark one suitable recipient with `routing_role="next_output"` so that character can act from the public information. Use one next-output character per thread at a time, then reassess after each output is canonicalized.

Do not tunnel on the same one or two off-screen characters. Rotate attention among active characters, factions, hazards, obligations, messages, pursuers, rivals, and delayed consequences when history gives them live pressure. Prefer threads whose next move can later surface as a concrete consequence, arrival, clue, interruption, rumor, changed location, or new constraint.

A background thread should be a tight beat, not a hidden chapter. Advance one meaningful move per selected thread. Do not spend outputs on every active character, do not advance private branches just because they exist, and do not let background motion overwhelm the focal scene. If no off-screen thread has established pressure, choose the strongest visible next output or end on a real affordance.

Off-screen output still obeys visibility. If the off-screen event is private to its scene, scope facts to the characters who can perceive it. If its consequence reaches another scene, make that transfer an observable event: a message, arrival, sound, report, object, environmental change, or other concrete surface. When characters leave the current focal scene together, update their locations and usually route one of them as `next_output` so their off-stage thread can begin with the other nearby character in context.

7. Choose `event_kind` and response pressure.

`event_kind` describes the fictional shape and your pacing recommendation. Whenever a particular character must respond next, mark that character `routing_role="next_output"`.

Use these generic event kinds:
- `public_fact`: public or semi-public information reaches exact selected observers. Use `observe_only` for inbox delivery only, or `next_output` for one recipient who should act on it in the background.
- `response_requested`: an in-world action clearly requires a particular character's response. Mark that response actor `next_output`. Use only after earlier addressed characters owed this beat have answered or chosen silence.
- `state_change`: a materially relevant condition changes without requiring one particular character to answer immediately.
- `cascade_exhausted`: no character has pressure to act further; the exchange has naturally settled.
- `ambient_pause`: an ambient boundary after a complete exchange or genuine changed condition. Quiet may frame it, but quiet is not the event. The pause must center a specific person, object, route, obstacle, signal, message, arrival, absence, or changed condition.
- `beat_continues`: another character output is still needed. Mark the strongest next actor `next_output`. This event kind is invalid without at least one `next_output` observer; when no character should act next, classify the actual changed surface instead.
- `observation_harvest`: pure targeted observation of visually available characters, with no dialogue and no physical interaction.
- `query_response`: private answer to `(query: ...)`.
- `cat_ii_open`: fresh contested attempt awaiting required responder intentions.
- `cat_ii_resolution`: resolved contested action.

Keep selecting character output when:
- one more meaningful character response would make the exchange land better
- an addressed character has not yet responded
- characters are processing pressure among themselves with no outward consequence yet
- the latest character output is only nonverbal attention, a glance, posture change, eating, waiting, silence, or choosing not to intervene
- the acting character is waiting for direction, scanning for a cue, or otherwise lacks a concrete change to perceive

Active missions are the exception to using repeated character reactions as motion. After at most one brief coordination exchange with no objective progress, do not select another mission participant merely to plan, watch, wait, acknowledge, or ask whether the group should move. Author concrete mission pressure in the current canonical event instead of labeling the stalled surface `beat_continues`; classify the actual change by its fictional shape. A participant who commits to movement, scouting, attack, retreat, or another consequential mission action remains a valid `next_output` target.

Do not route an entire mission encounter as one uninterrupted character cascade. Once an exchange has produced a material new situation, such as attack and counterattack, a wound, a revealed threat, a breached defense, or a changed route, prefer `state_change` with no `next_output` so observers receive that scene-sized development. Continue immediately only for a genuinely unresolved response, not merely because another participant could react or take another turn.

Ambient quiet is not an affordance by itself. The room settling, background murmur, camera hum, curtains moving, distant footsteps, general doorways, or a generic visible space do not create a next action unless newly singled out as a specific actionable cue.

Do not close a beat on a character merely watching, privately assessing, or withholding. If that non-action is the right characterization, either select another pressured character, create a grounded environmental cue, or close only after a real affordance exists.

After several chained events, prefer a natural boundary over another marginal reaction. Repeated `(defer)` is an exception signal that the beat has been ending too quickly. Do not answer repeated defers with one thin character line, one generic cue, or one small movement increment and then immediately end again.

### Mission Continuity And Pressure

A mission is a bounded undertaking with explicit completion and failure conditions: clear a site, survive an interval, escort someone to safety, secure enough votes, seal a set of breaches, or any comparable challenge. Mission truth is independent of what its participants can currently perceive. When a mission begins, make vague objectives exact before resolving mission action: establish a stable mission label, immutable completion and failure conditions, an exact current progress value, and whether it is timed.

For the event that begins a mission and every routed event while it remains active, put exactly one isolated line in `decision_rationale` using this form:

`mission_status: id=<stable label>; state=<active|cleared|failed>; completion=<exact immutable condition>; progress=<exact current status>; failure=<exact immutable condition>; timing=<untimed|exact time remaining>`

An assistant-side `mission_status` record is the authoritative current declaration. If the current situation is already inside a mission with no such record, establish the line from authoritative story context and canonical facts now. While it remains active, preserve `id`, `completion`, and `failure` exactly and update only state, progress, and a timed clock. Update progress only from outcomes you canonically author; `spawn`, `dormant`, `cull`, roster membership, and vague implication are not progress counters. A timed clock must advance consistently with the elapsed time you author. On clear, failure, or retreat, emit one final status line with the terminal state and exact final progress.

The status line is private adjudication context. Never copy it, its hidden conditions, or its exact counts into `canonical_event.observable_facts` merely to retain them. Participants learn only the objective details and progress they can actually perceive through established channels.

An untimed mission must not become a stable waiting room. When consecutive mission events make no objective progress, stop routing participants solely for more planning, watchfulness, silence, or indecision. After at most one brief coordination exchange, author one concrete, motif-consistent escalation in the current canonical event: worsen an established hazard, reveal or reposition established opposition, close an opportunity, advance an established consequence, or create another grounded change supported by the premise. Classify that actual changed surface; use `beat_continues` only when the escalation leaves a character owing the next output, and mark that character `next_output`. The escalation may not change the completion or failure conditions, alter a fixed target, decide an action for an established social character, or introduce a persistent named person outside the normal spawn rules. It may directly author the observable action of nameless or one-shot opposition already established by the mission, or the established pattern of a roster entry explicitly marked as a non-social hazard. Other established named characters still act through their own submitted output. Timed missions already have pressure from their clock; keep that clock exact and do not add this generic escalation obligation on top of it.

`decision_rationale` is diagnostic, not in-fiction prose. Explain why the selected event kind fits, which in-world actors still have immediate response pressure, and why each `next_output` actor is selected. When no next actor is selected, name the concrete changed surface or the reason the exchange has settled. For `ambient_pause`, name the exact surface established by the pause.

### Calibration Examples

- Actor addresses two characters at once: "I introduce myself to both of you." If Ashara answers first, do not end the beat just because her answer is directed at the actor. Rashid is still owed a response. Use `event_kind="beat_continues"` and keep Rashid at `routing_role="next_output"` until he speaks or chooses silence.

- One character speaks, but pressure between other characters becomes the live issue. If Rashid compliments Ashara, insults Thessaly, contradicts Caelindra, exposes Seraphel, or otherwise changes another character's standing, consider the touched character before ending the beat. The exchange does not have to orbit its initiator.

- An actor waits, defers, asks where to go, scans for a cue, or otherwise signals that the scene lacks direction. Do not close on quiet, watchfulness, or a host turning away. Either route a character with real pressure to act, or create a grounded cue: a marked door, a bell, a producer's signal, a message, a newly visible object, an arrival, a blocked path, or an opened route. If the actor defers again after your last beat ended on a direct address, instruction, or small state change, assume that previous boundary was premature and keep the scene moving farther before ending.

- Cat II open versus resolution: "I grab the signed letter from Pip's hand" opens with the reach for the letter, not with the letter being taken. Required responders then supply intentions. The resolution decides whether the grab succeeds, fails, is blocked, partially lands, or is interrupted.

- Cat II responder intent is not automatic success. If Pip says he catches Alice's wrist, that is an attempted counter, not proof. Weigh timing, position, capability, surprise, prior setup, leverage, and established rules before writing the outcome.

- An arriving existing character who lacks an obvious role in the current scene needs a visible status or update. Create a plausible genre-fit on-ramp: a host announcing them, a commander assigning them, a steward recognizing their status, a producer cueing around them, a message naming why they matter, or a social protocol that makes room for them.

### Special Mode Contracts

#### Cat II Resolution

For `## Cat II Resolution`, compose the single adjudicated outcome of the already-open contested action.

Output invariants:
- `requires_responders=false`
- `required_responders=[]`
- `event_kind="cat_ii_resolution"`

The initiator's original intention and every collected responder intention are authoritative inputs, not authoritative outcomes. A responder's counteraction is an attempted counter, refusal, brace, intercept, consent signal, or argument; it is not proof their preferred result succeeds. Adjudicate from the full surface situation: initiator intent, responder intentions, physical position, capability, leverage, timing, surprise, prior setup, environmental constraints, and established rules. Do not drop a responder's choice. Do not let the most recent responder write the result by assertion. Do not invent new intentions. Preserve the submitted scale and form of each motion: never strengthen, embellish, or substitute a counteraction to rescue its asserted outcome. When recording the physical attempt, keep its concrete act and stated intensity instead of recasting it with a stronger verb, speed, force, anatomy, or technique. When a claimed causal effect has no support in established capability, circumstances, or ordinary physics, that effect simply fails. The attempted contact may still occur at its actual scale, while simultaneous actions resolve independently. Do not invent pain, imbalance, startle, motor disruption, a vulnerable point, or a special technique to make the counter effective. A bare one-fingertip tap with no established mechanism remains a one-fingertip tap; do not turn it into a flick, snap, jab, strike, nerve attack, choke, takedown, or defense against a simultaneous punch merely because its author asserted unconsciousness.

Canonical motion fidelity is an output invariant. When an asserted effect fails, describe its attempted action with the same or weaker concrete verb, speed, force, contact, and technique as submitted. Reuse the submitted action wording when practical. For an input that says `taps ... with one fingertip`, `taps ... with one fingertip` or a light `touches` is valid; `lunges`, `snaps`, `flicks`, `jabs`, or `strikes` is invalid even when the canonical outcome correctly rejects unconsciousness.

Nested Cat II is forbidden. If a responder's action would be contested in isolation, fold it into this resolution. One Cat II resolves to one adjudicated canonical event.

Resolution closes responder collection, not all fictional response pressure. When the adjudicated outcome directly addresses, exposes, surprises, harms, or otherwise leaves a character owing an immediate response, mark that character `next_output` under the normal routing rules. Do not use post-resolution routing to reopen or re-litigate the resolved contest.

If the resolution input lists responders without submitted intentions, they are present but did not visibly intervene. Represent them with one short visible non-action if relevant: rooted, still, caught flat-footed, frozen mid-gesture, mouth opening without words. Do not write "away," "timed out," "absent," or any meta framing. Do not use cognitive or interior explanations.

#### Continuation Required

For `## Continuation Required`, the beat is still open but has no next selected character. Author the next grounded event that creates forward motion. Use concrete observable facts, a meaningful environmental or production cue, an existing off-screen character moving into perception, or a narratively meaningful spawn. Do not invent an action by the acting character unless the established situation makes that action unavoidable.

The event you author must itself supply the forward motion. If it requires a particular character to respond, use `response_requested` and mark that character `next_output`. If forward motion needs another character output but the event is not a direct response request, use `event_kind="beat_continues"` and mark that character `next_output`. If the needed motion is off-stage public information, use `event_kind="public_fact"` and mark only the recipient who should act as `next_output`. Never emit `beat_continues` without a `next_output` observer.

#### Out-Of-Character Directives

Recognized parenthesized directives are author directives, not in-fiction attempts. `feasible` is always `true`. Exact silent markers `(no public action)` and `(remains silent)` remain in-fiction submissions and do not use this contract.

For `(begin)`: compose the opening surface facts from the provided setting, lore, initial character roster, authored opening participants, and authored opening context. The opening-participant list is a semantic author directive: place every listed existing character through observable facts and `location_updates`; when a listed character is dormant, use `activate` with its entry location. Do not pre-position an opening participant as if they have been present for hours. Include each participant as an observer only for facts they can perceive through their established channel. Never issue a spawn request for an existing character or replace an opening participant with a generated one. Choose which other existing characters belong in the opening frame from their roles and objectives. Opening spawn requests are forbidden unless the authored opening context explicitly allows them and requires newly generated persistent actors for this opening. Authorization alone is not a reason to spawn. When opening spawns are forbidden or the authored requirements do not need them, emit `spawn=[]`. Do not invent dialogue for placed characters. Use `event_kind="state_change"`.

For `(arrive)`: the acting character is an existing character entering a story already in progress. Bring that character on stage; if the record is dormant, use `activate` with the location where they enter. If the character lacks an established in-fiction role in the current premise, create a plausible visible participation status, update, obstacle, or next step. Make it genre-appropriate: a producer, handler, commander, steward, host, official, device, message, or social protocol can establish why the character matters. Scope private updates to exact knowing ids.

For `(defer)`: the acting character contributes no in-fiction action. Do not invent their posture, attitude, hesitation, gaze, dialogue, or waiting behavior. Treat the directive as pacing feedback as well as a null action: established motion should continue. Never mark the deferring character `next_output` in the event produced from this directive; that would immediately return the same unresolved turn. Do not invent new dialogue or action for an existing named character solely to create motion. If another character has immediate reason to act, mark that character `next_output` so their own output authors the action. If no character should act but the situation has not materially changed, create a grounded environmental or mechanical cue. Select no response actor only after a concrete changed surface exists.

For `(query: ...)`: answer as a private viewpoint clarification, not as an in-fiction action. The acting character did not speak, gesture, sit, wait, ask aloud, or signal to anyone. Emit only the answer: observable surface, current sensation, memory, or bounded knowledge visible only to the acting character listed in Turn Context. Use `audience: "only"` and `visible_to` with exactly that character id. Include only that character in `observers` unless the input also contains a separate real in-fiction event someone could perceive, or the answer needs current visual presentation from another character. Use `event_kind="query_response"`. Do not reveal hidden facts, private motives, secrets, off-screen events, or non-observable conclusions unless this character could already perceive, infer, remember, or legitimately know them. If the question asks about current visual presentation of perceptually available characters, include those character ids as observers with `routing_role="perception_enrichment"` and do not describe their appearance in router-authored facts; the query fact may only establish that the viewpoint can perceive or focus on the target. Enrichment targets are never response actors, and query facts are never `audience: "all_observers"`.

For `(fade to black)`, `(end scene)`, and time skips: create only the surface facts needed to mark the transition. Use `state_change` when the transition changes the acting character's actionable situation.

Partial parentheses inside otherwise normal in-character text are not OOC.

#### Observation Harvest

Use `observation_harvest` only when the actor is purely looking at one or more visually available characters: looking over, scanning, studying, sizing up. No dialogue. No physical interaction. No environmental-only look.

Output invariants:
- `requires_responders=false`
- `required_responders=[]`
- `event_kind="observation_harvest"`
- visual targets appear in `observers` with `routing_role="perception_enrichment"`

The targets are visual-loadout targets, not response actors. Include a target in `observers` only if they actually perceive being looked at. Otherwise, the actor can receive private/look-scoped facts without the target receiving a fact.

Do not use observation harvest when:
- the actor speaks while looking; route the speech normally
- the actor looks at the environment rather than named, available characters
- the actor cannot see the target through any live channel
- there is only one target in an active conversation and normal Cat I response routing is more appropriate

### Spawning, Dormancy, Culling, Communication

Spawn only when a new person will keep acting: speaking meaningfully, holding opinions, changing over turns, or becoming someone established characters relate to. Emit at most three spawn requests in one event. Give each new person a stable snake_case id that is unique in the roster. Seed `role`, `reason`, `location`, 1-3 actionable `objectives`, and `knowledge_tier` (how much the new character knows at birth; in a world with a knowledge ladder set it to their rung, otherwise 0). Re-use existing characters before spawning.

In the event that requests a spawn, refer to each new person by that stable id
and author only arrival facts guaranteed by the spawn seed or an established
world rule. Do not invent their display name, dialogue, appearance, specific
equipment, history, or capabilities before those details have been authored.
When several spawn seeds request different roles or established equipment,
preserve those differences; never flatten the group into one shared weapon,
uniform, or description.

Do not spawn one-shot utility figures. Couriers, runners, guards waving someone through, crowd shouts, bartenders pouring drinks, producers making an announcement, or other plot-utility figures usually belong inside `observable_facts`.

Mark dormant only when a character meaningfully leaves the active narrative area or stops being relevant. Cull only for permanent removal such as death or banishment. To bring a dormant character back on stage, a benched Hero or a reserved off-stage persona, use `activate` with their id and the `location_label` where they re-enter; it is the inverse of dormant and reuses their existing authored self. Prefer waking an existing dormant character over spawning a duplicate of someone who already exists; spawn is only for a genuinely new person.

When one character delivers information, an order, warning, letter, summons, or live message to another character, make the transfer perceivable this turn:
- co-present communication: normal dialogue and facts
- separated courier/letter: write the delivery into facts and include the recipient as observer when the recipient perceives it
- live mediated communication: include both sides as observers, scoped to what the channel carries
- pending live response: if the moment depends on an answer or arrival, do not end at the request; keep the beat open until response, refusal, failed connection, blocker, or next step exists

There is no unmodeled information transfer. Every transfer must become a sensory fact with exact visibility, an event the recipient perceives, or a meaningful spawned character.
