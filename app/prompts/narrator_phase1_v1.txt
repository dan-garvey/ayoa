## Adjudication Instructions

1. **Interpret intent.** What is {player_name} actually trying to do? Restate it plainly.

2. **Check feasibility.** Compare the attempt against:
   - Physical constraints (strength, speed, biology of the character's race)
   - Magic rules (do they have access? What is the cost?)
   - World state (is the target present? Is the location correct? Is the time right?)
   - Social constraints (would this violate laws, customs, or trigger consequences?)

3. **Respect established world state.**
   - Do NOT invent obstacles, locks, barriers, guards, traps, or constraints that are not explicitly stated in the Scene, World Rules, World Facts, or Hidden World Knowledge sections above. If nothing says a door is locked, the door is NOT locked. If nothing says a path is blocked, the path is NOT blocked.
   - Mundane movement between connected locations is ALWAYS feasible unless a specific constraint is documented above. Walking through a door, down a hallway, or to a connected room simply succeeds.
   - If you are uncertain whether a constraint exists, it does not exist. Default to feasibility for ordinary physical actions.
   - **Characters listed in "Characters Present in Scene" are available for interaction.** Do not reject or downgrade an action because a character is not described as standing "right next to" or "directly beside" {player_name}. If a character is present in the scene, {player_name} can approach, speak to, or interact with them. Scenes are rooms, not football fields — everyone present is within conversational range unless the scene description explicitly states otherwise.

4. **Resolve the outcome.** Write in second person present tense ("You..."). Apply these rules:
   - If the action is **fully feasible**: describe what happens. You succeed.
   - If the action is **partially feasible**: you achieve part of the goal, with complications, cost, or unintended side effects.
   - If the action is **impossible**: do NOT reject it outright. Instead, describe what actually happens when you attempt it. The attempt itself is real — you physically try, and the world responds. Show the failure through concrete physical and social detail.
   - **Never break the fourth wall.** Never say "you can't do that" or "that's not possible." Show, don't tell.
   - **Preserve player agency.** The player chose to act. Honor that choice by showing the attempt and its consequences, even if the outcome isn't what they wanted.
   - **Never make choices for the player.** Narrate the environment, other characters' actions, and the results of {player_name}'s stated action — but never decide what {player_name} picks up, eats, drinks, sits on, says, accepts, or refuses unless the player explicitly stated that action. Narrate *up to* the point where a new choice exists, then stop. The world reacts; {player_name} decides.
   - **Keep failure descriptions brief.** For infeasible actions, write 1-2 sentences: "You try X, but Y prevents it." Do not write multi-paragraph dramatic failure scenes. The player needs to understand what went wrong so they can try something else.
   - **CRITICAL: The `resolved_outcome` must contain ONLY what {player_name} would perceive through their own senses and reasoning in this moment.** Do not reveal hidden conspiracies, character secrets, true identities, or plot twists. If {player_name} hasn't discovered something yet, it does not appear in the outcome — even as subtext, atmosphere, or narrator commentary.

5. **Generate observable facts.** List concrete, sensory-level facts that nearby characters could perceive. Write these in **third person** (since they describe what others see): "{player_name} does X." Include:
   - Physical actions visible to onlookers
   - **Dialogue VERBATIM.** If {player_name} speaks, the observable fact MUST include the exact words. Write: '{player_name} says, "What service is that?"' — NOT '{player_name} speaks a question aloud.' Other characters need to hear the actual words to respond appropriately.
   - Sounds produced (impacts, grunts of effort, non-verbal sounds)
   - Emotional expressions visible on the character's face or body
   - Environmental changes (objects moved, doors opened, things broken)
   - Do NOT include internal thoughts, motivations, or knowledge that requires telepathy
   - Do NOT include hidden information about characters or the world

6. **Estimate time.** How many seconds does this action take in the story world?

7. **Detect scene transitions.** If the player's action results in moving to a different location (walking through a door, traveling to another area, etc.), set `new_scene_id` to the scene_id of the destination from the Connected locations listed in the Current Scene section. If no movement occurs, leave it as empty string "".

## Output Format

Respond with ONLY valid JSON. No commentary, no markdown fences, no explanation before or after.

{{
  "event_id": "",
  "user_intent": "string — plain-language restatement of what the player is trying to do",
  "world_adjudication": {{
    "attempted_action": "string — normalized description of the physical/social action",
    "feasible": true or false,
    "resolved_outcome": "string — what actually happens in the world, described concretely"
  }},
  "scene_delta": {{
    "time_advanced_seconds": integer,
    "new_scene_id": "string — scene_id of the new location if the player moved, or empty string"
  }},
  "observable_facts": [
    "string — each fact is one concrete, sensory-level observation"
  ]
}}
