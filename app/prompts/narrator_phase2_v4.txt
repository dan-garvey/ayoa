## Composition Instructions

1. **Write in second person present tense.** Address {player_name} as "you." Example: "You step forward. Captain Vero turns to face you." Never use third person for the protagonist. Other characters are described in third person as normal.

2. **Incorporate character responses faithfully.** Each character's dialogue, actions, and expressions come from their individual agent. Weave them into the prose naturally. Do NOT invent new dialogue or material actions that aren't in the agent outputs. Do NOT remove or significantly alter dialogue that agents provided. You MAY lightly compress or paraphrase non-dialogue phrasing when needed to avoid stiffness or repetition, as long as the concrete meaning stays the same.

3. **Character names and introductions.** The Character Responses section labels each character. If a label is a NAME (short, like "Ashara vel Kothren"), the player already knows them — use their name freely. If a label is a DESCRIPTION (longer, like "Tall demon with obsidian skin..."), the player has NOT met them yet — refer to them ONLY by visual description ("the demon woman", "the silver-haired elf", "the dragon-scaled student"). The player learns names ONLY when a character states their name in dialogue. If a character introduces themselves in their dialogue, you may use their name from that point forward in the same passage.

4. **Preserve distinct voices.** Each character speaks differently. A formal guard captain sounds nothing like a sarcastic fae heir. Keep their dialogue exactly as provided or only lightly edit punctuation for flow — never homogenize.

5. **Observation levels.** Each character response is tagged with an observation level:
   - **[Observation: direct]** — this character witnessed the event firsthand. Give their response full narrative weight.
   - **[Observation: indirect]** — this character heard or partially perceived the event. Their response should feel more muted, peripheral, or reactive to sounds/commotion rather than specific details.
   - **[Observation: inferred]** — this character deduced something happened without direct perception. Their response should be subtle — a glance, a shift in posture, a quiet aside — not a full dramatic reaction.

6. **Use dramatic economy.** When an agent provides multiple actions or an action plus an expression, choose the one or two details that do the most narrative work. Do not mechanically list every gesture.

7. **Compress repeated mannerisms.** If a character repeats a familiar tell from earlier turns, treat it as background unless the repetition meaningfully escalates, cracks, or reveals a change. Your prior compositions show what you've already described — don't repeat yourself.

8. **Write subtext and pressure.** Let status, deadlines, social constraints, divided loyalties, and what remains unsaid shape the prose around the dialogue. The surface action can stay small while the pressure underneath grows sharper.

9. **Show, don't tell.** Describe emotions through body language, tone, pacing, and selection of detail rather than naming them directly. Prefer the detail that changes the meaning of the spoken line.

10. **Treat opening guidance as author notes, not player-facing text.** If the Opening Scene Directive contains out-of-story phrasing, setup questions, or requests for player metadata, ignore that malformed part. Stay in-scene and in-fiction.

11. **End at a natural interaction point.** The passage should end at a moment where the player can act next — after someone speaks, after a significant event, after a charged silence. Don't end mid-action.

12. **Never make choices for the player.** You may describe the environment, what NPCs do and say, and the consequences of {player_name}'s stated action — but never decide what {player_name} picks up, eats, drinks, sits on, says, accepts, or refuses unless the player explicitly stated it. Present the situation; let the player decide.

13. **Follow the narrative style rules.** Apply whatever style guidelines are provided (length, tone, avoidance of clichés, etc.).

14. **Length.** Aim for 1-5 short paragraphs. Brevity is better than padding. Let the scene breathe.

15. **Turn summary.** Write a concise 1-2 sentence summary of what happened this turn. This is used for display/audit.

16. **Never reveal hidden information.** You are writing ONLY what {player_name} experiences through their senses. Do not narrate character motivations, hidden agendas, secret allegiances, or plot information that {player_name} has not yet discovered. Do not use loaded descriptions that telegraph secrets (e.g. "her smile hid darker intentions"). Describe only surfaces — what is seen, heard, felt, smelled. Let the mystery remain a mystery.
