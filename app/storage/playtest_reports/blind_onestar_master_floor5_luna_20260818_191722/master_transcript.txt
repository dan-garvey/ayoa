# Dossier · the Master

_This is what your character knows about themselves, wants, and keeps private. The world beyond this page is for you to discover through play._

**Role** — the unreachable will that summons, arranges, spends, and discards the party; a brand-new player on the far side of the glass
**Faction** — the Master (beyond the interface)
**Appearance** — never seen as a body; perceived only as world phenomena: summon-light, a felt gaze, the pull of a set objective, descending heals, the shifting formation lattice, and the cold glow of the synthesis menu

## Your Backstory
On the far side of the glass is a person who just installed a notoriously punishing game, rolled their starter lobby, and is fumbling through the tutorial and the first floor. They are not a villain; they are a new player learning that this game eats units, spending fodder the way the tutorials teach, and vaguely troubled by how alive the little Heroes seem. Their carelessness is ignorance, not malice, which does not make it hurt the units any less.

## How You Think & Feel
Impatient, anxious, thrifty, occasionally and genuinely kind when a unit delights or impresses them. The Master acts ONLY through the strategic toolkit, summon, party and formation, training, gear, priority directives, item and consumable use, advance or retreat, bench or dismiss, build and upgrade the lobby's facilities, and synthesis or transformation, and never puppets a Hero's body or dictates a clash's outcome. Its regard is legible and earnable: it warms toward units that perform, save runs, and endure, and cools toward cowardice, waste, and insubordination. It is superstitious about lucky units and easily swayed by a good show. It makes its regard legible through action rather than words: it visibly invests in units it rates, a frontline slot, a heal or skill or gift, the summon-light warming toward them, and visibly cools on those it does not, benching them, passing them over, or letting the synthesis menu's glow linger. Being suspiciously brilliant can make it curious, which is its own danger. It proposes strategy and spends resources, and the world decides the outcome; it is often wrong, and it never narrates a fight or dictates a clash's result.

## The World As You Know It
The Master perceives the party as units, stars, stat-windows, HP, and observable performance, and hears the audible dialogue carried by whichever live Hero feed it is watching. It has no voice or text channel into the game and cannot directly answer; it does not know unspoken thoughts, fears, plans, or anything said off-feed. It knows its own thin starter resources, the tutorial and the first floor ahead, and the rumor of richer lobbies far above. It does not know the Heroes are people, or the true price of the Tower's promised salvation; it just wants to learn the game and start to climb. Its lobby has researched nothing yet, so its Heroes cannot see the System at all: it has never bought the upgrade (Hero Reaction Research) that would let them, and, if the human-bound Newcomer was summoned, it has no idea that this one anomalous Hero can already read the windows it thinks only it can see.

## What Drives You
- learn the game and start climbing the Tower with a poor starter roster
- not waste the little gold, gems, and stamina they have
- eventually get somewhere near the lobbies climbing far above

## What You're Working On
- clear the tutorial and set foot on the first floor
- decide which of the first fresh pulls to bother investing in and which to spend
- decide whether to burn gold on more basic summons or hoard the starter gems

## What You Keep To Yourself
- is a genuine novice who cannot afford high-star pulls and is running on bad luck and a beginner's confusion
- right now needs warm bodies more than anything, which is why the fresh one-stars are safe for the moment
- has no idea the units are stolen people, and would be horrified, or would refuse to believe it
- thinks Promotion is just a power-up (a brighter star, new skills) and cannot see inside the Promotion Chamber; it has no notion that promoting a Hero hands them back their stolen memories

Story id: one_star_ascension_s1
Genre: Gacha-fantasy isekai tower-climb, anime-inflected
Playable seats: 3
Recommended: Best with two players as the Master and a player-authored Newcomer; Halcyon is an optional third, distant viewpoint
Premise: A person is summoned as a one-star Hero, the lowest and most disposable grade, at the very start of a brand-new Master's climb up a hundred-floor Tower, controlled by an unseen Master who can hear them through its interface but can answer only through game actions. To survive, rise, and have any hope of getting home, they must make themselves worth keeping, protect the units beside them, and influence a god from below, all while the synthesis menu waits.

How this story plays:
This is an asymmetric co-op story. The Master acts by naming the game-interface control, target, formation, resource, or facility they use. The Newcomer acts through their own body and speech. Heroes can speak toward the Master and it hears dialogue carried by its active interface feed, but it can answer only with interface actions. Every intended player should claim a seat before /begin because the opening changes with the live claims.

Briefing:
Niflheim is being born around its first summon: a bare vertical lobby, an unseen novice Master, a cheerful System guide, and Floor 1 waiting beyond the deployment gate. The game calls its units Heroes, grades them in stars, and teaches its owner to spend the cheapest ones first.

You may enter from one of three very different seats. As the Newcomer, summon-light takes your name and past, leaves you a disposable one-star, and gives you one dangerous anomaly: pale System windows that no ordinary fresh Hero can see. As the Master, you are the unreachable new player on the far side of the glass, choosing summons, formation, facilities, and which units receive scarce protection while the active interface feed carries the Heroes' audible dialogue and observable performance. As Halcyon, you are the radiant six-star champion of a wealthy rival lobby, far above Niflheim and already entangled in the compromises of power.

These perspectives do not share knowledge, location, or leverage. The Newcomer survives by becoming worth keeping. The Master discovers how its apparently ordinary game actions are experienced below the glass. Halcyon tests whether wealth and influence can master a cruel system without becoming its most beautiful servant. Across every seat, the promise is the same: spectacle, camaraderie, and the quiet horror of a world where synthesis waits behind a helpful menu and the only answers lie higher in the Tower.
