## The relay before dawn (`ritual_register_k61_b19`)

**Model:** `gpt-5.6-luna` (offline-authored simulation)

**Scene:** At a small communications institute, a damaged relay has begun answering questions that nobody asked. The repair room is nearly empty after a long night. One person wants the relay tested before it is sealed; the other has to decide whether to permit that risk while a supervisor waits outside.

**Turn 1 — Okabe Rintaro**

I set the relay's cover beside the bench instead of replacing it. “Kurisu, give me one test. Not because the machine deserves another chance—because I do.”

**Turn 2 — Kurisu Makise**

I align the three loose screws in a row and keep my hand off the switch. “One test means one recorded input, one measured answer, and a stop that neither of us can reinterpret afterward. What did you recognize?”

> **Public pressure after turn 2:** A courier knocks once and says the supervisor will enter in five minutes. The relay answers with a sound that resembles a third knock.

**Turn 3 — Okabe Rintaro**

The knock from the corridor makes me put the cover farther away. The relay answers it, and I write the time in the margin. “That was not an echo. The rhythm is from the night my colleague vanished. I do not know whether it is a message, but I know I have been pretending not to know.”

**Turn 4 — Kurisu Makise**

I write the first line of the record beneath the loose screws. “Then it is not ‘machine malfunction.’ It is ‘Okabe Rintaro recognized a pattern.’ If you want a joint decision, your name belongs beside mine, not beneath an apology.”

**Turn 5 — Okabe Rintaro**

I open my coat and place the fuse on the dry side of the bench. “There. I stole the machine's refusal and called it caution. I will put it back if our stop condition says the test is unsafe.”

> **Public pressure after turn 5:** The only maintenance log for the relay is found warm beside the drain, with one page missing.

**Turn 6 — Kurisu Makise**

I lift the warm log by its unburned edge and keep it between us. “The missing page was removed before this log reached the drain. I copied it. It says the relay was asked the same question twice, and the second answer was crossed out.”

**Turn 7 — Okabe Rintaro**

I turn the fuse between my fingers, then set it beside the log rather than into the relay. “We test only the copied question, with the supervisor waiting outside and both signatures on the stop. If the rhythm returns, we seal it; if it changes, we leave the room.”

**Turn 8 — Kurisu Makise**

I write the two stop conditions beneath the copied entry and leave a blank line for the result. “I authorize the test as a joint decision. I do not authorize us to call a voice harmless because it uses a familiar rhythm.”

## The letter at the winter board (`status_privacy_clara_adrian`)

**Model:** `gpt-5.6-luna` (offline-authored simulation)

**Scene:** At a winter relief board's closing supper, a junior archivist discovers that a private letter has been copied into the committee packet. A senior patron who can stop the packet from circulating is present, but doing so would reveal that he read the original. The room is polite enough to make every refusal public.

**Turn 1 — Elizabeth Bennet**

I lift the packet by its corner and keep the copied letter face down. “Mr Darcy, a private letter has acquired an audience without acquiring permission. Will you correct the packet, or should I correct the record myself?”

**Turn 2 — Fitzwilliam Darcy**

I remove my gloves and place them beside the agenda. “The record can be corrected before the vote. Procedure is not the obstacle; my knowledge of the original is.”

> **Public pressure after turn 2:** The chair announces that the committee will vote on the packet in three minutes and asks whether anyone wishes to correct the record.

**Turn 3 — Elizabeth Bennet**

I set my marked copy beside the packet but keep one finger over the sentence that identifies the circulator. “Then say what kind of knowledge it is. Seeing a copy is not the same as reading the hand that was never offered to you.”

**Turn 4 — Fitzwilliam Darcy**

I turn the packet so the copied page faces the chair, not Elizabeth. “I read the original. I copied it to keep it from a committee member who meant to use it against you. That reason may explain the act; it does not make the act yours to forgive.”

**Turn 5 — Elizabeth Bennet**

I withdraw my finger from the marked sentence and fold the copy once. “Your reason has a shape, and it is still a hand on my paper. I will give the chair the circulator's mark if the packet is withdrawn before the vote.”

> **Public pressure after turn 5:** A servant returns the original letter to the archivist instead of placing it in the locked desk, saying that the senior patron instructed him to do so.

**Turn 6 — Fitzwilliam Darcy**

The returned original rests on the table, and I leave it untouched. “Withdraw it, and I will state that I read it. I will not attach your name to any remedy, appointment, or favor. If the board asks why, I will answer with my own conduct.”

**Turn 7 — Elizabeth Bennet**

I slide the marked copy toward the chair and keep the original under my hand. “The packet is withdrawn. The mark identifies the person who copied it, not the person whose life it describes. Record that distinction before anyone congratulates themselves on discretion.”

**Turn 8 — Fitzwilliam Darcy**

I put my gloves back on and sign the withdrawal line. “The distinction is entered. I read what was not mine, and I will accept your refusal to let my correction purchase a conversation tomorrow.”

## The dry blanket (`care_agency_c03_d14`)

**Model:** `gpt-5.6-luna` (offline-authored simulation)

**Scene:** A flood has cut the mountain station off from the road. A medic and a courier have one dry blanket, a damaged radio, and a delivery that must leave before the next spillway opens. The medic wants to make care a decision; the courier wants help without becoming somebody else's patient.

**Turn 1 — Saber**

I fold the dry blanket and place it on the bench between us. “Shirou, take it. I can work the radio without it, and you should choose the route after I tell you what your leg is doing.”

**Turn 2 — Shirou Emiya**

I keep one hand on the delivery box and touch the blanket only with my knuckles. “Blanket first, truth second: how long can I walk before this becomes a rescue?”

> **Public pressure after turn 2:** The station siren gives two low pulses. Water is now audible under the floorboards, and the road marker outside has disappeared.

**Turn 3 — Saber**

I open my kit and set a dry stimulant tablet beside the radio. “Your pulse is fast, your left side is compensating, and the road will not forgive a stumble. The tablet is yours to refuse. The delivery leaves only if you can make the choice standing or sitting—your choice.”

**Turn 4 — Shirou Emiya**

I take the tablet but keep it on my palm. “The radio is damaged because I forced it into the box. I did that. Let me choose the delivery without making my mistake evidence that you own the choice.”

**Turn 5 — Saber**

I set a cup of water beside his hand and turn the radio toward the window. “You may carry the package to the marker, then return for the radio window. Or I can send it with the next maintenance boat. I altered the schedule before the flood; the urgency is mine, not a debt you owe.”

> **Public pressure after turn 5:** The damaged radio catches one clear sentence from the valley: a second person is waiting for the delivery, but the signal will not hold.

**Turn 6 — Shirou Emiya**

I wrap the blanket around my shoulders without fastening it. The radio catches a sentence: someone else is waiting in the valley. “I can reach the marker once. I cannot promise the road after the water rises. Give me the package, the time, and the choice.”

**Turn 7 — Saber**

I put the hidden tablet beside the first and remove my hand. “The recipient is someone I left behind when duty was easier to name than apology. You may still send the package, turn back, or refuse the route. I will carry the radio to the window; I will not carry your decision.”

**Turn 8 — Shirou Emiya**

I put the package against my chest and mark the marker's distance on the wall. “I will go once, with the blanket and the radio in your hands. At the marker I decide whether to continue. That is help I can accept because it ends where my choice begins.”

## The missing ledger (`authority_debt_m02_t09`)

**Model:** `gpt-5.6-luna` (offline-authored simulation)

**Scene:** A civic inquiry is deciding who may reopen a closed ferry route. A junior clerk who copied a disputed ledger sits across from an appointed examiner who can make the copy admissible or dismiss it as theft. Both know the hearing's polite language hides a fight over who gets to name the facts.

**Turn 1 — Ryunosuke Naruhodo**

I place the copied ledger on the witness table and keep my hand on its blank margin. “Examiner, examine the page before you judge the hand that carried it here. The ferry route needs a fact, not a convenient owner for one.”

**Turn 2 — Barok van Zieks**

I draw the ledger toward the lamp without touching the ink. “The rule permits inspection of a copy when its chain is stated. State the page, the hand, and the reason this copy exists.”

> **Public pressure after turn 2:** The ferryman changes one date in his testimony and says he was advised to forget the earlier version. The chair asks whether the ledger copy still matters.

**Turn 3 — Ryunosuke Naruhodo**

I turn to the copied date and tap once beside the overwritten number. “Page seven, ferryman's office, copied after access was denied. The original was in the office when I saw it. One page is absent from the copy because I removed it before bringing this here.”

**Turn 4 — Barok van Zieks**

I write ‘page seven’ in the margin and leave space beneath it. “A missing page changes the weight of the copy, not necessarily the fact it preserves. I also recognize the handwriting. It belongs to the examiner who trained me, and that makes my neutrality narrower than I would prefer.”

**Turn 5 — Ryunosuke Naruhodo**

I take the obsolete form from my pocket and flatten it beside the ledger. “I promised the ferryman his name would stay out of the record. The corrected date may make that promise impossible. I would rather break a promise aloud than let a quiet omission reopen the route on my behalf.”

> **Public pressure after turn 5:** The chair asks the examiner for a formal ruling before the clerk can call the next witness. The room's recorders are all turned toward the examiner.

**Turn 6 — Barok van Zieks**

I close the folder containing the proposed ruling and face the recorders. “The date is admissible for examination. The ferryman's name remains protected until the chair finds it necessary to test the contradiction. I will issue no conclusion before that test.”

**Turn 7 — Ryunosuke Naruhodo**

I set the copied ledger beside the chair's untouched evidence seal. “Then call the next witness and keep page seven open. I am asking for a record that can survive both our loyalties, including the part where my own omission is evidence.”

**Turn 8 — Barok van Zieks**

I sign the examination order and leave the final ruling line blank. “The copy will be examined. The route remains closed until the contradiction is tested, and the cost of that caution will bear my name as well as yours.”
