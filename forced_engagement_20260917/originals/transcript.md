**Story**

The lamp on your desk wakes before the sky has decided whether it is evening. Its globe fills with amber light, catching in the ink beneath your fingernails and along the careful stitches at your charcoal cuff.

Beyond the window, the gardens lie pale and orderly between high walls. Farther out, one of the hard-light bridges runs from the plateau into the storm: a white line suspended over blue lightning, disappearing into cloud.

The room contains the sort of things someone thinks a young heir ought to own. A made bed. A wardrobe full of clothes selected by lawyers who have never met you. A desk with good pens arranged in a tray.

Above it hangs one thing that feels less newly supplied: a handwritten letter in a narrow dark frame. Your dead aunt writes warmly about airing these rooms for a planned visit from her named great-nephew. The letter never explains why the visit mattered, or why someone chose to preserve it here.

From below, the house is beginning to fill itself with dinner. Chairs scrape over the dining hall floor. A serving cover clinks. Several voices travel up the stairwell, too blurred by distance to sort out, followed by a burst of laughter that someone immediately tries and fails to suppress.

A brief knock sounds at your door.

Warm gold light lies beneath it, brighter than the lamps in the corridor.

“Mr. Garvey,” a young woman says from the hall, each phrase falling with an odd, deliberate rhythm. “Dinner is set. / They have saved you a place.”
