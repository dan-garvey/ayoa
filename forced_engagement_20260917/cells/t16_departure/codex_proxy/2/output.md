“I do,” Ashara says, and turns back to her plate.

No one rushes to rescue the moment. The server brings pear tart with cream instead, and Caelindra declares the pastry tolerable while Ysolde quietly takes a second slice. Seraphel asks Aldric whether chapel bells are always this loud; he begins an earnest answer and stops when Ashara says, “They are bells, Aldric. Their whole purpose is volume.”

From the common room, Dena’s voice carries over another shuffle of cards.

Rashid wipes his hands, then pushes back his chair. “I’m late enough.”

“Then go,” Ashara says.

“I will.”

“You’re still sitting down.”

He gives her a look, rises, and heads for the archway. Dena catches sight of him and points at an empty chair with her uninjured hand.

“Deal. And don’t explain the rules again. You only do that when you’re nervous.”

“I explain them when people cheat.”

“That is not what happened last time.”

Ashara watches him disappear into the common room, then reaches beneath her chair for a narrow leather practice case. “I’ll find someone at the octagon.”

Aldric looks up. “You shouldn’t drill alone before a ranked bout.”

“I won’t.” She fastens the case at her hip. “There are always Military students pretending they aren’t waiting to be asked.”

Thessaly folds her sweet wrapper into a small square. “That sounds efficient.”

“It is.”

Ashara leaves by the garden door, the latch clicking shut behind her. Beyond the dining hall, cards begin to slap against the common-room table, followed by Dena’s sharp laugh.

A moment later, she calls through the archway, “Garvey—five hands is better than four. There’s a chair.”
