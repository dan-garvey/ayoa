Ashara gives a short nod. “I will.”

Then she turns to Rashid. “Eat. Go to cards. I can work the footwork sequence alone.”

“That isn’t the practice I promised.”

“No,” Ashara says. “It’s what remains after you make two arrangements for the same half-hour.”

Rashid looks toward the common-room arch. Dena has returned to the card table; her splinted wrist rests on a cushion while one of her friends deals around it.

“I should’ve told her I’d be late,” he says. “And told you I had the earlier plan.”

“Yes.”

He lets out a breath that is almost a laugh. “You make agreement sound very encouraging.”

“You’re welcome.”

The servers remove the roast plates and set down small tarts of dark berries with pale cream. Caelindra tests the crust with her fork.

“Lavender,” she says, displeased.

“It’s not subtle,” Ysolde agrees.

Ashara takes a second tart from the platter. “Then don’t eat yours.”

Caelindra slides her untouched plate across the table without comment. Ashara accepts it with the satisfaction of someone who has won a minor, entirely justified campaign.

Across the room, cards snap against the common-room table.

Rashid rises once the desserts have been served. “Dena, I’m coming.”

“You’d better be,” Dena calls back. “Tavi’s already cheating badly enough to need supervision.”

“I am not,” says a voice from the common room.

“You are losing badly enough to accuse me,” another voice answers.

Rashid gives Ashara a brief nod. “Good luck tomorrow.”

“Don’t be late to anything else tonight,” she says.

He leaves for the card table. A moment later, Ashara pushes back her chair and heads through the garden door, where the night wind briefly stirs the candle flames before it shuts behind her.

Dena looks over from the common room, holding a hand of cards in her uninjured hand.

“Garvey. If you want a hand, we’ve got room. No speeches and no practice talk.”
