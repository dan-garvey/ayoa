Ashara looks at you for a moment, then returns to her roast.

“Fine.”

A server clears the dinner plates and sets down small pear tarts, the pastry dark and glossy at the edges. Before Aldric can reach for his, Ashara turns to him.

“You have chapel at dusk.”

“I do.”

“Afterward, twenty minutes at the octagon.”

Aldric’s brows rise. “You could ask.”

“I just did.”

“You gave me an instruction.”

Ashara takes a bite of tart. “Will you be there?”

He holds her gaze a moment, then exhales through his nose. “Twenty minutes.”

“Bring a practice blade.”

Rashid glances between them. “He doesn’t know your timing.”

“He knows enough not to stand still,” Ashara says. “It will do for one night.”

Aldric picks up his fork again. “I can still hear you.”

“Then you’ll know what to improve.”

From the common room, cards snap against the table. Dena’s voice carries through the archway.

“Rashid, if you’re waiting for an engraved invitation, you’ve missed it.”

Rashid rises, smoothing one cuff. “I promised them a game.”

“Go,” Ashara says. “Be at the octagon before second bell tomorrow.”

“I will.”

He heads through the archway. Dena, seated at the card table with her splinted wrist resting carefully beside a growing pile of coins, looks over at you.

“Bing Bong, there’s room next hand if you want it.”
