The servers bring out dessert: thin wedges of dark plum tart, the fruit still steaming beneath a crisp sugar-glazed crust. Caelindra takes a bite and appears, reluctantly, to approve.

“At least the kitchen understands plums,” she says.

Ysolde considers her own slice. “They have not cooked them very much.”

Thessaly folds her sweet wrapper into a precise square. Seraphel watches the candlelight tremble in her glass. For a few minutes, nobody asks anything of you.

“I’ll be at the octagon,” she tells Rashid.

Her tail follows her through the dining-hall arch, and the door to the corridor closes behind her.

Rashid waits only long enough to finish his tart. In the common room, Dena has taken over the card table with her splinted wrist resting on a folded cloth. Two other students sit opposite her beneath the portraits and firelight, shuffling a deck between them.

Dena looks past Rashid toward you.

“There’s a chair open, Bing,” she says. “No money, no speeches, and I’ll explain the rules before Rashid starts pretending they’re complicated.”
