Professor Stone’s expression shifts by a fraction.

“Those are sensible questions. Titles are poor insulation against hunger.”

He slides the brown volume toward you at last. Its cover is rubbed smooth at the corners; a former borrower has written a date inside from forty years ago, then crossed it out so hard the paper has thinned.

“Food, clothing, and a stipend are provided under the residence charter. If someone tries to make you think otherwise, they are either mistaken or hoping you won’t ask.”

He gathers his brittle papers into their folder with slow care.

“Keep that book in the house. The first section is enough for tonight. You’ll find that the human seat was not meant to be ornamental, whatever the Academy’s current materials imply.”

Stone looks toward the locked cabinet at the far wall, then back to you.

“Do you know what happened to your parents?”
