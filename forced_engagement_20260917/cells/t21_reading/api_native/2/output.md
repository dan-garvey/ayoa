Seraphel takes the chair beside yours with a quiet rustle of white fabric and folded wings.

“Thank you.  
I can manage quiet.”

She opens her slim book. For several minutes, the only sounds are pages turning, the soft tick of cooling lamps, and the muffled card game through the wall.

The Garvey volume is denser than Professor Stone admitted. Early human seat-holders appear in the margins of disputes rather than at their center: certifying translations, recording concessions, refusing to let a treaty be called voluntary when one party had not understood its terms. Several entries are marked with Stone’s neat slips of paper.

Beside you, Seraphel reads without speaking. Then she lays one finger against the line you have reached.

“‘Consent must be known  
before it can be given.’  
A useful sentence.”
