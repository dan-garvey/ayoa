Seraphel takes the chair without ceremony, smoothing her white sleeve clear of the lamp’s oil-stained edge. She opens her own book—a collection of old travel accounts, its margins crowded with tiny printed maps—and reads in silence.

The quiet is companionable only in the practical sense: neither of you is required to fill it.

Stone’s account of the human seat is more useful than the primer. Past Garveys are listed beside disputes rather than portraits: fisheries rights between dragon coastholds and elf-managed rivers; a beastkin petition to amend trade access; a demon objection to inspections imposed after the war. Again and again, the office appears where someone has agreed to words they did not entirely understand.

A burst of laughter from the common room reaches the library, followed by Dena declaring that Rashid is “too pleased with himself to be trusted near a deck.”

Seraphel turns a page.

“Professor Stone lends that book  
to very few students,” she says quietly.
