Seraphel takes the chair beside yours and sets down her book: a narrow volume of old travel accounts, its spine repaired with pale thread.

“Thank you.  
I admire devotion to cards  
from a safe distance.”

She opens it without another word. For several minutes, the library holds its own small weather: lamp flames ticking in their glass, Professor Stone turning a brittle page beneath his magnifying lens, laughter muffled through the wall whenever Dena wins an argument.

The first section of Stone’s book is denser than the primer, but clearer. It describes the human seat-holder’s work at negotiations: ensuring every party understands a proposed term, recording consent exactly, and refusing to let hurried language become binding by accident. A margin note in an older hand corrects one printed date, then adds, *Ask who benefits from urgency.*

Stone gathers his papers at last. “Thursday, Mr. Garvey. Bring questions, if the reading produces any.” He gives Seraphel a brief nod and leaves by the garden door.

Seraphel waits until it closes. Her gaze rests on the note in the margin.

“Your predecessor had habits  
worth preserving, I think.”

She turns a page in her own book.

“Do you like being read to?”
