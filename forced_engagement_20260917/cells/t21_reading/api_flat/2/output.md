Seraphel takes the chair beside yours, folding her small wings close before they catch on the carved back. She sets a slim book beneath the lamp: printed music, its cover softened at the corners and marked with several narrow ribbons.

Professor Stone returns to his brittle papers at the central table. His magnifying lens moves steadily down one page.

The brown volume is as dry as promised, but clearer than the primer. A human seat-holder’s work is laid out in practical terms: compare every version of an agreement, record consent, identify terms omitted in translation, preserve sealed copies for later dispute. One example gives seven columns of the same trade compact, each with a different script and a note about an argument over river access that lasted nine days.

From the common room comes Dena’s voice, sharp with triumph.

“I told you he bluffs with his ears.”

Rashid says something too low to make out, followed by general laughter.

Seraphel reads quietly for several minutes. Then she closes her music book over one finger and glances at the open chapter beside you.

“Does it say why humans  
were given that work?”
