Seraphel takes the chair, folding her small wings close so their feathers do not brush the table.

“Thank you.  
I can be quiet  
when quiet is wanted.”

She opens her slim book beside the lamplight. Its pages are filled with narrow musical notation and handwritten notes in several inks. For a few minutes, the library settles around the soft work of turning pages.

In Stone’s volume, the human seat’s old duties are set out with more bluntness than the survey text: witness negotiations, preserve exact records, identify terms no party has understood in the same way, and register dissent before an agreement becomes binding. There is no mention of great magical power. There is, however, a great deal about being difficult to ignore politely.

Across the table, Seraphel reads a line twice, then looks at the heading on your page.

“That section is better  
than the Academy survey.  
Does it say what they expect of you?”
