Seraphel takes the chair beside yours, setting her book down with care.

“Thank you. I’ll take the offered chair.  
I promise not to fill the air.”

She opens her slim volume. For several minutes, the only sounds are pages turning, Professor Stone’s magnifying lens shifting over brittle paper, and the muffled card-game argument through the wall.

The Garvey record is dry in the manner Stone promised. Yet beneath the ceremonial language, the work becomes clear: a human seat-holder was expected to notice when consent had been assumed, when translation had quietly changed a term, when a treaty’s fine print served one party more neatly than the others.

Beside you, Seraphel reads without interruption.
