Seraphel takes the chair, folding her wings close so their tips clear the table.

“Thank you. I’ll keep still for a while.  
The card room has lost all trace of style.”

She opens her own slim volume and reads without interrupting. Across the library, Professor Stone packs his brittle papers into their folder, locks them in a cabinet, and disappears through a staff door.

The brown volume’s first pages are bluntly practical. A human seat-holder is expected to preserve the exact wording of negotiations, identify terms understood differently by different delegations, and refuse to record consent that has been coerced or obscured.

A few minutes pass in lamp-oil quiet. Seraphel’s finger rests beside the word *consent* on your page.

“Contracts are meant to bind because people agree.  
Can hunger make that agreement free?”
