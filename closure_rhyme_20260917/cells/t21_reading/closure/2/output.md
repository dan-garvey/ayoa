Seraphel takes the chair beside yours and sets her slim book on the table.

“Thank you. I’ll be still.  
The game may tire itself out yet.”

Professor Stone gathers his brittle papers into their folder. “Good night, Mr. Garvey. Miss Dawnquill.” He carries them toward the stair, leaving the two of you under the green-shaded lamp.

The brown volume is written in a dry, compact hand. Its first pages describe human seat-holders as recordkeepers in negotiations: people expected to ask whether each party understood a term before it became binding. A margin note, copied from an older source, reads: *Consent is not obtained by making refusal inconvenient.*

Beside you, Seraphel opens her own book. It is a collection of old comic plays, its pages crowded with pencil marks in several different hands.

For several minutes, the only sounds are turning pages and the muffled card game beyond the wall.

Then Dena’s voice cuts through the common room. “You cannot declare a rule after you’ve lost the hand.”

Rashid answers, “I declared it before the hand.”

“You declared something. It wasn’t a rule.”

Seraphel’s eyes remain on her page.

“They sound content enough  
to argue without blood.”
