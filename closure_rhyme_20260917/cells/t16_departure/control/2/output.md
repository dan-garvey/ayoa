Ashara’s tail strikes the chair once. “I will.”

Rashid looks as though he might argue, then lets his hand fall from the bread basket. “Dena had the earlier promise. I should have said so.”

“You should have,” Ashara says. “Go play cards. I’ll see who’s at the octagon.”

Thessaly folds the sweet wrapper into a small, precise square. “That is a worse arrangement for both of you.”

“It’s the arrangement I made,” Rashid says.

Ashara takes another bite of roast. “Then make good on it.”

The servers clear the main plates. Conversation finds safer ground: tomorrow’s orientation, the quality of the Academy’s tea, whether the octagon drains properly after rain. Ysolde says it will be slick by morning. Ashara says she has fought on worse ground. Caelindra says this is not a persuasive argument for permitting it.

When small pear tarts arrive, Dena appears again at the archway, her splinted wrist held carefully against her side.

“Rashid,” she says, “we’ve dealt the cards. You’re already losing.”

Rashid rises, giving Ashara a brief nod. She pushes back her own chair a moment later and heads toward the hall without waiting for anyone to stop her.
