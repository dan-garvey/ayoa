Rashid takes a measured drink of water. “That’s kinder than it sounded.”

“No, it isn’t.”

Thessaly turns the last of her sweet between two fingers. “It was still useful information.”

Caelindra looks past you toward the serving platters. “At least someone here knows when an offer has been declined.”

The next course arrives before that can become an argument: pears baked in red wine, dark crusted bread, and a sharp white cheese that makes Ashara’s expression improve at once. Conversation breaks into smaller pieces. Rashid and Dena’s friends call something from the common room; he answers that he is coming. Ashara says nothing further about the octagon.

Seraphel accepts a pear from a server.

“The food has done  
its work tonight.  
No one has started  
a diplomatic fight.”

“That is premature,” Aldric says.

“It’s a rhyme, not a guarantee,” Ashara replies.

Even Caelindra smiles briefly at that.

By the time the candles have burned lower, Rashid pushes back his chair. “Dena has been waiting long enough.”

“Try not to lose your coat,” Ashara says.

“I only lose things I can afford to replace.”

“That is an unpleasant answer.”

“I know.”

He gives the table a courteous nod and heads for the common room. Dena’s voice rises at once beyond the archway, accusing him of arriving late on purpose; Rashid answers something too low to catch, followed by the slap of cards against wood.

Ashara stands a moment later, pulling her dark hair tighter at the nape of her neck. “I’m going to the octagon. Someone there will want the practice.”

She leaves by the garden door, her footsteps firm on the pale path outside.

The dining hall grows quieter around the remaining plates and guttering candles.
