Rashid looks past the table to Dena, then to Ashara.

“I did,” he says. “I also promised Ashara the octagon. I should’ve told you both.”

Ashara’s gold eyes narrow. “You made another arrangement after we spoke?”

“Before. I thought they could begin without me.”

Dena shifts her splinted wrist against her side. “We can play without you. That isn’t the point.”

The two students behind her exchange a look and stay quiet.

Rashid pushes back his chair a fraction, then stops. “You’re right. I handled it badly.”

Ashara leans forward, elbows near the table edge. “Tomorrow’s bout isn’t going to wait because you were afraid to say you’d overcommitted.”

“I wasn’t afraid.”

“No? Then what was it?”

Dena’s expression tightens. “Don’t turn it into a meeting. I asked for cards because I wanted cards. Not everyone looking at my wrist and deciding what it means for the practice group.”

For a beat, Rashid says nothing. The dining hall’s lamps buzz softly overhead; someone in the common room places another deck on the card table.

Then he nods once. “Cards first. I gave my word.”

Ashara sits back. Her tail strikes the chair leg once. “Fine. Find someone else for drills, then.”

“I will.”

Dena looks unconvinced, but steps aside from the arch. “We’ll be in there.”

Rashid takes his plate with him rather than abandoning it. As he passes Ashara, he says quietly, “I am sorry.”

“I know,” she replies. “That doesn’t make you less irritating.”

The common room gathers them in its firelight: Dena settles carefully into a leather chair, while the other two students pull out cards and argue over whether Rashid has forfeited the right to choose the game. His laugh, when it comes, is genuine but distracted.

At the table, Ashara cuts into the roast with unnecessary precision. Caelindra regards the remaining glazed roots as though they have personally disappointed her.
