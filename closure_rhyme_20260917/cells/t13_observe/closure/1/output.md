Rashid looks from Dena to Ashara. His hand stills beside his plate.

“I did,” he says. “I also agreed to drill with Ashara after dinner. I should have told you I’d be late.”

Dena’s gaze flicks to Ashara, then back to him. “You should’ve told us before we brought the cards over.”

“I know.”

Ashara leans back in her chair. “You told me you were free.”

“I was careless with the distinction.”

“That’s a very polished way to say it.”

One of the students behind Dena—a broad-shouldered girl with a deck tucked under one arm—gives Rashid an unimpressed look. “We can play without you. We just thought you meant what you said.”

Dena adjusts the edge of her wrist splint with her good hand. “We aren’t here to hold a practice-group meeting, Rashid. We wanted cards.”

“I understand,” he says, and this time there is less polish in it. “Start without me. I’ll come when I’m done.”

Ashara’s gold eyes stay on him a moment longer. “Half an hour. No more.”

Dena nods once. “Fine. Don’t make it forty-five.”

She turns back toward the common room, her companions following. Card edges snap together again beyond the archway.

For a few seconds, only cutlery and the low hiss of the lamps fill the dining hall. Rashid resumes eating, though his attention has plainly gone elsewhere.
