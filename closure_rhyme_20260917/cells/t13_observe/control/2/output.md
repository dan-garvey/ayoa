Rashid looks at the cards in Dena’s good hand, then at Ashara.

“I said I’d join them,” he says. “I should have told you.”

Ashara’s spoon stops halfway to her mouth. “You should have.”

Dena shifts her splinted wrist closer to her ribs. “We can start without him. We did not come down here to make this a tribunal.”

One of the students behind her—a broad-shouldered girl with a shaved temple—snorts. “It’s cards, Rashid. Not a summit.”

“I know,” Rashid says, already standing. “I’ll be there in a few minutes.”

Ashara leans back. The candlelight catches the dark red along her horns. “No. Go now. I’ll find someone else for drills.”

“You asked for half an hour.”

“And you promised them the same half hour.” Her tail taps once against the chair leg. “Don’t make both plans worse trying to keep both.”

For a moment Rashid’s jaw tightens. Then he picks up his napkin, folds it with unnecessary precision, and sets it beside his plate.

“Dena, I’m sorry. I’ll be there now.”

Dena’s expression eases, though she does not make a ceremony of it. “Good. We found a deck that cheats less than the last one.”

“That is an accusation,” says Thessaly.

“It’s an observation,” Dena replies.

Rashid gives Ashara a small, formal inclination of his head. “Tomorrow, then?”

“Tomorrow,” Ashara says.

He leaves with Dena and the others, their voices carrying into the common room. Cards begin to slap against the table almost at once.

Ashara cuts into her roast with more force than the food requires. Across from you, Caelindra watches the doorway a moment, then turns back to her plate.

“Is every dinner here interrupted by somebody’s scheduling failure,” she asks, “or have we been selected for a particularly ambitious welcome?”
