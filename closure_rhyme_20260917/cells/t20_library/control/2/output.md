Professor Stone’s expression tightens slightly.

“Food and clothes. Then they began with the things they could not omit.”

He slides the brown volume across the table beside your textbook. A folded sheet, crowded with seminar dates and page numbers, protrudes from its cover.

“Take it tonight. Return it before Thursday. And ask the lawyers for a complete inventory of the inheritance—property, accounts, correspondence, obligations. Ask in writing. They become much clearer when required to answer plainly.”

The card game erupts beyond the wall; Dena’s voice rises over the laughter, apparently accusing someone of arithmetic.

Stone rests his hand on the brittle papers he had been studying. “Did they give you anything personal from your parents, or only the formal inheritance papers?”
