Professor Stone looks once at your mended cuff.

“Food and clothes,” he says. “Then you asked what mattered that day.”

He slides the brown volume across the table. “The Academy provides board, texts, a medical allowance, and a clothing grant for Conclave students. If any of that becomes difficult, speak to the house steward. Don’t wait for a lawyer to remember you exist.”

His finger taps the first heading. “You may read this here tonight. Leave it in the library when you go; I need it tomorrow.”

Then he returns to his brittle papers.

The opening section is drier than the title suggests: procedure, records, disputes over translated wording. The human seat once kept the certified account of negotiations, confirmed that each party understood a proposed agreement, and helped turn arguments into language the Council could actually vote upon. It holds no special veto. It does, however, appear to have been expected to notice when everyone in a room had agreed to different things.

Beyond the library wall, the card game swells briefly—Dena accusing Rashid of counting cards badly, Rashid denying it with grave precision—then settles into quieter talk.

By the time you reach the section titled *The Vacancy of the Human Seat*, the lamps have burned lower. Professor Stone is still at his table beneath the spiral stair, magnifying lens poised over a page, while the blue-bound textbooks wait in their neat row nearby.
