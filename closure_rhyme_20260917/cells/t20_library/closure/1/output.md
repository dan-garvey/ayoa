Professor Stone nods once.

“Practical questions. The lawyers ought to have answered them before they began speaking of duty.”

He slides the brown volume toward you at last. “Keep it until Thursday. Return it to me, not the shelf.”

The reading list tucked inside is brief: the human seat’s voting powers, its old role in treaty disputes, three Council cases, and a chapter on consent in cross-racial agreements.

Stone gathers his brittle papers into a neat stack. “Start with the first section. The rest can wait until you know what, exactly, this place expects from you.”

The card game erupts again through the wall. Someone—Dena, by the sound of it—declares a hand “criminally suspicious,” followed by Rashid protesting that rules cannot be criminal.

Professor Stone returns to his table, leaving you with the book, the first-year texts, and a quiet stretch of evening.
