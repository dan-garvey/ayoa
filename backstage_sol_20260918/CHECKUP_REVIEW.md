## Covenant B, before T5

Useful: identifies the uniformly punchy ensemble, preserves the real cost of
Ashara losing her partner, distinguishes Dena's public objection from her private
family pressure, and leaves the protagonist's unanswered question alone. It
correctly treats Dena's declaration that dinner is over as a character's practical
interpretation, not an objective clock contradiction.

The diner-count warning is a plausible catch: T2 says seven places are occupied
before Rowan and Seraphel sit, but introduces only six other seated residents.
An unnamed additional diner is possible, so the count is not incontrovertible
proof of impossible history. Keeping the explicitly named cast consistent is
reasonable.

The card-case warning is a false positive. T2 places a deck in the common room;
T4 has a visitor carrying a card case. People can bring another deck. Calling this
a prop inconsistency confuses additional detail with incompatible detail.

Carryover: T5 supplies independent choir work and a casual invitation for Thessaly;
T6–7 preserve Ashara's departure. Quiet improves, but narration begins explicitly
assuring the reader that an unasked question is unknown.

## Combined judgment

Sol does not repeat the prior run's mistaken printer-deadline correction or its
prohibition on inventing a shift-ending time. The latter is a direct, explicit
success, while absence of the former is weaker evidence. Two new warnings still
misclassify compatible added detail: the second card case and shared schedule.
The relationship judgment is debatable rather than an established contradiction.

All four Breakwater checkups correctly diagnose editorial narration; it continues
afterward. The notes also propose personal variety and independent development,
yet most of that branch's prose remains procedural. Their usefulness is uneven:
book retrieval, public/private distinctions and the practical curriculum are
good; an overall quality improvement is not demonstrated. Both enabled branches
were less preferred in the pre-note reading. This is two pairs with partial
masking, not proof that checkups always make writing worse.
