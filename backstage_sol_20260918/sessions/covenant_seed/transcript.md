# covenant: published story

## Turn 1

**Player**

Begin the story.

**Story**

The Academy primer devotes eleven pages to precedence and exactly one sentence to tonight:

*The first-year welcome table observes the custom of its residence.*

On the desk, the sentence looks almost smug beneath the lamp.

Garvey House offers no clarification. Voices rise through the floorboards—chairs shifting, silverware being counted, someone objecting that the centerpiece is “an armed shrub.” Warm air carries rosemary, baked pears, and woodsmoke up the central stair.

Your carefully mended coat is the only garment in the room that wasn’t selected by the Garvey lawyers. The inherited signet catches the light on your hand. Across from the bed, a framed old letter promises some long-dead woman’s great-nephew that his room will be ready when he visits. He evidently never did.

Beyond the window, the Academy gardens descend in pale terraces toward the plateau’s edge. One of the six bridges burns across the dark as a narrow road of hard white light. Far below it, lightning spreads soundlessly through the perpetual storm.

A brass chime rings downstairs.

Your door, which has been drifting open on an unreliable latch, admits a wedge of brighter hallway light. Footsteps approach and stop short of the threshold.

A young woman stands there in white and gold. Warm light rests beneath her skin, gentler than the lamps, and two small white wings sit folded behind her shoulders. Her blue eyes flick from the signet to the open primer.

“You must be Rowan Garvey. The signet makes that clear.  
I’m Seraphel Dawnquill; the dining hall is near.

“If you’re waiting for a rule that tells you when to go,  
The bell downstairs is all the rule I know.”

She remains in the hall, giving your room its boundary.

“Would you like some company when you walk through the door?  
They may stare a little less if they’ve seen me before.”

## Turn 2

**Player**

I go down to dinner, introduce myself as Rowan, and take an available place at the table.

**Story**

You accompany Seraphel down the main stair. It curves through the common room, where firelight shines on the varnished faces of dead Garveys. A lacquered deck of cards and four empty glasses wait on one low table; books, practice gloves, and an abandoned teacup occupy another. The house already bears the disorder of people living in it.

The dining-hall conversation thins when you enter.

All seventeen places have been set, though only seven are occupied. Candles double themselves in the polished table, and an arrangement of silver-leafed thornvine leans noticeably toward the heat of a tall, ember-skinned woman midway down the table. Beside her sits a brown-skinned man in an immaculate coat, fine dark fur visible across the backs of his hands.

He rises first.

“Rashid vel Amara. Welcome to Garvey House.” His amber eyes briefly take in the signet. “The name on the door doesn’t make you responsible for finding anything. None of us knows where they keep the salt.”

“Top cabinet beside the serving hatch,” says the horned woman.

Rashid looks at her. “You knew this entire time.”

“I looked.”

A corner of her mouth lifts. Her long tail has been tucked around the legs of her chair to keep it clear of the servers.

You introduce yourself simply. “I’m Rowan.”

“Everyone suspected,” says a pale-gold woman with elaborately arranged silver hair. One pointed ear angles toward you. “Caelindra Vaeyn.”

The snow-pale woman beside her sets down a goblet beaded with condensation. The water inside has begun to film with ice. “Ysolde Thornmantle.”

“Ashara vel Kothren,” says the horned woman.

At the far side of the table, a golden-haired man in formal white inclines his head. “Aldric Verantus. The return of a Garvey to the Conclave is an event of great significance.”

“It’s also dinner,” Ashara says.

Aldric’s expression tightens. “Those statements aren’t in conflict.”

“No, but only one is getting cold.”

The nearest open place is beside a slight woman whose skin carries a pale green cast. Her entirely black eyes follow the movement of the chair as you draw it back, and her blue-black hair drifts against her shoulders despite the still air.

“Thessaly Morrow,” she says. She moves a bread plate clear of your sleeve. “That place is free. The napkin belongs to the Academy, apparently. They count them.”

Seraphel takes the chair on your other side.

“Eight names at once tend to vanish through the floor;  
By pudding you’ll know five of us, and breakfast may bring more.”

A server places bowls of dark onion broth around the table. The thornvine bends farther toward Ashara and hooks one silver barb into Rashid’s cuff.

Caelindra watches it happen. “It has attacked a Conclave heir within six minutes. I maintain that it is an unsuitable centerpiece.”

“It only caught the cloth,” Rashid says, carefully freeing the fabric. “Its political judgment may be sound.”

Ashara laughs outright. Ysolde reaches across, pours a little water into the centerpiece’s dry basin, and turns it away from the demon’s heat. The vine slowly relaxes.

“It was thirsty,” she says.

For several minutes, the room settles into the practical sounds of dinner: spoons against porcelain, servers replacing bread, the low hiss of a lamp being adjusted. No one bows to you. No one asks you to claim the empty head of the table beneath the largest Garvey portrait.

Caelindra’s gaze pauses on the ink along your fingers. Rashid notices the carefully repaired seam at your cuff. Ashara looks from both to your inherited ring.

“The verification notice says you inherited a name,” she says. “It says very little about the life you had before it. What did you do?”

## Turn 3

**Player**

I say, "Most recently I earned my keep copying books. I haven't had much practice at being an heir." I help myself to dinner.

**Story**

“Most recently I earned my keep copying books,” you say. “I haven’t had much practice at being an heir.”

You help yourself from the dishes within reach: hot brown bread, roast onions glazed with honey, and slices of herb-crusted bird. The serving fork is dragon-made, heavy enough to be a minor weapon.

Ashara looks from your ink-stained fingers to the signet.

“Most heirs practice in private for years before they’re allowed to make mistakes in public,” she says. “You’ll be doing both at once.”

“What she means,” Rashid says, “is that no one arrives prepared.”

“I meant what I said.”

“Yes. That’s generally the difficulty.”

Ashara gives him a level look, then returns to her meal. Her tail nudges his chair leg once beneath the table. Rashid moves the chair half an inch without looking down.

Aldric folds his hands beside his bowl. “The Conclave track is designed to supply what prior circumstances could not. Law, diplomacy, history, moral formation.”

“Duelling,” Ashara adds.

“Duelling is not moral formation.”

“That depends on the instructor.”

The dark bread has a crisp salt crust. The onions carry enough pepper to make the air above Ashara’s plate shimmer more strongly when she exhales. Beside you, Thessaly disassembles a pastry into exact layers, eating the sugared fruit and leaving the crust arranged in a neat spiral.

Caelindra has not resumed eating. Her gaze rests on the ink worked into the creases of your fingertips.

“A competent copyist is more useful than an incompetent heir,” she says. “Though the pay is usually reversed.”

Rashid lifts his glass slightly in agreement. “Accurate books are one of the few things everyone claims to value and no one budgets for.”

“They budget for decoration,” Caelindra says. “I once received a botanical compendium with eighty-seven hand-painted borders and the same poisonous mushroom labeled as edible on four separate pages.”

Ysolde cuts a thin piece from the chilled, nearly raw meat on her plate. “Was anyone harmed.”

“No. My mother sent it to an aunt.”

A brief silence follows.

Caelindra’s pointed ears shift backward. “The error was found before she used it.”

Ashara covers a laugh with her cup. Even Aldric’s mouth twitches.

Seraphel breaks her bread and says,

“A murderous mushroom makes scholarship exciting;  
Though less for the reader who trusts what you’re writing.”

“That is precisely why copying matters,” Caelindra says. She turns her violet eyes back to your stained fingers rather than the inherited ring. “What sort of books did people pay you to copy?”

## Turn 4

**Player**

I eat and listen to the conversation around me, without trying to take charge of it.

**Story**

Caelindra waits for an answer, but she doesn’t repeat the question. When none comes, she returns to her dinner, occasionally glancing at the ink on your hands.

Conversation moves on without requiring you to move it.

Rashid reminds Ashara that her opponent in tomorrow’s ranked bout has changed his declared weapon from spear to paired blades.

“He wants me preparing for paired blades,” Ashara says.

“He may simply prefer paired blades.”

“That would be disappointingly straightforward.”

Ysolde lifts her eyes from her chilled meat. “The wind will turn east tomorrow. The octagon’s western edge will be difficult for anyone with wings.”

Aldric’s shoulders draw subtly straighter beneath his white-and-gold robes. “The rankings ought to test discipline, not accidents of weather.”

“The weather is present whether you approve of it or not,” Ysolde says.

Thessaly eats another sugared layer from her pastry. “So is Ashara.”

Ashara laughs, and the table loosens around the sound.

The meal continues through spiced roots, soft white cheese, and pears baked until their skins split in the syrup. No one asks you to account for humanity, defend your inheritance, or pronounce on Council law. Rashid and Caelindra argue over whether a mistranslated treaty is still binding. Ysolde contributes one sentence about a border accord that spent forty years buried beneath an avalanche. Seraphel listens while quietly tapping a rhythm against her cup.

As the last pear disappears from Ashara’s plate, a woman’s voice carries in from the common room.

“Amara, this house owns seventeen kinds of fork and no corkscrew.”

Rashid stops with his cup halfway to his mouth.

A compact demon woman appears in the doorway. One short horn bears a brass cap, and her right wrist is confined in a dark leather splint. Two other students wait behind her, one carrying bottles and the other a wooden case of cards.

The injured woman looks at the cleared dessert dishes. “Also, you’re late.”

“Dinner has not formally ended,” Rashid says.

“The pears are gone. That’s an ending.”

Ashara looks between them. “Dena.”

“Vel Kothren.” Dena’s attention returns to Rashid. “The cards are set up.”

Rashid places his cup down with great precision. “Start without me. I’ll join you after drills.”

Dena’s eyes shift to Ashara, then to the wrapped practice blades resting beside her chair.

“You scheduled drills.”

“I expected the three of you could begin.”

“We can begin. We could also have stayed in Commerce House and begun.” She adjusts the splint against her chest. “We came because you said you’d spend the first half-hour with us.”

Rashid’s courtesy becomes noticeably more exact. “I said I would.”

“Yesterday,” Dena says. “Before you agreed to this.”

Ashara turns her gold eyes on him. “You accepted my invitation this afternoon.”

“Yes.”

“And neglected to mention that you’d already promised someone else.”

“I thought you needed a partner who knew your habits more than they needed a fourth player.”

Dena’s mouth flattens. “You decided what I needed again.”

Rashid draws breath, but she continues.

“This isn’t a meeting about the practice group. I told you I don’t want one. I wanted cards, bad wine, and an evening in which no one uses my wrist to launch a campaign.”

The two students behind her study the ceiling with practiced concentration.

Rashid looks down at the table. When he answers, the polish remains, but the argument has gone out of it. “You did tell me. I’m sorry.”

Ashara pushes back her chair. “Go play cards.”

“I can give you twenty minutes first.”

“I asked for thirty.”

“Then I’ll—”

“No. You made the first promise to them.” She picks up her practice blades. “I’ll drill footwork alone. Tomorrow, sixth bell, you give me the half-hour you owe me.”

Rashid nods once. “Agreed.”

Dena doesn’t smile at winning. She steps aside to let him pass, and the four of them move into the common room. A moment later comes the snap of cards being shuffled and Dena saying, “If you organize us into teams, I’m leaving.”

Thessaly rises with her unfinished pastry and follows at a measured distance toward the game. Aldric departs for evening prayer. Ysolde opens the garden door, admitting a ribbon of cold air, while Caelindra gathers a book she had left beneath her chair.

Ashara carries her practice blades toward the quadrangle alone.

The welcome dinner ends not with a ceremony, but with the house dividing into its separate evening pursuits around you.
