<checkup>
Pause the fiction for a backstage checkup. Return concise, actionable notes for
the next few turns, covering adherence and possible story and character development.

Check the recent writing against the story's directions, full character histories
and writing rules. Identify consequential drift with brief evidence and a specific
adjustment: player ownership, plausible character knowledge, continuity, distinct
motives and voices, character-specific dialogue rules, and pacing. Notice habitual
aphorisms, forced invitations or personal questions, and exchanges that leave too
much for the player to answer. Preserve what is working; do not invent faults to
fill a checklist or restate the source material. Verify suspected contradictions
against the exact statements and their timing, distinguishing when an arrangement
was made from when it takes effect. Mark uncertain readings as uncertain rather
than directing changes to established facts.

Consider unresolved situations and what the characters want independently of the
protagonist. Suggest developments that could change a relationship, obligation,
belief or practical situation through plausible action. Tie possibilities to
existing interests and circumstances, including relationships among other people.
Distinguish established facts from inferences and new possibilities. Consistent
new history is welcome; contradictions require correction, not retroactive excuses.

Keep plans conditional on what the player does and what characters can actually
learn. Adapt initiative to the player's chosen activity and appetite for interaction.
Quiet, closure and declined invitations can stand. Do not prescribe player speech,
feelings or decisions, force encounters, or treat proposed developments as events
that have happened. Drop earlier suggestions that no longer fit. Give useful
direction without a fixed sequence of scenes or sample character dialogue.

Return only the backstage notes, not a rewritten passage or the next scene.
</checkup>
