# Covenant: one-model playtest

Exact submitted actions and model prose.

## Opening

**Player**

My character: Rowan Garvey. A twenty-three-year-old human man with brown skin, unruly dark hair, and ink-stained fingers, wearing a carefully mended charcoal coat and the inherited Garvey signet. Newly arrived and unsure of Conclave customs. He grew up unaware of his heritage, lost his parents young, and does not know whether he has any magic.

Begin the story shortly before the welcome dinner, in my room at Garvey House. I play Rowan Garvey.

**Story**

The room has been waiting for a Garvey longer than you have.

Your bed is made tightly enough to bounce a coin. New clothes fill the wardrobe, all chosen by lawyers who appear to believe human heirs favor black wool and punitive collars. Beyond the window, gardens spill pale flowers toward the plateau’s edge, where a bridge of hard blue light vanishes into the storm.

On the desk, beside your open primer, stands a framed letter in a dead woman’s hand.

*For my dear great-nephew, when at last he comes to Garvey House—*

He never did. Dust has gathered inside the frame.

The primer is less personal. It calls the human collapse a natural plague, records the empty Council seat in three tidy sentences, and devotes twice as much space to dinner precedence. A red-edged page summarizes Article Nineteen with legal clarity: procreation between signatory races; execution of both parents; jurisdiction over issue subject to strict interpretation.

Below, someone has added in pencil: *Do not make jokes about this at formal dinner.*

Your inherited signet sits heavy on your finger. Downstairs, crockery chimes and unfamiliar voices pass beneath the floorboards. One low voice objects to the seating arrangement. Another answers, smooth and amused, “You object because you are beside me rather than opposite her.”

Three precise knocks sound at your door, followed by a softer fourth.

A woman speaks from the corridor in a lilting rhyme:

“The candles wake, the hour draws near;  
Will Garvey’s heir be joining here?”

Another voice, quieter and oddly measured, says, “There are seventeen candles, and none were asleep. Also, Rashid sent us because the others could not agree who had standing to fetch you.”

A brief silence follows.

Then the first woman sighs musically.

“You’ve gutted courtesy with fact.  
I liked it better left intact.”

Ashara looks from the bell to you. “And if there were, someone here would already have objected to it.”
