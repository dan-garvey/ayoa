You take the marked chair as the servers begin passing the dishes. Dinner moves on without arranging itself around the new arrival. Ashara and Rashid carry a disagreement from the dueling grounds into the room, trading brief objections over a ranking posted that morning. Across from them, the silver-haired elf closes her book only when a server removes the lamp oil from too near its pages. The man in white and gold at the far end speaks with careful approval of the Academy's order; Thessaly answers him only when he addresses her directly.

By the time the last plates are cleared, the candles have burned low enough for the lamps to take over. Chairs draw back. Conversations divide toward the common room, the chapel walk, and the upper stairs.

The Commerce annex stands beyond the quadrangle in a low stone building faced with narrow windows. A porter has left its outer corridor open for notices and late applications. Behind a glass panel, the copying room is dark, but the shapes of two presses sit beneath dust covers. The smaller one has a handwheel and a rack of wooden cases against the wall.

The schedule is pinned beside the door: ruled columns for the coming week, most early hours already marked in different hands. A two-hour block on the third evening remains unclaimed, as does an hour after morning lectures two days later. Beneath the timetable, a stack of reservation slips waits in a shallow tray. Each asks for a project description, paper size, estimated impressions, type case, and a requested block; a stamped note at the bottom directs students planning more than ten copies to reserve setting time as well.

The sheet and pencil rest in your hand beneath the annex lamp.
