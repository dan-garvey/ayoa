**Story**

Garvey House settles around you in small, unfamiliar sounds: pipes ticking behind paneled walls, a door shutting somewhere below, the faint brush of wind against the garden-facing glass. Your room occupies a second-floor corner above the common rooms. The made bed has tucked corners; the clothes sent ahead by the lawyers hang in the wardrobe with a tidiness your mended charcoal coat does not share. Beyond the window, pale garden paths lead toward the outer walls, and one of the bridges begins to show as a white line against the dark storm.

Across the room, the framed handwritten letter from the aunt you never met remains where someone chose to hang it, addressed to a great-nephew who never came.

Below the floorboards, chairs scrape over the dining hall floor. Several voices carry up through the house, indistinct beneath the clink of serving dishes; one laugh cuts short, and conversation resumes at a lower pitch.

Measured footsteps stop outside your door. A knuckle raps once against the wood.

“Dinner is being laid,” a voice says from the corridor. “You may come down when ready.”

The footsteps continue toward the stair. In the hall beyond your door, lamplight falls across the runner toward the dining room.

## Turn 1

**Story**

The dining hall is brighter than the common room above it, with lamps working alongside the candles and a long varnished table running nearly the length of the paneled room. Only the nearer end is occupied. Place cards stand among the settings; beyond them, nine more places wait in orderly rows of silver and glass.

Your introduction draws the table's attention without stopping it altogether. A tall woman with swept-back black-red horns sits nearest the fireplace, a cup held in one broad hand. Her gold eyes rest on you for a moment.

“Ashara vel Kothren,” she says. “Your place is there.” She indicates an empty chair between a white-haired woman whose hands are folded beside her plate and a small figure with blue-black hair drifting slowly about her shoulders.

The man opposite Ashara has dark fur over the backs of his hands and the cut of someone who has dressed precisely for the occasion. “Rashid vel Amara.” His attention shifts to the question of the press. “There is a hand press in the Commerce annex. Students can book it through the quartermaster if they have a project and time outside their track work. The schedule is posted at seventh bell.”

“It is a good press,” says the white-haired woman without moving from her place. “The type is kept clean.”

The green-tinted woman beside the empty chair looks at the card before her. “Ysolde Thornmantle,” she says, then, after a beat, “Thessaly Morrow. Your card says Garvey. Therefore that chair is yours, unless the card is mistaken.”

At the far side of the table, a silver-haired elf turns a page in the small book resting beside her wineglass. “It will not be mistaken,” she says.

A server sets a covered dish in the center of the table and lifts the lid. Steam rises with the scent of pepper and roasted roots. The empty chair waits between Ysolde and Thessaly.
