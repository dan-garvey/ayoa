Beyond the window, the gardens lie in pale, sheltered patterns beneath the storm's low flicker. Six bridges of hard light leave the plateau in different directions, their far ends lost in cloud. The room itself has been made ready with an impersonal thoroughness: a bed drawn tight, a wardrobe full of clothes sent ahead by lawyers, a desk polished clear except for the Conclave primer and a framed letter in an older hand. Its salutation is for a great-nephew who never came.

The charcoal coat's mended seams are neat against the room's finer furnishings. The Garvey signet catches the lamplight whenever your hand moves near the primer's open pages: the Council, the seven races, the Academy's customs, the article printed in severe type among the notes on conduct.

Below, chairs scrape against the dining hall floor. A brief murmur rises through the open stairwell, then falls under the clink of silverware being laid. The smell of roast citrus and pepper reaches the landing before the knock does.

Three measured taps sound at your door.

"Dinner will be served in ten minutes, Master Garvey. The first-years are assembling," a server says from the landing.

Below, the voices in the common room shift around the fire, then trail toward the stairs leading down to the long table.
