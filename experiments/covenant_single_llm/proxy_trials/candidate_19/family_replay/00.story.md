The frame is cool in your hands when you return upstairs. Under the glass, the letter fills less than a page; the narrow hand is neat except where the ink has feathered at the end of a few long strokes.

*To the great-nephew I never expected—*

*Tomas,*

*Your mother claims you now prefer hotels to houses, which I mean to test in person. The blue room is aired, the north window has at last stopped rattling, and I have ordered enough plum cake to embarrass us both. Come by the garden gate on Friday, as we agreed. If the train is late, send a note from the station and I will keep supper.*

*With affection,*

*Miriam Garvey*

The library is quieter after dinner, its lower lamps leaving the upper gallery in a small amber island. At the lit desk, a lean old man in a dark waistcoat is copying a column of dates into a notebook. He looks up at your voice without capping his pen.

“I have a little time.” His eyes settle on the signature, then the frame. “Miriam Garvey kept the gardens here when this house still had a household in it. Once she made a florist return through sleet because two hellebores had been replaced with tulips. Tomas was her great-nephew. He was meant to come that Friday, and he did not.” Stone sets his pen across the notebook. “May I see the date?”
