The frame catches lamplight across your knees. Stone eases back in his chair, as if deciding which argument can survive being told.

“Often enough. I was a junior lecturer then, and the portraits downstairs had their brass labels freshly installed. Miriam found two names wrong within five minutes. I told her the library did not run the common room; she said she knew that, and the Academy did not run itself either. For three evenings she came here with old household ledgers and made me write the corrections. The plaques were changed.”

His fingers rest on the closed notebook. “She brought tea the first night. Terrible tea. By the third, I had learned to make it before she arrived.”
