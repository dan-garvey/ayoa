The last light catches on the uneven stitching at your cuff and turns the thread almost silver. Beyond the window, the garden paths are beginning to pale beneath their lamps; farther out, a bridge of hard light holds its white line over the storm. The room around you has the tidy, prepared feeling of a place someone else expected you to need. Above the desk, a framed letter hangs slightly crooked, its old brown ink visible but too small to read from here.

Below, crockery touches crockery, and a few voices rise through the floorboards before becoming indistinct again. Then a single knock lands on your door. Heat gathers briefly along the gap beneath it.

“Rowan Garvey?” says a woman outside, her voice low and clear. “Ashara vel Kothren. The others are gathering downstairs. This house has enough stairs to make a poor first impression on anyone. I am going that way now, so I can show you the route. Are you coming?”
