Thessaly holds still for a moment, one hand resting on the closed game box.

“Fair,” she says. “I had a good game and then made it tedious. I am sorry.”

She turns the box so its painted lid faces squarely with the table. “I invited you because I wanted company, not an audience for my opinions. I sometimes tangle those together.”

The apricot dish remains between you, its sugar dust catching the lamplight. Thessaly looks toward the small tea tray near the fireplace. “I was going to make tea. Would you have a cup with me?”
