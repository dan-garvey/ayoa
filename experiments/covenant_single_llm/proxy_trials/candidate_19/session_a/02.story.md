Rashid's eyes flick to the unsigned sheet, then back to you. He does not reach for it.

“That is fair,” he says. “There is the octagon, but it is open to the weather, and after dusk the wind comes hard across the plateau. We use it for footwork when we can. It is no place for a mixed group to practise falls or sparring.” He takes a measured spoonful of broth before continuing. “The Military halls have roofs, instructors, and schedules already spoken for. The smaller rooms can be hired privately. The covered court was the one place where a student could show up with a practice blade and another student who had an hour free.”

The soup is warm with saffron and lemon, the broth clear enough to catch the candlelight. Across from you, Aldric rests his hand beside his untouched bowl.

“Then the request should say precisely that,” he says. “Supervised drills, no sparring, until the report is reviewed. A temporary measure does not need to become an accusation.”

“It does say that.” Rashid turns over the top page and taps a paragraph halfway down. “The dean's office returned it with the court closure pasted over the answer.”

Ashara glances at the wording. “You led with the restriction's effect on students without private tutors. They may have heard an accusation whether you intended one or not.”

“They should hear it,” Rashid says, more quietly. “That part is true.”

Caelindra, who has been separating a curl of orange peel from the rim of her glass, looks up. “Truth can still be arranged badly. Ask for the report, establish whether the injury had anything to do with the group, and make them explain why supervised access is impossible. Their first refusal will be less comfortable on paper.”

Rashid studies her for a beat. “That is better than my first draft.” He folds the petition once along its existing crease. “I will rewrite it after dinner.”
