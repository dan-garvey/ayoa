In the morning I dress, have breakfast, and follow the first-day schedule in the primer. I explore the public grounds in my free time and have something to eat before going to the north gallery by the route Ashara gave me, a little before seven in the evening. I watch her whole booked hour without interrupting. Privately, I wonder whether Jory Hale, the fencing master I delivered books to, still opens his school at dawn; I do not mention his name or that thought. When Ashara finishes, I come down and ask whether the ten minutes she offered still suits her.
