Morning begins with the house bells and a breakfast of black tea, soft eggs, and toast already disappearing from the long table. The primer's first-day schedule carries you through an orientation in the Conclave seminar room, a short briefing on bridge permissions, and an afternoon introduction to the Academy's libraries and public study halls. Professor Windwhisper conducts the legal portion in an even voice, pausing only to make certain every name is entered correctly on the attendance sheet.

In the hours between, the public grounds offer their own map of the Academy: the black-and-white geometry of the quadrangle, the chapel's high doors, the packed earth of the dueling ground beyond its posted rankings, and the sheltered paths of the gardens. By early evening, after a quick meal, the east gate and exterior stair lead where Ashara said they would. The north gallery overlooks the covered court from behind a waist-high rail. Below, lamp-globes make clear circles on the floor while the storm presses a muted blue-grey against the high windows.

Ashara enters shortly before the seventh bell with a case over one shoulder and a practice blade in hand. At the final strike, she sets a small brass timer beside the court wall and begins.

For the first part of the hour, she works the marked circle at the center of the floor: step, turn, retreat, then the same sequence reversed without looking down. Her tail follows the changes in balance without ever touching the ground. When she takes up the blade, the movements become shorter and sharper. She cuts at padded posts fixed around the circle, stops just before each contact, then begins again from a different angle. The only sounds are the timer's faint tick, leather soles on the court, and the occasional hard tap of wood.

At the midpoint, heat shimmers briefly along her forearm and around the blunt edge of the blade. Three of the practice posts show dark scorched arcs where the previous cuts nearly landed. She drinks water, resets the posts herself, and continues with a faster sequence that carries her from one end of the court to the other and back again. The name Jory Hale crosses your mind with the remembered certainty that his school opened at dawn; it remains unspoken in the gallery.

When the timer finally clicks shut, Ashara lowers the blade, wipes the handle with a cloth, and returns it to its case. You come down the stair to the court level and ask about the ten minutes.

She looks toward the gallery once, then back at you. “It still suits me. You watched without trying to advise me, which is already useful.” She opens the rack beside the wall and indicates the line of blunted practice blades. “Take one. Which hand do you write with?”
