“Evening,” Ashara says. “Tomorrow evening. My booking begins at seven. Go through the dueling ground's east gate and take the exterior stair on the left; the north gallery door is at the top. If you reach the ranking board, you have gone too far.”

Dinner loosens around the next dishes. Seraphel waits until a server has passed with a platter of glazed roots, then says, “Before anyone asks me / for a ceremonial song, / I have a complaint. / The dawn bell has no sense of proportion.”

“It is the chapel bell,” Aldric says, though he is smiling.

“It is an assault on sleeping people,” Rashid replies.

Ysolde considers this. “It is quieter in the north rooms.”

Caelindra looks at her over the rim of her glass. “Of course it is.”

“That is where the sound has less distance to travel,” Ysolde says. “The stone is thicker.”

For once, Caelindra has no immediate answer. Thessaly's smile arrives a moment later than everyone else's.

The talk drifts from bells to the least useful Academy traditions, then to food. Rashid maintains that the kitchen makes every sauce as though it expects an argument; Seraphel defends the lemon broth in three short lines; Aldric insists that no sauce has ever required anyone to raise their voice. By the time baked pears and cream have replaced the main dishes, even Ashara has stopped correcting Rashid's account of a disastrous first-year breakfast queue.

When the last plates are cleared, Rashid tucks the petition beneath his arm and carries it through to one of the common room's study tables. He draws a fresh sheet of paper toward him and begins crossing out his first paragraph. Aldric excuses himself for vespers and goes toward the front hall, white and gold disappearing past the fireplace.

Thessaly takes a small lacquered box from a pocket inside her sleeve. “I am taking Lanterns to the common room,” she says. “It has rules that fit on one card, no stakes, and requires at least two people. I would prefer not to play both sides.”
