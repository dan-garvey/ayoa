The tile you choose carries a clean bend of road toward the moon gate. It fits against the growing map, and the blue lantern begins at the nearer entry circle before moving two marks along it.

“Legal,” Thessaly says. “I will stop illegal moves. Bad moves belong to their owner.”

Her next tile gives the amber road a branch of its own. She moves one lantern toward the east gate, then eats half an apricot while considering the board. “The box says Lanterns was invented by travellers snowed into a bridge house. I think it was invented by a clerk who wanted a disagreement with an ending.”

The first blue lantern reaches the moon gate two turns later. Thessaly returns it to your side of the table with a small nod, then lays a tile that turns one of the shorter remaining routes toward the center. When you try to place a road tile whose edge would leave a path ending in bare wood, she taps the unjoined side with one fingernail.

“That road does not meet. It cannot become a road merely because you would like it to.”

You choose another. Later, an amber lantern sits at a junction on the route you have planned; when you begin to move a blue one past it, Thessaly raises a finger. “You may stop beside it. You may not pass through it.” The blue lantern takes its one permitted space instead.

The game lengthens across the table. Blue reaches the south gate; amber reaches the east, then the painted sun at the northern edge. Thessaly does not soften the map for you, but neither does she hide the limits of a move or rush a choice. She tells you that her first opponent insisted a lantern could remain safely at the center forever, then spent half an hour losing every route around it. “He had a very satisfying theory,” she says, setting another amber piece down. “It did not improve the board.”

By the time her fourth amber lantern reaches the final gate, two blue lanterns sit returned beside your hand and a third waits one tile from the west gate. Thessaly gathers the amber pieces first, then the blue ones, keeping the road tiles in their neat stack.

“You found the close gates quickly,” she says. “Then you assumed the close roads would stay open. They rarely do.” She closes the lacquered box and pushes the remaining apricots toward you. “You made fewer illegal moves than my first opponent.”
