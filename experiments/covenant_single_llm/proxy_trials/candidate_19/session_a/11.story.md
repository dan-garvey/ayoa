Back at Garvey House, the space between the bed and the desk is just wide enough for the stance. Five minutes of right foot forward, weight held loose through the knees, then the same steps in reverse leaves the room otherwise unchanged. Reading carries the rest of the evening to sleep.

The next two days acquire the steadier shape of a timetable. Professor Thorn's history lecture fills a board with competing accounts of the Sundering War and his objections to each of them. In Practical Rhetoric, Professor Kira has the class reduce a dispute to a claim, an audience, and an ask before anyone is allowed to argue it. Windwhisper's law sessions move more quietly through citations and precedents. In the free hours, the public paths become easier to choose: the terrace above the storm, the garden wall that holds the wind back, the low arches leading past the dueling ground, and the broad steps outside the library.

At shared meals, the table rarely lacks its own business. Rashid brings the revised court request to dinner on the first evening and reads the new opening aloud: a request for the incident report, the stated basis for the closure, and a temporary supervised practice period if neither establishes a need for a total ban.

“Better,” Ashara says. “It asks them to account for the decision before it tells them what they are.”

“It tells them that in the second paragraph,” Rashid says.

“Then the second paragraph can earn it.”

Aldric reads the page over Rashid's shoulder. “The first paragraph will survive a committee.”

“That is a lower standard than I hoped for,” Rashid says, but he does not change it back.

The next evening, the petition has gone to the dean's office and no answer has returned. Conversation at dinner wanders instead through an argument about whether the library's spiral stair is beautiful or merely inconvenient. Ysolde says it is structurally efficient; Caelindra says that proves nothing; Seraphel remarks, “A staircase may be both / a bad idea / and lovely.”

Each night before bed, the five minutes of stance practice return: no blade, no correction, only the measured shifts of feet on the floorboards.

After the second dinner, Thessaly sets her lacquered box on the common-room table. “I am playing Lanterns again,” she says. “The invitation remains free of stakes. I have added dried apricots, which are also free.”
