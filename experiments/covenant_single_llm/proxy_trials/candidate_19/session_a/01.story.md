Ashara waits in the doorway with one hand resting against the jamb. Up close, the air around her carries a dry furnace warmth, though the hall beyond is cool. The black-red sweep of her horns nearly reaches the lintel when she straightens.

At your remark, her mouth shifts by the smallest degree. “Then your lawyers confused ownership with hospitality. You live here. That does not make you responsible for everyone in it.” She turns toward the stairs. “Dining room, ground floor, left at the fireplace. There is no test on the route.”

The long table below is laid for more people than have arrived, candles burning among working lamps and polished serving dishes. Ashara brings you to an open place midway down one side. Around it, the introductions answer yours in a loose circuit: Rashid vel Amara rises long enough to offer a measured welcome; Ysolde Thornmantle inclines her pale head from the end of the table; Caelindra Vaeyn gives her name with the formality of a court announcement. Thessaly Morrow says, “Welcome. I was instructed to say that, but I do mean it.”

Seraphel's small wings settle close behind her chair. “Seraphel Dawnquill. / The house is complicated, / but the soup is usually honest.” Aldric Verantus smiles at that despite himself. “And I am Aldric. Welcome to the Conclave, Rowan.” A server sets down a bowl of saffron broth before you as the first course begins to travel around the table.

Rashid has a sheaf of papers tucked beside his plate. He waits until the first clink of spoons has softened, then draws them out. “Since we are all here: the covered court has been closed after an injury during evening practice. I am collecting names for supervised access while the faculty decides what happened.”

“Before they know what happened,” Aldric says.

“Which may take weeks.” Rashid squares the pages. “The students who cannot buy private instruction should not lose every place to train in the meantime.”

Ashara lifts her spoon. “The closure is broader than it needs to be. That does not mean we should pretend we know the cause.”

“I am not pretending,” Rashid says. “I am asking for a supervisor.”

For a moment, Aldric studies the petition rather than Rashid. “Get the report. If the restriction is arbitrary, that will help your case.”

Rashid considers him, then nods once. “Fine. I will get the report.” He sends several copies along the table anyway. One comes to rest beside your bowl, the ink still dark on its heading.
