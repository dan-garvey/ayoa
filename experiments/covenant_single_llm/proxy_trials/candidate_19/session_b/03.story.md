“That is all I asked,” Rashid says. “Mostly we drill: footwork, falls, partner work, basic wards. Some people use blunted blades. It is an evening practice group for students who are not on the Military track, not a militia pretending to be one.” His thumb stays on the folded petition. “The injured student was in a partner drill. I have accounts from the people who were there. I have not seen the instructor's report.”

“Then the request should say exactly what supervision you want,” Ashara says. “A supervised evening session is not the same as a private booking, and it should not pretend to be.”

Two seats farther down, the gold-haired man in white and gold sets down his wine. “Nor should it pretend that no one was hurt. A temporary closure is not an injustice merely because it inconveniences us.”

“No,” Rashid says, still even. “An indefinite one is.”

The server returns with a basket of bread before either can take the point farther. The pheasant is tender beneath its orange glaze; the potatoes crack open with salt and steam. For a few minutes the table is occupied by the uncomplicated work of eating.

Introductions come in pieces rather than ceremony. The man in white and gold gives a precise nod. “Aldric Verantus.” A pale, silver-haired woman beside him says, “Caelindra Vaeyn,” as if the name has already been placed in the proper order. Across from Seraphel, the white-haired woman who has been studying the candle flames offers, “Ysolde Thornmantle.” The small woman with blue-black hair and entirely black eyes lifts her glass last.

“Thessaly Morrow,” she says. “I have no title that improves it.”

“A useful policy,” Caelindra says, and reaches for the bread.

The table gradually finds a less formal rhythm. Aldric and Caelindra discover that they both object to the way first-years are required to sign for bridge access, though not for the same reasons. Thessaly declares the pear wine not worth its price, accepts a second glass when the server offers one, and adds that the second is included in dinner. Even Caelindra's mouth gives a brief, unwilling shift at that.

Ysolde says little until the conversation turns to the storm below the Academy. Then she explains why the western terrace makes its clouds look green at sunset, tracing the angle of the garden lamps with one cool fingertip on the tablecloth. She continues through the arrival of a dark berry tart, absorbed enough that the detail becomes a small account of wind, height, and the difficulty of seeing a bridge in heavy rain. Seraphel listens with her chin propped on one hand and answers, when Ysolde finally stops:

“On the third stair
the storm hid every door.
I followed the smell of tea
and found the chapel pantry.”

Rashid laughs first; Ashara follows a moment later.

By the time the final cups are cleared, the petition is still folded beside Rashid's place. He does not raise it again. Beyond the dining hall, the common room has gone quieter around its fire. At the far end, the library door stands open a hand's breadth. A single lamp burns somewhere past its threshold, and the air carries the dry, familiar smell of oil and old paper.
