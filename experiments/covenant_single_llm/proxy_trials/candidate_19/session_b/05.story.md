Stone waits until the frame is safely on the desk before he answers. His attention catches briefly on the signet as it turns, then returns to the letter.

“Yes. Lenora is connected to the inheritance.” He pulls a small, leather-bound notebook from beneath one of the open volumes but does not open it yet. “She was an aunt to your grandfather. The branch that led to you passed through her younger brother's children. She is not a direct ancestor, but she belongs to the same household line. The solicitors did not find you by chasing a name through unrelated families.”

He adjusts the frame until it sits square with the desk's edge. “Willan was kin through another branch. That is the ordinary answer, I am afraid. Families tend to look more dramatic from a distance than they do in their records.”

For the first time, his expression softens into something almost companionable. “The pears are useful information, though. They tell us Lenora was expecting someone she cared to feed, and that she had strong opinions about whether he would remember his papers.”

Stone taps the closed notebook once. “Tomorrow evening, after your first day has had its way with you, come back here. I will have the household index ready. It may not give you a grand revelation, but it should give you more than a framed letter and a missing visit.”
