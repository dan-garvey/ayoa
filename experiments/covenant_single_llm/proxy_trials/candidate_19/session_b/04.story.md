Rashid folds the petition into his inner pocket at your words. “All right. I will be in the common room for a while.” Ashara gives you a short nod over the remains of her tart. Seraphel's parting wish follows you to the doorway.

“May the shelves be kind.
May the answers be smaller
than the questions.”

By the time you return from the second floor with the brass frame, the dining hall has begun to empty around the last cups. The library door is still ajar. Inside, lamplight pools over a broad desk and climbs the iron spiral stair in thin bands. A man with greying hair sits amid several open volumes, his coat hung over the back of his chair. At your knock, he looks up, then rises and opens the door fully.

“I know,” he says. His eyes settle on the frame before they return to you. “Rowan Garvey.”

He does not take it from you. Instead he bends close enough to read the page through the glass. For a moment, the only sound is the soft tick of a cooling lamp.

“Lenora Garvey,” he says at last. “I recognize the hand before I recognize Willan. She wrote to half the family as though each of them had misplaced something important.” One corner of his mouth moves, then stills. “Willan was one of her great-nephews, from another Garvey branch. He was expected to visit. He did not. This letter will not tell us why, and I would rather not invent a tragedy because an aunt mentioned pears.”

Stone straightens and glances toward the orderly shelves behind him. “I keep an index of the old human household papers. It may give you his parents and a later address, if anything was recorded. I cannot tell from memory whether his line meets yours.” He looks back at the brass frame. “Would you leave it with me overnight so I can check?”
