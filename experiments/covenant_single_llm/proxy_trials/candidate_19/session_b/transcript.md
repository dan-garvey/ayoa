Would you join me for tea?” I give the storm one last look, then head back toward the house at an easy pace.

**Story**

Ysolde lets the last remark sit for a moment. The edge of her mouth moves, brief and unmistakable.

“I am glad I did as well,” she says. “Tea would be sensible. I do not need the terrace to prove it has better weather than the house.”

She gives the storm one final, measuring look before turning from the wall. “And I will come another evening. The wind decides which evenings are worth it, but the terrace will remain.”

The return walk is quieter than the climb out. By the time the two of you reach Garvey House, the green has faded behind the windows into ordinary dark weather. A server in the dining hall is clearing the last service but pauses long enough to provide a small pot of black tea, two cups, and a dish of honey. The common room's fire has been built up again, and its warmth catches in the worn leather chairs.

Ysolde takes the chair nearest the garden window, setting her cup on the side table until the steam thins. For a while there is only the low crack of wood and an occasional white flash beyond the glass.

“Most people assume the green is a spell,” she says at last. “It is not. I think they prefer it to be one because then it belongs to someone.” She lifts the cup and considers it before taking a careful drink. “There is a north terrace, too. It is less dramatic, but when rain reaches the bridges, you can hear them hum differently. If the weather turns, I can show you that one.”
