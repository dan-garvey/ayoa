Rashid reads the new signature once, then folds the petition with more care than he used at dinner. “Then I will make sure the first thing you learn is how to fall without bruising your pride. Wards can wait.” He tucks the paper away again. “If we get the sessions back, I will hold you to that.”

For a while, the fire gives the room enough occupation. Rashid tells you about the first balance drill he ever attempted: a low beam over a sand pit, an instructor who refused to accept that a well-made argument could improve his footing, and the precise moment he discovered she had been right. “She called it an educational compromise,” he says. “I was twelve and did not speak to her for three days.”

The house quiets around the fire. When you leave for the night, Rashid is still in the chair with a book open across one knee and the petition safely out of sight.

Morning light reaches the room pale and clean through the garden-facing window. The primer's first pages are less history than instruction. A stiff leaf of paper has been tucked into the binding under the heading *First Day for New Conclave Students.*

*Breakfast is available in the dining hall from first bell. At third bell, all first-year Conclave students are to report to the Conclave Seminar Room for orientation, course registration, and the Academy address. Bring this primer and your proof of admission. Do not report to a track seminar or faculty office before orientation unless specifically summoned.*

Below it, a small diagram marks the route from Garvey House through the quadrangle: past the gardens, beneath the low dragon-carved arch, then along the marble-and-black-stone walk to the oval-door entrance of the seminar room. The third bell has not yet sounded.
