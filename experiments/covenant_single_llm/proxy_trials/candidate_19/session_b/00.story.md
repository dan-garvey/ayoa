The last of the daylight lies in a long bar across a bed made too neatly to have been slept in. A trunk stamped with a law office's seal waits beneath the window; the clothes sent ahead have been hung in the wardrobe with exacting care. Your mended charcoal coat rests over the back of a chair, and the Garvey signet catches a muted glint in the fading light.

On the desk, beside the Academy primer, a narrow brass frame holds a sheet of browned paper. The salutation reads *My dear Willan,* in a firm, slanted hand. The rest is an ordinary request about a delayed visit, a basket of pears, and whether he will remember to bring the blue folio his aunt left in the city. It closes with an irritated postscript about rain.

From below comes the softened clatter of plates and the brief scrape of a chair across the dining hall floor. A laugh rises, then breaks into several voices too blurred by the stairwell to separate. The house has begun doing something without you.

Three measured knocks sound at the door. A warm gold wash of light gathers beneath the threshold, thin as spilled candlelight.

“Rowan Garvey?

The lamps are lit below.
I am Seraphel Dawnquill.

Rashid has counted the chairs twice.
I think a third count
will make him declare the table a border.

May I show you the way?”
