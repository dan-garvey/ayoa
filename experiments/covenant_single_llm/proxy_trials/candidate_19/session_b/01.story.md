The door opens onto the second-floor landing and a young woman in white and gold. Her honey-blonde hair is pinned back from her face; the small wings at her shoulders lie close enough that their tips nearly brush the doorframe. Her warmth is mostly light, a soft glow along the hall's dark wood.

“Then we will postpone it
until after the soup.
A ruined meal is harder to forgive.

I do not know Willan.
The frame was there last term
when I was shown the rooms.
Professor Stone may know.
He keeps old names in his head
and sometimes resents lending them out.”

She turns toward the stairs without waiting to make the uncertainty larger than it is. The landing opens onto the common room below: portraits in heavy frames watching over the oversized fireplace, leather chairs pushed back from study tables, a pair of discarded books facedown near the grate. Beyond its wide arch, the dining hall is all candlelight and polished wood. A server in blue Academy livery passes through with a covered dish, and the smell of roasted citrus and pepper follows him.

The long table has places set for seventeen. At its middle, Rashid is moving a cluster of name cards by fractions of an inch. Fine dark fur shows along the back of one hand beneath an immaculate cuff. He looks up as Seraphel brings you through the doorway; his attention takes in the coat, the signet, and the fact of you with the same quick efficiency.

“Good,” he says. “We have avoided a diplomatic disaster before the first course.” He lifts one of the cards, where *GARVEY* has been written in a formal black hand. “The staff asked whether to use your house name or your given one. I told them I would rather ask than invent a custom for you. Rowan, Garvey, or something else?”
