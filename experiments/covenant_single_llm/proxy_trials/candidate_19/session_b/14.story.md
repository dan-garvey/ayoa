Ysolde glances toward you when you thank her, then back to the storm.

“It would be unpleasant if it were bright,” she says. “The storm would look ill.”

A thread of lightning opens far below and closes again without sound. The green does not disappear with it; it shifts slowly along the lowest cloudbank, thinning at one edge and darkening at another.

“I found this during my first term,” Ysolde says after a while. “I wanted a place above the storm that did not require permission to fly, and the west stair was empty. At sunset, I thought one of the garden lamps had failed. The cloud turned green only at its edge, and I waited to see which lamp would go out.”

She rests her hand on the wall again, looking at the nearest fold of weather rather than at you. “It did not. The next evening, the color was somewhere else and much fainter. After that, I began keeping track of the wind.”
