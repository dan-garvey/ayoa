Rashid takes your hand in a firm, brief clasp. “Rowan, then.” His voice drops with yours. “Stone is usually in the library after dinner. He may know about the letter. He may also decide the answer is less urgent than whatever he is reading, but that is a risk you can take with your own time.”

He puts the Garvey card down, draws a line through the surname with the tip of his pen, and writes *ROWAN* beneath it in smaller letters. When you take the chair, Seraphel settles to your left, folding her wings with practiced care. Across the table, a tall woman with black-red horns swept back from severely tied hair watches Rashid return the pen to his pocket.

“You will give him names before petitions,” she says.

Rashid's mouth shifts. “A fair correction.”

The woman inclines her head toward you. “Ashara vel Kothren. Welcome, Rowan.” There is a trace of heat in the air when she reaches for her water glass. “Rashid believes orderly introductions are an affront to efficiency.”

“I believe they can survive until the first course,” Rashid says. A server sets a platter between two candles: sliced pheasant glazed with bitter orange, potatoes crisped in their skins, a dark green sauce fragrant with pepper and herbs. The first proper scent of food reaches the table whole.

Rashid pulls a folded sheet from beside his place setting but leaves it closed under one hand. “There is one thing I will ask when you have had a chance to eat. The Academy closed the covered court after an injury, including the supervised evening sessions some of us use. I am collecting signatures for a request to reopen it with oversight.” He looks directly at you. “After dinner, would you read it and decide whether you want your name on it?”
