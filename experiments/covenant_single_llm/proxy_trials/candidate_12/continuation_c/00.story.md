Caelindra sets the pages beneath the green-shaded lamp and reads them without a word. The library's upper gallery creaks once as someone crosses it. She reaches the end, returns to the first sheet, and then squares both pages against the tabletop.

"Yes," she says. "I would read more."

Her fingertip rests near the bottom of the second page. "The bell is not interesting merely because someone has stolen it. The ferrywoman is interesting because she has no clean choice left to make. If she searches, the next crew travels unwarned. If she goes upriver, the bell remains gone and whatever happened here may continue."

She draws the pages a little closer. "The deputy needs more weight, though. At present he is conveniently wrong. Losing passengers may be a selfish concern, but it is still the concern he has chosen to protect. I would like to know whether he thinks he is saving the company, his position, or only himself. You need not excuse him for any of those."

Her mouth tightens briefly at the thought, then eases. "And do not immediately make the thief explain everything. A culprit with a speech would make the whole harbor smaller. Let the choice alter something even if the bell is still missing afterward."

She passes the sheets back, their edges aligned precisely. "I have a seminar. If you write more, leave it with the library desk. I will collect it."

Caelindra gathers her own notes and rises from the table.
