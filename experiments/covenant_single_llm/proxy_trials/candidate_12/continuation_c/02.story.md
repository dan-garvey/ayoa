Rashid sets down his spoon at once. "Sunday after dinner. East gardens." He repeats each part as if making an entry he does not intend to lose. "All right. I will be there."

"The garden walls will be better than a terrace if the wind is bad," Ashara says. "I may come. Do not arrange your evening around that."

Rashid's mouth moves in a brief smile. "No one is arranging an evening around you."

"That would be unwise."

Later, the common room has thinned to the fire, the portraits, and the low creak of the house settling around them. Your notes lie open in the hearth's uneven light.

Rashid comes in carrying the narrow signature sheet and a clean page. He pauses by the nearer study table, then takes the leather chair across from yours instead. For several minutes he works in silence, copying a heading, crossing it out, and beginning again.

"The instructor's report will decide whether the request gets heard at all," he says eventually. "I have asked for it. Until then, I am trying not to write three pages of anger."

He turns the clean page toward you. The first sentence is neatly formed, then struck through hard enough to leave an impression in the paper.

"You write stories. Is this a request, or merely an accusation wearing a polite coat?"
