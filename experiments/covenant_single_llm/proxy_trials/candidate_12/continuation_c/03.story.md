Rashid looks once at the clean page, then draws it back toward himself.

"All right," he says. "No apology needed. I asked."

He folds the page over the signatures and tucks both beneath his arm. "Good luck with yours."

Rashid leaves the chair across from you and heads out through the dining-hall door. The common room settles around the fire again. For the next half hour, the only interruption is the occasional shift of a log and the faint clatter of dishes being cleared in the next room.

When you put your notes away, the hearth has burned down to a low red bed. Upstairs, your room is dark except for the light from the gardens caught in the window. You sleep until morning.

By the time you wake, pale light has reached the foot of the bed. Water runs somewhere in the house's old pipes, and voices have begun to gather below.
