By the time dinner breaks apart, the candles have sunk low enough to turn the long table into a row of separate pools of light. The common room feels less ceremonial: the fire has burned down to red coals, and the west window stands open on the dark gardens.

Seraphel has settled into the window's deep stone seat with a small harp across her knees. Its wood is dark and glossy from use. She adjusts one of the strings, listens, and turns at the sound of your arrival.

"Rowan. There is room
by the window."

Thessaly sits near the hearth with a square wooden game board between her hands, arranging its black and ivory pieces in neat ranks. Without looking up, she says, "There is also room by the fire. The window is colder."

"The window has the better sound," Ysolde says from the other side of it. She stands with one hand on the sill, watching the gardens below. "The wind is steady now."

Seraphel nods toward the empty chair beside her.

"I am beginning with the slow version.
You may listen,
if you would like."

The harp waits across her knees, its strings catching the firelight.
