Rashid smiles over his bowl. "They would have called it a utensil protocol."

"And given it a seal," Ashara says.

The broth is hot with ginger and something citrus-bright. Seraphel's spoon pauses when you ask about the evening.

"After dinner, I mean
to play in the common room.
I have been practicing an old river-song,
and the chairs have heard it enough."

"The chairs were fortunate," Rashid says.

Seraphel looks at him. "They have offered
no opinion."

"A diplomatic audience, then."

At the other side of the table, Aldric sets down his cup. "Seraphel doesn't play badly."

"I did not say I did," she answers. "I said the chairs
have heard enough."

Ysolde lifts her gaze from the soup. "The west window in the common room is open. It carries sound into the garden." Her grey eyes rest on Seraphel. "It would suit a river-song."

Seraphel's expression softens into a brief smile.

"Then I will use the west window.
You may listen, Rowan,
if that is what you are asking."
