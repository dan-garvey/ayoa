The primer lies beneath the brass lamp, a red thread laid across Article Nineteen. Its few lines on heirs and bloodlines have been printed in the calm type of rules that expect to outlive whoever reads them. The desk has an old ink blot at one corner; fresh blue-black ink marks two of your fingers. At the window, the storm far below gives the gardens a brief white skeleton, then leaves them dark again.

Clothes the lawyers sent ahead occupy one end of the made bed with improbable confidence: a dark formal jacket, shirts folded sharply, boots polished enough to show the room's lamp. The patch at your charcoal coat's elbow is plainly visible beside that display. The Garvey signet catches a strip of light at your hand.

Somebody moves chairs downstairs. A woman says, "If that portrait is meant to look welcoming, it has failed."

"It is a portrait," answers a man's voice. "Its duties are limited."

"Then it has fulfilled them magnificently."

A soft knock touches the door.

"Five minutes, Garvey heir," calls another woman from the hall.
"The soup is losing heart."

"The soup is fine," the man downstairs says. A small laugh travels up the stairwell. "It has spent all day being soup."

"Then it deserves to be served
at its best," the woman at your door replies.

Her footsteps head away. Below, plates begin to clink against the long table, and the house's ground floor fills with voices you have not yet met.
