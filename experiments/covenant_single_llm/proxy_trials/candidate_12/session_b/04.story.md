Stone's hand stills on the edge of his book.

“Edda?” He slides a thin marker between the pages before he looks at you again. “Yes. I knew her.”

He leans back in his chair. “One night she arrived with a stack of Council circulars and announced that they were unreadable. She assigned me the angel parts and performed everyone else herself. Ashworth came in halfway through, listened to three lines, and walked straight out again.”

The corner of Stone's mouth lifts, briefly. “Edda called that a successful reading.”

He looks toward the dark windows, then back to the papers in front of him.

“She was older than she looked, and determined to make herself inconvenient whenever someone told her a thing had always been done a certain way. Your letter is yours. But if you decide you want another pair of eyes on it, bring it here. I would recognize her hand.”

The repaired volume remains open beside him, its pages dense with names and dates. Stone rests one finger on the margin, waiting.
