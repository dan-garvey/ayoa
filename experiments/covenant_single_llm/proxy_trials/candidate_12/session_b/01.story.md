Seraphel steps aside when you open the door. Her white wings are tucked close behind her formal coat, leaving room in the narrow corridor as she leads you toward the stairs.

The dining hall is warmer than the rest of the house. Candles burn between working lamps, and the long table holds more places than people. At the near end, a young man with dark fur along his hands rises and shifts a leather folio away from the chair beside Seraphel's.

“Rashid vel Amara,” he says. “Welcome.”

Across from him, Ashara vel Kothren's horns catch the candlelight. “Sit before he asks you to sign something.”

“After soup,” Rashid says.

You greet the table and take the offered place. Names travel around the candles: Ysolde Thornmantle, very still beside a glass of water; Caelindra Vaeyn, closing the book she had brought to dinner; Thessaly Morrow, who gives a small wave; Aldric Verantus, upright in white and gold. The serving platters pass from hand to hand: roast vegetables, dark bread, bowls of thick soup bright with herbs.

When you help yourself and ask Seraphel whether she ever met anyone in your family, she sets down her spoon.

“No, not in person.
Only from old lessons,
and from watching people
say the name carefully tonight.

What does her letter say?”
