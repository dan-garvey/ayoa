On the desk, the Academy primer lies open beside a brass place card: **ROWAN GARVEY**. The lawyers' clothes have been folded into a precise stack on the bed, though the framed letter above the desk has been hung slightly crooked. Its faded hand remains legible behind the glass, addressed to a great-nephew who never came.

Beyond the window, slow lightning walks the storm below the gardens without making a sound in the room. The house itself is less quiet. Plates touch down somewhere on the ground floor. A man's voice, carrying up the stairwell, says, “I said I would ask after dinner.”

“Then ask after dinner,” a woman answers.

“Dinner has begun. This is close enough.”

“Your sense of time is opportunistic.”

A knock comes at your door.

“Rowan Garvey?” The voice on the other side is clear and young. “Seraphel Dawnquill.”

She continues in short, plain lines.

“Your place is set downstairs.
Rashid has brought a petition,
and Ashara has brought objections.
Neither will wait forever.

Would you like a guide
through the first descent?”

The corridor beyond the door smells of candlewax and browned butter. The stairs are to the left, down toward the dining hall and its long table.
