For a few seconds, the only sound is the fire breathing through its iron screen and the faint tick of cooling lamp glass. Seraphel has not moved while you read. The blue cloth has slipped from one corner of the harp across her lap; her thumb rests against its dark frame, still.

“The last line is good.

It lets Ada win,
and then makes the win small.”

She plucks one low string, once. The note fades into the room.

“Nell speaks of Friday
as if Friday is certain.
Ada has answered Voss
when Nell was gone.

She is sharp with her.
I do not think she is wrong.”

Beyond the hearth, the common room stays quiet. Gilt on one of the Garvey portraits catches the slow pulse of lightning beyond the high window. Somewhere upstairs, a door closes with measured care.

Seraphel looks at the page again, intent rather than ceremonial.

“I laughed at the coat guard.
Then I wanted Nell
to notice what she had asked.

That is enough for twelve lines.”

She gathers the blue cloth beneath the harp but does not rise.

“Does Ada own a coat?”
