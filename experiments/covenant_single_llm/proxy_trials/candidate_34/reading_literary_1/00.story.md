By the time you come back down, the fire has settled lower in its grate. A small bank of ash has gathered beneath the iron screen, and the gilt faces above the mantel have taken on the uneven color of old coins. Seraphel has set the harp on the rug beside her, its blue cloth folded beneath the neck.

You smooth the page on your knee and begin. Nell's quicker voice catches on the first instruction, already trying to get somewhere. Ada's lower answers give the words room to land. At first there is only the sleeve, the interview, the rent collector's name. Then Friday and last Friday begin to pull against each other. By the time Ada asks her last question, the fire has gone quiet enough for the sentence to hold the whole room.

Seraphel keeps her eyes on the page after you lower it. One of her thumbs rubs lightly across the side of her other hand.

“The last question is good.

Nell hears a plan.
Ada hears the hole in it.

They measure time differently.
Nell counts forward to Friday.
Ada counts back
from last Friday.”

She lifts her gaze to you then, and the warmth from the hearth catches along the gold at her throat.

“That is enough
to make me want another page.

I would put one sound near the beginning.
Not an answer.
Only something in the room:
a pipe knocking,
or rain at a window.
Something they both hear
while they decide what not to say.”

The suggestion comes without the softened tone of an apology, but not unkindly. She reaches for the folded blue cloth and draws it over the harp, leaving the strings exposed at the top.

“May I hold the page?

I want to see the first exchange
without your voices.”
