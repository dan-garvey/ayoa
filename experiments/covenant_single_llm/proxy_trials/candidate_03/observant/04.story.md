The fire has burned down to a red, steady bed by the time you settle with your tea. Its heat reaches the front of the leather chair; the rest of the common room stays cool beneath the high ceiling and the rows of dark Garvey portraits.

Rashid takes a study table near the hearth rather than one of the armchairs. He lays out the petition, three sheets of student accounts, and a pot of ink. For several minutes he changes little things in the request: a line about the proposed supervisor, the order of two names, the time of the morning meeting.

The garden door opens. Three students in practice clothes come in together, carrying the damp chill of the paths with them. They go directly to Rashid's table.

“I thought you said you were waiting for the report,” says the shortest of them.

“I am,” Rashid says. “The request says so. Read it before you decide whether your name belongs on it.”

One of the students bends over the page. The other two remain standing. After a moment, the tallest taps a paragraph with one finger. “If the director refuses access, do our statements go to the instructor?”

“I do not know,” Rashid says. “I will ask before I give anyone's account to faculty.”

That answer holds the table quiet for a few beats. Then the student who has been reading takes the pen. She signs beneath the existing names and passes it to the shortest one, who reads from the beginning instead of taking it.

Ysolde enters from the dining hall with her tea pot cradled in both hands. She pauses by the open garden door, looking at the papers spread beneath the lamp. Rashid moves an empty chair aside with his foot, making space at the table without looking up from the new signature.
