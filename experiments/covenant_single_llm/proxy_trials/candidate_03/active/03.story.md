The board's first unclaimed afternoon interval sits beyond the class periods printed along its upper rail. You enter your name there and describe the job as twelve copies of a two-page personal story. In the notes column, you add the setting and proofing time you expect to need, along with a line for the steward about the two-form requirement.

The ink takes a moment to lose its shine. At the foot of the sheet, the same printed notice makes clear that the entry reserves the block only if the steward approves the work before the day arrives. The shuttered pressroom gives no sign of anyone still inside.

You return across the quadrangle while the lamps are being extinguished one by one. Garvey House has gone mostly quiet by the time you reach your room; a strip of light shows beneath one door farther along the second-floor corridor, then disappears.

Morning brings a pale wash over the gardens and the distant bridges. The storm below the plateau has settled into a low grey churn. A covered tray waits on the small table outside your door, set with tea, bread, and a sealed Academy envelope addressed to Rowan Garvey in a careful clerk's hand. From downstairs comes the intermittent sound of chairs moving over the common-room floor.
