“Thank you,” you say, taking the chair beside Rashid.

Servers bring peppered broth, dark bread, and glazed pears in the course of the meal. Conversation finds its own paths around the table. A tall angel in white and gold, seated farther down, introduces himself as Aldric Verantus when the dishes are first set out. Later, after the bowls have been cleared, he notices the folder at Rashid's elbow.

“You mean to collect names before the instructor's report is finished,” Aldric says.

“I mean to collect the students' accounts before the restriction becomes habit,” Rashid replies. He turns one of the papers over without offering it across the table.

Ashara keeps eating. “The report should say why someone was hurt.”

“It should,” Rashid says. “It has not said anything yet.”

No one presses the matter through the next course. Candles burn lower; one by one, chairs scrape back from the long table and the room thins into smaller departures.

With a sheet and pencil, you cross the lamp-lit quadrangle to the Commerce wing. The pressroom itself is shuttered for the night, but its booking board hangs in the passage outside, beside a glass-fronted case of old student circulars. The smell of oil and paper lingers around the door.

Several blocks have already been claimed in the coming days. A blue-ruled sheet waits beneath a brass clip, with columns for a name, a requested period, the press or proofing table, and the work to be done. A printed notice at the bottom says that jobs using more than one form must be cleared with the steward before the type is distributed.
