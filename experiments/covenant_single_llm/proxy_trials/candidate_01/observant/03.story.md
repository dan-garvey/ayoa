The meal winds down without any formal signal. Your cutlery rests on the cleared edge of the plate; across the table, the last of the soup disappears with the quieter sounds of spoons and bread crusts. Servers begin their work in earnest, lifting bowls and replacing them with small plates of spiced plums. No one rises all at once. A chair shifts near the far end, then another; the conversation breaks into smaller pieces rather than ending.

Rashid leans toward Ashara while a server passes behind him. “First bell. North side. One bout.”

“You have repeated it three times,” Ashara says.

“Then you will remember it.”

“I remember useful things.”

He takes a plum from the serving dish and looks at it for a moment before setting it on his own plate. “I will bring the practice blades.”

“Bring whichever makes you less slow.”

“That is not how weapons work.”

“It is how excuses work.”

They leave the point there. Rashid opens a small leather notebook at his elbow and begins copying something from the week’s posted timetable, while Ashara accepts a fresh cup of tea from the server and turns her attention to the steam above it.

At the other end of the table, Aldric folds his napkin with care. “The evening office is about to begin,” he says to no one in particular. “The chapel will be crowded if I wait.”

Seraphel glances toward the darkened windows.

“Let candles hold the chapel bright;
the garden has a gentler light.”

Thessaly considers this with her head tilted. “You are going to the gardens.”

“I am,” Seraphel says, in a soft pair of lines. “The paths are clear, the lamps are kind; / I would prefer them to my mind.”

“That is comprehensible.” Thessaly sets down her cup. “I will come as far as the walls. I want to see whether the wind can be heard from inside them.”

Caelindra makes a faint sound of disbelief. “It is wind. Its principal habit is being heard.”

“I have not heard it there,” Thessaly says. “That is sufficient reason to find out.”

“A sound method,” Ysolde says from beside you.

Caelindra turns a violet-eyed look toward her. “You encourage this?”

“I did not know encouragement was required.”

For the first time that evening, Caelindra’s mouth nearly gives way to a smile. It vanishes when she notices Rashid watching the exchange and reaches for her own cup instead.

“The library closes its upper stair at ten,” she says. “I have an annotation to check before then.” Her chair moves back. “If any of you intend to make noise in the garden, do it where it cannot travel through the reading rooms.”

“We will make only virtuous noise,” Seraphel promises.

“That phrase has never reassured anyone,” Aldric says.

“Then let the night remain free of fright;
we will be quiet, out of sight.”

The angel’s expression holds for a beat, caught between objection and amusement. He gives Seraphel a small bow instead, then takes the white-and-gold fall of his coat in hand and heads for the door. Caelindra follows after gathering a slim folio from beside her plate. Seraphel waits for Thessaly to stand; together they drift toward the hall, Seraphel’s small wings brushing once against the doorframe as she passes.

The dining room becomes larger as people leave it. Rashid remains bent over his notebook. Ashara drinks her tea, one hand loose around the cup. Ysolde has not risen. She turns a pear seed between thumb and forefinger, then places it carefully on the edge of her dessert plate while the last of the lamps throws gold across the empty settings.
