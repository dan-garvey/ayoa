The chair near Ysolde gives a low scrape as you take it. Her place is cool beside you, even with the lamps and candles warming the rest of the table. She moves the butter dish a little farther from her plate, leaving it between the two settings, then returns to the pear she has been dividing into pale, exact pieces.

The bread is still hot enough to send a little steam from its torn center. A server lifts the tureen lid and begins filling bowls from Ashara’s end of the table, working steadily through the places already occupied.

“The north side of the octagon is free at first bell,” Rashid says, as if the exchange about the kitchen had simply made room for this one. “I checked before dinner.”

Ashara tears a strip from her bread. “You checked because you intend to use it.”

“Yes.”

“At first bell?” Caelindra asks. Her spoon pauses over the soup. “A useful hour for people who have declared war on sleep.”

“It is a useful hour for people who want the ground before the Military cohort gets mud across it,” Rashid says.

Ashara looks at him over the candle flames. “One bout. No audience.”

“One bout,” he agrees. “No audience.”

Seraphel tucks a strand of honey-blonde hair behind one ear. Her wings give a soft, restless rustle against the chair back.

“No audience, then no cries of pain;
the grounds may keep their peace again.”

“We do not cry out,” Ashara says.

“You did in the final round last term,” Rashid says.

Ashara’s gold eyes narrow. “I said your name.”

“At volume.”

“Because you were being slow.”

Thessaly turns her cup between both hands. “Was that the occasion when the instructor stopped the match?”

“No,” Rashid says.

“Then I have confused it with another occasion.”

“There have been several,” Caelindra says, and takes her first spoonful of soup.

At the far end, Aldric reaches for the salt. “If you intend to settle the matter tomorrow, you might spare the rest of us a report at breakfast.”

“You need not attend breakfast,” Ashara says.

“That was not the point.”

“Then it was poorly made.”

Rashid’s mouth shifts, almost a smile, before he lowers his attention to his bowl. The conversation does not stop for it. A pair of students farther down the table begin arguing softly over whether the Academy’s chapel bells have rung early all week; one insists the sound carries differently in the storm, while the other says a bell cannot be affected by weather and is told, with some satisfaction, that the windows certainly can.

Ysolde sets one crescent of pear on the edge of her plate. “The north side has a crack near the second boundary stone,” she says. “It widened after the last rain.”

Rashid glances toward her. “Thank you.”

“If you fall through it, the groundskeeper will be inconvenienced.”

“A compelling concern.”

“It is the concern I have.”

For a moment, the table’s attention drifts to the covered windows, where the storm flashes a dull violet through the curtains. Then the server reaches your place with the soup, and the dinner carries on around its small knots of argument: Ashara and Rashid fixing the terms of a morning bout; Caelindra objecting to the hour on behalf of no one in particular; Seraphel watching the exchange with a smile she does not explain; Ysolde returning to her pear.
