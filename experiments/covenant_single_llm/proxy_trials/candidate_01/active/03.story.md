The pencil moves across the open hour beneath tomorrow's midday seminar, leaving your name beside the estimate: twelve copies, two pages. The wet graphite darkens the grain of the paper. Above it, the workshop's brass plaque catches the hooded lamp; behind the locked glass, the press remains a black, patient shape among its drawers of type.

No one comes through the quadrangle as you leave the Commerce annex. The storm below the terrace has thinned into a pale churn of cloud and distant light. At this hour the Academy's paths are empty except for the lamps, which have begun to dim one by one as their globes surrender to the night.

Garvey House receives you in layers of old wood and settling stone. The dining hall has been cleared. A stack of chairs waits upside down on one end of the table, and the scent of roast squash has given way to soap, extinguished candles, and the faint mineral smell that blows in from the gardens. Upstairs, the framed letter remains above the desk. The primer, the lamp, the wardrobe, and the bed hold their places without commentary.

The night passes without calling anyone back into it.

Morning reaches the Nexus as a broad, pearl-colored light behind the storm. It lays across the garden paths and catches on every drop left along the hedge leaves. The hard-light bridges are still visible beyond the walls, paler now against the cloud. Somewhere in the house, a door closes softly; farther off, the chapel bell gives a single measured note and falls quiet again.

The first academic bell has not rung. From below comes the early work of breakfast: the muted clatter of cups, a server moving along the dining hall, the smell of tea and fresh bread beginning to climb the stairwell. The day has opened with time still left in it.
