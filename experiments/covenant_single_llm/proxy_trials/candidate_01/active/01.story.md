Ashara stands on the landing with one shoulder near the wall, tall enough that the swept curve of her black-red horns nearly meets the dark beam above. Her hair is drawn back so severely that the small loose strands at her temples look accidental only until the rest of her does not. Ember-red skin catches the hall lamp in coppery planes; the air around her carries a dry, contained heat.

“Good,” she says at your answer. “The central stair.”

She turns without another word, her tail clearing the narrow passage behind her. The stair broadens as it descends, opening into the old center of Garvey House. Portraits look down from the walls: generations of men and women rendered in formal collars, embroidered sleeves, and the patient expressions of people painted for rooms they expected to outlast them. The sounds from below sharpen with each flight—the scrape of chair legs, a server's murmured apology, the soft percussion of cutlery finding its place.

The dining hall is warm with lamps and the first breath of cooking. Its paneled walls and varnished table belong to the house's oldest bones, but the room has collected other languages over time: a demonstone hearth broad enough to stand before, pale carved panels worked into the ceiling, a living strand of green timber trained along one cornice. Seventeen settings run the length of the table. Only eight have narrow cards laid before them, their names newly written in dark ink.

The man from the landing has stepped away from the runner at last. He is broad-shouldered beneath an immaculate dark coat, with fine fur visible across the backs of his hands as he straightens a wineglass that needs no straightening. Across from him, a white-haired woman stands by the window with the stormlight washing a cool gleam across her face. A pale elf in silver hair has claimed a place near the lamps; her attention rests on the arrangement of plates as if it has presented an argument. Beside her sits a small woman with black eyes and blue-black hair that stirs in the still room. At the far side, an angel in white and gold has been speaking quietly with a second angel whose smaller wings lie folded close beneath a pale mantle.

Ashara brings the room's attention together with a few names.

“Rashid vel Amara,” she says, indicating the man at the runner. “Ysolde Thornmantle. Caelindra Vaeyn. Thessaly Morrow. Aldric Verantus. Seraphel Dawnquill.”

Rashid gives a brief inclination of his head. Ysolde's grey eyes settle on you for a moment before she returns the gesture. Caelindra offers a precise nod, one pointed ear shifting beneath the fall of silver hair. Thessaly's smile arrives a little after the nod it answers. Aldric's greeting is formal but unstrained.

“Welcome to Garvey House.”

Seraphel lifts two fingers from the edge of the table. Her voice, when it comes, is light and measured.

“May supper find a kinder course
Than treaties carried here by force.”

When you introduce yourself and ask after a press, the table's attention changes shape. Rashid's hand leaves the wineglass.

“There is one in the Commerce annex,” he says. “A hand press, not a large shop. The lower workshop keeps the type drawers and a ledger for student use. You will need the clerk to show you the chase and the ink room before they give you a key.”

“The use-sheet is posted on the workshop door,” Thessaly says. “There is an open hour tomorrow after the midday seminar.”

Rashid glances toward her. “You saw the new sheet?”

“Yes.”

Ashara moves a candle half an inch from the table's edge. “Then the useful answer has been given.”

“A press,” Caelindra says, looking now at the ink on your fingers rather than the place settings. “What did you print?”

Ysolde turns one of the unused knives between thumb and forefinger, studying the line of its edge. “The separate letters are kept in cases?”

“A letter set in common lead,” Seraphel says,
“May cross a bridge no foot has tread.”

The dinner bell sounds in full from the hall outside, low enough to tremble in the table's wood. A server in Academy livery enters with a covered tureen, followed by another carrying a basket of dark bread. Among the named places, the card marked GARVEY waits at an empty chair between Thessaly and Ysolde.
