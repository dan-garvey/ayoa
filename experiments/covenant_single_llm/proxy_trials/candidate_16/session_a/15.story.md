Rashid gives a brief nod across the dinner table. "So am I."

On Tuesday, the eastern garden path is already marked with small chalk lines by the time you take a bench beyond the low wall. Your notebook rests beside you while the practice group gathers in twos and threes, shedding satchels and jackets onto the stone.

Rashid begins with foot placement. He walks the length of the path once, heel and toe falling between the chalk marks, then has them repeat the pattern in silence. A pair of students keep drifting toward the gravel and are sent back to begin again. When they move on to turns, he makes them stop before the pivot becomes a stumble: step, turn, recover, reset.

The hour finds its own pace. There are stretches of counted movement, short breaks for water, a complaint about a torn shoe, and the dry scrape of practice blades drawn only to measure distance. No one strikes. Near the end, Rashid has them work through the same sequence without the chalk lines, each person choosing where to stop before the flowerbeds.

When the Academy bell carries over from the quadrangle, he calls the session finished. The group collects its things and leaves the eastern path as it found it, except for a few pale chalk smudges on the stone.
