Dinner has drawn most of the house back to the long table. You find a place near Caelindra while servers set down bowls of peppered lentils and flatbread still warm from the oven.

She listens to your verdict on the poems with her hands folded beside her plate. At the mention of the magistrate, one pointed ear shifts beneath her hair.

"The poem means you to feel sorry for him," she says. "A little. The parrot does not accuse him of anything. It merely repeats him."

She tears a piece of bread in two. "What I found less forgivable was that he left the cage outside because he expected a servant to notice it. The speech was private; the carelessness was not."
