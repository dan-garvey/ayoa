The next few days acquire a rhythm. Classes fill the mornings and afternoons; in the gaps, you read where you can find a quiet table. Your walks take you along the garden walls, where the sheltered air holds the scent of wet stone and flowering vines while lightning moves slowly below the plateau. At meals, the long table makes room for ordinary complaints about assignments, jokes that do not need repeating, and the practical business of passing dishes.

On the fourth evening, Rashid arrives at dinner with the petition stack gone from under his arm. He waits until the servers have set down the soup before speaking to the table.

"The group chose Tuesday and Thursday, one hour after the last bell, on the eastern path," he says. "Six people can make both evenings, and everyone understands there will be no sparring. I submitted the request this morning. We will see whether the Academy answers before then."
