"See that you do," Caelindra says.

The next three days settle into the Academy's rhythm. Classes leave annotated handouts on your desk and new names in the margins of the primer; free hours find you back beneath the library lamps with *The Minor Catastrophes*. The book goes on finding trouble in overlooked places: a woman files a complaint against the rain for spoiling her laundry, a gardener mistakes a duke's private apology for a seed catalogue. Its last poem ends with a family dinner delayed by a missing key and never explains who has it.

When you return the blue-bound volume, Caelindra turns it once in her hands and opens it at random. The pages are clean.

"You finished it quickly," she says. "I hope the key disappointed you."

On the third evening, you come down to dinner as usual. The table is louder than it has been for the past few nights, with small conversations breaking and reforming over bowls of stew. Rashid has set a neat stack of papers beside the water carafe; several signatures already fill the first sheet.

He straightens the stack. "I am submitting the request for supervised court access tomorrow morning. If you train with the group and want your name on it, it is there."
