By the time you return from your room, the dining hall has quieted behind you. The library smells of lamp oil and old leather. Its upper galleries disappear into shadow above the iron spiral stair, while pools of yellow light lie across the long reading tables.

Caelindra has taken a place near the windows, where the storm makes a dim, ceaseless motion beyond the glass. She accepts the two pages without ceremony and reads them standing at first, then sits when the cooper reaches the dead man's address.

She does not interrupt. The only sound for a while is the soft turn of paper and, somewhere in the stacks, wood settling in its joints. When she reaches the end, she reads the final paragraph once more. Her thumb holds the pages together at the corner.

"Yes," she says when you ask. "The cooper takes the advance. Sensible. Then half her work vanishes, and I want to know whether she goes after the money, the barrels, or the person who paid her."

She lays the pages flat on the table between you. "You stop at the right place. Why did you stop there?"
