Caelindra gives the book up without comment. Its blue cloth cover is softened at the corners from use.

Classes divide the day into bells, stairwells, and rooms full of other students learning how to sound certain. A morning survey traces the Council's votes through old border disputes; after midday, Practical Rhetoric has the class identify the concession hidden inside a polished argument. By the last bell, the quadrangle is washed in the blue-grey light of the storm.

You take *The Minor Catastrophes* to the library. The room is quieter than it was the night before, its lamps already burning against the early dark. At a table beneath the lower gallery, you begin at the first page.

The poems keep their disasters small and precise. A baker misdelivers a wedding cake to a funeral and spends six stanzas trying to reclaim the roses. A tenant writes increasingly formal notes to the mouse in her pantry. By page twelve, the missing parrot has learned a magistrate's private speech and repeats it from a pear tree while the man insists, with dwindling success, that the bird belongs to someone else.

An hour passes in the pool of lamplight. The book remains open before you, the next poem beginning on the facing page.
