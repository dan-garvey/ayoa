The last of the daylight lies blue across the window glass, turning the gardens below into pale paths and dark hedges. Beyond them, one of the hard-light bridges vanishes into the storm as neatly as a line drawn through wet ink.

Your room is orderly in the way a place prepared for a stranger must be: the bed smoothed flat, the mended charcoal coat given a hook beside the door, a row of clothes from the lawyers folded in the wardrobe. On the desk, the Academy primer rests beside a lamp with its wick already lit. The framed letter leaning against the wall has a narrow, slanting hand across its first line: *To my great-nephew.* The ink has faded brown at the edges.

Somewhere below, chairs scrape over a floor. A burst of conversation rises through the house, then settles again beneath the clink of plates being laid out.

A light knock comes at the door. When no one enters, a woman's voice says from the hall, "Dinner will be served shortly, Mr. Garvey. The dining hall is downstairs, past the common room."

The hall beyond your door carries the warmer smell of candlewax and roast herbs. At the far end, the stair curves down toward the voices.
