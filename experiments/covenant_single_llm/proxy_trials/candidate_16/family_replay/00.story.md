Professor Stone's thumb stops against the rim of his cup. He sets it down among the loose notes and looks past you, toward the upper gallery where the lamps make the iron stair shine black.

“I did. In my first spring here, I had wagered six silver that a map's border was decorative, and I was trying to persuade three first-years of the same thing when Iona came through that door.” He touches one finger to the tabletop. “She took the map from me, held it to the window, and found the surveyor's notation hidden beneath a repair strip; then she paid the students' tea, took my coin, and told them never to trust a lecturer who calls an unread thing ornamental.” His mouth pulls briefly at one corner. “They did not.”
