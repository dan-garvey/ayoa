The signet on your hand catches the lamplight whenever you look down at the primer. Its gilt border has worn thin where some previous owner worried it with a thumb; the page beneath it explains, in brisk black type, that first-years dine together on their arrival night and that formal introductions are customary before the soup.

Beyond the window, the gardens are beginning to gather their evening color. Pale flowers lean over the paths below, and farther out, beyond the house wall, one of the light-bridges hangs above the storm like a line drawn across dark water. The room has the peculiar finished look of a place prepared for somebody else's life: a mended charcoal coat laid across the end of the bed, a row of clothes with unfamiliar tailor's tags, a desk whose drawers stand empty. On the wall beside it, the framed letter addressed to a great-nephew who never arrived waits under its glass.

From downstairs comes the clink of plates and the warm, uneven drift of voices. Someone laughs—a low, quick sound—then another voice answers too softly to make out. The door to the corridor stands open. At its far end, the stair turns down toward the common room and the dining hall beyond it.
