At the seventh bell, the primer's list proves brief: Academy dress, identity seal, timetable, and something to write with. Breakfast is quieter than dinner had been. Schedules lie beside the plates; the conversation rises and falls around instructors, rooms, and whose assigned lecture has the earliest hour.

The Conclave seminar room holds fifteen chairs around its oval table, beneath the framed Accords in seven scripts. Chancellor Mordecai Ashworth conducts the orientation from a stack of neatly marked pages. He covers bridge permissions, library access, seminar conduct, and the expectation that first-years will meet with their advisers before choosing additional courses. His attention passes evenly around the room, though the old angel's eyes rest a moment longer on the Garvey signet when he reaches your name.

A registrar leads the tour through the quadrangle after the meeting: black demon stone warming under the morning sun, white angel marble bright at the chapel steps, living timber grown into the elven colonnade, and the low carved arches that open toward the dragon terraces. From the gardens, a covered passage leads directly to the library. Inside, lamp-oil and old paper meet the cool air from the iron spiral stair; open shelves fill both stories, while the restricted-history cabinet sits locked beneath its signed-access notice.

Just inside the library entrance, a faculty board gives Professor Gareth Stone's hours in a narrow north-gallery office: the second and third bells after midday. At the registry desk, your own appointment card reads: **Professor Kira vel Shaan, Practical Rhetoric — fourth bell after midday, Faculty Hall.** The walk between the library and Faculty Hall is marked on the tour map as less than ten minutes.

By the time the group disperses back into the quadrangle, the library doors stand open behind you, and there is a clear stretch of afternoon before the adviser appointment.
