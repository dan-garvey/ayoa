You take the chair. A server sets a plate before you: a small round of onion tart, its browned top broken by threads of sharp white cheese, with a spoonful of dark greens beside it. The soup tureen begins its slow way around the table.

Rashid glances toward the common room and its ranks of painted Garveys. “Not personally. The brass plate by the fireplace calls her a resident trustee, and credits her with making this a Conclave residence.”

“Then she had decent priorities,” Ashara says. “Food first, inquiries second.”

Rashid settles into his own chair, the folded paper still at his elbow. “Was the letter merely a welcome, or did it say something you need help with?”

Around the table, the others resume the small movements of getting settled: a silver-haired woman turns a page in her menu; the white-haired woman by the window lifts her bowl to inspect the steam; the angel with the small wings reaches for the bread. The tart is still warm when the server pours the first ladle of soup.
