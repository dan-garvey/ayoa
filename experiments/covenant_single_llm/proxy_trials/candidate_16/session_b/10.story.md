The line about Sel and the tart draws the smile it seems designed to earn. The second letter is dated *3 Frostwane, 312 C.R.*, also addressed to Mrs. Pella.

> Dear Mrs. Pella,
>
> Keep the third place at Thursday's breakfast. Sel says she is leaving before dawn, which means she will appear at ten looking tragic and want toast.
>
> Ask the gardener to move the blue pots along the east wall. The roses have taken the path, and Aunt Bea will pretend not to notice until they catch her skirts.
>
> Mara

The household account is headed *Pella & Co., 15 Rainmoot, 311 C.R.* and records a carpenter's fee paid to Rill Fen for the lower garden hinge, eleven dinner covers, cheese, onions, lamp oil, and a crate of lemons. The carpenter's note, dated the following day and addressed to Master Fen, asks him to mend the gate rather than replace it, and to leave the latch low enough for young guests to use without help.

The attendant checks the open-consultation stamp on the request slip. “Handwritten copies of the letters are permitted at this table; use pencil, and keep the originals in their sleeves.”

You copy both letters onto the supplied paper, preserving Mara's dates, addressees, and the uneven turns of her handwriting. When the third bell after midday sounds, the attendant has taken back the green box and left the copies with you. The route to Faculty Hall remains clear on the tour map beside your notes, with the fourth bell still ahead.
