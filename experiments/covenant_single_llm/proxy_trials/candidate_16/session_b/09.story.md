Stone turns the request form toward you and guides you through its narrow fields: collection, item class, consultation table, return time. “Mark only the open household material, not the general Garvey holdings. That will reach the desk today.”

At his direction, you take the completed form to the circulation desk. The librarian checks the adviser appointment card, stamps the request, and directs you to a long oak table beneath the north-gallery windows. “Bring the file back when you leave. It will wait for tomorrow; Professor Shaan will not.”

Your writing paper rests beside the blotter when an attendant arrives with a shallow green archive box. Inside are four pieces: two folded letters to Mrs. Pella, a household account, and a note to a carpenter about the garden gate. The first letter is dated *12 Rainmoot, 311 C.R.*

> Dear Mrs. Pella,
>
> Please have the garden gate repaired before Tuesday. It has begun to complain whenever it rains, and I refuse to let our guests think the house is falling down.
>
> We will be eleven at supper rather than eight. Put the onion tart on the sideboard if you want anyone else to have a slice; Sel says she dislikes cheese and has taken three portions already.
>
> Mara

The other letters remain folded in their sleeves, their dates and names visible through the thin paper. The north-gallery clock has not yet reached the next bell.
