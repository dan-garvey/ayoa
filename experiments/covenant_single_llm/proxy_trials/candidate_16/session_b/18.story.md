Sol takes out a two-part form and writes the result in a narrow, exact hand: **No measurable resonance in standard channels. No practical placement recommended.** A registry seal presses blue into the lower corner.

“Yes. Keep the original; registry receives the duplicate.”

The signed copy is dry by the time you leave the lower colonnade. In the gardens, afternoon air carries damp earth and the faint sweetness of the flowering hedges. The practicum map leads away from the main pale path, past a clipped yew wall, to a rectangular terrace of raised beds.

Each bed is edged in dark stone and marked with a small slate stake. Some hold newly turned soil, some close rows of seedlings, and some last season's dry stalks waiting to be cleared. A painted board at the gate reads **FOUNDATIONS OF LIVING GROUNDS — FIRST-YEAR PLOTS**. Beneath it, a list of student names has been pinned up, with the individual bed assignments still blank beside them.

Beyond the terrace, the garden wall catches the bridge's white light even in daylight. The map remains open in your hand, its practicum plots neatly numbered around the beds.
