The fourth bell has barely finished when a woman's voice answers from inside. “Come in, Mr. Garvey.”

Professor Kira vel Shaan's office is warmer than the corridor. She sits behind a desk crowded with marked speech drafts, a copper tea pot, and a rack of slim practice blades used as rhetorical examples rather than weapons. Her swept-back horns are polished to a dull dark shine; her sleeves are rolled neatly above her wrists. She takes the timetable, reads it once, then sets it between you.

“Practical Rhetoric has no magic prerequisite: each week you will write and defend a brief, negotiate a live disagreement, and learn to say what you need without hiding the cost; I expect preparation, attention, and revision.”

She draws a line through the open elective spaces with a red pencil. “Take the required Conclave Institutions course, one history course, and an Arcana assessment before you decide magic has no place in your life; the remaining place should be something you would study even if no Council seat waited at the end of it.”

Kira turns the timetable so its blank choices face you again. On the shelf behind her, course catalogues stand in labeled rows: history, law, diplomacy, arcana, commerce, military theory. The advisor appointment has only just begun.
