Professor Stone's office is narrow enough that the bookshelves seem to meet over the desk. A green-shaded lamp burns beside a card catalogue and a stack of student essays. At the knock, a chair scrapes across the floorboards.

The man who opens the door has silver threaded through his dark hair and ink on the heel of one hand. His eyes settle on the signet before they return to your face. “Come in, Mr. Garvey.”

He listens without interrupting, then pulls a thin drawer from the catalogue and scans a series of handwritten cards. “Mara was your grandfather's younger sister; her personal correspondence went to the Academy archives, though most originals require the chancellor's countersignature.”

Stone takes out one card and lays it on the desk between you. Beneath *GARVEY, MARA* are entries for household correspondence, garden accounts, and several letters marked open to Conclave students. “Some household letters are available to you, and I can show you how to request them; they will tell you more about her than the family chart will.”

The request shelf stands against the far wall, supplied with cream forms and a bottle of sand for drying ink. Through the open library doors, the north-gallery clock marks the beginning of the first office-hour bell.
