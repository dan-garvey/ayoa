Rashid answers the wish with a brief lift of his hand before turning back toward the common room. Ashara waits only long enough to let him go, then takes the garden doors with you.

Outside, the air has a cool green scent from the beds along the path. Ashara's warmth travels beside you through the evening chill. At the wall, the bridge rises in a white, unwavering arc, broad enough for a carriage and somehow without visible support. Far below it, the storm turns over itself in slow blue flashes. The light catches the pale walls, the wet-looking leaves, the gold in Ashara's eyes.

“A whole evening is usually an administrative error,” she says. “When I find one, I take the dueling ground after dark—no crowd, no ranking clerk—and then I eat something hot enough to make everyone else complain.”

She rests one hand on the wall and looks along the bridge rather than down at the storm. “The grounds are better when nobody is there to mistake practice for a performance.”
