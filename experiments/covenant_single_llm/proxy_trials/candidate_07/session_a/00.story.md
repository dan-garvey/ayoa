The room has been made ready by people who knew your measurements only from a solicitor's list. A charcoal coat hangs beside the narrow wardrobe; a pair of boots, polished enough to show the window's pale light, waits beneath it. The desk holds fresh paper, an ink bottle, and a framed letter in a hand that thins at the ends of its strokes.

Great-nephew, it begins.

Beyond the glass, the gardens lie in sheltered terraces below the house. Lamps are coming awake along their paths, each one a small fixed pearl against the bruised clouds beyond the plateau. Farther off, one of the bridges cuts a bright line into the storm.

The house is filling below. Footsteps cross the common room's old boards; chairs scrape in the dining hall. A burst of conversation rises and is swallowed by the ceiling, followed by the low clink of serving dishes. Something warm and spiced reaches the landing before the next knock does.

An Academy server stands outside when the door opens, silver buttons catching the corridor lamps. She holds no tray, only a folded seating card.

“Dinner is being set, Mr. Garvey,” she says. “The other first-years have arrived.”
