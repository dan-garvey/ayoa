Caelindra reads without touching the inked margins. The library's window light travels slowly over the silver at her temple while she turns the second page, then the third.

At the line where the replacement has failed to come, she stops. One pale fingernail rests beside the sentence before she looks up.

"Yes," she says. "It holds my interest. He has made a choice with no tidy shape to it, and you have not rushed to excuse him. That is sufficient." 

She draws the pages into a neater stack. "The neighbor is still only a condition, though. Sick is not yet a person. Give the reader one thing that makes him leave the tower himself, rather than send for help or wait another hour. And tell us what he believed about the light when he went. Was he certain the replacement would come? Did he know he was gambling?"

Her eyes return to the last unfinished line. "When the lamp goes dark, whose ship is due through that channel? Do you know?"
