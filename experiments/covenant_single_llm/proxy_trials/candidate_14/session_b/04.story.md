The name holds Professor Stone still for a beat. He takes off his spectacles and folds them with both hands before setting them atop the closed book.

“Miriam Garvey,” he says. “Yes. I knew her well enough to be corrected by her, which was how she preferred to be remembered.”

He leans back in his chair, looking for a moment not at the shelves but at the rain-dark window beyond them. “She worked first in the human delegation's correspondence office, and later taught source reading here when I was still young enough to think a full archive was the same thing as understanding it. Miriam had a remarkable gift for making the rest of us feel lazy.”

One memory seems to settle into place. “There was a seminar on the First Transit Accord. A dragon student announced that the human text was merely a translation, and that the elven original was therefore the only version worth consulting. Miriam did not argue. She gave every student a copy in each available script and asked them to find the clause about safe passage.”

Stone's mouth shifts, almost a smile. “They spent an hour at it. The elven wording concerned tolls. The angel wording concerned conduct. The human version had been drafted to carry both meanings, because the negotiators had known exactly where later governments would try to make a gap. Miriam's point was not that one language was truer than another. It was that calling something a translation could be a convenient way to stop reading.”

He reaches for the cold tea, then sets it down again without drinking. “The dragon student came back the next morning with a better question. Miriam gave him plum cake from her desk drawer and told him that improvement ought to be rewarded before it became a habit.”

His gaze returns to you. “She was exacting, funny when she forgot to be careful, and impatient with anyone who treated people as furniture in an argument. She was not always easy. She would have disliked being made into a saint.”

Stone moves a stack of books from the other side of the table, clearing a space beneath the lamp.
