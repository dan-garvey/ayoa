The first marker leads to a letter dated **3 First Rain, 294**. Miriam writes that Tomas has “made a house of a place with a crooked roof,” and that Leona has learned every neighbor's name before deciding which of them is worth inviting to supper. In the margin, Miriam has added that Leona corrected the school register when it misspelled two children from the next street.

The next, dated **20 Sunheight, 295**, is shorter. Tomas and Leona are expecting a child. Miriam tells Stone that they have declined a formal visit to Garvey House: *They want one life that begins without a council chamber staring at it. I think that is sensible, though Tomas has never been sensible where a broken hinge is concerned.*

On **8 Frostfall, 296**, Miriam writes again: *Rowan is here. Tomas tried to repair the bassinet before he had learned how it was broken. Leona says the child will have an ordinary childhood, which is a promise worth keeping even if we have always been bad at ordinary.* The line is copied into the notebook beneath its date, the pencil pressure darkening slightly over the names.

The fourth marked passage, **14 Thaw, 298**, catches the library visit Stone remembers. Miriam describes the water-damaged maps, Tomas's insistence that the pencil annotations be preserved, and Leona finding errors in a children's history before the tea had cooled. *Rowan slept through the entire argument on Stone's shoulder,* she writes. *He was the most sensible person in the room.*

In the letter from **2 Harvest, 299**, Miriam complains fondly that Tomas has sent a kettle to be mended instead of buying a new one. She reports that Leona's pocket notebook now contains the names of twenty-three pupils, three grocers, and “at least one dog who has no business being included.” Then comes a quieter sentence: *They are determined you will know the world before you are asked to represent it.*

The final marker sits in a letter dated **19 First Rain, 300**. Miriam's handwriting is less even there. Tomas has been ill with a fever that breaks and returns; Leona is tired enough that she has stopped teaching for the week. The doctor calls it lingering and advises rest. *They mean to come when the weather improves,* Miriam writes. *For now, I have told them the house can wait.*

There is no later marked passage.

The six dates and their copied lines take up several pages of the notebook in your own hand. Across the table, Stone keeps the lamp steady over the correspondence and does not hurry the work.
