The stairwell narrows around its turn, old dark wood giving way to pale stone at the ground floor. Rashid takes the inside of the curve without seeming to choose it. The list from his pocket makes a soft, repeated tap against his coat as he walks.

“Professor Stone is the likeliest person to ask,” he says. “He teaches human Covenant history, and he is usually in the library after dinner; whether he knew your family himself, I cannot tell you. Ashworth has been chancellor for sixty years, but I have never found him generous with old stories.”

At the foot of the stairs, he glances toward the library's half-open door, where a lamp burns beyond the ironwork. “Stone may have an answer worth having. Or he may have a reason not to.”

The dining hall is already bright with candlelight and the steadier glow of working lamps. Seven places along the long table have been set with the same white plates and different drinking vessels: a broad black stone cup at one end, a narrow silver goblet at another, a clear green glass that catches the flame in its stem.

Several heads turn as Rashid enters with you. A tall woman with swept-back black-red horns has claimed a seat halfway down the table, her severe black hair catching a red shine from the candles. Beside the window, a white-haired woman sits so still that the frost-pale outline of her profile seems carved into the chair. A silver-haired elf has a small book open beside her plate, one finger marking a page; she closes it without hurry.

Near the far end, an angel in white and gold is saying something low to a smaller woman with blue-black hair and entirely black eyes. The angel's voice reaches the doorway in the tail end of a line: “—and the wording is still an excuse.” Across from them, another angel straightens a folded napkin with exacting care.

Rashid pauses beside an empty chair, then looks toward the table. “Everyone, this is Rowan Garvey.”
