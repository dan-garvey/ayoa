The notice names the second hearing for fourth bell the following day. Its wording has changed since the first: **ACCESS REVIEW FOLLOWING PRELIMINARY INCIDENT SUMMARY.**

The afternoon and evening at Garvey House leave time for the primer, the sketch, and the ordinary creaks of the old residence. At fourth bell the next day, the office beside the covered court holds more people than before. The dean's representative and clerk have returned; a facilities officer has spread inspection sheets across the desk. Rashid sits with the petition. Ashara takes the wall again, and Aldric is among the observers with the preliminary summary folded in his hand.

The facilities officer reports that the failed target rig has been removed. The worn pin should have been caught in the previous maintenance check, though the inquiry has not yet determined who was responsible for that omission. The rest of the court's fixed equipment has passed an initial inspection, with two minor repairs scheduled before the following evening.

“The fault was in a piece of equipment,” Rashid says. “That is a reason to repair it and improve the check, not a reason to tell students without private instruction that they may simply do without practice.”

Ashara adds that a limited schedule can be tested without treating an individual booking as the Academy's whole answer. Aldric, after asking whether the supervising instructor will have authority to stop a session, says that he will support a trial if the safety conditions are written rather than assumed.

The dean's representative takes the point. Once the two repairs are certified, the court will reopen for a two-week pilot: three supervised evening sessions each week, twelve students per session, no participant fee, and a published sign-up list. The instructor will record equipment checks before and after each session. The confidential account from the injured student remains in the file but will not be circulated. The separate inquiry into the maintenance lapse continues.

The clerk reads the decision back into the ledger. In the notebook, the new page fills beneath the earlier hearing notes: rig removed; repairs due tomorrow; supervised pilot after clearance; twelve places; no fee; equipment checks logged; maintenance inquiry unresolved.

When the meeting ends, the office door opens onto the court's packed earth and its suspended targets, one space now empty where the failed rig had hung.
