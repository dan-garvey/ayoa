The lamp on the desk burns with a dry, peppery smell, turning the brass edge of the Garvey signet warm against the dark wood. Beside it, a blue-paper primer lies among the clothes the lawyers sent ahead. Its gilt title catches the light whenever the flame bends: *A First Guide to the Conclave of Thrones.*

Across from the bed, the framed letter has been hung a little too high, as if whoever arranged the room thought it belonged to the house more than to its intended reader. The ink is brown with age. *To my great-nephew, should one ever come to Garvey House—* The rest disappears beneath the frame's narrow lower rail.

A bell sounds somewhere below: not grand enough for chapel, but clear enough to travel through the old timber and plaster. A moment later, the house begins answering it. Chairs scrape in the floor beneath this one. Someone laughs in the corridor, then a door shuts with deliberate care.

At the threshold, a young man pauses without crossing it. He is tall, impeccably dressed in a dark green coat cut close through the shoulders, with fine dark fur visible over the backs of his hands where they rest on a folded sheet of paper. His amber eyes flick once toward the letter, then return to you with the practiced courtesy of someone who has had to make courtesy look effortless.

"Rowan Garvey?" he asks. "I am Rashid vel Amara. Dinner is downstairs, and the dining hall has enough doors to make a first arrival feel like a tactical error. I was headed that way."

Behind him, the corridor opens onto a stairwell washed in candlelight. Voices drift upward with the smell of roast citrus and something peppery enough to compete with the lamp oil. The folded paper in Rashid's hand is covered in closely set names, most of them crossed out in a precise black line.

He tucks it into an inner pocket. "Would you like company on the way down?"
