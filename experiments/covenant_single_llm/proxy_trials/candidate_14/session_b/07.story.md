Professor Stone acknowledges your good night with a small inclination of his head, then reopens the history beneath the green lamp. By the time you leave the library, he is reading again, one hand keeping the marker ribbon in place.

The dining hall has thinned to its after-hours shape. The servers have cleared the plates and most of the candles, leaving only a pair of lamps burning over the middle of the long table. At the window end, Ysolde Thornmantle remains in her place from dinner. A small iron teapot sits on a trivet before her with two plain cups beside it, and she has turned one of the chairs to face the gardens rather than the table.

Beyond the glass, the lamps along the pale paths shine steadily while the light bridges flare and fade over the storm below. Ysolde watches them with her hands folded around her cup. When she notices you in the doorway, she glances at the unused place setting, then reaches for the teapot.

“There is enough,” she says. “It is mountain mint. Would you like some?”
