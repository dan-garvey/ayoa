The office beside the covered court is narrow and bright, its frosted side window giving onto the empty packed earth beyond. A dozen chairs have been set in two uneven rows. Rashid sits at the small table nearest the dean's representative with his petition, while Ashara stands against the wall in a dark training coat, her reserved place at the court visible through the glass behind her. Aldric takes a seat among the observers. A clerk with an open ledger records the fourth bell as it sounds.

The hearing begins with the request read into the record: supervised evening access for students outside the Military track, an explanation of the present restriction, and review once the instructor's report is complete. Eighteen signatures follow it.

Rashid keeps his argument close to the page. “Students with private instruction have alternatives. The people who use the covered court do not all have that option, and a temporary safety measure should not become an indefinite exclusion because no one has finished asking what happened.”

The dean's representative asks whether anyone can establish the cause of the injury. Rashid says that he has an account from the student hurt, but was asked not to make that person a public example. The clerk writes this down without adding a name.

“The report before this office does not yet distinguish equipment failure, negligence, or ordinary training risk,” the representative says. “I will not reopen the court on inference.”

Ashara speaks only after the official invites additional comment. She does not offer her own booking as a substitute for the group, but she makes the narrower point that an individual reservation and a blanket suspension are separate decisions, each requiring a reason. Aldric says that he agrees the report should be produced promptly, though he will not support reopening the court until it is.

The discussion circles through supervision, cost, and who can reasonably be expected to attend an evening session. No answer comes that turns the injury into a simple fault. At the end, the representative orders a redacted preliminary summary of the instructor's report by noon the next day, invites confidential accounts to be added to the file, and sets a second hearing for two days hence. The court remains closed until then.

Your notes fill a page: eighteen signatures; no public witness; cause not established; report by noon; review at the second hearing. When the clerk closes the ledger, the observers begin to file out past the silent court.
