Thessaly gathers the practice tiles before they can be disturbed by a passing sleeve. “It permits correction,” she says. “That is different from forgiving.”

She turns the pieces facedown, mixes them with quick, practiced movements, and lays out a fresh pattern with five border gates. This one has a narrow loop near the center and two forks placed close enough together to make the routes look simpler than they are.

“My cousin Rill taught me,” Thessaly says as she turns the first tile faceup. “She set the opening board badly on purpose and called it a lesson in attention; after three games, I stopped letting her arrange the first board.”

The last tile clicks into its place at the far edge of the grid. Thessaly straightens it with the side of one finger, then turns the board so the five gates face you.
