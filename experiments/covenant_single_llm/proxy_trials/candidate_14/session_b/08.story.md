Ysolde fills the second cup with a careful, level pour. The tea is pale gold and sharp with mint, its steam briefly whitening the window before it vanishes.

For a while, the hall contains only the small sound of the pot settling on its trivet and the distant pulse of light beyond the gardens. The westward bridge brightens, thins to a line, then broadens again over the storm.

“No,” Ysolde says at last. “I have seen it from above, from the spans, and from this window in every season. It changes each time.”

She raises one finger toward the west bridge as its light takes on a faint green cast near the far end. “That color arrives before the wind reaches the gardens. The storm will climb for an hour, then fall away.”

Her gaze stays on the bridge. “Getting used to it would mean ceasing to look.”
