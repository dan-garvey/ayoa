Ysolde inclines her head over the last of her tea. “Good night, Rowan.”

In your room, the lamp gives the desk a small circle of certainty against the dark windows. Stone's account of Miriam, the water-damaged papers, the corrected captions, and the ordinary details of your parents' life take their places on the page before the edges can blur. The library slip goes beside the blue primer, where its compact writing will meet the eye on the way out.

The house settles around you after the lamps are put out: a distant stair creaks, pipes knock once in the wall, and then the storm's slow pressure is all that remains beyond the glass.

Morning finds the gardens washed pale beneath a high grey light. By the time you reach the ground floor, Garvey House smells of toasted oats, coffee, and the orange peel someone has dropped into a warming pot of tea.

Breakfast has begun in the dining hall without the ceremony of the previous evening. Thessaly sits near the sideboard with a small grid of painted wooden tiles arranged before her, turning one over in her fingers as she studies the possible moves. At the long table, Rashid has claimed a place with a bowl of porridge and a notebook opened beside it. He looks up when you enter, then closes the notebook without covering the page.

“Morning,” he says. “The porridge is better than it looks.”
