The rest of the day at Garvey House passes quietly. The primer yields more names and procedures on a second reading; the sketch gains the darker join between the house's lower stone and its timbered upper story, then a few cautious leaves along the path. By evening, the court hearing has become a page of notes among other pages rather than the whole shape of the day.

At noon the following day, a fresh notice has been pinned outside the court office: **PRELIMINARY INCIDENT SUMMARY AVAILABLE FOR STUDENT INSPECTION.** The clerk behind the desk checks the timepiece on the wall, then brings out a single stamped copy when asked. It remains on the desk beneath a clear paperweight.

The summary identifies no student. It records that the injury occurred during an evening avoidance drill when the retaining pin on a suspended padded target gave way and the target swung unexpectedly into the participant's path. The injured student's wrist was treated at the infirmary.

An initial inspection found wear beneath the pin's enamel and an incomplete maintenance entry from the previous week. The report does not determine when the damage occurred, whether the instructor's pre-session check should have caught it, or whether the drill itself was being run within the proper staffing guidance. A full inspection of the covered court's equipment is ordered before any reopening.

The closing recommendation keeps the court shut for now, but distinguishes between repair of the target rig and the broader question of whether supervised access can resume once the equipment is cleared.

The notebook receives a new entry beneath the hearing notes: suspended target; worn retaining pin; maintenance record incomplete; no finding on supervision; full inspection pending; access question still open.
