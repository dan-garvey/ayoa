Your greeting passes around the table with the small pause reserved for a name everyone has heard before meeting its owner. The horned woman inclines her head once. “Ashara vel Kothren.” The white-haired woman offers Ysolde Thornmantle; the elf, after a glance from you to the signet, gives Caelindra Vaeyn in a voice as smooth as the varnished table. Thessaly Morrow lifts her green glass in acknowledgement, while Aldric Verantus rises far enough from his chair to make the courtesy formal.

Seraphel's smile reaches her blue eyes as she opens one hand toward the empty place. “Good evening, Rowan Garvey—\nSit and be fed.”

A server in Academy livery sets down covered dishes: rice steamed with saffron and nuts, slices of dark-crusted fish in a citrus glaze, charred greens shining with oil. The heat rising from Ashara's place makes the nearest candle lean away from her plate. Rashid waits until you have served yourself before drawing out the folded sheet from his coat and laying it beside his glass.

“I am collecting names for supervised evening access to the covered court while the faculty decides what actually happened,” he says. “The present ban has shut out students who cannot pay for private training.”

Aldric's fingers remain on his folded napkin. “I will not sign a request concerning an injury until I have seen the instructor's report.”

Ashara takes a piece of fish with the point of her knife. “Then read the report,” she says. “Do not mistake an unanswered question for a reason to punish everyone who uses the court.”

Rashid's mouth tightens for a moment before he writes something beside one of the names on his sheet. He does not press her about the individual booking she has for tomorrow. Across the table, Thessaly reaches for the paper when it comes near her, reading the first lines with her head tilted. Caelindra takes another measured bite of dinner and watches the exchange over the rim of her glass. Ysolde's attention has settled on the covered dish nearest her, where the steam has begun to cloud the silver lid.

The disagreement does not fill the room so much as give it a current. Cutlery resumes its quiet work around it; outside the tall windows, the garden lamps have begun to kindle along the pale paths.
