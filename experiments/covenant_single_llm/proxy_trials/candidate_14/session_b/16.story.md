The sketch ends with the arcade's shadow falling across the lower wall, a darker wash of pencil beneath the unsteady lines of the upper windows. Back inside, clothes find drawers, books find a place on the desk, and the room gradually loses the look of an arrangement made for a stranger.

The blue primer gives the Covenant in a clean sequence of dates and principles: the end of the Sundering War, the Council's seven seats, the bridges and treaties that followed, the Academy's charge to teach future leaders before they inherit a crisis. Its account of the empty human seat is brief. Its pages on Conclave conduct are more exact, including Article Nineteen in the dry procedural language of law.

By late afternoon, the library slip still waits beside the primer, its appointment written beneath Miriam's name. The dinner bell is timed early enough to leave the ground-floor hall quiet again by the hour Stone set. When the lamps are lit in the library, you find him at the same window table beneath the green shade.

A grey linen packet rests beside his cold tea. It is tied with a faded blue ribbon, and several narrow paper markers stand from its edge.

“On time,” Stone says. “I found six of Miriam's letters that mention your parents; I marked the relevant passages, but left the pages whole. Context belongs to a letter, too.”

He unties the ribbon and sets the packet between you on the cleared table.
