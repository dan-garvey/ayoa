Thessaly begins sorting the tiles into their small cloth bag. “Some morning,” she says. “Do not expect me to make the first board gentle.”

The gardens hold the morning's dampness in their leaves, but the paths are dry beneath the pale sheltering walls. From there, Garvey House looks less like a single building than an old agreement that has acquired additions without ever quite settling the argument. Dark lower stone rises beneath white, inward-curving window frames; above them, a second-floor bay is wrapped in weathered timber that has grown around its own iron brackets. The upper windows catch the grey light in uneven ranks.

Past a bed of late-blooming blue flowers, a covered stone bench sits beneath the overhang of a narrow arcade. The wind reaches the garden only in softened breaths there, carrying the sharp green scent of clipped hedges rather than the storm's colder air.

With the notebook open across your knees, the first pencil lines find the roofline, then the taller chimney at the north end, then the curve of the bay window above your room. The proportions resist a simple outline. One side of the house leans into the garden; the other seems to turn away toward the library and the unused upper floors. A path and its low wall take shape beneath it, followed by the dark mass of the lower stone.

For a while, the only interruptions are the scrape of pencil on paper and the small movements of leaves along the arcade. The house remains in view beyond the page, larger and stranger than the sketch, but held there in a beginning of lines.
