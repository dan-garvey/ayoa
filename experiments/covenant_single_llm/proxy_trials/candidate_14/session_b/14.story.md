Thessaly's black eyes rest on the board for a moment. “Count the gates twice,” she says. “Rill was very good at moving one when no one was looking.”

Then she folds her hands beside the sideboard and leaves the tiles to you.

The first few turns go slowly. From the central circle, one pale road can reach both the red and white gates if the near fork is left facing inward. The blue gate has only one usable entrance. The green gate appears to have two until a turn near the outer edge sends its path straight into blank ground.

That dead end takes the board apart again. The tiles return to their earlier positions one by one, and the next arrangement begins from the central loop rather than the nearest gate. A road that seemed wasted on the first attempt becomes the only clean route around the loop; the second fork gives the green gate its way in without cutting across the blue path.

By the time the half hour has passed, four gates reach the center. The fifth, set at the far edge, still points toward the wrong side of a narrow curve. Turning it closes the last road without disturbing the others.

The finished board holds together beneath the morning light: five painted routes, each continuous from its border gate to the gold circle in the middle.

Thessaly leans forward only then. She traces each path once, beginning from the center as you had, and gives a small nod.

“You counted,” she says.
