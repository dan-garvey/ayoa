The tea cools slowly between your hands. Outside, the bridge lights keep their measured brightening and dimming while the garden lamps hold steady along the paths.

Ysolde does not seem to require speech to fill the interval. Once, she rises to adjust the lid of the teapot; once, she traces a short line through the condensation on the window and watches it vanish. The dining hall remains otherwise empty, its far end receding into polished shadow.

Near the end of the half hour, the green at the far end of the west bridge deepens. A moment later, the leaves in the nearest garden bed turn their pale undersides toward the glass. The wind arrives without thunder, pressing a cool hand along the walls of Garvey House and stirring the candle flame beside the unused places at the table.

Ysolde watches it pass through the gardens. “There,” she says.
