Stone considers the request without looking away. At last, he nods once.

“I do still have them,” he says. “You may read the passages about your parents and copy what you wish in your own hand; the rest belongs to a woman who trusted me, and I will not make all of it public merely because she is gone.”

He opens a shallow drawer beneath the table and takes out a blank library slip. In a careful, compact script, he writes *Miriam Garvey — personal correspondence*, then adds a time beneath it.

“I keep the packet in my rooms rather than on these shelves. I will bring it here tomorrow evening, after the dinner bell.”

The slip remains on the table between the closed history and the cold cup. It is not an official summons or a stamped permission—only a name, a place, and a promise made in ink.
