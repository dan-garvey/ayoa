Thessaly gives the blue tile one last quarter-turn. Its painted road curves into a pale lane, then into the small gold circle at the center of the grid. She traces each of the other border gates in turn with a fingernail, following their paths inward until she reaches the same circle.

“Finished,” she says.

She gathers the tiles into a neat pile and replaces the larger grid with a smaller board marked by four gates instead of seven. The central circle is fixed; around it, she lays out nine painted squares in a loose pattern of roads, forks, and blank ground. While you finish breakfast, she explains that each move turns a single tile, that every gate must reach the center, and that no route may cross itself or end on an unused edge.

Rashid finishes his coffee, slips his notebook into his coat, and leaves the dining hall with the quiet purpose he had brought to breakfast. Thessaly does not look up as the door closes. She squares the first tile toward you.

The practice board begins simply. Working from the nearest border gate, you turn a tile until its road runs inward, then shape the next one to meet it. A second gate joins the center by way of a forked square. On the fourth move, the remaining green route can be turned toward the open lane or toward the last unused fork; either choice appears to preserve the road in front of it.

When you ask for help, Thessaly leans closer but leaves the tile where it is. “Trace from the center this time,” she says. “The road you are saving has somewhere to go. The other one does not.”

From that direction, the dead end is visible: the open lane would leave the green gate stranded behind a turn that cannot be corrected later. The unused fork gives it a route around the central circle instead. With that tile turned, the last two paths settle into place one after the other.

Thessaly follows each route with her fingertip, checking the board in silence. Then she nudges the final tile a fraction straighter.

“That is a win,” she says. “The board does not care whether you asked for help.”
