The server steps aside, and the main stair carries you down through the house’s warmer layers: old wood, the edge of the common room’s fire, a lamp burning somewhere near the library door. Portraits in dark frames gather over the fireplace below, their painted Garveys looking past the stairs toward an age when the house held a family rather than a set of rooms.

The dining hall is brighter than the rest of it. Candles burn between working lamps along the long table, and the servers have filled the first bowls before everyone arrives. There are plenty of empty places among the seventeen settings. You take one and find the nearest dishes arranged for choice rather than ceremony: dark bread, roasted squash, a peppered lentil stew, and a platter of glazed onions that explains the smell upstairs.

At your greeting, the tall beastkin nearest the middle of the table rises just far enough to make the gesture clear. His cuffs sit perfectly over the fur at his wrists. “Rashid vel Amara—welcome to Garvey House.”

Beside him, the horned woman with ember-red skin has already claimed a bowl and set her napkin across her lap with military neatness. Her long black hair is tied severely back; her tail curves around the chair leg. “Ashara vel Kothren; eat while it is hot.”

Across the table, snow-pale Ysolde Thornmantle gives you a small inclination of her head, grey eyes briefly returning to the window. Caelindra Vaeyn’s violet gaze passes over the Garvey signet before she attends to her own place setting. Seraphel Dawnquill folds her small white wings more closely behind her chair, her warm gold light catching on the silver-haired elf’s goblet. Thessaly Morrow offers a quick, oddly timed smile from beneath blue-black hair, while Aldric Verantus straightens a fraction in his white-and-gold clothes and acknowledges you with a formal nod.

The stew is hot enough to steam the ink from no one’s fingers but yours, and the bread has a crisp blackened edge. Conversation starts again in a few low currents around the table. Rashid tears off a piece of his own loaf and looks across at you. “Did the primer tell you how we are meant to get through our first dinner?”
