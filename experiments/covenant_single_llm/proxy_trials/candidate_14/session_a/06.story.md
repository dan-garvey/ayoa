For the second hand, the cards on your side make nineteen at once: a nine, an eight, and a two. Rashid’s make twenty-three. He gives up a court card for a four and finishes at seventeen, and the two-copper pot crosses the table to you.

The third hand asks more of the same rule. Your nine, eight, and seven make twenty-four; the nine goes into the discard pile, and a five brings the total down to twenty. Rashid keeps a court card, a six, and a five for twenty-one.

The last two coppers left unused in the wooden dish return to you. Rashid stacks the three finished hands into a neat square, two pots on his side of the table and one on yours. He is one copper ahead when the agreed game ends.

The fire has lowered to a red seam beneath its ash. Beyond the common-room windows, the gardens are dark except for their pale paths, and the hard-light bridge hangs motionless beyond them.
