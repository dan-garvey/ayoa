Dinner carries on around the long table until the lamps have burned lower and the first bowls have been traded for fruit and small cakes. Near the end, Rashid’s folded notes become a quiet argument instead of a piece of paper: he maintains that closing the covered court strands students who cannot pay for private instruction; Aldric holds that an injury deserves caution until someone has established what caused it; Ashara finds the restriction broad without offering up her own booked training time. Nothing settles before the plates are cleared. Rashid takes the notes away with him afterward.

Upstairs, the primer’s compact public history gives the Covenant the tidy shape of dates, articles, and civic virtues. Its pages on conduct devote far more care to possible insults than to ordinary conversation. By the time you set it aside, the house has quieted around the occasional stair creak and a door closing somewhere above.

At nine, the common room holds a low fire and the warm brass light of two lamps. Rashid is already at one of the study tables, his petition gone and a worn deck of cream-backed cards in its place. He has set out a shallow wooden dish for the coins. The five coppers land there with a modest, bright clatter.

He squares the deck against the tabletop. “Crossing: three cards each, one exchange before the reveal, and the hand nearest twenty-one wins unless it goes over. Five coppers is enough to teach the game without making an enemy of the evening.”

He shuffles with quick, exact hands, then deals three cards face down to each side of the table.
