The remaining coins disappear into your hand. Rashid leaves his small winning stack where it is for the moment, one thumb resting on the squared deck.

“An Academy trial in Kothren territory, two years ago. I questioned one of her figures in a mock council, and she handed me a practice blade afterward to see whether I could challenge anything with my hands.”

He describes an afternoon in a false council chamber, where a group of prospective Conclave students had been given an invented dispute over a mountain pass and three hours to draft terms. Rashid had objected to the way Ashara’s proposal assigned control of the checkpoints; Ashara had answered the objection closely enough to leave no room for a second one before the proctor called time.

Afterward, in the covered practice court, she had passed him a wooden blade. The first exchange lasted long enough for him to discover that watching a ranked fighter and meeting one were separate skills. The second ended when the padded tip of her weapon touched the hollow below his jaw.

“Apparently, I could not,” Rashid says. “The problem is that she is usually worth arguing with.”

The cards sit between you in the quiet common room, their cream backs bright under the nearest lamp.
