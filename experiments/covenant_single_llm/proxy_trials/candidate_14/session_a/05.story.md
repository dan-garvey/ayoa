Rashid places one of his own coppers beside the dish, then nudges one of yours to meet it.

“Fair enough: numbers count themselves, court cards count ten, and an ace may be one or eleven.”

The three cards on your side show a nine, an eight, and a seven. Twenty-four. The nine goes into the discard pile; the replacement is a four, leaving you at nineteen. Rashid studies his own hand without changing a card, then turns over a six, a five, and a nine.

Twenty.

He draws the two-copper pot to his side of the table and gathers the cards with neither apology nor ceremony. The second deal follows in the same quick rhythm, three fresh cards face down before you.
