Rashid nods once and opens his folio. For half an hour, the common room holds the fire's soft settling, the dry movement of your notes, and his pen moving in narrow deliberate lines. Once, he scratches out an entire paragraph and rearranges it on a fresh sheet; later, he pauses to count something on his fingers before writing again.

By the time you put your notes away, the sheaf has become two tidy piles and Rashid is still bent over the second. He raises two fingers in a quiet farewell without looking up.

Upstairs, your room is cool after the hearth. You sleep, and Saturday morning opens pale beyond the window, the gardens below already holding their impossible bloom.
