Ysolde keeps her eyes on the blank window for a moment after the bridge has gone.

“You are welcome,” she says. “From the gardens, the lamps look closer. The storm makes more space around them.”

She returns to her meal. You do the same, taking your time over the last of the meat and the sharp green sauce while the table finds smaller subjects.

A server lifts the empty dish from the center of the table and replaces it with pear tarts, their sugared edges dark and crisp.

Caelindra looks at them with cautious approval. “At last. A course with no tarragon.”

Seraphel glances into the silver dish.

“Pear, butter, crust.
A gentle conclusion
to a disputed meal.”

Caelindra takes one. “I accept the conclusion.”

Rashid has drawn the folded petition back from beneath his napkin. Ashara notices before he has opened it fully.

“I did.”

He passes the page across. Ashara reads with one hand resting beside her plate, her expression giving nothing away. Her eyes stop halfway down.

“You wrote that the Academy would assign one.”

Rashid leans over the page. “That is different.”

“It is exactly different.”

He takes the paper back and scores out the line with his pen. “Request supervised access, subject to the report and staff availability.”

Ashara considers the revision. “Better.”

Aldric closes the book beside his cup. “When you have finished, leave it by my place. I said I would read it.”

“I have not withdrawn the offer.”

“Then I will hold you to it.”

“That is what offers are for,” Thessaly says, taking a tart.

The paper disappears beneath Rashid’s napkin again. He does not reopen it.

Caelindra has found the commentary volume Aldric set aside and lifted it by two fingers as though testing its weight. “This is Windwhisper’s preliminary reading?”

“It is,” Aldric says.

“Two hundred pages of preliminaries.”

Ysolde looks over. “The first edition was shorter.”

Caelindra pauses. “Was it better?”

“No.”

That settles the matter more effectively than an argument could. Caelindra sets the book down and finishes her tart.

The candles burn lower as the serving dishes vanish one by one. Beyond the windows, lightning works silently through the cloudbank, too far below to disturb the warmth of the hall. Ysolde watches it once more, then reaches for her water. Around the table, conversation thins into fragments: a complaint about pepper, a question of whether the library stays open past midnight, Seraphel quietly describing a song someone heard in the chapel.

By the time the last plates are cleared, the storm has shifted westward, and the dining room has grown comfortably dim around the people still sitting in it.
