Seraphel takes the stairs at an unhurried pace, her wings making a soft, dry hush when she turns at the landing. In the common room, firelight catches on the stern faces in the Garvey portraits.

“Those portraits look severe,” she says.
“They are easier after soup.”

The dining hall is warmer than the corridor and loud enough to make its size seem smaller. The others have gathered near one end of the long table, where platters of bread, glazed roots, and something darkly spiced are already going around. When you introduce yourself and take an open chair, your name moves around the table with a few nods and returned names before the interrupted argument reclaims everyone.

Rashid has a folded sheet beside his plate, held flat under one furred hand. “A splint is reason to supervise practice,” he says to Aldric. “It is not a reason to bar every student who lacks a private tutor.”

“The notice says a week,” Aldric replies. “We do not know whether someone ignored the rules, or whether the court itself is unsafe.”

Ashara cuts into her dinner. “Then find out. Indecision is not a policy.”

“It is a temporary precaution,” Aldric says.

Caelindra turns one violet-eyed look toward the paper. “What exactly are you asking people to sign?”

“A request for supervised access when faculty can staff it,” Rashid says. “Nothing tomorrow. Nothing reckless.”

“A schedule that exists,” Ysolde says, “would be useful.”

Thessaly reaches for the bread. “That is a different request from reopening the court.”

“Eat before you turn a request
into a civil war,” Seraphel says, taking the serving spoon from the nearest platter.

The argument loosens into a few quieter comments as you help yourself to dinner. Rashid listens to Ysolde's question about supervision, then folds the sheet once and sets it beside your plate, its pen laid neatly across the top.
