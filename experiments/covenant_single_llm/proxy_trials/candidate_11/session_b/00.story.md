The brass frame on your desk has been polished more recently than anything else in the room. Inside it, a page of narrow, slanting handwriting is addressed to a great-nephew who never came. The bed is made with military severity. A row of clothes, new enough that the creases still know their packing, waits in the wardrobe. The careful mending at your charcoal cuff catches the lamplight when you move.

Outside the window, the garden paths shine pale between clipped hedges. Beyond them, a bridge of hard light disappears into cloud. The storm below throws a slow white pulse across the ceiling; no thunder follows.

Someone has left the door open an inch. From the hall comes the smell of butter, pepper, and something sweetened with citrus. Downstairs, chairs draw back over the dining-room floor. A low burst of laughter follows, then a voice saying, “That was not a defense,” with the amused finality of an argument already in progress.

Warm gold light gathers across the threshold.

A young woman stands in the hall, one hand loose at her side, the small white wings at her shoulders tucked close so they do not brush the doorframe. Her clothes are white and gold, less formal than the Academy portraits downstairs. She glances past you at the framed letter, then politely looks away.

“I am Seraphel Dawnquill,” she says.
“The dinner is not begun.
They are only making noise.
May I walk down with you?”
