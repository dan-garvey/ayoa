Rashid takes the paper back without unfolding it. His claws tap once against the table as he squares the edges.

“Fair,” he says. “I prefer that to a signature because everyone is watching.”

The petition stays at his elbow through the rest of dinner. The argument does not disappear, but it changes shape: Aldric asks what a supervision rota would cost; Ysolde wants to know who has keys to the covered court; Ashara dismisses one of Rashid's proposed faculty names as too busy to be useful. By the time the dessert plates are cleared, Rashid has begun a list in the margin of the request.

The common room is quieter after the dining hall. Firelight picks out the worn arms of the leather chairs and the gilt edges of the Garvey portraits. Thessaly has claimed one of the low study tables, where a wooden board is laid between four small bowls of colored counters. Seraphel sits cross-legged in the chair opposite her, a cup cooling on the floor beside one boot.

“Caelindra has chosen the library,” Thessaly says when you ask about staying up. “She believes her book has better dialogue.”

Seraphel's mouth curves.

“She said it has fewer losers.
That is not the same thing.”

Thessaly moves a brass rider two spaces along the board. “It is, if you are losing.”

The pieces make a neat little city of their own: painted towers, narrow bridges, riders with chipped enamel cloaks. One chair is empty. Thessaly hooks it out from the table with her foot.

“The rules are simple,” she says. “Seraphel has already broken one.”

“I asked before I did it,” Seraphel says.

“You asked after.”

“The question was very polite.”

Thessaly sets the little rulebook beside the empty place and begins sorting a fresh set of counters into a row.
