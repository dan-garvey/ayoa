For a moment, the room holds the last line. A knot in the fire opens behind its iron screen with a dry pop, and the lower edge of the paper catches white in the shifting light. Seraphel does not reach for it. Her harp rests across her knees, one corner of its blue wrapping gathered beneath the dark wood, while the small feathers at her shoulders lie still along her hair.

Then she draws one finger across two strings. The notes land close together and do not quite settle.

“It is a good place to stop.
The coat is still one coat,
but it has two claims
pulling at either sleeve.

Nell has ten o’clock.
Ada has last Friday.
Neither says everything.
That is why I listen.

The goat would pawn it,
of course.
It has no dignity
and too much practice.”

The corner of her mouth lifts before she looks back down at the harp. She turns a peg by the smallest degree, listens, and finds a clear note.

“Do not hurry them
past that question.
I would hear more
when there is more.”

She plays four notes after that: the bright skipping turn of the courier song, slowed until it falls into the lower cadence of the river road. The fifth note never comes. Beyond the high windows, pale lightning runs soundlessly through the clouds below the gardens. Seraphel rests her palm across the strings and lets the common room remain quiet around the unfinished page.
