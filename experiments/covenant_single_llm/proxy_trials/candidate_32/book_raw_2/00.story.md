Ysolde watches the last pale stroke from the bridge disappear behind the cloudbank. Then she looks back across the table.

“You are welcome,” she says. “I forget that they are strange.”

Caelindra lifts her wineglass a fraction. “It would be tiresome if the Academy managed to surprise no one until the first formal reprimand.”

“The bridges have warnings,” Ysolde says.

“Then there is hope for them.”

Seraphel's small wings settle more closely against her back as she smiles into her cup.

“An old road still shines
differently
for someone new to it.”

Ysolde considers this with the same seriousness she gave the lamps. “Yes,” she says. “That is accurate.”

The next few minutes belong mostly to food. The meat is dark at the edges and tender within; the tarragon keeps its sharp green bite all the way through the last of it. Bread disappears from the basket in pieces rather than slices. A server replaces the empty water pitcher before anyone has to ask. Across from you, Caelindra finally gives up treating the sauce as a moral offense and takes another spoonful.

No one turns the earlier argument into a test for the newest person at the table. Rashid leaves the folded sheet beneath his napkin until the worst of everyone's hunger has been answered. When the server has cleared the main dishes and set down a tray of roasted pears with honeyed cream, he draws it out again.

“Before this becomes a permanent dinner subject,” he says, “can we settle three words?”

Ashara leans back in her chair. “You have eight.”

“Three first.”

Aldric sets his spoon down. “Which?”

Rashid flattens the paper with the heel of his hand. “The request says the Academy should provide ‘immediate supervised access.’ I can remove immediate. But I want more than an acknowledgement that a door is shut.”

“Of course you do,” Ashara says. “You want them to do something.”

“Yes.”

“Then ask for the thing you mean.”

He turns the page slightly toward her. His handwriting is narrow and controlled, though a few of the earlier lines have been crossed through hard enough to ridge the paper. “An account of the cause of the accident, and a plan for supervised practice once the court can be used safely.”

“Who decides that it can?” Thessaly asks.

“The instructor and whoever maintains the wards,” Aldric says. “Presumably.”

“Presumably is not a person.”

Rashid nods at that. “Then the request can ask the Academy to name who is responsible for the decision.”

Ashara reaches across the table. He gives her the sheet without making her ask twice. Her gold eyes follow the revised paragraph. The tip of her tail taps once against the chair leg.

“This does not claim to know why the student was hurt,” she says.

“Because I do not know.”

“It does not demand your group be given my booking.”

“Because you declined that.”

“Correct.”

She reads the last two lines, then passes the sheet to Aldric. “It is better.”

Rashid's mouth shifts as though he has decided against a reply with too much satisfaction in it. “I will accept that as high praise.”

“Do not.”

“Too late.”

Aldric reads more slowly. The candlelight makes the white and gold of his cuffs look almost flat against the varnished table. “You should leave the signatures separate from the request,” he says. “People may want the Academy to consider it without having their names attached to a document that circulates.”

“They are separate,” Rashid says. “Anyone who signs will know exactly what they are signing.”

Thessaly takes a pear slice with two fingers. “That is an improvement over many political papers.”

“It is an improvement over my first draft,” Rashid says.

Ashara makes a small sound that might be agreement.

“You may show me the copy before you collect names,” Aldric says.

“I said I would.”

“You said you would show it to Ashara.”

“I meant both of you.”

“Then write both of us.”

Rashid looks from Aldric to Ashara, then takes the clean sheet the server has left beside the salt cellar. “Very well.”

While he copies out the revised request, the table gives him room without making a ceremony of it. The scratch of his pen joins the soft clink of dessert spoons. Ashara eats the rest of her pear quickly, then reaches for the bread basket and finds it empty.

“Who finished this?”

“Everyone,” Thessaly says.

“That is not an answer.”

“It is the complete answer.”

Caelindra points her spoon at the basket. “The kitchen knows how many of us there are. This is a failure of planning.”

“There was sufficient bread at the beginning,” Ysolde says.

“There was sufficient innocence at the beginning of the term as well. We have since used it.”

Seraphel's laugh comes out in a brief, bright breath.

“The loaf was brave,
but not prepared
for a table of heirs.”

“It should have had a guard,” Ashara says.

“You would have eaten the guard,” Rashid says, not looking up from his page.

“If it was breaded.”

Even Aldric smiles at that, though he covers it by reaching for his water.

The conversation moves on before it can become a contest. Caelindra complains that Professor Windwhisper has assigned a response paper on the legal status of the bridges, a subject she describes as “a dispute about roads conducted by people who have never needed to use one.”

“The question is whether they are common infrastructure or territory under Academy protection,” Aldric says. “It is not only a road.”

“It is visibly a road.”

“It is also a treaty made solid.”

Thessaly glances toward the tall windows. “Can a treaty be walked across?”

“That is nearly the question,” Aldric says.

“Then it is a badly framed question.”

Ysolde sets her spoon down. “The bridges are both things. Their purpose changes depending on where one is standing.”

Caelindra studies her for a moment. “That is annoyingly useful.”

“It is true.”

“Those conditions are not mutually exclusive,” Rashid says.

His fresh copy is finished. He sands the wet ink, taps the excess into a little brass tray, and folds the page along a careful crease. Ashara takes it when he offers it, reads the two short paragraphs, and hands it back.

“No immediate redress,” she says. “No borrowed training time. No invented conclusion.”

“No invented conclusion,” Rashid agrees.

“Good.”

There is nothing soft in the word, but it ends the matter for tonight. Rashid slips the letter inside his coat, where it disappears beneath impeccable dark cloth.

By the time the dessert plates are cleared, the candles have burned low enough that wax shines in thick rims around their holders. Outside, the cloud has shifted. A strip of the western bridge appears again beyond the garden wall, clean and pale above the rolling black, its lamps stretched eastward in luminous strokes.

Ysolde sees it at the same time. For a little while she says nothing. Then she rises, smoothing the front of her pale sleeve.

“The wind is easing,” she says. “The lamps will be straight by dawn.”

“A comforting prospect,” Caelindra says. “I cannot bear to think of them spending the night visually irresponsible.”

Ysolde looks at her. “They do not mind.”

Caelindra's composure breaks just enough for her to laugh, once, into her hand.

Aldric pushes back his chair. “I should make chapel before the lamps prove you wrong.”

“They will not,” Ysolde says.

“Then I will be late for a different reason.”

He inclines his head around the table and goes out through the hall, his white-and-gold coat passing briefly through the firelight of the common room. Seraphel watches the doorway after he has gone, then turns her attention to the last line of storm beyond the glass.

Rashid gathers his pen and ink. Ashara stands beside him, already looking toward the open doorway as though the evening has given her just enough stillness to make her restless. Thessaly pockets a sugar crystal from the dessert tray with the open lack of secrecy of someone who sees no reason to pretend she has not done it.

Around the long table, the names now arrive with their voices: Rashid's measured persistence, Ashara's exacting corrections, Caelindra's dry complaints, Thessaly's sideways clarity, Seraphel's spare songs, Ysolde's unhurried certainty. The dining hall has grown quieter, but the house beyond it remains awake with firelight, footsteps, and the storm moving under the plateau.
