The last question hangs in the room after the page comes down.

Seraphel keeps the harp across her knees. Beyond the high windows, pale lightning opens the garden for an instant: the wet paths, the black ribs of the farther bridge, then only the firelight again. Her thumb rests against a string without sounding it.

“It is a good place to stop,”
she says at last.
“Which is not the same thing
as a kind place.”

The corner of her mouth lifts. She looks at the page on your knee, then at the fire.

“‘I was the one who answered’—
there. That is where Ada
becomes more than the person
who says no.

“Nell is speaking toward Friday.
Ada is speaking from last Friday.
They are both counting,
but not the same thing.”

Her fingers find three quiet notes: one higher, then two low ones that leave a little space between them. She plays them again, not quite a tune, and lets the last fade into the crackle behind the iron screen.

“And the coat is useful
because they both need it.
If only one of them did,
the argument would be easy.

“Most people make a quarrel
by giving one person a fine reason
and the other a bad habit.
You have given them both
a hand on the same sleeve.”

Her small wings shift once against the back of the chair. The blue cloth that wrapped the harp has slipped into a loose fold on the hearthrug.

“I do not know if Ada
wants the coat sold,” she says.
“I only know she wants Nell
to understand that noon comes.
That is enough to make me wait
for the next page.”

She settles her palm over the strings and looks back to you.

“Do you know what Ada wants?”
