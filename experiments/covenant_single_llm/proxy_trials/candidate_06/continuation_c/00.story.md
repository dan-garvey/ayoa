Caelindra reads without turning the pages quite flat, holding each by one lower corner as though the ink might transfer at a touch. The library's lamps catch in the silver of her hair while the minute hand climbs the small brass clock beside the window.

At last she sets the third sheet over the first and looks at the dark garden beyond the glass.

"Yes," she says. "Not because the keeper is admirable. He has chosen between one person he can see and people he cannot. That is common enough."

Her fingertip rests near the middle page without touching the writing. "The useful part is that he has no way to know which failure will be counted against him. The neighbor may live. The ships may not come. Someone may have sent the replacement and found the road closed. You have made the absence do work before you explain it."

She gathers the sheets into a precise stack and slides them back across the table. "Do not hurry to tell me which disaster arrives. I am more curious what he does while he still has time to wait."
