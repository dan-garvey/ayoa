Garvey House holds itself together in layers: dark demon stone along the hearth wall, a narrow strip of pale carved timber above the door, old human joinery in the window frame. Your room is orderly in the way a place arranged by lawyers can be orderly. A made bed faces a desk already stocked with ink and clean paper; trunks of sent-ahead clothes stand shut beneath the sill. Beyond the glass, the gardens lie in their sheltered geometry, and farther out the hard-light bridges hang above the storm’s slow white flicker.

The Garvey signet catches the window light when you shift your hand. Ink remains in the creases of your fingers. On the desk, a framed handwritten letter rests beside the lamp, its fading address meant for a great-nephew who never arrived. The room gives no indication of what to do with a name that has outlived nearly everyone who used it.

Below, chairs scrape across the dining-hall floor. A burst of conversation rises through the open stairwell, then settles beneath the clink of laid silver and the muted movement of Academy-liveried servers. Seventeen places wait at the long table downstairs. The brass latch of your door is cool under its plate.
