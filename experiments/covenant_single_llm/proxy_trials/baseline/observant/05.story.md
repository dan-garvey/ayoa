“Then you have begun,” Thessaly says. “There is no fixed route after dinner.” She folds her napkin with careful corners as you leave the dining hall.

The quadrangle has cooled beneath the lamp-globes. Black stone, white marble, living timber, and low carved arches enclose the open octagon of packed earth. Ashara has left her outer coat on the rail. Rashid has rolled his sleeves to the elbow. Neither carries a weapon.

They face one another in the center, hands open. Rashid moves first: a high feint, then a sudden drive toward Ashara’s middle. She drops into the low turn he named at dinner, but he is ready for it this time. His hand catches her shoulder and his other closes around her wrist, forcing her back a step across the dirt.

Ashara gives him that step. Her tail braces behind his supporting leg as she turns inside his grip. Heat shimmers off her forearm. Rashid’s balance breaks; one knee strikes the earth, and her open palm stops a hand’s breadth from his throat. He taps the ground twice. Ashara steps away at once. Rashid rises, dusts his knee, and says, “Again tomorrow.”

“Improve your footing first.” Ashara looks past him to you at the edge of the octagon. “You watched. What did he give me?”
