Caelindra finishes the third page, then turns back to the first and reads its last paragraph again. Her own book remains face-down beside the inkwell. The window draft lifts a corner of your manuscript; she pins it beneath two pale fingers.

"Yes," she says at last. "The keeper's reason is ordinary enough that I believe he would leave. That is more interesting than making him a fool."

She lays the pages into a neat stack but keeps her hand on them. "There are already three claims on him: the neighbor, the light, and the person expected to relieve him. You haven't told me which claim he thinks he has failed. I want to know whether he understands that before midnight."

A cart rattles beyond the library shelves. Caelindra glances toward the iron stair, as if measuring the passing minutes, then looks back at the manuscript.

"I also want to know why the replacement does not come. Have you decided that?"
