Rashid's fingers stay on the sheet after you hold out your hand. The dining hall's noon clatter fills the pause: knives against plates, a chair scraping stone. Then he lets the paper go.

"I wasn't asking you to give up your booking," he says. His voice is even. "I thought we would read the wording at sixth, and I would arrange the printing afterward."

He shuts the notebook, then opens it again to copy the west workshop's location beneath an existing note. "Fourth bell. Fine. I'll ask Sela whether the hand press has another opening, or take it to Commerce. I need it printed, not treated as a favor."

Fresh ink darkens the request's headings; its margins carry small, crowded revisions and a blank space beside the signatures.

Rashid taps the folded pages now in your hand. "Read it. I kept it to supervised access and left the injury out. If you see a weakness, tell me before I put my name under it."

Only then does he pick up his fork again.
