The entry is dated *18 Harvest, 391 C.C.* Its language is dry even when the complaint is not: names, charges, testimony, ruling. Edda's name stands at the foot of the page beside the tidy seal of the open session.

“You may copy it,” Stone says. “That is what a public record is for.”

He finds a clean sheet of Academy paper and sets an inkstand within reach of the lamp. He does not turn away while you work, but neither does he hurry you. The scratch of pen on paper joins the quiet tick of cooling glass chimneys.

When the copy is finished, Stone closes the red volume and returns it to the upper shelf himself.

“Keep the date with it,” he says on his way down. “People remember verdicts and lose the years around them.”

He accepts your thanks with a brief nod. By then the library has grown stiller around its pools of lamp light.

You carry the framed letter back through the sleeping house and set it in its crooked place above the desk. The room holds the faint smell of oil and rain. Outside the window, lightning moves inside the storm without finding the ground.

When morning comes, the storm has thinned to a pale bank beyond the gardens. Light lies across the desk, the letter's walnut frame, and the copied page beside it.
