Professor Stone draws the lamp nearer, then bends over the frame without touching the glass. His eyes move through the letter once, return to the closing, and stay there.

“No,” he says. “I had not heard that story before tonight.”

He glances up at you.

“I recognized Edda in it. She loved a wager with a second set of terms concealed inside the first. And Lady Vaeyn had a talent for treating a technical distinction as a fortress.”

His attention returns to the slanted hand.

“This is hers. The capitals, especially. She always made a G as though it were trying to occupy more of the page than the other letters allowed.”

The library's upper shelves disappear into the dimness above the lamps. Stone reads the last line again.

“Unreasonable hope,” he says quietly. “She used that phrase when she meant someone ought to try anyway.”

He straightens, resting both hands on the table's edge.

“Edda wrote letters like this to people who had not yet disappointed her. Sometimes to people who did not exist yet.” A small breath leaves him through his nose. “She believed that was a practical habit.”

Beyond the tall windows, a flare of lightning turns the garden paths white for an instant. Stone looks at the framed letter one more time.

“Keep it out of direct sun. The ink will go first at the edges.”
