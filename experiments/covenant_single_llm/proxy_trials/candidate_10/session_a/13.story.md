Ashara's practice does not hurry toward a finish. Her opponent changes tactics twice; Ashara changes with her, less interested in landing a showy strike than in making the other student work for every opening. When the student overreaches, Ashara catches her wrist with the flat of the practice blade and steps back at once.

“Again,” she says.

Near the rail, Dena finishes reading the request.

“Don't put the accident in the second paragraph,” she tells Rashid.

“It isn't in the second paragraph.”

“Or the third.”

Rashid unfolds the page enough to show her. “It says the restriction affects students who use the court outside their track. It asks for supervision. That is all.”

Dena reads it once more, then signs. A Commerce student beside her adds his name after hers; another asks whether the academy will answer by week's end.

“They will answer when they answer,” Rashid says, folding the sheet carefully. “I will file it before the offices close.”

Inside the octagon, Ashara's opponent finally gets a hit against her shoulder. The watching students make a brief, appreciative noise. Ashara gives a single nod, resets her guard, and the practice goes on.

By the time Rashid tucks the request into his coat, Dena has moved toward the shade of the low wall with two friends. Ashara's practice blade knocks sharply against her opponent's, and the ground keeps its separate rhythms: signatures, footwork, and the scrape of boots in packed earth.
