Stone gives a small nod, as if relieved by the simplicity of the request.

“Yes. The public arbitration rolls are on the upper shelves.”

He takes the spiral stair without hurry, one hand grazing the iron rail. A minute later he returns with a broad, red-leather volume dusted pale along its spine. He sets it beside the frame and opens it with the care of someone who has done so many times.

The pages are printed in dense, orderly columns. Stone finds an index, then turns to a marked page.

“Here.”

At the top is a plain heading: *Academy Student Arbitration, Open Session.* Beneath it, a case concerns the withheld wages of a Garvey House laundress after a donor damaged the steam pipes and refused to pay for repairs. The entry names Edda Garvey as the student's advocate for the laundress, and records the decision: wages restored, repairs charged to the donor's household.

Her name appears again at the bottom in dark archival ink. The capital G curls broadly across the line.

“She was in her third year,” Stone says. “That case made the Academy newsletter. The donor's father complained to the Council for a month.”

He shifts the lamp so the page lies fully in its circle of light.

“It is not a grand record. But it is hers.”
