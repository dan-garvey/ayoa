“I could have,” Stone says. “I should have.”

He pulls a brass magnifier from beneath a stack of catalog cards and leans over the frame again. This time he studies the lower margin, where the paper meets the walnut backing.

“There is no date. But the stationery is old Garvey stock; the watermark was discontinued before the collapse. The ink and the frame fit that as well.” He lowers the magnifier. “I could place it within a few years, not a season. Edda was alive when she wrote it.”

His finger hovers above the salutation without touching the glass.

“And I do not think she meant a particular boy. Not by name.”

He sits back in his chair.

“Edda had no great-nephew at the time, at least none anyone expected. She was writing to a possibility: whoever eventually inherited the house and needed someone to say it had once been full of ordinary people.”

Stone glances around the library, at the lamps and high dark shelves.

“That was her habit. She treated an uncertain future as though it were already waiting for tea.”
