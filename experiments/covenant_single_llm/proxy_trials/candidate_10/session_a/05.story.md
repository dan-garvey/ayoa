Caelindra's gaze comes back to you.

“Then you may have liked Edda,” she says. “She liked people who noticed the terms of a wager.”

The remark is almost civil. She returns to her dinner before anyone can make more of it.

By the time the last plates have gone, the long table has loosened into smaller conversations and empty chairs. You leave it behind for the quieter second floor.

The letter waits where you saw it, its narrow walnut frame a little crooked above the desk. Up close, the hand is quick and slanted, with several words pressed hard enough to leave dents in the paper.

*Dear great-nephew,*

*If this room ever becomes yours, open the curtains before you decide it is lonely. Garveys have a habit of making houses into mausoleums after a bad year. The people who live here are not yours to arrange, but they may let you share a meal if you ask properly. If the cooks still make the plum tart, take two slices before any diplomat begins explaining why one is sufficient.*

*With unreasonable hope,*

*Edda Garvey*

The ink has faded to brown at the edges, but the signature remains firm.

The library is lit when you reach it. Lamps burn along the lower floor, turning the iron spiral stair into a black corkscrew against shelves and shelves of books. Professor Gareth Stone sits alone at a long table beneath it, a heavy volume open beside a cup gone cold. He has loosened his collar, and his attention is fixed on a page of cramped notes until the sound of your approach draws it up.

His eyes settle briefly on your signet.

“Mr. Garvey,” he says. “I heard we had a new student. What can I help you find?”
