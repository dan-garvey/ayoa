Caelindra's fork stops above her plate.

For a beat, she looks at you as though the answer ought to have been obvious. Then a faint color rises beneath the pale gold of her skin.

“You are right,” she says. “I told it badly.”

She sets the fork down with particular care.

“My mother did not say that a human poem could not make her laugh. She said it could not make her show pleasure. Lady Garvey wagered that it could make her smile.”

The elf's gaze shifts to the candle flame.

“The poem produced a laugh at the turnip. It did not produce a smile. Mother declared the wager settled. Lady Garvey declared that a laugh was evidence of a more interesting case.”

“And then she made the second wager,” Seraphel says.

“Yes.” Caelindra reaches for her wine, then pauses. “She wagered that Mother would ask to hear it again before dessert. Mother did so when she thought no one was listening.”

Rashid's brows lift. “Was anyone?”

“Everyone,” Caelindra says. “Lady Garvey had told the entire room what to listen for.”

Ashara lets out a short laugh into her cup.

Caelindra gives her a level look, though the color has not quite left her face. “It was an effective poem. I refuse to defend its literary merit.”
