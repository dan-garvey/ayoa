Breakfast is quieter than the welcome dinner, with students coming and going around the long table according to separate timetables. The day gathers speed after that.

Professor Windwhisper spends the morning on treaty notices and the difference between an agreement that has been breached and one that has merely become inconvenient. In Practical Rhetoric, Professor Kira makes the room strip flourishes from a public statement until its actual demand is visible. By the final bell, the Academy's corridors have begun to smell of damp wool, chalk, and the wind brought in from the bridges.

The public dueling ground lies open to the afternoon sky: an octagon of packed earth, low rails, and a ranking board nailed beside the gate. Ashara vel Kothren's name holds the top line. Rashid's is several places below it.

Inside the octagon, Ashara practices with a wooden blade against a broad-shouldered Military student. She gives ground once, twice, then catches the next strike on her guard and turns it cleanly aside. The other student retrieves her balance before Ashara moves again.

Near the rail, Rashid stands with a folded sheet of paper and a small cluster of students. Dena Arl is among them, her wrist splinted in pale cloth.

“No names,” Dena says, tapping the paper with her uninjured hand.

“No names,” Rashid answers. “It asks for supervised access. Nothing else.”

She studies his face, then nods and takes the pen he offers.

Beyond them, two students argue quietly over whether the covered court should remain closed at all. A practice blade cracks against another inside the octagon. Ashara's opponent stumbles back a pace, grinning despite herself, and the small crowd at the rail shifts to make room for whoever wants a better view.
