You take the offered chair and pass yourself roast, roots, and a piece of black bread. The meat is peppered hard enough to warm the back of your throat.

Seraphel tears her bread into neat pieces while you speak. For a moment, the only sound from her is the faint brush of feathers as she settles one wing more comfortably against the chair.

“Not I.
I came after the house emptied.
Professor Stone knew some of them.
He is often in the library after supper.”

At the far side of the table, Caelindra lowers her glass. Her violet eyes have been on you since the word Garvey.

“I met a Lady Garvey when I was young,” she says. “She lost a wager to my mother, invented a second wager, and had all her money back before dessert.” One pointed ear lifts slightly. “She did not hurry. That was unusual.”

Rashid glances from Caelindra to the food in front of him. “That sounds like a dangerous family trait.”

“It sounds like bad supervision,” Ashara says, reaching for the salt.

Caelindra ignores them both. Her attention returns to you, more direct now than before.

“Does the letter have a name?”
