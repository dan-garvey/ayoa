The window in your room has been left open a finger-width. Storm-scent rises from the drop beyond the gardens: rain, cold stone, and the metallic edge of distant lightning. It catches in the lamp-oil air.

The room door stands open a hand's breadth; its swollen lower edge will not quite meet the jamb.

Your primer lies open on the desk beside a stack of clothes sent ahead by people who know your measurements only from letters. A ribbon marks the paragraph on welcome-dinner customs. Lower on the page, the heading *Article Nineteen* waits in severe black type. The Garvey signet at your hand flashes once when the lamp flame shifts, then goes dull again.

Downstairs, a chair leg scrapes across the dining-hall floor. Someone laughs—briefly, then more loudly—and another voice cuts in too low to make out. The house has no trouble sounding occupied without asking permission of its new owner.

Three light knocks sound against the exposed strip of frame.

Seraphel Dawnquill waits in the corridor with one hand resting against the frame. Her white-and-gold sleeve is rolled over the wrist, and the small wings at her back fold neatly enough to clear the wall. Warm light from the stairwell catches in her hair.

“The lamps are lit below,” she says.

Her blue eyes move once over the room, taking in the desk and the open primer without lingering.

“They have begun to argue about seating.
No blood has been spilled.
It is, I think, an encouraging start.”

From the landing comes a clearer burst of conversation. A man's voice says, “There are seventeen places. This cannot be difficult.” Another answers, dry and unmistakably amused, “You have made it difficult by counting them aloud.”

Seraphel's mouth tips at one corner. She steps aside, leaving the door open to the lamp-lit stairs and the noise below.
