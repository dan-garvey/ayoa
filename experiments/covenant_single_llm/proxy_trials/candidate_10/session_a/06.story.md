Professor Stone's hand stills on the edge of his notes.

“Yes,” he says after a moment. “I knew Edda.”

He closes the heavy book, leaving a ribbon tucked between its pages. The guardedness in his face does not vanish, exactly, but it gives way to something more ordinary.

“She was a better host than most diplomats and a better diplomat than most hosts. She could make people stay at the table after an argument, which is rarer than it sounds.”

His mouth shifts, almost a smile.

“Once, she persuaded a visiting dragon envoy to eat a whole bowl of plum tart by telling him it was an insult to refuse. It was not an insult. She had simply made too much.”

The lamplight trembles across the rim of his cold cup.

“Edda liked wagers because they made everyone declare what they actually believed. She also liked winning them, which was a separate virtue in her view.”

He looks toward the shelves, then back at you. “The version Caelindra told you sounds like her. Edda would have considered getting Lady Vaeyn to ask for a second reading a finer victory than the coins.”

For a few seconds he studies the signet on your hand, not its value but its workmanship.

“Your letter may tell me when she wrote it. May I see it?”
