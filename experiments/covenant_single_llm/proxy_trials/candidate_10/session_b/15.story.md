By the time the garden lamps come on, a small queue has formed at the tea cart. Steam curls from a dented brass kettle, carrying the sharp smell of over-steeped leaves and baked sugar. The vendor passes your tea and paper-wrapped pastry across the counter without slowing the line.

Dena is along the west wall beneath a climbing vine, one shoulder against the stone. Her splint is cleaner now, though it still catches the lamp light at the brass fastenings. Two students nearby are arguing over a Commerce problem neither of them seems eager to solve; Dena listens to them with the expression of someone enjoying the mistakes.

She spots you and lifts her uninjured hand.

"You made it. Good. The best pastries disappear first."

She shifts her satchel from the wall, leaving space beside her. The students at the next stretch of stone continue their argument about shipping costs as the tea cart bell gives a small, impatient ring behind you.
