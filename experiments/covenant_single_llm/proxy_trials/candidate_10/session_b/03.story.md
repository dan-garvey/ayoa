The blue counters are cool and smooth in your palm. Thessaly turns the board so its three carved paths point toward the three chairs.

"No money," she says. "No favors, either. We don't need extra rules."

"Good. I brought only
my dignity,
and it is fragile."

"It is not a stake," Thessaly says.

Seraphel's mouth twitches.

"Three Streams," Thessaly continues. "We each begin at an edge. A path to the center wins. On a turn, you set a stone on an empty crossing or move one stone along a line. If two stones touch, that line belongs to neither of the other players until one moves. Nothing is taken from the board."

Her clear counters wait along the left edge; Seraphel's amber ones line the right. She points out the center, a small brass circle worn dull by other hands.

"Start at the outer crossing," Seraphel says, indicating a notch near your side. "It gives you two routes."

Thessaly nods once. "It does. That is why she suggested it."

The first few turns move slowly. Seraphel explains the ways an open path can become useless without anyone blocking it. Thessaly corrects her only once, when Seraphel calls a crossing a gate.

"It isn't closed," Thessaly says. "It is occupied. Those are different conditions."

"They are different conditions
with the same inconvenience."

By the fourth round, amber and blue glass have made a narrow fork toward the brass center. Thessaly's clear stones sit farther back, leaving space that looks harmless until she shifts one across a diagonal line. Seraphel leans over the board.

"That is rude," she says.

"It is permitted."

"Those are also different conditions."

Thessaly reaches the center two turns later. She sets her last clear counter against the brass circle and looks at the joined line.

"I win."

Seraphel gathers the amber stones into a loose heap. "She always says it
as if the board has announced weather."

"It did," Thessaly says, sliding the blue counters back within your reach.
