The counters click softly as they return to their piles. Thessaly takes the blue stones as you pass them over and squares their edges against the table with unnecessary care.

"Sometimes," she says. "The board at home is larger. There are nine players, five paths, and someone always insists on announcing an alliance before the second turn."

"Does it help?" Seraphel asks.

"No. But it makes them feel organized."

She fits the blue pile into its shallow compartment beside the amber and clear stones.

"This version is better for three people. It ends before everyone becomes hungry again."

Seraphel rests her chin briefly against one hand.

"A game should know
when it has been played enough.
That is a rare virtue."

"Most things have that virtue," Thessaly says. "People ignore it."

She closes the wooden lid, then leaves the board where it is instead of carrying it away.
