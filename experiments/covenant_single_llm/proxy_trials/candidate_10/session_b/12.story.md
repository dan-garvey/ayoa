The north ground sits beyond the public dueling octagon, a broad rectangle of packed earth marked with low rope and white chalk. The afternoon is clear enough for practice, though the wind keeps worrying at the ropes.

Rashid's group has gathered in two uneven lines: Commerce students in rolled sleeves, an Arcana student with her hair tied back, two Conclave students you do not recognize. An Academy supervisor in a padded grey coat watches from the boundary with a whistle at his throat and a written schedule in hand.

"Pairs," Rashid calls. "No full-speed strikes until you can stop on the count."

The first exercise is footwork and distance rather than sparring. At the far end, Dena Arl works through the same marked steps with her splinted wrist held close against her ribs. When a partner drifts inside the chalk line, the supervisor blows the whistle once and sends both of them back to the start without raising his voice.

Rashid moves between the pairs, correcting a shoulder here, a stance there. He is sweating through the collar of his practice shirt by the time the group begins the second drill. The arrangement is slower than a private lesson and louder than the covered court had been, but no one is waiting at the edge for a turn that never comes.

During a water break, Rashid notices you by the rope and lifts two fingers in greeting. Then the supervisor calls the group back into line, and Rashid turns toward a student whose practice blade has slipped loose in his grip.

"Again," Rashid says. "Slower this time."
