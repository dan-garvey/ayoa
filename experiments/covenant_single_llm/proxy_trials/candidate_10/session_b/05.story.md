Thessaly looks at the closed game box.

"It was not an examination," she says. After a moment, she adds, "You're welcome."

Seraphel lifts two fingers in farewell.

"Good night, Rowan.
Take the west terrace.
The lamps are brighter there."

The public terrace lies beyond the quadrangle, where the Academy's black stone and white marble give way to a low carved rail. Lamp-globes hover at even intervals along its edge. Below, the storm moves without sound: broad folds of cloud opening now and then around slow veins of lightning.

For a while, the terrace belongs only to its lamps and the wind pressing cool through the arches. A pair of older students cross from the seminar rooms and disappear toward the gardens without slowing. Farther along the rail, someone has left a damp cloak over the stone to dry; its hem snaps once, then settles.

When you return to Garvey House, most of its windows have gone dark. The common room fire has burned down to red seams beneath ash. Upstairs, the letter above your desk catches a thin stripe of moonlight beside the primer and the signet.

Morning arrives with the first Academy bell and the sound of doors opening below.
