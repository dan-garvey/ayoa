Classes divide the day into bells, stairwells, and rooms full of new names. By the time the last students spill out into the quadrangle, dusk has begun to turn the lamp-globes pale against the sky.

Ysolde is already at the east terrace rail. Her white hair moves once in the wind and then falls still against her dark coat. She has a small notebook open on the stone beside her, though she is watching the storm rather than writing in it.

"You came," she says.

Below the terrace, two lamp-globes mark a descending path toward the bridges. Ysolde points at them.

"Watch those. The one nearer the garden is leaning west. The next is leaning east. The air divides there."

The lights sway by degrees, their halos trembling against the dark.

"From the ground, it looks like nothing," Ysolde says. "At flight height, it tells you which wing will have to work harder. If you fight the current, you tire. If you use it, you can turn without losing height."

She draws a shallow curve through the air with two fingers, following the line where the lamps disagree.

"Can you see it?"
