Breakfast has not yet filled the dining hall. Ysolde sits near one of the tall windows with a cup of tea and a plate of dark bread, her white hair loose over one shoulder. No one else has taken either chair beside her.

She looks up when you sit, then turns her attention to the storm beyond the glass.

"Not down," she says. "I fly over it when I fly."

Her fingers settle around the warm cup.

"Below the plateau, the air folds back under itself. From the terrace it looks like a single cloud layer, but it isn't. There are currents moving in opposite directions, and the lower ones are hard to see until you are already inside them."

She lifts one hand, tracing an arc over the tablecloth.

"The lightning is useful. It shows where the pressure is climbing. The quiet places between the flashes are usually worse."

For a moment, she watches a pale fork of light travel through the clouds.

"The bridges disappear first if you descend. People try to turn toward their light and lose their direction. From above, the storm has its own horizon. It is easier there."

Ysolde breaks off a piece of bread and sets the basket nearer your place without looking away from the window.
