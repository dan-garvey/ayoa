Rashid takes the sheet when it comes back. His eyes rest on you for a beat before he folds it along its old crease.

"Fair enough," he says. "I'd rather have an honest no than a decorative yes."

Aldric gives a small nod and returns to his soup. Ashara tears off a piece of bread.

"Get the report," she tells Rashid. "Then make the request difficult to ignore."

"That was always the plan."

The argument loosens after that. Servers bring spiced pears with the roast, and the candles burn lower. When wind brushes the dining-hall windows, Ysolde looks up from her plate.

"It's turning east," she says.

Rashid follows her glance. "The wind?"

"The lamps on the north terrace are leaning. The upper current has changed."

Ashara looks toward the dark glass. "I thought Garvey House simply had drafts."

"It has both," Ysolde says.

Seraphel's quiet laugh comes in three soft lines.

"A house that stands above a storm
should be allowed
one or two drafts."

By the time the plates are cleared, Rashid has tucked the petition into his folio. Aldric leaves by the chapelward door with a murmured excuse; Ashara goes upstairs carrying her practice case. Caelindra vanishes toward the library without taking the last pear.

The common room holds a smaller, easier light: fire in the oversized hearth, lamps over the study tables, old Garvey faces watching from their frames. Thessaly has claimed one table with a square wooden board and three neat piles of glass counters. Seraphel sits opposite her, sleeves pushed back from her wrists.

Thessaly taps the third pile with one pale finger. "This game is better with three. Sit if you want the blue stones."

"The blue stones are kinder," Seraphel says.

Thessaly looks at them. "They are blue."
