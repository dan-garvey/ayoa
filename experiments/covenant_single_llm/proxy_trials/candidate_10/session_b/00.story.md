The Garvey signet leaves a dark ring on the desk blotter, its wax-red stone catching the last of the window light. Beside it sits an Academy primer with a blue ribbon tucked at Article Nineteen. The type on the marked page is crisp enough to make the warning seem colder: penalties, bloodlines, witnesses. The framed letter above the desk has been here longer than any of the clothes sent by the lawyers. Its small, slanting script fills a page addressed to a great-nephew who never came.

Beyond the window, garden lamps turn the pale paths gold. Farther out, the hard-light bridges shine through the storm below like lines drawn across black water. Somewhere on the ground floor, chairs scrape over wood.

"No," a man says from the dining hall, level but carrying. "A supervised court is exactly what we're asking for."

"You're asking for a court after someone was hurt there," another man replies. "The precaution isn't a punishment."

A third voice, lower and sharp, cuts in before the answer comes. "Then inspect the court. Don't bar everyone from it."

A knock lands twice against the open door.

The woman at the threshold wears white and gold without any of the stiffness of a uniform. Warm light catches in her honey-blonde hair; two small white wings rest close against her back. She looks past the room only long enough to find the stairwell, then back to you.

"The first bell has gone.
The soup is still hot.
I am Seraphel Dawnquill."

Her smile moves quickly, almost apologetically.

"I can show you down,
if you would like company."
