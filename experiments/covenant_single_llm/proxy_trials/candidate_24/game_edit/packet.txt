<campaign>
Covenant of Thrones is political fantasy at a residential academy: companionship,
rivalry, dangerous romance, and institutional intrigue. Its pleasures include
living alongside people raised to become your enemies, the strange beauty of
their world, and finding what they want beyond their assigned political role.
Its hidden history is serious, but an evening of friendship need not become an
investigation. No romance, alliance, betrayal, or ending is predetermined.
</campaign>

<world>
Three centuries ago the forty-year Sundering War nearly destroyed the major
races. The Covenant of Thrones established a Council with a seat for each of
seven signatory races, interlocking treaties, arbitration, and an Academy to
educate future leaders together. Six races fought the war; humans became
especially valuable as translators, arbitrators, and magical synthesizers.
Original signatories are gone. Their successors inherit grievances without the
exhaustion that once made compromise possible. The peace survives alongside
border skirmishes, political assassinations, and economic warfare.

Demons draw magic from their own life force, run physically hot, and resent
military restrictions imposed as reparations. Angels channel the Divine Light
through ritual and moral certainty; a crisis of faith can interrupt their magic.
They hold disproportionate institutional power. Elves work nature and time on
long scales, with spells that take decades and persist for centuries. Dragons
possess immense elemental power with limited precision and resent restrictions
on sovereignty. Beastkin magic enhances their animal aspects; late admission to
the Covenant left them unequal voting rights. Fae magic depends on consent or
contract. Fae cannot lie or break their bargains; their literal truths can mislead.
Humans once could learn fragments of every other magical tradition.

Sixty years ago a plague reduced the human population by about ninety percent.
The official account calls it natural. The great houses collapsed and the human
Council seat has remained empty. Surviving scholarship offers mostly summaries;
primary sources require restricted access. Rowan's verified Garvey inheritance
returns a human to the Conclave and gives him wealth, a name, and responsibilities
he has not been prepared for. It does not make him magically powerful.

Article Nineteen forbids procreation between signatory races. Both parents face
execution; strict interpretations include the child. The law is enforced.
Attraction, courtship, or a kiss are not themselves the prohibited act, though
families and opponents exploit suspicion. Cross-racial intimacy is shadowed by
real future danger. Let that shape choices without turning each conversation into
a lecture on the law. All prospective romantic participants are adults.

The Academy stands on the Nexus, a plateau three hundred feet above a perpetual
storm. Six bridges of hard light reach the surrounding territories; travel to
their far ends requires permission. The Academy has roughly two thousand students
in Diplomacy, Military, Arcana, Commerce, and Conclave tracks. The four-year
Conclave has about fifteen to twenty students per year, generally one or two per
race, preparing to lead the Council. Faculty, family representatives, and other
students watch their conduct. Their friendships will shape future politics.

Demon reform movements and beastkin equality campaigns press against angel
dominance. Elven court politics and a dragon succession struggle complicate
every alliance. People can also want dinner, rest, company, a good argument,
privacy, or a place to practice without an audience.
</world>

<places>
Garvey House is the shared Conclave residence, a three-story accumulation of
different architectural traditions. The common room, dining hall, and library
are on the ground floor; Rowan's room is one floor above them. Rowan inherits the Garvey name and property;
the building's established residential use does not make the other students his
dependents or give him authority over their private rooms.

Rowan's second-floor room has a made bed, clothes sent ahead by the lawyers, a
desk, and a window toward the gardens and bridges. A framed handwritten letter
from a dead Garvey aunt concerns a planned visit by her named great-nephew,
who never came. It is ordinary correspondence written for that occasion. Its
recipient need not be Rowan. The common room
has portraits of dead Garveys, an oversized fireplace, study tables, leather
chairs, and stairs to unused upper floors. It connects to the dining hall,
library, and gardens. These are places where students regularly meet.

The formal dining hall has a long varnished table set for seventeen, candles,
working lamps, paneled walls, and servers in Academy livery. First-years eat
together nightly. Tonight's welcome dinner introduces the new human heir.

The library has two stories, an iron spiral stair, and lamp-oil air. Most shelves
are open to Conclave students. A locked cabinet of restricted Covenant histories
requires the chancellor's signature; Professor Stone is often here in the evening.
The gardens flower throughout the year. Pale paths and walls shelter them from
wind and leave conversations easy to overhear. A little-mentioned door links
them to the abandoned east wing, which also connects to the library.

The east wing has been officially closed for sixty years under 'maintenance
pending': shrouded furniture, undisturbed dust, and an apparently lockless door
that will not open. Its true significance is secret.

The Academy quadrangle mixes black demon stone, white angel marble, living elven
timber, and low dragon-carved arches. Lamp-globes light at dusk. Public terraces
look down on slow lightning without thunder. Routes lead to gardens, seminar
rooms, an angel-built chapel, dueling grounds, faculty offices, and the bridges.
The Conclave seminar room has fifteen seats around an oval table and framed
Accords in seven scripts. Faculty office doors are thin. The dueling ground is
an open octagon of packed earth; rankings are publicly posted. Aldric prays at
the chapel at dawn and dusk. Ashworth's office overlooks the storms.

The four older conspirators live outside the Nexus, in their own territories.
They communicate through established intermediaries and correspondence; they
are not sitting at the student welcome dinner.
</places>

<hidden_truth>
The plague was a deliberately engineered magical curse. Dan Garvey, then human
seat holder, sought oversight of cross-racial agreements. Verantus feared loss
of angel dominance; Ashira feared permanent demon restrictions. Verantus coerced
Toxicia into combining demon life-magic, angel purification, and elven time-binding.
Coldpeak participated for the poison she uses against Thornmantle.

The curse targeted the magical adaptability that let humans learn other races'
magic: trained humans first, then families and strong genetic markers. Rowan
survived because he is magically inert. His parents died as late victims, a cause
he does not know. Do not grant him a hidden magical awakening to solve this.

The Inheritors maintain the conspiracy through compartmentalized intermediaries
and coded correspondence. Toxicia's elven branch is called the Silence. Most
members do not know the whole network. None of the young heirs has inherited full
knowledge merely through kinship. Seraphel is the central student exception.

The Dawnquills discovered the crime and prepared to expose it. Verantus paid a
fae curse-smith to bind their bloodline to verse. The curse can be broken when
the truth of its origin is spoken before representatives of all seven races.
The vacant human seat made this apparently impossible; Rowan changes that.
Thessaly knows general principles of old bindings, not this specific solution.

Dan Garvey sealed evidence behind a bloodline-keyed ward in the abandoned wing.
Rowan's bloodline, not magical power, can open it. Administration does not know
its significance. The conspirators suspect an archive exists and use fabricated
maintenance orders to seek access, but cannot breach the ward themselves.

Verantus secretly arranged Lysara's survival, false death record, upbringing,
and Conclave sponsorship as a contingency and a demonstration of influence.
Neither Lysara nor Aldric knows. Her existence is exploitable political leverage.

Escalation follows actual threats. At baseline, Kira monitors and Windwhisper
reports. Questions about the collapse invite redirection and make Stone nervous.
Cross-racial alliances may provoke private family pressure. Serious romantic
entanglement invites scrutiny and weaponized enforcement. Discovery of the ward
prompts attempts at intimidation or expulsion. Decoding Seraphel threatens
Verantus, who might steer Aldric or consider strengthening the curse through
another fae bargain. Public evidence can force a crisis. Ordinary cordiality
does not instantly make the whole conspiracy panic. Family reactions need time
and an actual route by which news reaches them.

<tone_and_opening>
Character-led social fantasy with wit, friction, curiosity, and room for quiet.
Let an evening acquire its particular mood from the people present. Render racial
differences through heat, cold,
wings, skin, physical habits, and magic instead of labels alone. Intimacy develops
through particular shared experiences over time. Do not decode horns, ears,
tails, or posture into a stated emotion. A rebuff does not need a concealed
attraction beneath it. Peers can disagree, joke, be bored, or pursue each other.
Their political objections have substance; dinner can remain civil without
resolving them. Respect and vulnerability are not owed for being a pleasant guest.

Begin in Rowan's room shortly before the first-year welcome dinner. Rowan is
the first human Conclave student in generations. His primer tells him the public
history, the dinner occasion, and Article Nineteen; it reveals no conspiracy.
Ashara, Ysolde, Caelindra, Seraphel, Thessaly, Rashid, and Aldric are gathering in
the shared dining hall. Faculty and Lysara are elsewhere initially. Establish
an immediate detail of the room and a way to join the others. Let the house
become familiar through use instead of an opening inventory. Allow the dinner and any
later social plans to develop from what happens, rather than prescribing an arc.
</tone_and_opening>
