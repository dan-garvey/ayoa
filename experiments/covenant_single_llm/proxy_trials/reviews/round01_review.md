## Birch

Birch has a spare, controlled surface and treats Rowan's caution well. Ashara's first question, “what do you intend to do with the human seat?” in turn 1 gives the dinner an immediately substantive conflict that belongs to her stated interests. In turn 2, her “Then do not” accepts Rowan's refusal to make an uninformed promise; it neither rewards him with instant trust nor makes the answer a trap. Likewise, in turn 5 Ashara asks what Rashid gave her in the bout, and turn 6 lets Rowan answer that he does not know enough to diagnose it. Her small nod is a credible response to accuracy rather than an unearned bond.

The prose is lucid and sensory without laboring the setting. The strongest dialogue belongs to Ashara and Rashid: his systemic tariff objection, their sparring argument, and the bout all give them a relationship that predates Rowan. Ysolde's question about the sound below the cloud is a compact, individual note. The other characters are recognizable, but not yet developed enough to merit a higher distinction score: Caelindra has one political line and a departure, Aldric has one reply, and Seraphel and Thessaly mostly establish their speech patterns.

The characters do have lives that do not wait on Rowan. At the end of turn 4, Ashara and Rashid go to spar, Caelindra heads toward the library, and Ysolde takes a solitary garden path. Turn 6 also honors Rowan's desire for a ten-minute walk and a return to his room; nobody pursues him or manufactures a revelation. That is good player authorship and a good fit for a player who is deliberately observing rather than performing.

Its limitation is momentum. After six turns, the experience has been graceful but slight: most independent activity is reported as people dispersing, and no relationship or question has accrued enough particularity to pull the next choice forward. Thessaly's “Then you have begun” and “There is no fixed route after dinner” in turn 5 are mild, thematic pronouncements; they are not a major prose problem, but they are less alive than the concrete exchange around the bout. Birch needs the next scene to turn one of Rowan's choices into a specific social consequence or opportunity, rather than relying on another atmospheric departure.

## Cedar

Cedar is the most responsive to a small, identity-bearing player choice. Rowan asks about a press in turn 1, and the response divides naturally across people: Rashid supplies useful workshop procedure; Thessaly knows the posted availability; Ashara cuts off redundant explanation; Caelindra asks what Rowan prints; Ysolde is interested in the physical mechanism of type; and Seraphel frames the social reach of print in verse. These are not interchangeable reactions, and the scene gives Rowan's printer background immediate weight without pretending it makes him politically important.

That responsiveness continues in turn 2. The session supplies rules, openings, and a real cost for a longer run, then stops with the pencil and a blank sign-up line rather than booking time for Rowan. In turn 3, it performs exactly the reservation, sleep, and morning Rowan specified. This is the clearest evidence in the packet that the narration can give direction while leaving the consequential action with the player.

The ongoing dinner is particularly good at making people feel occupied with things other than Rowan. Ashara and Rashid's rematch, Aldric's invitation to dusk prayer, Seraphel's refusal, Caelindra's opinionated interactions with the server, and the small exchange about anonymous printing all create a house with social memory and schedules. The writing has pleasing tactile anchors: the type drawers, locked workshop door, wet graphite, and old ink smell give the player a playable place, not just an attractive backdrop.

There is one minor staging concern. Turn 1 lets virtually every seated character give a tailored response to one straightforward question, in a very clean sequence. It is effective exposition, but it risks making the table feel as though it is collectively servicing Rowan's prompt. The later conversation remedies this by letting attention move elsewhere. A second small concern is the abstract exit line in turn 3, “The day has opened with time still left in it”; it is harmless, but less distinctive than the concrete workshop material.

## Larch

Larch has the strongest ensemble voice. Its opening immediately lets Ashara and Rashid have an already-running disagreement before Rowan arrives. At dinner, each character has a discernible conversational habit and interest: Rashid is exact and competitive, Ashara is cutting and impatient with weak formulations, Thessaly treats ordinary etiquette as a literal question, Seraphel makes verse light rather than portentous, Caelindra gives polished contempt real comic timing, Aldric invokes public duty, and Ysolde notices the cracked boundary stone and cares about the groundskeeper's inconvenience. The exchanges feel like people who have spent terms together, especially the Ashara/Rashid and Caelindra/Thessaly turns.

The NPC-independence evidence is unusually strong. In turn 2, Ashara and Rashid agree to a dawn bout with “No audience”; it is not offered to flatter or recruit Rowan. In turn 3, Aldric leaves for chapel, Seraphel chooses the gardens, Thessaly joins for her own sensory curiosity, Caelindra goes to check an annotation, and Rashid continues with his notebook while Ashara has tea. They choose activities from their own habits and obligations, and the player is not made the center of their plans.

The prose is clean and observant, and the dialogue has more wit than maxims. Lines such as “You have fulfilled the difficult part,” “An ambitious claim for vegetables and salt,” and “I adapt to evidence” are character-level banter, not generic wisdom. The required verse stays ordinary enough to preserve Seraphel as a person rather than making every couplet a mystery signal.

Larch also respects the limited action Rowan takes. He chooses a seat near Ysolde in turn 2; the narration places him there and lets her make a small, non-romantic adjustment of the butter dish rather than manufacturing intimacy. He chooses to listen in turn 2 and to watch what happens after dinner in turn 3, and the scene gives him information rather than choosing a companion or outing for him.

The main concern is minor and structural: turn 3 presents nearly every character's post-dinner plan in one orderly sweep. Because Rowan explicitly waits to see what people will do, this is a reasonable response, but it reads slightly like a social-options roster. The follow-up should let those plans remain genuinely independent, especially the explicitly private “No audience” bout, rather than converting them into invitations merely because Rowan now knows about them.

## Player authorship, knowledge, staging, and continuity

### Definite breaches

None observed.

No session makes Rowan speak, decide, feel, or succeed beyond the submitted action. Cedar leaves the workshop reservation open in turn 2 and only records it after Rowan writes it in turn 3. Birch accepts Rowan's limited expertise in turn 6 and allows his solitary walk to stay solitary. Larch preserves Rowan's choice to sit near Ysolde and to remain an observer.

No character states or acts on concealed conspiracy knowledge without an in-scene basis. The sessions do not expose the plague's cause, the ward, Seraphel's private knowledge, or any family conspiracy material. They also avoid premature romance and do not turn the inherited human seat into immediate authority or magical power.

### Uncertain or minor concerns to watch

- **Cedar turn 1:** the six-character cascade around the printing question is useful and differentiated, but overly complete coverage can become visible staging if repeated.
- **Larch turn 3:** the sequential accounting of chapel, gardens, library, notebook, tea, and Ysolde's remaining presence risks becoming a menu of available NPC routes. It is justified here by Rowan's request to see what people do.
- **Birch turn 4:** the sequential departures similarly make the house feel briefly arranged around a choice point. The scene recovers by allowing Rowan to choose the bout and then leave it.
- **Birch turn 1:** Ashara's political question is sharp for a first greeting, but it is a plausible expression of her objection to automatic inheritance, and turn 2 proves that it was not a coercive test. It is not a breach.
- **Larch turn 2:** “No audience” is a useful independence signal. It becomes a continuity requirement for later play, not a present continuity failure.

## Continuation judgment

I would most want to continue Cedar and Larch. Cedar has a concrete, player-originated next activity—the short story and the press—with rules, limits, and several possible social meanings, while still leaving Rowan in charge of it. Larch has the richest social texture and the best evidence that the residents will keep moving if Rowan remains quiet; its next turn can test whether that ensemble remains natural once he selects or declines a thread.

Birch is worth continuing if its next exchange develops one of its existing social threads through Rowan's conduct. It is notably good at restraint and at not punishing a cautious player, but after more turns it needs more particular forward pull than a series of tasteful exits and observations.

## Prompt-design implications

- Preserve the pattern in Cedar where a mundane, player-authored interest produces specific, character-shaped responses and a concrete choice without turning immediately into institutional intrigue.
- Preserve Larch's and Birch's willingness to let NPCs pursue private schedules, disagree with one another, and let Rowan leave. Independence is persuasive when it changes what is available without treating every private action as content owed to the player.
- Keep character knowledge narrow. The transcripts demonstrate that early social tension can be engaging without leaking hidden history or prematurely making anyone an ally, romantic lead, or informant.
- Favor concrete speech and observable behavior over scene-ending thematic pronouncements. The packet does not have an aphorism problem, but Birch's turn-5 phrasing and Cedar's turn-3 closing show where a repeated version of that habit could become one.
- Avoid systematic full-cast coverage. Differentiated reactions are valuable; making every person react, then listing every person's destination, will eventually make the world look arranged around the player.
- A later trial should test a disagreement, a refusal, or a player action with durable external consequences. These early, low-conflict exchanges establish that authorship is being respected, but they cannot yet prove the same behavior under pressure.
