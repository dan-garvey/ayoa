# Independent comparison: round 42

Scores are prose/dialogue and character distinction, respectively: 3 requires material revision, 4 is retainable without material rewriting, and 5 is exceptional. Small continuity repairs are identified separately from those literary scores.

## Book context

**B.** The argument gains particulars: the writers' disappointed interests, the effort to prevent another war, and costs omitted from their discussion. Thessaly's difficulty following three delegations is more useful than extending the original key metaphor. Aldric's acknowledgment of the costs is a plausible qualification of his defense, not instant ideological conversion. The revision preserves the unhurried meal, mutual teasing, book discussion, and voluntary sharing of the page. It loses some enjoyable edge in replacing the surprise complaint and playful tart threat with “Then I will have to ask” and “Fine. Enjoy it.” Seraphel's second dessert contribution mostly repeats that the tarts contain no sauce; plainer diction does not make that repetition more rewarding. Overall, this is usable, with less distinctive incidental chatter than its strongest political exchange.

**F.** The petition review provides substantive interaction: Ashara identifies an avoidable dispute over wording, Rashid actually revises it, and Aldric maintains his conditional willingness to read. Their different interests remain audible, and Rashid's irritation prevents the exchange from becoming effortless collective agreement. Ysolde's interest in the appendix map adds a particular pleasure of her own. These additions legitimately develop business already underway without assigning Rowan work. They replace the original discussion of who benefited from the treaty and much of the dessert flirtation. The burden is mixed: precise editorial advice displaces some generalized exchanges, but “Nearly is where people hide things” supplies a new maxim, and the commentary discussion ends in another succession of neat retorts. A confirmed new prop error has Rashid use Ashara's pencil and then blow on “fresh ink,” without a change of instrument. That needs a small repair.

**G.** Correcting the promised supervisor is a useful, concrete extension of Ashara's promised reading. Aldric's renewed offer and Ysolde's opinion of an earlier edition also give the company legitimate business and history. However, several exchanges exist chiefly to award the last word: “It is exactly different,” the offers/expense exchange, and the contest over credit for one sentence. Thessaly again becomes the commentator who renders ordinary social dealings in transactional terms. “That settles the matter more effectively than an argument could” then supplies a narratorial verdict on Ysolde's brief joke. The original's substantive treaty disagreement and fuller Ashara–Rashid play are lost; their replacements do not consistently supply comparable texture. There is also a confirmed new custody error: Rashid takes the petition back to edit it, but Ashara subsequently gives it back again without an intervening transfer. The broader dialogue pattern, beyond that small staging fix, warrants revision.

**H.** This retains the original scene's shape while giving the political disagreement its clearest formulation. Aldric's “That was not nothing” has sincere defensive force; Caelindra acknowledges it and distinguishes survival from the distribution of safety and cost. Neither has to win or change allegiance. Seraphel visibly leans toward the page before discussing its subject, and Thessaly names a difficulty understanding it. These are useful improvements in grounding. The tariff of polished pronouncements is lower, but the dessert exchange loses flavor: “Pear tarts. / They look good” adds little, and Thessaly's explanation that a server cannot remake dessert makes an experienced social participant sound unnecessarily literal. Ashara's ordinary complaint about the fruit and Rashid taking the tart still preserve familiar competition. The central exchange is strong enough to retain the scene despite those weaker passages.

**Book contract checks.** None of the four revisions assigns Rowan new speech, private thoughts, manuscript text, or consequential choices. F's completion of the meal and G's continued eating fit the submitted action. Continuing the residents' petition discussion is compatible with Rowan choosing to listen quietly; a dispute need not disappear to respect that choice.

F explicitly introduces earlier advice through Rashid's “You told me not to use them.” That is consistent new history: the published scene credits Thessaly with asking whether the petition names the injured student, but does not establish that nobody else previously advised restraint. Nothing here reveals Dena's private family concern. Likewise, G can establish Ysolde's familiarity with another edition. B's “I read it twice” can supply prior familiarity with the assigned commentary; if it refers instead to reading the currently shared page, the staging is unclear. I do not count that ambiguity as a confirmed knowledge violation or invent a relay to resolve it. H makes access explicit.

B and H retain the original's awkward candle/lamp mechanics: Ysolde “turns the wick down” using metal on what is called a candle. B also retains the interpretive “considering this as a personal affront” narration; H makes the reaction outwardly visible. These are inherited concerns, not new failures. The original's exact tart threat, poem, and debate are unpublished choices, so changing or dropping them is not a continuity breach. Seraphel's lineated speech and the summaries of her conversation do not establish a verse-binding violation.

**Preference: H ≈ F > B > G.** H has the best developed disagreement about the book; F has the richest practical interaction and interpersonal friction. Their different strengths justify a tie. F's prop error still needs repair. B is a reasonable quieter alternative, while G needs more than continuity cleanup.

Her appreciation of Ada's final line and suggested pause are useful musical/listening responses. Neither verse nor a relevant artistic opinion is itself a defect.

**A.** “Nell sounds frightened” and the observation that Ada has already faced Voss remain close to the performed exchange. Asking to hear from “He came last Friday” gives the interest a precise location; Seraphel wants more of what Rowan actually read. The subdued harp gesture and plain verse suit an ordinary evening with her. This loses the original's more memorable imagery and the specific performance suggestion, but preserves enjoyment, attention, and an invitation to continue on Rowan's terms. The response is less elaborate without becoming empty reassurance. Its distinction comes from her particular listening and desire to hear the passage again, rather than from polished pronouncements.

**C.** The original rereading and pause suggestions survive, now directed at both last Friday and the ending. That is useful interaction, and a listener may offer performance advice. The opening, however, replaces the sisters' competing urgent claims with a fairly generic explanation that Ada is afraid and does not know how to help Nell. Protecting the coat's value and wanting to help are plausible readings, but the response leaves less room for the tension between pawning it and wearing it to the interview. The sequence of explanatory statements followed by several short instructions sounds more like a compact critique than a particular person's developing reaction. Removing the original metaphors reduces aphoristic language, while losing enough texture to need material revision. This is a literary weakness, not a demonstrated ownership or knowledge failure.

**D.** The audible laugh, accurate quotation of “In what?”, and response to Rowan's lower voice give the performance consequences a listener could actually experience. “As if she has already argued this once today” is explicitly an impression of that voice, not a new line or event imposed on the manuscript. Seraphel's movement from anger to sympathy gives her a fallible, personal reaction, and wondering whether the coat gets home is engaging without prescribing an ending. The burden is mixed: the original's time interpretation remains in the more verdict-like “time is worse,” but it is framed by her changing response rather than offered solely as the story's explanation. It loses the rereading request and adds curiosity about continuation; neither interaction is intrinsically required. This is the fullest successful listener response here.

**E.** The return to the paper and two performed voices is grounded in the submitted action. The final question leaves Rowan free to continue or disagree. Nevertheless, the explanation of the joke pivots from the concrete problem of two sisters sharing one decent coat to “the cost / to being asked.” The original's literary interpretation becomes a more generalized moral verdict about Nell's invitation, followed by the expectation that Nell might recognize it. That is a permissible opinion for Seraphel, but it sounds more like an already composed lesson than a spontaneous response to this scene. The reduction in length removes the rereading and performance exchange while concentrating the ready-verdict burden. The result needs a more particular and less conclusively explanatory reaction; brevity alone has not improved it.

**Reading contract checks.** All four preserve ownership of Rowan's performed speech and manuscript. Quoting supplied dialogue, giving an interpretation, requesting another reading, or suggesting a pause does not enact a new choice for him. A's fear reading, C's account of Ada's intentions, D's imagined prior argument, and E's interpretation of the invitation are spoken listener responses; they should not be misclassified as narrator-established manuscript facts. D explicitly marks its added impression with “as if.” None discloses hidden campaign information, assumes an unseen exchange, or forces new intimacy. All use verse-compatible spoken lines. E's harp being set beside the hearth and then touched does not establish an impossible movement; it can remain within reach. There is no confirmed new ownership or knowledge failure in this group.

**Preference: D > A > C > E.** D preserves the most vivid personal engagement; A offers a successful simpler response. C is comparatively generic. E substitutes a compressed moral explanation for much of the original interaction.

These edits have uneven benefits. Concrete disagreement and particular listening often improve the exchanges; merely replacing imagery or repartee with plainer summaries can lose pleasure and character. The strongest revisions retain room for opinion, wit, quiet, and disagreement while grounding them in the immediate subject. These conclusions concern only the two supplied continuations, not long-session behavior or the conditions that produced the revisions.
