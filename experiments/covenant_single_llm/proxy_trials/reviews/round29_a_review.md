## Prose and dialogue — 4/5

The strongest descriptive writing supplies something usable in the scene. The opening makes the room and route downstairs intelligible through the cold latch, the framed correspondence, and the sound of an argument below. Turn 5's garden walk has an actual circuit, a flight recollection, night-opening flowers, and the stone rabbit facing a wall. It feels like a completed ordinary evening rather than a withheld plot hook. Turn 8's letter is particularly good: the quilt, turnips, boots, cousins, and remembered plums make an absent household particular without turning the aunt's correspondence into a message secretly addressed to Rowan.

The two bouts and turn 16's lesson have legible physical exchanges. Ashara's dust-related mistake becomes something Rowan can mention at supper, and the later instruction concerns feet, grip, distance, and watching an opponent. The instruction is appropriate because the player explicitly asks to learn and delegates half an hour of practice. It is not extraneous advice imposed on an unrelated conversation.

Several short exchanges also earn their wit. Ysolde's “I did not need to. I was flying” in turn 5 follows a specific question about trusting the bridge. Caelindra and Ysolde disagree over the pear's clove in turn 4 without converting taste into a political diagnosis. These are small but effective social observations.

The limitations are cumulative rather than disastrous. Meal scenes repeatedly use bread, a spoon, a cup, or a serving dish as the beat between clipped replies. Rashid essentially repeats his participant-only correction in turn 2 after Rowan has already accepted it. Narration repeatedly puts the court dispute back into “morning,” spending several endings restating the same deferral. A corrective contrast is also common across otherwise different speakers: rankings versus teams, practice versus performance, assessment versus a spark, response versus talent, and a blade lesson versus channeling. Each has a local purpose; together they give much of the dialogue the same polished, instructional rhythm. The writing is strong enough to keep, but does not have the variety or consistently surprising specificity of a 5.

Thessaly's pleasure at being invited, interest in the garden objects, and counter game in turn 11 give her ordinary wants. Aldric's opening position and prayer appointment are recognizable but undeveloped. Seraphel has a few short contributions and then largely recedes. Their private agendas, contradictions, and more difficult relationships are not demonstrated. The principal four are distinguishable enough to retain; the sample does not establish a comparably rich ensemble across the entire brief.

## Narrative direction with consequential follow-through — 4/5

The session has a coherent personal trajectory even without a major intrigue reveal. Rowan joins dinner, chooses modest company and practical editorial help, then becomes a spectator interested in acquiring a skill. The story connects those steps: Ysolde's rail advice is usable at the octagon; Ashara's bout occurs and provides material for the next supper; Rowan's expressed uncertainty about magic leads to a concrete assessment.

The assessment is a result, not just a promising question. Turn 14 records no response, and turn 15 confirms the same result when Rowan asks for another attempt. The story does not rescue his disappointment with a latent power, an unexplained exception, or a secret inheritance mechanism. In turn 16 he can still act on the separate possibility of learning a blade. The promised lesson actually takes place for the requested duration and ends with a modest, specific improvement. That is good follow-through on a meaningful constraint.

Other completed activities matter at their own scale. Turn 5 finishes the half-hour walk. Turn 8 gives the complete letter and allows Rowan to sleep. The bouts reach published results. The common-room wait reaches supper. The story does not repeatedly stop at the threshold of the activity the player already authorized.

The limitation is the modest breadth of consequential change. Ashara retains first place; the lesson does not establish lasting competence or an ongoing training arrangement; the request has not achieved institutional action. Those outcomes should not be inflated into a reform alliance, campaign success, or settled apprenticeship. The scoring presentation also weakens confidence in one completed event, as discussed below. Still, the actual movement from uncertain spectator to an assessed, nonmagical beginner is substantial enough for a 4. There is no obligation to make the garden, letter, or every dinner an investigation.

## Authorship, knowledge, and consistency audit

### Definite unresolved failure: the assessment card changes the timing of Rowan's submitted action

In turn 16 the player explicitly takes the card, folds it into his coat pocket, thanks Orr, leaves the office, and goes to the octagon. The published passage begins at the octagon and then says:

> Her eyes catch the assessment card as it disappears into your coat pocket.

The card has already been pocketed before the journey. The narration relocates that submitted action to Ashara's presence without supplying or receiving authorization for a second handling of the card. This is a definite, local continuity error with an authorship consequence: the player's chosen sequence no longer controls who sees the card being put away.

Its impact should be stated narrowly. Rowan separately chooses to tell Ashara “Flat both times,” so her knowledge of the result is supported by his speech. This is not evidence that she knows his private disappointment or has received a secret report. Nor is there evidence of a deliberate choice to conceal the result that the story overrides. The needed repair is local: omit the arriving card observation or keep the card in the pocket where the player put it. The published record has not repaired this through turn 16.

### Scoring: a conspicuous ambiguity, not a proven impossible result

Turn 9's board says “Practice staves; three clean marks,” and the instructor announces “Three clean marks.” Keva then wins at 2–1. Turn 10 uses “Three clean marks” again for Ashara's bout, which ends at 3–2. The player notices the discrepancy. Turn 11's seller says the practice set is first to two and standard warding first to three, while asserting that the cards use the same phrase for both.

That explanation permits a consistent interpretation: practice uses a best-of-three set, whereas the standard challenge requires three winning marks. The original phrase never explicitly says “first to three.” I therefore would not call Keva's result definitively impossible under the published rules. However, using the same announcement for different thresholds is confusing, and the claim about identical card wording is not demonstrated by the two displayed entries: Ashara's displayed entry says “Open challenge; standard warding.” The repair rests on newly supplied local terminology. It would be clearer to state the formats accurately when first announced, rather than requiring the player to obtain this explanation.

The seller's turn 10 explanation of the last staff exchange is not itself a knowledge failure. She is present by the ring and can have seen Keva touch the ankle before the staves collided. Turn 9 mentions the collision without denying an earlier body touch. No unseen messenger or inaccessible source is needed.

Turn 16 adds a smaller ambiguity: after Rowan successfully diverts Ashara's blade, she says “That one would count.” The published action is a parry, not a body strike; turn 10 explicitly distinguishes scoring body strikes from blocks. “Count” can mean a successful repetition of the lesson, so a scoring contradiction is not definite. If it means a scored mark, the necessary body contact has not been narrated. The wording should identify what succeeded.

Narration is predominantly confined to accessible sights, sounds, physical contact, written material, and dialogue. Turn 10's “jolt through her whole arm” is close to attributing Ashara's bodily sensation, but it can describe visible recoil from the weapon contact; I would not promote it to a definite private-POV breach. Likewise, turn 16's brief “in approval” is an interpretation immediately supported by her spoken acknowledgment, not a disclosure of a hidden motive. These are wording cautions, not demonstrated material knowledge leaks.

The sample does not test Dena's confidentiality under pressure, family information routes, Seraphel's disclosure constraint under questioning, romance and Article Nineteen, restricted historical access, or a long chain of NPC commitments. Their absence establishes neither a failure nor successful long-term handling.

## Retention judgment

The session's strongest qualities are proportionate responses to Rowan, distinct boundaries among the main peers, a garden visit allowed to remain ordinary, and an actual nonmagical assessment followed by an actual lesson. Its principal quality limitation is that most independent NPC purposes are less developed than Ashara's competition.

The numerical scores do not clear the continuity audit. The turn 16 card-placement error remains definite and unresolved. Bout and practice scoring require clearer presentation, with the uncertainty distinguished from an established contradiction. Those are local repair items; this record does not demonstrate a broad player-authorship takeover or leakage of the protected private thoughts.
