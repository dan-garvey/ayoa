The main weakness is repetition across complete exchanges. In Turn 1, Aldric wants the report and Ashara raises inspection. Other quiet scenes are completed more competently than engagingly. In Turns 12–13, the rules and game are delivered, but the actual game supplies almost no interpersonal response beyond corrections to the explanation, the result, and another invitation. A game need not contain revelations or a separate plot, but the shared experience could acquire more individual texture. Turn 11's “She had a boat. The recurring problem is not excessive ideology or a flood of prepared maxims. Much of the teaching is invited and useful. It is repetition of settled positions and limited conversational development between successful actions. These weaknesses occur often enough to keep the dimension at three.

**Most useful generic improvement direction**

Retain the rich histories, the independent court progression, and the willingness to carry delegated activities to actual outcomes. Improve sustained social writing by removing repeated statements once a position is established and letting the activity's changing circumstances affect what the participants contribute.

It does not require additional plot pressure, mandatory questions at the end of each turn, forced intimacy, or less background. The session already demonstrates that quiet play can move and that NPCs can act independently; the next improvement should make more of those exchanges enjoyable to inhabit.
