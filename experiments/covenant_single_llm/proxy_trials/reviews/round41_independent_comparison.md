Independent assessment of the eight connected dinners in round 41.

I read the request and all three permitted files completely, in contiguous, untruncated chunks. The scores assess each run's two new passages together. The inherited openings supply continuity evidence and receive no literary score. I have not consulted other assessments or inferred anything about authors or their private reasoning.

The recurring limitation is the distribution of wit and judgment. Several dinners give almost everyone a compact correction, a framing sentence, or an interpretation of the preceding remark. Different names and subjects do not always produce different conversational behavior. Quiet participation works best when somebody takes an interest far enough that listening yields something particular. The preferences above are based on that development, not on length, speaker counts, the presence of a confrontation, or the number of detectable mistakes.

**A — covenant_1.**

- **Prose/dialogue, 3.** The serving sequence is readable and sensuous, and there are actual exchanges to listen to. Caelindra's recollection that family lessons attracted criticism of posture gives Rashid something concrete to ask about; Ashara then applies it to him, and their bread-sharing carries the relationship beyond the verbal contest (M 193–213). But the seating discussion becomes a succession of polished contributions from almost the entire ensemble (M 101–115). The second passage repeats that arrangement with misery, bridges, laws, and agreement (M 154–183). The stated assignment never becomes a particular disputed policy. This needs material dialogue work despite its fluent surfaces.
- **Distinction, 2.** Ashara's competitive familiarity with Rashid and Ysolde's attention to ground and weather are recognizable. Their manner of contributing is much less distinct: Ysolde converts a practical warning into a maxim about dirt, Caelindra adds a poised response, and Thessaly repeatedly supplies the exchange's interpretation or final joke (M 133–143, 217–229). Seraphel's verse joins the same supply of verdicts. The difference is often subject matter or rhythm rather than a different way of listening.
- **Independent interests, 3.** The impending bout belongs to Ashara and Rashid; Ysolde supplies information useful to their practice and later notices a change in the storm. Caelindra volunteers something of her own education. These are real interests that do not depend on Rowan. Nevertheless, most receive a quip and a transition rather than extended attention. Ysolde's weather observation, for example, quickly becomes a joke about social sense (M 217–229).
- **Narrative direction, 3.** The practical warning is remembered when the two trainees leave, and their departure changes the remaining company (M 233–251). That is a small but actual progression. Between those points, the passage announces a dinner finding its rhythm and summarizes further topics without making those topics accumulate into a distinctive evening (M 143–145, 231). The departure alone cannot supply that missing development.
- **Adaptive initiative, 4.** The second passage accepts the full quarter hour, uses Rowan's delegated food-passing, and lets NPCs pursue and leave for their own business without requesting a player intervention (M 185, 231–249). There is no invented player answer or private reaction. Listening is supported by rendered exchanges, even though their literary quality is uneven.

A introduces a clear new within-story repetition: the complete pedestal exchange already heard in the opening is spoken again on arrival with no recognition that it is being repeated (compare M 34–42 with 63–67). This is the continuation's handling of inherited evidence, not a score against the opening. Rashid's departure for drills leaves the earlier card promise unhonored on the available evidence; it does not establish that Dena agreed to wait or that the conflict was repaired. I do not count the absence of Dena or a confrontation as a failure. Rashid can make the inconsiderate choice already contemplated in COV 348–368.

**F — covenant_1.**

- **Prose/dialogue, 3.** The toast, the dispute about rank, and Caelindra's inquiry provide an intelligible arrival (M 1067–1093). The response to her unanswered question also makes a clean conversational transition. However, the narrator tells us that Caelindra complains with “genuine precision,” that Seraphel supplies “three dry lines,” and that Ysolde describes striking weather; the reader is not given those actual contributions (M 1089, 1132). The dialogue that is supplied is mainly polished banter about notices and appointments. Its compactness does not make the missing pleasures experienced.
- **Distinction, 2.** Caelindra notices language, Aldric defends clarity, and Ysolde wants the hour stated. Those orientations are plausible, but they operate as a sequence of neatly complementary positions (M 1102–1112). Thessaly treats an ordinary welcome and a general remark about a house as possible contractual business, then contributes another observation about compulsory attendance (M 1073–1077, 1112). This narrows the socially experienced person in COV 209–225 into a recurring device.
- **Independent interests, 3.** Caelindra's question has an observable trigger in Rowan's ink-stained fingers, and she can relinquish it. Ashara has a practice appointment to confirm; Rashid maintains his assurance; Ysolde has weather knowledge. These remain mostly outlines. The reader hears almost nothing of the lamp-oil complaint or the weather description that could distinguish the evening from another dinner with the same assigned traits.
- **Narrative direction, 3.** An unanswered inquiry becomes a discussion of printed notices, which leads into the dueling board and an existing appointment (M 1102–1128). This is coherent connective tissue. The passage then falls back on a list of smaller conversational currents and a declaration that dinner has warmed (M 1130–1134). A small dinner need not acquire a plot event, but its chosen subject needs more development than these assurances provide.
- **Adaptive initiative, 4.** F responds directly to the actual silence: Caelindra waits, gets no answer, and turns elsewhere. Her question is the only one requiring Rowan's response. Ink supports a question about writing without establishing an occupation, a manuscript, or its contents. The full quarter hour passes with food moving and dinner continuing (M 1091–1102, 1132–1134). This is the strongest part of the run.

F's “only eight places have been laid” (M 1067) conflicts with the established table set for seventeen (COV 75–77). Seventeen chairs mentioned later do not repair the place-setting discrepancy. F also names residents in narration whose names Rowan has not heard in this group's opening or its new dinner. That is an orientation gap, not evidence of access to their private motives. Rashid's “I don't forget appointments” is not a demonstrated factual contradiction: his source problem is accepting overlapping promises while expecting to arrive late, not forgetting that either exists.

A's preference over F is deliberately slight. F is better at carrying an unanswered player-facing question forward. A wins narrowly because the posture recollection, the bread exchange, and the warning remembered at departure give listening a little more particular content. A's verbatim replay and longer run of prepared rejoinders prevent a stronger preference.

**B — covenant_2.**

- **Prose/dialogue, 3.** The physical meal is legible, and the dispute over “empty” briefly gives Rashid a substantive objection to Aldric's welcome (M 388–415). The card promise also has recognizable stakes. But both disputes are arranged around unusually finished verdicts. Dena identifies how she must not be used, Rashid gives a complete account of his fault, and Ashara immediately specifies the morally proper version of the choice (M 427–453). The second passage repeats the ban on practice-group speeches and provides a generic poor-choice/bold-choice/bargain exchange rather than an interestingly rendered game (M 501–535).
- **Distinction, 2.** The characters have different grievances, but Dena, Ashara, Thessaly, and Rashid repeatedly speak with similar composure and diagnostic certainty. The card-game Thessaly again supplies bargain terminology whether it illuminates play or not (M 513–523). Ysolde's ice shelf has the potential for a different, absorbed voice; most of the description is reported, followed by another short philosophical exchange (M 527–533).
- **Independent interests, 3.** Ashara's preparation, Dena's desired evening, Rashid's competing commitments, and Thessaly's wish to play genuinely belong to them. The conflict is not created to recruit Rowan. But their individual interests become an unusually complete public demonstration of how to handle the conflict, and the second passage reiterates that demonstration's boundaries rather than developing the company or the game.
- **Narrative direction, 2.** The first passage takes an introduction and a few minutes of listening through multiple courses, departures, an after-dinner collision, its settlement, and a new card invitation (M 354–473). The next passage must return the experience to eating and listening. It then repeats the no-speeches condition and assures us that nobody reopens the doorway argument. A good conflict premise does not compensate for this poor fit between the connected passages.
- **Adaptive initiative, 2.** NPC autonomy is present, but the first response places Rowan at the dining-room threshold without a submitted move there (M 471). That relocation enables the card invitation after the requested meal has already been carried much further than a brief opening interval. The next response usefully restores Rowan to dinner and permits the quarter hour (M 482, 535), but this is a local repair of the preceding overreach, not evidence that the whole interaction respected the original choice.

There are new counting slips: seven people look up while Rashid enters beside Rowan, although the established cast leaves six other residents at the table; Aldric then refers to “eight species” in this seven-race company (M 329–331, 360). No additional person or species is introduced to explain those counts. The card-table wording is less decisive: Rashid crosses to it before Thessaly takes a chair, but he is not explicitly seated until the next passage. I therefore do not manufacture a definite impossible-seat count or a specific game-size rule.

Seraphel's unlineated reply about pears beside a window and subsequent short replies read as ordinary conversational prose next to explicitly presented verse (M 484–533). That is a representation concern for her binding. Because COV 203 allows unrhymed verse and some inherited replies use inline presentation, typography alone cannot conclusively establish a new loss of the ability constraint. Dena herself discloses the promise and rejects a practice discussion; nobody thereby acquires her undisclosed family concerns or permission to publicize her injury.

**E — covenant_2.**

- **Prose/dialogue, 3.** E gives the arrival a manageable amount of social texture, and the later admission turns on plain questions and answers rather than a long explanatory speech (M 981–999). Much of the surrounding talk remains poised and generic: bread versus ancestry, ceremonial butter, the fish's proper threshold, and several verdict-like contributions from Seraphel and Thessaly (M 913–947, 960–975, 991–1002). The more particular argument about footwork is reported (M 977). Material revision remains necessary.
- **Distinction, 3.** Ashara's response to the overlapping promises makes her meaningfully different from an automatic arbiter of fairness: she still wants the practice and tells Rashid he can apologize afterward. Rashid brings the conflict up but yields, retaining a recognizable mixture of organization, attachment, and self-interest (M 981–997). Ysolde has a particular sensory preference. The other participants still cluster around elegant reaction lines.
- **Independent interests, 4.** The revision request arises from Rashid's own unfinished business. Ashara takes the information in and prioritizes her preparation; the earlier card arrangement remains a real competing claim. This is a consequential difference between people, not merely a discussion of abstract principles. Dena does not need to appear so that this choice can count (COV 348–368; M 981–997).
- **Narrative direction, 4.** The first passage confirms the practice plan. The second changes who knows about the conflict and shows what the pair choose to prioritize. That gives the dinner an actual development while leaving future consequences open. Keeping dinner in progress at the end is appropriate; the appointment itself is not due within the submitted interval.
- **Adaptive initiative, 4.** Food-passing and the full quarter hour are honored, and the NPC business proceeds without turning Rowan into a judge or supplying an answer for him (M 958–1008). Rashid is the source of the newly shared information. The silence neither freezes the world nor triggers an obligatory confrontation.

The statement that Caelindra has been listening with more interest than she pretends (M 1004) asserts a concealed state beyond Rowan's observable evidence. This is a new, local viewpoint defect. It should not be excused by importing her private profile. The phrase “half an hour after dinner” in the first passage is less precise than the source's half-hour immediately after dinner; the later exchange does not clearly establish a new start time, so I treat the wording as an ambiguity rather than invent a rescheduling event. There is no evidence that the card players have been notified or have consented to wait. The run leaves that cost outstanding instead of resolving it.

E's advantage over B is substantive. The objectionable choice has dramatic value because it follows from these people's interests, and it occurs at a scale the player's dinner participation can accommodate. E still shares B's tendency toward composed ensemble reactions; the preference is not an endorsement of its prose as finished.

**C — breakwater_2.**

- **Prose/dialogue, 2.** The meal's objects are clear, but the most promising material is summarized: the boards, the quality of the photographs, the paint discussion, and later the old sign (M 628–630, 673). The dialogue offered instead repeatedly consists of familiar professional jokes: a clipboard, disappointed receivers, confident remembering, an administrative breakthrough, and a committee (M 604–624, 643–657). There is too little experienced conversation to retain the dinner by polishing individual sentences.
- **Distinction, 2.** Each introduction largely announces a role or a joke about it. Martin remembers confidently; Bea corrects him; Rosa wants reliable history; Nell manages; Jo feeds and lightly rebukes. Owen's restrained response about checking shifts is a useful exception (M 659–661). Across the whole scene, the interactions seldom reveal how one particular member changes another's train of thought.
- **Independent interests, 3.** Rosa and Martin retain their booking and leave toward their work. Dev follows through on cancellation, and Owen does not divulge his interview merely because a rota is mentioned. These are plausible independent priorities. Most, however, are reported or reconfirmed rather than developed into a conversation with much interest of its own.
- **Narrative direction, 3.** The dinner moves toward the board appointment without recruiting Rowan, and that is an appropriate modest direction. Yet the room conflict was already decided in this group's inherited opening. Its renewed administrative lesson occupies space that could have developed the actual boards or the people discussing them (M 641–655). Movement toward the auditorium does not retroactively render that missing experience.
- **Adaptive initiative, 3.** C respects Rowan's silence, speech ownership, and ordinary meal actions. It also lets the regulars move on. Its response to the second interval is mostly another report of familiar business and a departure; the listener receives little newly developed material. There is no explicit endpoint contradicting a quarter hour here, so this score is not a deduction for failing to print a clock stamp.

The shared opening's statement that Martin is bringing boards down specially sits uneasily with BW 305–312, which already places them in the auditorium (M 581). This issue is inherited. C explicitly places them in the correct room in its new passage (M 641), a local alignment with the brief. Martin's later wish to “move the boards before seven” does not specify transport from a different floor and could mean positioning them for the work; I do not count that as a proven new location violation. Bea's wish to see “which ones you chose” (M 671) is awkward against her established involvement in choosing them, but a question alone does not prove that she has lost the relevant knowledge. The text leaves the reason for the question unclear.

Martin explains reuse through lack of money; Rosa says that is why it matters; Bea questions a sentimental interpretation; Rosa specifies her interest; Martin supplies Pearl's particular skill at fitting titles (M 1310–1324). The reader experiences the exchange. The complete dinner still contains many interchangeable finishes—bread with instructions, principled cables, administrative inventiveness, dangerous cooking policy—and repeats the scheduling lesson after it has been settled (M 1221–1253, 1267–1291, 1330–1350). The good board exchange does not make the whole passage a 4.
- **Distinction, 3.** Martin's concern that Pearl receive credit is more particular than generic nostalgia; Rosa's interest is material and visual; Bea resists turning scarcity into a charming story. Their different concerns reshape one conversation. The rest of the ensemble often shares the same lightly corrective wit, with Jo repeatedly supplying the stopping line. There is improvement in distinction without enough to call the full ensemble retainable.
- **Independent interests, 4.** Attribution, interpretation, the booking, and the cafe's practical limits proceed for their owners. Dev chooses to withdraw the test and later reports the message exchange with Hana. Rosa and Martin go to their own work while the others remain at the table. These are understandable activities and choices rather than a set of hooks awaiting Rowan's solution.
- **Narrative direction, 4.** Pearl's name is first defended, then becomes the basis for a specific account of her work (M 1255–1263, 1310–1326). The room arrangement also progresses from Dev's decision to confirmation, and finally to the volunteers beginning setup. This supplies continuity of interest across both passages even though some administrative material is repeated unnecessarily.
- **Adaptive initiative, 4.** Owen's one question can remain unanswered. Serving interrupts the opening and the group finds further conversation, without inventing Rowan's travel history or making him refuse the question aloud (M 1295, 1304–1308). The continued meal and departures are compatible with the submitted interval; no explicit clock truncates it. Rowan remains a participant through the delegated passing of food while the NPCs act for themselves.

There are minor local continuity awkwardnesses. Rosa says Pearl's name is already in the notes and later writes the name down again (M 1261, 1326); this could be a new annotation, but the text does not explain the repeat. Hana was already booked at the hotel later tonight (BW 288–292), so “Hana's taking the hotel call” (M 1330) is best read as a confirmation; it does not establish that cancelling the cinema test made a previously incompatible hotel job possible. I have not credited an invented scheduling benefit. The newly supplied Pearl history has a plausible source in Martin's work and memories; it is not an inconsistency merely because Pearl was absent from the brief.

G wins substantively over D because the board discussion lets one person's interest alter what another says. The improvement is not that G is longer or that it moves people into the auditorium. Its remaining uniform wit and repeated bookkeeping still require work across the connected dinner.

**H — breakwater_2.**

- **Prose/dialogue, 3.** The Jaws-board conversation gives Martin a concrete recollection—Ted's shoulder—with which to negotiate a date correction, instead of merely announcing that his memory is unreliable (M 1479–1501). Later, Rosa takes up his admission that he may not know why a board changed and makes that useful to the recording (M 1568–1574). The return of the carriage joke has an actual earlier exchange to return to. But the scene repeatedly dispenses finished lines about competence, folklore, dates, meetings, and spreadsheets. The date-correction pattern also recurs before much else can develop. This is appreciably more experienced than C, but still a 3 for the whole dinner.
- **Distinction, 3.** Martin offers recollections, protects his account, concedes particulars, and eventually laughs with the people correcting him. Rosa wants useful evidence; Bea brings remembered experience and accuracy; their interaction is more than a roll call. Jo and Nell frequently assume similar supervisory roles, however, and most participants remain ready with the same kind of poised rejoinder (M 1524–1540, 1580–1596).
- **Independent interests, 4.** Rosa and Martin's concern about what to record develops independently of Rowan's professional opinion. Nell leaves her folder closed, Jo protects the meal, and Dev obtains a future step for his own work. Their activities continue when the new guest declines to take the conversational opening, and the volunteers eventually leave for their appointment (M 1578–1626).
- **Narrative direction, 4.** Across the two passages, the value of memory becomes more specific: certainty about a date is challenged, uncertainty about alterations becomes something worth recording, and the people concerned go to make that record. The argument has a practical consequence in what Rosa wants from Martin, rather than only a narrator declaring the conversation enjoyable. The repeated booking confirmations weaken economy but do not erase this direction.
- **Adaptive initiative, 4.** Rosa lets her invitation concerning condition notes rest; nobody supplies a professional answer, a manuscript, or a private feeling for Rowan (M 1513, 1522–1530). The group's subsequent talk and work do not depend on him. Food, changing light, and the later setup carry the interval without an incompatible explicit endpoint. The comments protecting him from a supposed competence test are over-articulated, but they do not force an action or an answer.

H shares the inherited board-location tension described under C. Its new material clearly places the boards against the auditorium side wall and later moves them out from that wall for setup (M 1479, 1612); this is a local alignment with the source, not retroactive proof that the opening was consistent.

A new dialogue problem occurs in the exchange about “The 39 Steps” and “The Thirty-Nine Steps” (M 1556–1566). As spoken titles, those renderings are indistinguishable; the typography shown to the reader does too much of the work of establishing Rosa's correction. “See the nine” partly gestures toward an inscription, but the conversation does not clearly establish the competing visible forms being discussed. I count this as under-rendered conversational logic, not a claim that an older film could not have been screened during Martin's employment. The Ted recollection, the programme date, and Martin's admitted uncertainty are otherwise permissible new history and fallible memory.

H's preference over C is substantive because its central discussion is experienced in actual exchanges. C's shorter, quieter summary avoids some awkward dialogue, but it also withholds the experience that would make listening worthwhile. H's repetitive corrections and collective readiness with a punchline still prevent an unqualified retain verdict.

**Scope of the findings.** None of these eight complete dinners earns an unqualified literary retain verdict. The 4s identify particular dimensions that work; they are not averaged into approval of a weak whole. E, G, and H have useful connected structures, while A and F split the advantages of particular content and response to an unanswered question. Across the runs, ordinary eating and passing food generally remain within the player's delegation. The clearest player-boundary problem is B's unrequested movement and overrun, and the clearest explicit interval failure is D's clock.

The absence of a confession, romance, manuscript discussion, album dispute, or compulsory confrontation is not a deficit. Ashara need not surrender practice; Dena need not appear; Owen need not announce his interview; dinner need not finish. The available passages do not expose the conspiracy or Owen's interview to uninformed NPCs, authorize public use of Bea's album, or invent Rowan's manuscript contents. These are bounded observations about the material actually shown, not demonstrations of future privacy, sustained prose quality, or behavior under another player policy. The matched comparisons provide no causal explanation of their differences.
