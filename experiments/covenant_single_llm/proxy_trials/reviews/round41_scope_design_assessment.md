**Finding: one bounded test is justified, with weak and mixed evidence for a literary benefit.** A firm publication budget that also covers quiet observation changes an observable constraint that C33 and C20/21 did not test. It is more than another request to make dialogue natural. Its defensible hypothesis is that allocating a limited passage to a particular interaction can improve the complete reading experience while preserving the submitted activity. It is not that shorter passages, fewer participants, or fewer opportunities to fail constitute better prose. The present evidence does not justify a C41 launch, selection, or promotion by itself.

This assessment reads the complete C40 system, root pilot review, four opening-and-dinner transcripts, both complete story briefs, C33's scene system and decision, C20/21's systems, and the protocol. It does not use the pending independent C40 assessment or any other review, log, private reasoning, or model output. Because the root review was supplied, this is a design assessment, not a blind literary judgment.

The relevant distinction is between fictional duration and publication length. Both frozen dinner submissions ask to eat and listen; the second specifies another quarter hour. That endpoint must happen, but it does not require a transcription of every conversation during those minutes. Conversely, quiet participation is not an explicit request for a short reading experience. The proposed word range is a product-default hypothesis, not a preference established by these submissions, and nothing in the sources identifies 250–350 words as an optimum.

The distinction should not be exaggerated. The desired mechanism still overlaps C33: spend less publication space on incidental material and more on something worth hearing. The new element is a binding allocation constraint, whose effect can be checked separately from literary quality. Adding another selection paragraph, a speaker quota, examples, or voice prescriptions would weaken that isolation. All rich biographies and the permission to invent consistent history must remain intact.

The complete C40 dinners provide a reason to investigate allocation, but reject a simple diagnosis that length causes their weakness. Whitespace word counts of the exact publications are:

Covenant1 is the shortest connected dinner and still needs material prose revision. Breakwater1 is the longest and contains the strongest developed interaction. That follows the [locked root assessment](../candidate_40/pilot_review.md), and the transcripts show why: the useful difference is what the speakers do with one another's contributions. Neither comparison isolates length, and the differing settings cannot isolate a biography or genre effect.

Breakwater1's second dinner supplies the strongest specific support for the proposal. The continuous segment from Dev rejoining the discussion through Nell confirming the limited test is 338 of its 743 words. There are 239 words before it and 166 after it. Rosa asks what the test requires, learns that the back rows will suffice, offers photographs during that interval, and makes quiet from 19:20 a condition. Dev accepts limits and Nell preserves the booking. This is an interaction whose participants have different interests and whose replies change the arrangement. The [Breakwater brief](../candidate_40/second_story.txt) supports that negotiated change: the reservation and assistant's availability constrain them without prescribing refusal or agreement.

Those numbers support testing whether the author can give the useful interaction the publication's attention. They do **not** establish that cutting to 338 words would produce a retainable passage. The segment itself contains the thirty-years/not-continuously rejoinder and the room/interruption formulation. At a 350-word ceiling it also leaves almost no space for setting or a clear passage of the requested time. The present wording is not a minimum length for the interaction, but improving its allocation would require fresh composition, not retaining the first 350 words or extracting a favored middle. Much of the interesting material in the first dinner also arrives after the introductions and food banter. A cap applied as a stopping rule could remove exactly what makes the scene worthwhile.

The other three sequences limit the hypothesis further. Covenant1's levy discussion starts from substantive interests and then produces the familiar succession of scale, ledger, and history formulations. Covenant2 spends the beginning of the second dinner turning Rowan's non-answer into an ensemble exchange; its literalist Thessaly is a portrayal problem that can fit easily inside 350 words. Breakwater2 gives Jo authority over her menu and lets Rosa, Martin, and Dev leave for their own work, but its menu and lettering discussions still rely on prepared corrections. Thus weak repartee is present inside substantial topics, not merely in removable material around them. A budget may leave the underlying manner unchanged or concentrate it.

For the proposed intervention to be distinct, its scope must be settled before any run. Treat the range as an approximate ordinary length with a fixed upper default, not a minimum that inflates an adequate short reply. A quiet wait must remain within that default despite covering several minutes. An exception named “action” cannot cover every submitted activity, and an author cannot acquire an exception merely by inventing an NPC anecdote or performance. Otherwise the original activity/time-skip exemption has returned. Express requests for a detailed account or a complete performance need the corresponding reading experience. These are clarifications of the one length policy, not additional prose changes. If the default and the requested endpoint cannot both be honored in a case, that is an unfavorable result; silently suspending the budget whenever the author wants more scene would make the test inconclusive.

A defensible screen would compare fresh C40-prompt controls and budget conditions with the same complete briefs, common published openings, and both exact quiet dinner submissions. Both conditions would use one persistent whole-story author per route: first dinner publication, then the second submission with that author's first publication retained. There would be one author request per publication and no editor or selector. Both settings and both opening contexts in each setting matter; selecting only Breakwater1's favorable starting point would overstate the evidence. This is a matched continuation screen, not four new substantial campaigns. Every first publication must remain available, including noncompliance. Reading the connected dinners prevents a strong opening, isolated negotiation, or preferred excerpt from compensating for a weak complete experience.

The likely regressions are concrete:

1. **Summary replacing the encounter.** C40 already announces enjoyable choir, practice-dummy, and projector-belt stories without providing their interesting words. A smaller allowance can increase that substitution. A concise statement that people negotiate, laugh, or enjoy one another is not a developed interaction.
2. **The same manner packed more tightly.** Introductions, jokes and verdicts may survive while atmosphere, hesitation, genuine uptake and particular history disappear. Full biographies remaining in the packet is necessary, but does not demonstrate that the published people still benefit from them. Fewer speakers can still sound interchangeable.
3. **A shallow agreement becoming the easiest ending.** The author may remove a substantive objection or make an NPC yield immediately to fit a complete-looking passage. Cooperation remains valid; the failure would be loss of the interests and terms that made the choice intelligible. A quiet exchange need not become a dispute or solve a plot problem to be worthwhile.
4. **Loss of the player's scope or initiative.** The budget may be consumed by arrival and recap, stop early at a question, or require the player to approve an NPC's already announced activity. Alternatively it may rush beyond the quarter hour or supply Rowan's new choice to produce closure. The endpoint is the requested interval, not the end of dinner. The [Covenant brief](../candidate_40/covenant.txt) does not require Dena's arrival or the after-dinner drills within this interval.
5. **Compressed knowledge and continuity.** A reported outcome can obscure who learned a plan, whose permission changed, or when a restriction became known. The original mandatory boundaries still apply. Fewer new claims or disclosures cannot by itself establish stronger boundary handling, and consistent newly invented history remains allowed.
6. **Poor adaptation outside quiet observation.** A universal length habit can flatten a pursued interest or inflate ordinary brief replies toward a word floor. A loose exception can instead reproduce the present expansive quiet dinners. Later explicit-detail and redirection probes must test the actual policy rather than reward either blanket brevity or routine escape from the budget.

The hypothesis should be judged against positive prose evidence: a particular interaction whose actual words are interesting, whose participants take up and alter what others contribute, and whose course reflects different knowledge, interests and relationships. The reader should experience the shared activity and arrive at the submitted endpoint. That can be a pleasurable recollection or disagreement without a new task for Rowan. Neither universal ensemble coverage nor a consequential plot resolution is required in each short publication.

The falsification and stopping conditions should be fixed in advance:

- If compliant budget passages still require material prose rewriting, or their apparent improvement consists only of less text, fewer jokes, fewer speakers, or fewer observable failures, the quality hypothesis has not passed. One good passage surrounded by the same weak connected dinner does not rescue it.
- If the interesting interaction is summarized, becomes less developed than the control, or loses meaningful character differences, the proposed mechanism has failed even when all word counts and endpoints are correct.
- If the endpoint, player authorship, consequential knowledge, or established agreements fail, brevity cannot compensate. If ordinary quiet publications repeatedly exceed the frozen ceiling or exploit an exception, the intervention itself was not demonstrated; that is not evidence of a prose improvement.
- Advancement should require both root and an independent reviewer to find all four treatment dinner sequences retainable without material rewriting, substantively prefer them to their matched controls, and find no material regression in the other dimensions. An unresolved boundary failure defeats advancement. These are qualitative judgments, not probability estimates. A mixed result should stop this proposed direction rather than automatically trigger another word target or stronger restatement.

Even a successful screen would only support continuing evaluation. The [protocol's selection requirements](../PROTOCOL.md) still require fresh sessions with differing player policies, at least two substantial trials of twelve or more player turns, held-out boundary and redirection situations, independent transcript review, and strong judgments across all five quality dimensions without a material unresolved failure. A firm budget is a plausible way to change publication allocation; the sources do not yet show that it produces the desired prose or sustained character life.
