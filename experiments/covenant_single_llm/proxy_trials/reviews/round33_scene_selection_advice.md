Changing scene selection warrants one narrow experiment, with low confidence. Its potentially distinct target is the allocation of sustained attention across an activity. Simply asking for less dialogue, fewer speakers, or more narration would substantially repeat existing guidance and behavior.

I read all requested files in full, including the complete Covenant and all four C32 dinner publications. I did not inspect C32's pilot or blind literary review, read private reasoning, launch a model, or modify prompts. These are qualitative observations from saved artifacts and an untested recommendation, not a new scored comparison or an estimate of causal effect.

[C21's system](../candidate_21/system.txt) already restricts participation to people whose involvement changes the exchange, encourages particular anecdotes, and varies length with submitted scope. [C22](../candidate_22/system.txt) permits ordinary responses and extended activities; [C26](../candidate_26/system.txt) adds structural deletion of surplus verdicts. [C27's revision](../candidate_27/revision_task.txt) preserves activities and outcomes while rebuilding dialogue. Most directly, [C29's revision](../candidate_29/revision_task.txt) follows the current subject and addressee, allows background company to stay occupied, and permits replacing whole proposed choices. Another instruction to select relevant speakers or stop quipping offers little new. C29's authoring system nevertheless asks to give a shared activity enough scene to experience it, without explicitly distinguishing the activity's routine duration from its focal encounter. Unequal depth across that duration is the remaining distinction worth testing.

The C32 publications already vary distance. [Raw 2](../candidate_32/book_raw_2/00.story.md) passes several minutes in food narration and summarizes copying the petition, then stages bread, coursework, and departure repartee. [Record 1](../candidate_32/book_record_1/00.story.md) summarizes dinner conversation before expanding the reading complaint and petition again. [Raw 1](../candidate_32/book_raw_1/00.story.md) and [record 2](../candidate_32/book_record_2/00.story.md) also carry coordinated jokes into indirect narration. Thus quotation marks and dialogue density are poor success criteria: a summary can preserve the same sequence of assigned witty contributions. All four retain recurring ensemble repartee. These samples support neither a diagnosis that biography causes it nor a conclusion that history presentation has no effect.

The hypothesis is that making one developing interaction the passage's center of attention would reduce the repeated restart in which every incidental subject becomes another ensemble performance. The author could allow elapsed dinner time around an encounter without treating every topic as material requiring elaboration. This could create room for a recollection, disagreement, or shared pleasure to acquire substance. It would not itself make the chosen exchange less polished or more individual. The [C31 diagnosis](round31_first_pair_diagnosis.md) already records useful particular recollections under unchanged C29 guidance; this proposal cannot claim that capacity as its innovation.

For an engaged player, potential gains are concentration on the person or activity they actually pursue and fewer interruptions by the cast. The danger is that the author designates a main encounter and demotes the player's next small question or change of subject to incidental business. An ordinary reply seldom needs compression in the first place. This axis is most relevant to an extended submitted activity; each new contribution must remain able to redirect attention. It must not turn an open conversation into a completed scene before the player's unsubmitted choices.

For a player choosing to watch, a particular encounter could provide something satisfying to witness without requiring them to manufacture subjects. But quiet company may itself be the requested experience. The existing court dispute is unusually easy to select because it has explicit stakes and an unresolved outcome; repeatedly foregrounding it would substitute a compulsory plot for the ensemble routine. Thin summary is an equally serious failure: completing dinner and reporting that people joked or became familiar does not provide the shared experience that could make those relationships matter. Changing distance must retain enough unfolding, observable behavior to inhabit the encounter. It grants no authority to summarize new player speech, assign their feelings, erase an NPC's knowledge boundary, or retroactively resolve an agreement. Consistent new history remains available.

If tested, replace only C29 system's two scene-allocation sentences at lines 36–38, leaving the full biographies and existing authorship, knowledge, scope, and endpoint rules intact:

> Within an extended activity, let routine stretches pass in brief, concrete narration. Give sustained scene time to the interaction the player pursues, or to a particular encounter among the people they watch. Let that interaction develop; incidental subjects can pass without becoming further exchanges. A quiet shared pleasure can hold the scene.

This is a modest change in emphasis, not a demonstrated remedy. Evidence of improvement would be a particular interaction that develops beyond its opening position, with surrounding time passing naturally and the requested experience intact. Fewer lines, fewer names, fewer maxims, or a neatly finished meal would not suffice. Reject the change if it merely conceals repartee in summary, produces one equally formulaic central exchange, or makes the player supply more effort to obtain lived company. No second axis or additional authoring stage is justified by this assessment.
