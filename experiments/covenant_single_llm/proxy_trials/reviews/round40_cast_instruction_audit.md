The original is not a pure history document either. Ashara's meritocratic objection is a conviction (S335), while her romantic-access criteria mix that conviction with player-facing prescriptions. Rashid's exhausting rehearsal of casual remarks is described coping behavior (S888); his requirement to win sometimes and avoid convenient confession is narrative direction (S912–916). Aldric's actual observations of his father are history (S956), whereas mandatory reactions to the player's romantic pursuits are prescriptions (S976–977). Seraphel's original metrical/rhyming constraint (S569) conflicts with the current verse interpretation; Thessaly's pervasive bafflement about convention (S692) conflicts with her current social competence. A wholesale original-text replacement would therefore restore more than biography and would erase current additions.

There is a meaningful representational alternative: retain a rich, continuous account of each person's life, convictions, habits, relationships, pleasures, wounds, and private knowledge, while retiring only author-facing menus or redundant corrective comparisons whose character substance is otherwise preserved. The generic system already supplies knowledge boundaries, player ownership, independent lives, and context-sensitive speech (G6–30, 41–47). It is possible to avoid repeating those instructions through hypothetical responses assigned to individual characters. That is a narrow semantic editing task, not an invitation to replace the cast with thin cards. Any eventual concrete version would need an assertion-by-assertion fidelity comparison, including current facts absent from the original. Mixed sentences cannot be removed wholesale merely because part sounds editorial.

Moving the same prescriptions into a new XML block would still expose the same author to the same cues; it is not this substantive alternative. Nor would converting every illustrative capacity into a newly invented anecdote preserve the source. No rewrite, new wrapper scheme, sample dialogue, or fixed voice recipe is proposed here.

The evidence makes the literary hypothesis weak. [C37's completed response](../candidate_37/review_response.md) and [decision](../candidate_37/decision.json) reject deletion of the 81-word generic speech paragraph: all eight outputs receive prose3 from both reviewers, with mixed preferences. That test retained the cast guidance, so it does not directly falsify an intervention on this residual layer. It also supplies no positive reason to expect another deletion to work. The [report](../../PROXY_REPORT.md:114) records earlier negative common-prompt shortening and delivery/order changes. Simply presenting this as “less instruction will free the author” would repeat an unsupported rationale.

[C38's completed response](../candidate_38/review_response.md) and [decision](../candidate_38/decision.json) likewise reject its 155-word restoration as a demonstrated rescue. All four new passages remain below prose4; one has the independently confirmed Ashara knowledge failure. Both reviewers nevertheless prefer both later restored passages over the historical later controls for enacted decisions, and recognize the particular flight conversation. Those gains are real review findings, but the historical comparison does not identify their cause. Personal material already appeared in earlier family anecdotes and C37's cloud recollection while still becoming lessons (PROXY_REPORT 54, 85–96; C37 response 31–38). Thus biography availability, meaningful decisions, and successful prose are distinct questions. None of these failures makes rich history optional.

A fuller nonconflicting import would improve **fidelity and character coverage** beyond C38: Ashara's private tension with her political agenda and expectations (S324, 342, 346), Seraphel's secluded upbringing and grandmother's discovery (S585–589), Aldric's remembered grounds for doubting his father and the father's delayed-disclosure plan (S936, 956), and Lysara's foster childhood and learning her history at nineteen (S788, 792). These include formative causes and relationships, not just additional sensory details. Some expand already-present psychology rather than supply wholly absent motives; Lysara is not even in the current dinner. Rashid's sister and exhaustion are already substantial, so restoring the letters' contents or fuller routine must not be advertised as restoring a missing relationship.

Consequently, broader import is different in content and completeness, but **“more source biography will fix the same dinner's dialogue” remains the C38 hypothesis at greater scope unless a specific missing causal premise and an occasion that can exercise it are identified**. No such missing premise is established by the completed reviews: the later decisions already improve while their rendering fails. C38's small scope cannot rule out every benefit from fuller lives, but word count alone does not establish a new mechanism or justify another rescue screen. A source-fidelity project could be valuable on its own terms; it should not borrow a literary-success claim it has not earned.

My recommendation is to retain all current biographies and restorations, finish the independent C39 assessment, and treat the identified editorial layer as a concrete design ambiguity rather than a demonstrated cause. If a source-preserving cast revision is later considered, its semantic merits can be reviewed before deciding whether there is enough new evidence to test it. This audit selects no C40 treatment and provides no reason to lower the existing literary or knowledge-boundary bar.
