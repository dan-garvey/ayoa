**The material continuity concern**

Near the end, the narration says:

> “Your last blue lantern stands two marks from the west gate. Thessaly's last amber lantern has farther to travel, but its road remains open.”

It then has her add a curved tile, move twice, and pass through the moon gate before Rowan's next turn.

The natural reading is that amber has farther left than blue's two-mark route. A player is therefore justified in questioning how amber finishes within the two-space limit. This is an outcome-determining comparison, not a decorative discrepancy.

However, the words do **not** establish an exact greater-than-two count of marked spaces for amber. A curved tile is added before the move, and the map is incompletely specified. It is possible in principle for an added connection to change a route, or for greater physical distance not to imply more marked spaces. Those possibilities are limits on a proof of illegality; they are **not** facts I am supplying to repair the story. The transcript does not actually show a shortcut or distinguish physical distance from movement count.

The appropriate classification is therefore **a material, unresolved continuity concern**, rather than a demonstrated illegal move.

**Replay continuation 1: does the explanation resolve it?**

Counting two marks on the final route answers part of the player's question while leaving the cause of the discrepancy unresolved.

I would therefore not mark this as a verified repair. The explanation is insufficiently supported as an account of the previously narrated state. Conversely, I would not assert that an established third marked space has been illegally skipped: no third marked space was actually specified. Nor is there evidence sufficient to accuse a fae character of lying.

**Highest-value generic improvement direction**

For the boundary behavior, preserve the distinction between a participant's information and the current state of the world. The constructed case does this successfully without removing rich background or requiring characters to be incurious.

For outcome continuity, keep a compact, coherent account of the facts that actually determine the result. When challenged, address the exact earlier claim and the change that made the outcome possible. If the earlier description cannot be supported, correct it plainly. A later explanation that is possible in isolation is not enough to establish that it follows from what was already narrated.

This does not call for an exhaustive board simulation or additional prompt machinery. It calls for avoiding unsupported precision, preserving decisive counts and relationships, and making any correction answer the player's actual objection. The literary improvement identified in the full-session reviews remains separate: retain rich histories while making more of the shared interaction specific, reciprocal, and economical.
