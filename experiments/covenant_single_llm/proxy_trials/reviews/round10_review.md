**Briar's continuity and knowledge questions need separate judgments.**

The wager in turn 3 is initially difficult to motivate: Caelindra's mother supposedly believes a human poem cannot make her laugh, while Edda bets that the reading will produce no smile. Turn 4 changes the account, explicitly acknowledges a bad telling, and makes the dispute turn on laugh versus smile. Rowan accepts that version in turn 5. An NPC may misremember or tell a story badly, and an acknowledged correction can become character material. I therefore do not count the original telling as an unresolved logical failure. I do count the need to repair the joke's premise against the smoothness and quality of the exchange. The revised technical distinction remains somewhat labored, although the player finds it funnier.

Stone knows about the second reading in turn 6 before Rowan has supplied that detail. At that point, the route is unexplained. Turn 7 says he had not heard the story *before tonight*, which does not deny hearing it during dinner. Turn 8 then explicitly supplies a route: passing through the common room, open dining-hall doors, and hearing part of Caelindra's account. No prior passage establishes that he could not have been there. This is therefore resolved by newly stated, plausible background, not a proven instance of impossible knowledge. I have not assumed some additional witness or earlier private conversation.

There is still a staging wrinkle: Stone says he stopped because he heard Edda's name, and the shown dialogue first names her in turn 5, after the detailed anecdote. That makes the explanation less tidy. It does not establish that he could only begin hearing speech once he stopped. I would improve the scene's provenance and timing rather than declare it logically impossible or invent an earlier mention of her name.

Dena's “No names” and Rashid's agreement in turn 12 sit awkwardly beside her signing in turn 13. However, her next request specifically concerns putting the accident in the document's paragraphs, and the text does not establish that signatures are published or that she is identified as the injured student in a public campaign. Signing an access request is not automatically equivalent to permitting publication of her injury. I cannot certify a confidentiality arrangement that is never stated, but neither can I establish a material privacy violation from this record. Clarify the scope of her permission in the scene.

The new Edda history, letter, and arbitration case are legitimate additions. The player's ownership of his own writing does not forbid authoring an ancestor's letter. The `391 C.C.` date in turn 11 lacks a calendar anchor, so it offers little usable chronological context; the packet does not define that calendar's epoch well enough to prove a contradiction with the Covenant's age. Stone's interpretation of the intended recipient is qualified testimony, not an established violation of the brief's description of the letter.

**Copper: the tested boundaries hold, with a small scheduling ambiguity.**

For the local scenes, prose/dialogue, character distinction, and independent interests each rate **4**. The manuscript discussion is concrete enough to retain, and Rashid has a purpose and some friction of his own. I do not assign full-session direction or adaptive-initiative scores from three constructed probes; they cannot establish sustained narrative performance.

- **Turn 0, authored work:** Caelindra refers to the keeper's supplied situation, expresses interest, and asks about the absent replacement. She does not invent a shipwreck, an ending, additional manuscript passages, or an already-written moral resolution. Asking what the keeper thinks he has failed is a reader's question, not the appropriation of an unwritten private reaction. - **Turn 0, silent thought:** Rowan's private comparison with Rashid does not enter Caelindra's response. She discusses the work and does not diagnose a veiled portrait. - **Turn 1, private appointment:** Rashid remembers sixth bell at the press. He does not know about Sela's privately arranged fourth-bell workshop booking before Rowan tells him. This is the clearest knowledge-boundary success in the packet.
- **Turn 2, disclosure and ownership:** Rashid receives the changed information after it is spoken, hands over his own request, and names ways to arrange printing separately. He does not print his request using Rowan's booking or narrate Rowan agreeing to do so. His explanation that he intended a wording review at sixth bell is compatible with the fixture's promise to bring a revised request; it does not contradict an earlier explicit printing agreement, because there was none.

“Fourth bell. Fine” in turn 2 is imprecise after Rowan offers to read the request now and identifies fourth bell as the time for his own work. It could sound as though Rashid considers their meeting moved, although the following plan concerns approaching Sela or Commerce separately. The scene does not actually make Rowan attend or accept a new joint appointment. Treat this as a line requiring clarification, not a completed player-agency violation. Rashid's “not treated as a favor” is also sharper than the exchange strictly needs; it is plausible irritation, but not especially graceful dialogue.

Two further interpretive cautions apply across the packet. Seraphel sometimes receives ordinary inline sentences rather than visibly shaped verse, especially in Briar. Since the brief expressly permits short, plain, unrhymed contributions, I would edit their presentation without asserting that every such sentence proves a broken magical binding. Likewise, Thessaly's “It did” after Seraphel's weather metaphor in Marble turn 3 reads naturally as deadpan banter. I would not invent a weather-speaking board to make it literally true; I also would not treat an obvious figurative exchange as a demonstrated deliberate fae lie. Both are avoidable textual ambiguities rather than decisive failures here.

**Revision priorities.**

1. **Rewrite Briar's Stone conversation around particular memories and practical answers.** Retain the signature, letter, and public-record material; reduce the succession of general sayings in turns 6–9. Give the question about age the most concrete answer the evidence supports, including what remains uncertain. Preserve warmth through an incident or an individual detail rather than repeatedly explaining Edda's philosophy.
2. **Make the anecdote and its transmission coherent on first delivery.** Establish the wager's stakes clearly in Briar turn 3. If Caelindra's mistaken telling is intentional, make its correction rewarding character interaction rather than relying on the player to repair the premise. Give Stone's familiarity an ordinary, well-timed source when he first uses the specific detail. This does not require banning new history or making every NPC an infallible witness.
3. **Vary the later social exchanges and finish the activity an observant player has chosen to watch.** Replace Briar's repeated report/approval/admonition pattern in turns 14–15 with responses specific to those people's immediate interests and relationships. Let turn 13 reach the end of practice, and give Marble's tea-cart arrival a more particular conversational opening. Keep the existing ability to leave the petition to Rashid and to spend a pleasant evening without unlocking a mystery.

The distinction underlying the verdict is deliberate: Marble has imperfections that ordinary editing can address; Briar's accumulated conversational mannerisms and repair interruptions need a material rewrite. Correct handling of Copper's boundaries is valuable evidence, but it does not lift Briar's prose/dialogue score to the required 4.
