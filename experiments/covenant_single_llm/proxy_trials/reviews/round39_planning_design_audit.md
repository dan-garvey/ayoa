The synopsis would be a different externally observable writing product from either a full draft or an instruction to edit privately. Here it means a deliberately requested artifact saved for the experiment and withheld from player-facing fiction. It is ordinary inspectable author output, not hidden chain of thought. C26 asks for private structural revision of a passage; C27–C31 expose prose before the second request. The proposed first artifact would contain no drafted dialogue. That is a real distinction in task and context. We cannot infer from these public artifacts whether the author already plans privately, whether C26 performed the requested private revision, or claim that this intervention turns planning on. No request for a rationale, chain of thought, per-character mental simulation, or hidden reasoning is needed.

For this distinction to matter, the synopsis must contain concrete prospective scene content: what people do, what they actually discuss, and how participation develops within the submitted scope. A description of desired effects, a list of character traits, or an instruction that the conversation should be natural is not such content. A short paragraph with a soft target around 100–180 words is adequate to test concision without imposing a fixed beat count or a quota of speakers. No enforced truncation or retry should turn that target into selection. Both a quiet exchange and a larger NPC interaction can qualify; neither a disagreement nor a new plot event is mandatory.

The same whole-story author should produce the synopsis and final fiction. The final request must retain authority to replace any new synopsis choice. Only prior published history, the authoritative brief, and the player's submissions bind the next passage. The synopsis is an unpublished composition proposal: a proposed departure, promise, disclosure, game result, or player response has not occurred. Requiring literal fidelity would risk both poor writing and authorship violations. This proposal therefore does not give a separate narrator a fixed event script or introduce a second source of canonical truth.

Full backgrounds must remain available throughout both requests. The synopsis is not a compressed substitute for the Covenant, persona, published history, or exact player-authored material. Use the same complete C38 restored Covenant in every arm; do not splice its changed current situation into older C29 checkpoints. Preserve its existing world constraints, including verse and truthfulness bindings, without adding voice lexicons, character-specific examples, or speech prescriptions. A new coherent memory remains permitted. A biography need not be recited or confessed to demonstrate that it informed conduct.

The principal risks are observable and worth separating. First, an omniscient author can turn private background into proposed public conversation at the synopsis stage. It can also turn a private player wish into a request, a delegated activity into new speech, or a possible NPC response into an assumed player commitment. The final stage sees the same full information, so this remains a behavioral knowledge boundary, with no structural privacy guarantee. Review consequential knowledge against its source and timing in published fiction. Ordinary consistent invention remains allowed; a possible unshown relay should not be invented by a reviewer to excuse a demonstrated gap.

Second, abbreviated composition may create a sequence of topical slots which the author fills with the same prepared corrections. Removing exact dialogue from the first artifact does not remove semantic templates or the influence of published prose. Distinct topic labels, fewer participants, more biographical details, or a newly narrated anecdote followed by the same chorus would not establish improvement. Third, a synopsis can be too thin to support an experience: final fiction may merely expand a summary, suppress useful incidental interaction, or fill every planned transition with gestures. The final author needs freedom to develop and depart from it.

Finally, discarded synopsis content can survive as an unexplained premise. [C31's first-pair diagnosis](round31_first_pair_diagnosis.md) traces a four-minute reminder to a negotiation removed from the current draft; the [decision](../candidate_31/decision.json) retains that local missing-premise finding. The blind reviewer, without the draft, was appropriately less conclusive about its source. That is a concrete warning about the current intermediate artifact, separate from C31's manipulation of older drafts. A final passage can reference a proposed event that it never actually establishes. Fixing this risk means respecting published authority, not prohibiting all invented shared history.

Require the written comparison to identify what the people actually discuss or experience, what one participant takes up from another, and how that exchange becomes particular to these people and this occasion. A preference needs evidence of developed participation, meaningful selection of material, or a better experienced activity. It need not involve a changed political outcome or new factual information. Fewer words, fewer lines, fewer speakers, quieter tone, or fewer maxims can accompany success, but none is sufficient. A synopsis that yields a tidy summary instead of an experienced scene fails this test; so does rearranged banter that still makes everyone contribute to one comic performance. Do not impose equal word counts or a required scene length, which would add another intervention.

After the final-only judgments, trace each treatment separately. Weak concrete content in both synopsis and publication points to failure at preliminary composition. A substantive synopsis whose final drops the interaction or turns it into matching quips points to final composition. A good final that substantially ignores its synopsis is a possible practical success of the two-request setup, but weak evidence for the proposed synopsis mechanism. Record whether any rejected proposal supplied an unexplained final premise. An erroneous unpublished proposal that the final correctly discards is not itself a committed story violation, and a permissible new final detail is not a failure merely because it was absent from the synopsis.

This screen cannot establish sustained adaptive initiative, long-term biography preservation, or isolation of unpublished plans across turns. At this stage, each fresh author receives only its frozen published checkpoint; its own intermediate remains present for its second request. In a subsequent persistent proxy session, old synopses would remain in the author conversation unless the context is actually rebuilt. Calling them noncanonical does not remove that exposure. Any later substantial trial must state which retention policy it really uses and check that unperformed plans do not become history after a player declines or redirects an opportunity. A locally successful screen warrants the existing two substantial sessions with different player policies and held-out boundary checks. It does not select a runtime architecture or establish that synopsis-first authorship is generally better.
