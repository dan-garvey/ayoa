# Independent assessment of runs A–D

I read the review request, both authoritative briefs, and the complete review material. This assessment uses only those four permitted files. A and D take place at the Breakwater Picture House; B and C take place at Garvey House. References such as A1 and A2 mean the first and second dinner continuations, respectively; turn 0 is the opening.

**Dinner ranking: A narrowly ahead of D at the Picture House; B clearly ahead of C at Garvey House.** None of the four dinner experiences earns a 4 for prose/dialogue. All contain worthwhile material, but the recurrent, similarly composed rejoinders require material revision for the intended reader. The Picture House runs provide more particular exchanges about shared activities; B has the strongest substantive political conversation in the fantasy pair.

The scores assess the two connected dinner passages, not their introductions. A 3 means material revision is needed, a 4 means the dimension is retainable without material rewriting, and a 5 would mean exceptional. There is sufficient evidence to score each dimension; these scores make no claim about unobserved campaign performance.

## Openings, assessed separately

**A0: 4.** The prepared room, welcome sheet, cooking smells, and sounds through the staircase establish the situation clearly. Dev supplies his newly available test opportunity in audible conversation, and Nell supplies the existing booking; Rowan therefore has an actual route to the information he overhears. Nell's eventual invitation gives a simple way into dinner. The opening already contains the polished small-bowl exchange and a generalized paragraph about people who know the building, but its practical and sensory orientation is effective. Establishing the scheduling problem here does not, by itself, earn the subsequent dinner credit for developing it.

**B0: 4, with a local letter-timing ambiguity.** The unrelated great-nephew's letter and silent lightning make the inherited room feel occupied by a history Rowan has not made. Seraphel's visit provides an intelligible social invitation and route downstairs. The pedestal exchange previews the ensemble's excessively ready wit, but it does not yet dominate the opening. The description of the letter as regretting that the great-nephew “never came” deserves clarification against the brief's ordinary correspondence concerning a planned visit. The source does not supply the precise writing date, so I do not call this a demonstrated chronological impossibility. It also supplies no basis for reading the letter as supernatural knowledge. This issue begins in the opening and is not repeated at dinner.

**C0: 3.** The signet, bridge, serving smells, and Rashid's courtesy provide good introductory material. The extended introductions across different floors are much less convincing. Nearly everyone obtains a perfectly timed response about danger, fish, heraldry, bread, or dignity before Rowan reaches the dining hall. Thessaly's comparatively quiet contribution is also conveniently intelligible at a distance. The cumulative staging makes the cast sound assembled for an introductory routine. The weakness is the coordinated exchange, not the number of introduced characters.

**D0: 4, with a definite continuity repair needed in the booking explanation.** Nell's recognition that keys and a timetable were a thin welcome is particular and warm. Her boundary around tonight's work is useful to this newly hired lodger. Dev's disappointment and acceptance establish a real outcome without requiring a confrontation. However, Nell says Martin is “bringing the boards down specially”; the brief explicitly puts the six boards in the auditorium already, leaning against its side wall. The passage does not mark Nell's statement as a mistake. That is an apparent physical-continuity error, introduced here, rather than a reason the scheduling decision itself is invalid. The opening is otherwise retainable with a local correction.

## A: a meaningful negotiation inside a dinner, with too much polished commentary

**Prose/dialogue — 3.** The meal remains physically present: imperfect pie cutting, circulating dishes, a hand stopping over a bowl, and windows becoming reflective. The best dialogue depends on what somebody has just misunderstood. Martin's “It's only twenty minutes” meets Rosa's “It's twenty minutes in the middle of forty”; this is a difference in what the same interval costs them, not merely a clever reversal. But the scene repeatedly packages its own significance into jokes. Jo's meeting “adjourned by chickpeas” prompts Owen's trust-letterhead response; later Jo announces the cinema's survival of its first scheduling dispute. The quiet player's unanswered question generates another small routine about a census, a threatening town, and hospitality. These cumulative closures make ordinary talk sound prepared. The final projector story is summarized, so its purported ease cannot substitute for a fully rendered, more varied exchange.

**Character distinction — 4.** The central participants are differentiated by their actual responses, despite the shared verbal polish. Martin tries to accommodate Dev before understanding Rosa's recording constraint. Rosa initially resists, then asks what the test entails before deciding what overlap she can accept. Dev moves from defensive clarification to acknowledging his failure to check the diary and making a specific request. Nell impatiently lets management enter dinner while preserving the prior reservation. Jo objects to the meeting and keeps feeding people rather than assuming control of either project. Bea supplies a different kind of correction around historical confidence. Owen has less independent definition, but a short dinner need not distribute depth equally. The distinction in this central exchange is strong enough to retain while revising its delivery.

**Independent interests — 4.** Rosa wants usable photographs and an uncontaminated recording; Dev wants to use Hana's narrow availability; Martin wants his memories and observations to contribute; Jo wants supper to remain supper. These aims exist without Rowan's participation. The blue-board discussion also becomes particular: altered lettering and narrow R's turn Martin's confidence into material Rosa can actually use. Neither a generic welcome nor a request for the newcomer to fix matters replaces those interests.

**Narrative direction — 4.** A1 brings the already disclosed collision to the table, reveals why Martin's first suggestion does not solve it, and leaves room for an informed decision. A2 advances that same issue through concrete questions about sound and movement. Rosa authorizes a bounded overlap, and Nell undertakes to record it. The result changes the evening's plan without pretending either task is already finished. The stated arrangement is somewhat overexplained, and the move back to reminiscence is smoother than the preceding irritation warrants, but there is a connected change in understanding and action.

**Adaptive initiative — 4.** Jo does not invent an answer to her question about Lowhaven. Her “census” line is more conspicuous than necessary, but she releases the question and the table continues with its own business. Rowan passes dishes within the submitted action. NPCs investigate their competing requirements and make their own accommodation. The response neither assigns Rowan a managerial role nor waits for him to adjudicate the reservation.

**Continuity and knowledge.** The booking and Hana's availability reach the table through participants who possess them; the opening already supplies Rowan's access to this information. The proposed seven-to-twenty-past test is a new arrangement within Hana's stated availability, not a contradiction of Dev's earlier hope to begin at 19:05. Rosa's assent matters and is present. Actual testing remains unobserved. The newly supplied board painters and staff anecdotes are permissible ordinary history; the text does not disclose Owen's interview or Bea's restricted album. I find no demonstrated material privacy or player-ownership breach in A.

## B: political positions briefly become conversation, then yield to the ensemble routine

**Prose/dialogue — 3.** The movement from a long reading list to the Marrowgate petitions is the strongest fantasy exchange. Aldric invokes revisions, Rashid points to eighty years, and Ashara adds the demon comparison. They discuss something with competing interpretations. Nevertheless, both passages repeatedly end small subjects with a poised extra contribution: agreement costs more than carrots; ledgers omit reasons; an audience arrangement without a contract is dangerous. The food-to-customs exchange also recruits almost the whole table into successive miniature verdicts. Physical details such as Ysolde's chilled glass and Ashara avoiding the server with her tail help, but do not undo the prevailing cadence.

**Character distinction — 3.** Rashid's institutional grievance and Aldric's defense of procedure give their disagreement identifiable owners. Ashara can support Rashid's comparison without becoming deferential to him. Ysolde's attention to the storm is initially distinct. The surrounding exchanges are less successful: Ysolde becomes a literal-minded commentator on chairs, Thessaly repeatedly supplies contractual or semantic asides, and Seraphel frequently offers a little thematic conclusion. Caelindra's dislike of the hour, page count, and choir becomes a series of elegant objections. The cast's underlying positions are available, but material revision is needed to stop so many contributions arriving in the same composed register.

**Independent interests — 4.** The beastkin levy and demon tariffs provide interests beyond pleasing or testing Rowan. Ysolde brings up a weather and bridge concern and states that she previously warned the wardens; this is a plausible extension of her expertise and attention. Ashara and Rashid retain their paired practice, and their familiar competitiveness does not give Rowan priority over their relationship. Seraphel's choir story introduces an ordinary shared experience, although its rendering is too compressed to show much of her particular storytelling. None of these interests requires disclosing the hidden conspiracy.

**Narrative direction — 3.** B2 deepens the content of the assigned-reading discussion, but the larger sequence repeats B1's pattern: curriculum, witty disagreement, plans for drills. The chair-and-railing exchange hints at spectators without producing a particular invitation or decision. The choir anecdote is largely reported, and the ending returns to the clock and the same practice plan. A quiet meal can be worth keeping without an external event; here, the social material repeatedly stops at a neatly stated position instead of letting one exchange develop enough to shape the evening further.

**Adaptive initiative — 4.** Rashid does not repeat the unanswered primer question or supply Rowan's response. An arriving dish provides a plausible change of attention. The NPCs then sustain a substantive argument and familiar social talk themselves, while Rowan's participation remains within eating and passing food. The question's disappearance is socially plausible, and no new confrontation is owed merely because the player stays quiet.

**Continuity and knowledge.** Ysolde's warning has an explicitly stated first-person source; it is not an unexplained acquisition of a secret. The petitions, tariffs, and choir incident are new history compatible with the supplied setting. Seraphel's quoted contributions can be read as short unrhymed verse; the summarized choir story does not expose its exact wording, so it is insufficient evidence for a binding violation. There is a small staging ambiguity when seven places are described as occupied while Seraphel is still accompanying Rowan: six other named residents would then be seated. That is local scene accounting, not a substantive information breach.

Rashid's earlier cards promise remains in force. Dinner has not ended, so his repeated confirmation of drills does not yet establish a missed appointment, a resolved double booking, or a broken promise to Dena. The absence of that issue from these few minutes is not itself a contract failure.

## C: recognizable roles repeatedly become an audience for one another's rejoinders

**Prose/dialogue — 3.** The sensory meal is competently rendered, and Caelindra's unplanned insult about human lifetimes briefly offers actual discomfort. But a chorus immediately annotates the offense, and her qualified withdrawal closes it. At the next turn, Rowan's silence similarly produces several successive judgments before the table moves into another routine about fish, rhetoric, brevity, and armed faculty. Seraphel's “Salt, bread, and fish. / A treaty has begun” in C1 is echoed by “Bread, bandages, salt. / Civilization continues” in C2. This is repetition within one connected dinner, not a penalty for resemblance to another run. The difficulty is the recurrent conversion of an exchange into a maxim and collective laugh.

**Character distinction — 3.** Ashara's question about whether Rowan chose his position is appropriate to her objection to inherited authority. Aldric's practical explanation for carrying bandages is a welcome, concrete expression of his concern for others. Those moments coexist with a much less particular ensemble voice. Thessaly's “Can a question remain somewhere?” treats ordinary conversational phrasing as a puzzle, against the brief's explicit socially experienced character. Her other semantic adjudications compound this tendency. Ysolde's hour-versus-meal distinction and rhetoric-faculty joke have little of the specificity of an experienced dragon's attention. Rashid and Ashara are recognizable competitors, but often through interchangeable elegant sparring rather than the particular pressures each is managing. This is a characterization problem, not evidence that the fae has lied or that the dragon cannot understand language.

**Independent interests — 3.** Ashara's preparation and Rashid's involvement in it remain real interests. Caelindra actually reads and annotates a book; Aldric carries supplies for other students. These are useful actions beyond attending to Rowan. Most receive only enough space to furnish the next reaction, however. Caelindra's reading becomes a human-lifespan joke, and the practical care behind the bandages becomes another efficiency joke followed by the civilization refrain. The dinner needs more sustained use of these interests, not a recital of additional biography or the compulsory revelation of a secret.

**Narrative direction — 3.** The inheritance question creates a possible point of social friction, and it is legitimate to leave it unanswered. Afterward, however, the passage substitutes a series of interchangeable conversational starts. The conclusion repeats “The octagon after dinner,” “I remember,” and the half-hour exchange already used in C1, with little observable change in the arrangement. The meal progresses through courses, but its central relationship material mostly circles the same competitive reassurance. As with B, the cards arrangement need not become tonight's required plot event; its continued absence does mean the repeated drill reminders cannot be credited as a demonstrated handling of Rashid's competing commitments.

**Adaptive initiative — 3.** Rowan's silence remains his own: no speech, thought, manuscript, or consequential voluntary choice is invented. Ashara does not demand an answer again. Yet the response turns that silence into a conspicuous discussion involving Aldric, Ashara, Rashid, Thessaly, Seraphel, and Caelindra. That could be socially awkward in a legitimate way, but here its symmetrical sequence of wise and comic responses resembles a prepared defense of quietness. Later conversation proceeds without Rowan, but the repeated exchanges limit how much new initiative it demonstrates. The necessary revision is in the social response and continued activity, not in making the player answer.

**Continuity and ambiguity.** C1 puts Rowan midway down the right side, Rashid beside him, and Ashara at the far end. C2 later has Rashid say he is “directly across” from Ashara. With no reseating described, that is an apparent local seating inconsistency introduced during the second continuation. It is not inherited from the authoritative brief, which did not assign these chairs.

Rashid's claim “I have never lost to you,” followed by the exchange about two ties, sits uneasily beside his established position trailing Ashara in combat. The brief does not specify a complete head-to-head match record, so I do not label it a proven impossible result. It is an unresolved implication that should be checked, rather than rescued by inventing unseen match categories. B's acknowledgement that Ashara wins avoids this ambiguity. C supplies no demonstrated conspiracy leak or unauthorized player action.

## D: practical companionship and actual departures, but an overready cast

**Prose/dialogue — 3.** The board discussions have concrete rewards. A disputed year becomes a distinction between the original work and later lettering; Martin names Alan and identifies different contributions. The blue-border discussion similarly turns disagreement toward paint, billing changes, and visible differences. Those exchanges make the work interesting in its own right. Unfortunately, introductions are almost uniformly miniature comic résumés, and later dialogue keeps producing finished buttons: payroll numbers with moral opinions, useful-and-sinister advice, the sign joke that Rosa must correct, and deciding only after photographing. The repeated rhythm matters more than the presence of any individual joke. The food and shared sweets provide welcome physical company that the verbal performance repeatedly interrupts.

**Character distinction — 3.** Martin's remembered labor and pride differ from Rosa's concern with reliable attribution; her change from teasing to attention when he identifies Alan is good reciprocal behavior. Bea taking a sweet without ceremony suggests an existing companionship. Jo's refusal to expand the menu suits her ownership of the concession and protection of her time. But Nell, Bea, Rosa, Owen, Dev, and Jo too often share the same instant corrective poise. Their jobs distinguish the subject of their remarks more reliably than the manner or development of their participation. There is material to preserve, but the group needs more variation than a round of occupational quips supplies.

**Independent interests — 4.** Jo protects an already agreed short menu, Martin wants his work and colleagues recognized, Rosa pursues accurate exhibition material, and Dev returns to technical work after accepting that tonight's test opportunity is unavailable. Their activities do not wait for Rowan to supply a plot or make a workplace decision. Dev is allowed a disappointment, and neither Owen's interview nor Bea's album is gratuitously exposed to manufacture another problem. Those private matters remaining private is compatible with an ordinary dinner.

**Narrative direction — 4.** The conversation provides particular information about the boards and then leads into Rosa and Martin preparing for their booked work. Dev's departure also follows his own purpose. The room actually changes as three chairs empty, leaving a different possible social space. This is sufficient direction for the requested interval; the test cancellation did not have to become an argument, and the meal did not have to unveil a larger crisis. The continued chains of banter make the route less convincing than its underlying sequence, but that sequence is retainable.

**Adaptive initiative — 4.** The group continues eating and talking during the delegated quarter hour and then leaves for activities it already owns. Rowan passes food and water without acquiring invented opinions, dialogue, or duties. The closing remark to Martin in D1 does not create a demand for Rowan to answer. D2's renewed board discussion and eventual movement into the auditorium fit that continuation. Beginning setup before the seven o'clock reservation is not inherently a violation when no competing use has been established.

**Inherited and new continuity.** The opening's claim that the boards must be brought down is the clear source discrepancy. D1's reference to moving the boards is compatible with that mistaken premise, but could also mean moving them from their existing place along the auditorium wall; it should not be counted as a second independent relocation error. The final audible movement into position is consistent with ordinary setup and does not explicitly repair the earlier statement. The new named painters, altered lettering, and mixed paint are otherwise compatible with the brief. Dev announces a deferred test rather than obtaining an unbooked room by fiat. No loan permission is broadened, and no unseen relay of Owen's interview is needed.

## Comparison and limits

**A over D is a narrow preference about the connected dinner.** A lets several people misunderstand, clarify, and accommodate an actual constraint. Its strongest distinctions arise from how the speakers change their participation, not merely their assigned occupations. D has the better concrete sense of a meal ending and contains enjoyable, particular discussion of working objects. It also spends more of the dinner in readily interchangeable joke-and-correction pairs. A's advantage is not that a scheduling dispute is mandatory; it is that the rendered exchange gives more room for differing priorities to affect one another.

**B over C is clearer.** B's dispute about the petitions has a subject and opposed interpretations, and Ysolde's storm observation originates in a particular competence. C more often turns possible subjects into occasions for everybody to comment. Its two food-and-civilization verse summaries, its repeated practice arrangement, and its ensemble handling of Rowan's silence are cumulative weaknesses across the same dinner. The bandages and book are promising, but do not compensate for how quickly they become setups.

The Picture House examples more often connect talk to shared objects, limited time, and particular work; the Garvey House examples more often connect it to broad statements about rank, customs, institutions, and other people's eloquence. B shows that the latter can still become a substantive exchange. D shows that concrete work does not by itself prevent generic banter. This is a contrast between these supplied experiences, not an isolated causal explanation for their quality.

The strongest revision priority across all four is the ownership and development of exchanges: allow a practical concern, a remembered detail, an irritation, or a pleasure to matter beyond the next polished response. That does not require fewer speakers, an absence of wit, an enforced confrontation, or a predetermined resolution. Local continuity corrections should remain separate from that larger literary task. These three-passage runs establish neither long-term campaign quality nor success with a different player policy.
