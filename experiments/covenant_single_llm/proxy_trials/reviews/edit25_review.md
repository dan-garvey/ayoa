**The game revision makes a material dialogue improvement, with a smaller improvement to the scene as a whole.** See the [game packet](/home/dan/ayoa-worktrees/covenant-single-llm/experiments/covenant_single_llm/proxy_trials/candidate_25/game_edit/packet.txt:603) and [game revision](/home/dan/ayoa-worktrees/covenant-single-llm/experiments/covenant_single_llm/proxy_trials/candidate_25/game_edit/00.story.md:1).

The full exchange matters here. Rowan initially declines Thessaly's invitation, spends the following days attending classes and practicing, then accepts a later invitation. He reads the rules, explicitly asks about movement distance and entry circles, and submits an entire game using shortest open routes with correction of illegal moves. This is an occasion for a knowledgeable companion to play, correct, compete, and talk. It is not an occasion that requires her to pronounce on his character or on people in general.

The original repeatedly turns small game events into such pronouncements. After a legal placement, it adds “The road does not care which one you prefer.” After the first successful route, it adds “Most people see a gate and begin arranging a grand arrival.” The revision answers those moments with “That fits” and “That was quick” and moves on. Those replacements work across the exchange: they lower the pressure on each ordinary move to reveal a philosophy. The simple reactions are adequate because the board, competition, and longer conversation already supply activity and interest.

The most effective deletion comes after Thessaly notices an opportunity Rowan did not use to obstruct her. The original interrupts the game with “The board between you has no answer to give,” then diagnoses other players' motives through a speech about calling mutual obstruction strategy. The revision identifies the particular alternative: “The tile before this could have turned my lantern south.” She then moves. It preserves her tactical awareness and willingness to comment without making Rowan's unaggressive play a moral distinction. Similarly, the illegal-move correction gives the relevant restriction and stops; the original's additional “Lanterns block light” is unnecessary.

This leaves a recognizable person. Thessaly still plays to win, sometimes lengthens Rowan's route, knows older versions of the game, notices its artwork, recalls a tutor, and offers another evening together. Removing her generalized commentary does not remove the knowledge, tastes, and social initiative that make this exchange hers. “I liked playing with you” is an ordinary expression of enjoyment after a completed shared activity. It does not confer political trust, romantic interest, or disproportionate esteem.

The revised scene remains limited. Most of its physical progression and atmosphere come from the draft: road placements, the apricot dish, the quieting common room, the final tile draw. Removing interruptions improves its rhythm, but the editing has not independently produced a much richer scene. The tutor anecdote is still especially thin. “A child beat him six times with straight roads” reports a potentially amusing occurrence without showing what made it memorable. Changing the tutor's claim from a lesson about patience to an accusation of bad planning is permitted here; it is not a retcon. The weakness is that the new anecdote still has little event or interaction inside it. It also comes immediately after the account of the two sets' artwork, so the conversation feels somewhat like a collection of available game facts.

The ending retains a small residue of the behavior-assessment pattern: “You asked when you weren't certain” precedes Thessaly's statement of enjoyment. This is milder and better grounded than the draft's “You kept asking what the rules allowed before you made the map worse.” It is also factually supported: Rowan really asked the movement and entry questions in the preceding player turn. It should not be counted as newly invented player speech. Nonetheless, the pleasant evening still ends with Thessaly explicitly approving a feature of his conduct. The operation has reduced this habit more than it has eliminated it.

The game preserves the material result and player scope. Rowan completes the game, uses short routes, receives a correction at an occupied road, and loses a close finish after both players have returned three lanterns. Thessaly's fourth reaches its gate while Rowan remains a turn away. The Thursday-after-dinner invitation remains without stakes, and the revision does not accept it for Rowan. It invents no new player dialogue, private feeling, or consequential commitment. The tutor recollection and attribution of toothless artwork to the Academy copy are permissible background elaboration within an unreleased passage; they do not change a prior player-visible event or a playable rule.

There is one inherited practical clarity weakness: the first returned blue lantern is placed with the three still waiting to enter, even though successful returns must be counted separately. Both texts subsequently track three returns and the fourth correctly, so this does not change the winner. It remains an awkward visual representation of game state. The narrative also compresses turns rather than providing enough board geometry to independently verify every move. The evidence supports preservation of the established movement and blocking rules and the stated outcome, not a fully audited game simulation.

**The Stone revision materially improves the library and receipt exchanges, while the central Stone scene is a modest refinement.** See the [Stone packet](/home/dan/ayoa-worktrees/covenant-single-llm/experiments/covenant_single_llm/proxy_trials/candidate_25/stone_edit/packet.txt:487) and [Stone revision](/home/dan/ayoa-worktrees/covenant-single-llm/experiments/covenant_single_llm/proxy_trials/candidate_25/stone_edit/00.story.md:1).

Rowan has explained that he lost his parents young, asked whether the letter connects to his inheritance, and said the aunt who sends pears already gives him something to picture. Stone has promised an overnight index check. Separately, Rowan has read and signed Rashid's actual petition. The submitted turn provisionally marks two courses, attends the library introduction, returns for dinner, and keeps the Stone appointment. Both the practical petition details and the modest emotional importance of ordinary family information are therefore established reasons for the dialogue.

Caelindra's change is effective. The original's definition of what counts as a translation, accompanied by narration announcing her satisfaction at accurate criticism, becomes “The merchant copy leaves out the amendments.” She points to the record and adds the omitted people. The librarian then explains why the versions are kept together and how to compare them. This is still instruction, but instruction belongs in a library introduction. It directly serves Rowan's declared interest in records and the students' use of sources. The narrator no longer has to interpret Caelindra's motive for us. Her later preference for Records follows her interest in the actual document instead of producing a new general claim about people who profit from misunderstanding.

Ashara's receipt exchange is the other strong improvement. “Clerks forget less readily when paper remembers for them” becomes “If their reply leaves out a piece, take both pages to the same clerk.” She also asks for, reads, and returns the receipt. The advice has an object, a possible problem, and a usable response. Her concern for precise commitments appears through something she does. Rashid's reference to “the incident findings” also fits the previously signed request for a short safety account without naming the injured student more closely than the draft's shorthand “incident report.” Neither version delivers that report or establishes the cause of the accident.

The dinner still sounds overarranged. Rashid announces the filing; Ashara verifies it; Aldric supplies a procedural assessment; Rashid confirms the hope; Seraphel offers an image; Thessaly supplies the final implication. The two latter contributions still stack neatly: “The door is already closed” is followed by “Otherwise it is a closure with a seal on it.” Thessaly's request for a review date is pertinent, but the closing metaphor preserves some of the polished-response pattern the revision is meant to loosen. Aldric's “addressed the petition as filed” is particularly administrative speech for dinner. Formality can fit these students, yet their different personalities still converge on orderly commentary about the same point. Adding visible seating and a shared bread basket helps stage the scene without substantially changing that conversational shape.

Stone's factual explanation was already answering Rowan's request. The revision makes it cleaner and removes the unnecessary comparison to worse ancestors at the close. Its concrete “pears, rain, and missing papers” fits the actual letter. The cleaned glass, prepared chair, overnight research, and copied family connections still convey attentive care without a narrator explaining their emotional significance. That is an important retention given Rowan's earlier account of his parents and wish for family connection.

However, “The letter is clear on that much” makes the closing slightly more clinical, and the body remains a sequence of compact explanations punctuated by handling papers. This is a limited stylistic gain, not a newly absorbing encounter. Removing the draft's consoling observation is allowed and need not be scored as lost canon. It simply does not automatically make the remaining exchange warmer or more particular. Stone also promises improved dates without either passage actually supplying dates; that small informational mismatch is inherited.

The material facts and boundaries survive. Course marks remain provisional. The open-stack layout, midnight table use, chancellor's signature requirement, noncasting resources, source checks, and map/route details remain available. The petition has been logged and a written response is due tomorrow evening; reopening is not granted. Seraphel continues speaking in short verse, and no one discloses the injured student's private identity or hidden conspiracy knowledge.

Stone preserves Tomas as Lenora's younger brother, Jude as Tomas's eldest son and Rowan's grandfather, and Willan as Elspeth's son from the other branch. Willan remains a municipal records clerk with a forwarding address and no further household entry. The Garvey trust's earlier placement of the house in Academy use, the mixed origins of its contents, and the letter's presence last term are retained. Rowan can keep the frame and copy; municipal research remains a conditional offer that may take time and yield only another address. It is not commissioned on his behalf. The papers' failure to document whether the visit occurred is also preserved. Stone earlier said the visit did not occur; the record's silence need not overturn his memory, though both versions could make that distinction clearer. This is an inherited ambiguity, not new evidence that the revision changed the visit.

**These results warrant a bounded transfer test with the rich backgrounds intact and common instructions.** The useful operations are concrete and portable: let an immediate answer end; remove an appended generalization about people or the protagonist; anchor an observation in an available object or event; remove narration that explains an already intelligible remark or gesture; let a joke pass without requiring another polished rejoinder. The strongest examples here preserve character through attention, action, interests, and relationships while cutting surplus judgment. None requires a character-specific dialogue sample, a reduced biography, or copying these characters' lines into another prompt.

The transfer test should judge whether ordinary sole-author generation can both invent the scene and apply those operations while retaining practical information and player ownership. It should also check for the failure mode already visible here: fewer maxims but bland acknowledgments, administrative conversation, or anecdotes reduced to summaries. Full exchanges should determine whether warmth and distinction survive, whether a practical question receives an adequate answer, and whether reported player conduct really occurred.

This evidence is two edits of completed passages, each with the desired sequence and outcome already supplied. There is no repeated sample, no isolation of the individual editing instructions, and no subsequent interactive continuation in these fixtures. The revisions demonstrate that substantial unwanted commentary can be removed without destroying these scenes' factual contracts. They do not establish that the same writer can reliably produce engaging first-draft dialogue, preserve these gains over multiple turns, or satisfy a live session without the supplied draft.
