Three conclusions follow from the pairs. First, the characteristic chorus is already present in both synopsis selections before dialogue is written. A no-dialogue preliminary is not automatically free of the abstraction or role allocation that later becomes repartee. Second, the author can make substantial new decisions during final composition: Prose 1 and Synopsis 2 prove that prospective choices were not universally treated as binding. Third, available concrete material is neither absent nor reliably developed: Prose 2 loses a personal anecdote; Synopsis 1 gains an incident but withholds its interesting particulars; Prose 1 supplies historical disagreement and then moves past it. These observations locate failures in selection, uptake, and rendering. They do not identify which prompt sentence causes them.

The strongest positive example is Prose 2's weather-to-rules exchange: information from one person changes what another needs to discuss. That supports a description of successful interaction, not yet a new prompting mechanism. The existing generic instruction already asks for speech appropriate to this listener and moment; earlier scene-selection and revision screens also addressed development. Rephrasing that aspiration, banning one more rhetorical form, demanding one central topic, or forcing the synopsis into another checklist would need evidence that it changes this behavior rather than another name for the desired result. None is supplied by C39. Likewise, another call, mandatory preservation of a draft, or an approved external event plan is not justified by discovering that one final copied heavily: the other prose final already revises substantially without reaching the literary bar.

A second context would investigate **context sensitivity**, which the repeated Covenant dinner comparisons cannot establish. The concrete next artifact should be a suitability comparison of an existing, authorized second brief against Covenant: comparable depth of formative history, tastes, current goals, private knowledge, and relationships among NPCs; a similarly sized active ensemble; and a quiet shared activity that permits independent business without forcing the player to mediate. Match richness substantively, not by padding to a token count. Do not write replacement biographies or character-specific speech examples to make that setting perform well.

If that comparison supports a diagnostic screen, keep the author model, effort, generic instructions, one-pass delivery, player ownership, and review bar fixed. Use fresh starts in **both** contexts with comparable observant player scope, rather than comparing a clean second setting to Covenant's repeatedly replayed, already-polished B00–02. Preserve the original Covenant run unchanged. A small prespecified set of repeats and the next social continuation would measure whether the pattern recurs; it would not qualify either setting as a successful substantial session. Freeze which differences in premise, social roles, magical constraints, and prior history remain unmatched before seeing outputs.

The outcomes would discriminate useful possibilities. If both matched contexts reproduce the same shared prepared-verdict pattern, a Covenant-only explanation becomes weaker; that still does not identify a model ceiling or isolate the proxy's surrounding instructions. If the second context produces retainable, differentiated interaction while Covenant does not, rich biographies are compatible with better dialogue and source-context dependence becomes more plausible. That result would not isolate prestige, fantasy, ensemble obligations, verse, or inherited dialogue as the cause, and passing another setting would not repair Covenant. If both improve on fresh starts, inherited route/history becomes a live question rather than evidence that a new setting solved it. Mixed repeats would supply no stable context effect.

This investigation is more informative than selecting another unsupported Covenant wording change. It is a bounded recommendation for what uncertainty to resolve next, not authorization, a selected intervention, or a reduced success criterion.
