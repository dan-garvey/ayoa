# First independent anonymous assessment

I read the complete review request, covenant, and review material in untruncated chunks. This assessment uses only those authorized files. I did not inspect a condition key, other trial artifacts, private reasoning, or another review, and did not call another model. The openings are mutually exclusive first scenes; the dinner continuations are mutually exclusive responses to the complete published dinner conversation. I have not treated similarities between alternatives as repetition within a story.

Scores describe the observed passages: **3** means a material weakness needs revision, **4** means retainable without material rewriting, and **5** means exceptional. Minor local corrections can coexist with a 4. N/O means the sample does not support the judgment; it is not a low score. Boundary and continuity findings are also reported separately so that an isolated repair does not become an unsupported judgment about an entire character or campaign.

References below are to `candidate_36/review_material.txt` and `candidate_36/covenant.txt`.

## Boundary, continuity, and source audit

1. **DINNER-1: definite new knowledge-source gap, locally repairable.** At material line 466, Ashara says Rashid gave his word "yesterday." The date is supplied privately in the covenant's current business, while the covenant explicitly says Ashara does not know the earlier promise. In the passage she hears that he arranged cards for the first half-hour and that the others made plans around it; nobody states the day on which he promised. Earlier published dialogue supplies no such information either. She can reasonably conclude that he double-booked himself, but cannot assert that exact date as established knowledge. No unseen conversation should be invented to rescue it. Repair the unsupported specificity while preserving the competing commitments.

2. **OPENING-1: definite small viewpoint overreach.** Material line 62 states that the destination table is set for seventeen while Rowan remains in the bedroom and Seraphel has spoken through the door. The route and sounds can be available; the exact unseen place setting is not. This is ordinary setup information from the covenant, not a leaked conspiracy secret, but it still exceeds the stipulated perceptual viewpoint. Keep that detail for an observation that supports it.

3. **DINNER-4: definite local causal/reference defect in the anecdote.** Material line 838 makes the chapel's dawn bell the alarm, then explains the student's failure to wake by saying he put "the alarm" under three pillows. The account switches between a communal bell and a movable bedside object without establishing another object or mechanism. The later joke does not repair that missing relation. I do not assume an unseen enchanted device. This needs a coherent account, but is not a failure of the campaign's concealed history or of Rowan's agency.

4. **Seraphel's verse: distinguish ambiguity from an established new breach.** DINNER-4's run-in "You make that sound worth seeing" at line 826 is less clearly presented as verse than her other contributions. A short, rhythmically plausible line can nevertheless be unrhymed verse; formatting alone does not establish a material binding failure here. The published source already includes Seraphel's extended run-in speech about the occupied places, so that inherited inconsistency must not be newly charged to every continuation. Her new multiline contributions in the alternatives do not disclose the forbidden history.

5. **Unassigned door movements are ambiguities, not demonstrated consequential choices for Rowan.** OPENING-2 ends with a partly open door; OPENING-3 and OPENING-4 say the door opens without naming its mover. A clearer transition would help, but none explicitly makes Rowan accept company or go downstairs. I do not promote this staging ambiguity into a material player-control violation. Similarly, OPENING-3's view toward the stair's foot depends on the unelaborated layout; the text does not establish an impossible sightline strongly enough to call another definite failure.

6. **Dinner actions are covered by the submission.** Breaking and eating bread, settling into the meal, and the brief smiles in DINNER-1 and DINNER-2 enact the activity and amused participation Rowan expressly submitted. DINNER-4 repeats his submitted line rather than inventing a new utterance. None of the alternatives assigns him fresh manuscript text, a consequential invitation acceptance, or a private commitment to another character.

7. **Do not confuse reasonable inference with secret knowledge.** In DINNER-2, Ashara's accusation that Rashid did not tell his friends is immediately answered by him, and her conclusion that they arranged their evening around a promised visit follows from the situation. This differs from DINNER-1's unsupported exact date. Dena herself can say that she does not want a practice-group meeting; that is not another NPC leaking her private refusal to join a public campaign. No candidate reveals that refusal through an uninformed speaker.

8. **No firm contradiction from Ysolde knowing desks "since last term."** DINNER-1 adds previous familiarity with the Academy. The source does not sufficiently establish that Conclave first-years could never have been familiar with the premises earlier, so this is at most a chronology question rather than a proved failure. I have not invented a particular prior enrollment or visit to explain it.

The usable elements are the ordinary invitation, shared food, particular weather interest, practical kindness, and independently arranged evenings. The main revisions concern repeated dialogue habits, with the three definite local corrections identified above. No backstory removal is needed. These short passages do not support conclusions about eventual romance, political alignment, an ensemble's long-term depth, or sustained adaptation over a campaign.
