**Session A provides a worthwhile practice scene, but its supporting dialogue needs more variety and its final game cannot sustain its own stated outcome.**

1. **Prose/dialogue — 3/5.** The opening makes the residence immediately usable: storm beyond the window, butter and voices below, a knock, and clear directions to dinner. C3 is the strongest sustained passage. Repeated drills actually change: Rashid moves out of a straight retreat, reaches Ashara's neck, lands an arm touch, then meets her adjustment to his successful counter. Watching gives the player something to follow. Against that, too much conversation uses the same construction of assertion, exacting correction, and answering maxim. C2's discussion of plays moves through speaking, silence, reasons, contracts, and old contracts with nearly everyone supplying a polished turn of the same conversational mechanism. C3 sometimes explains a demonstrated lesson again through another exchange about weakness or standards. The problem is cumulative sameness, not the presence of wit, a particular metaphor, or the length of a scene the player expressly chose to watch. The physical practice can largely be retained; the ensemble's conversational rhythm needs material variation.

2. **Character distinction — 3/5.** Ashara and Rashid are more successfully distinguished by behavior than the dinner ensemble is by speech. She sets the practice conditions and adapts pressure; he defends his competence, learns, and tries to maintain incompatible appointments. Passing her the larger piece of bread in C2 gives their prior relationship a useful quiet detail. Ysolde's concern about windows in the replacement classroom is also specific, and Caelindra's observation about short plays does not become instant admiration for Rowan. However, Thessaly repeatedly defaults to the truth/terms/contracts interpreter: dinner was promised and exists, unstated terms cause trouble, a reason is not a contract, and written refusals might avoid rhetoric. The Covenant expressly gives her ordinary social competence and says she does not interpret the table's conversation for everyone. Her actual interest in games offers a better basis for distinction than this recurring mannerism. Aldric and Seraphel mostly contribute decorum and epigrammatic punctuation. These are recognizable outlines, but several supporting voices still need material rewriting to become people with different conversational habits.

3. **Independent interests — 4/5.** The current business produces actual friction without making Rowan its adjudicator. In C2 Dena identifies the earlier promise as the first half-hour; Ashara offers to find another partner; Rashid nevertheless chooses the drills. Dena's accommodation is reasonable and does not erase her claim: in C4 she refuses his joke that he must already be losing and calls his absence a different failure. Her friends have played while he was elsewhere. Ashara and Rashid spend C3 training for their own purposes, with Rowan outside the boundary. Nobody transfers the practice problem, Dena's injured wrist, or a reform campaign onto the newcomer. The limitation is depth: the two card friends remain largely interchangeable, and one evening cannot demonstrate durable competing loyalties. Within this scope, the NPCs demonstrably have an evening beyond pleasing Rowan.

4. **Direction — 3/5.** The sequence has concrete progress: dinner finishes; the invitation conflict occurs; the player chooses practice; a full half-hour is rendered through changing drills and the half-hour bell; Rashid returns; the player then joins the card table. This is an important positive counterexample to stories that repeatedly announce an activity without delivering it. The failure arrives at the requested complete hand. The story closes the hand and marks a result, but the cards and winning condition do not establish a coherent result. This is material because the player's final activity is learning and playing the game, not merely listening to card-themed dialogue.

5. **Adaptive initiative — 3/5.** C2 lets a quiet player hear other people's conversation. C3 honors the express delegation of the whole practice interval. C4 follows Rowan's change from spectator to prospective card player and pauses for the first move after supplying rules. C5 answers the player's sensible question about making two banners from five cards with a compatible new refill rule; adding that missing specification is not itself a contradiction. It then completes a hand without inventing new Rowan dialogue, and keeps his place clear for the next deal, honoring his refusal to commit to another hand. Those are substantial successes. However, the subsequent teaching and resolution fail to respond correctly to the cards the story itself supplies. The local autonomy is useful, but the actual learning experience needs material repair.

The consequential A continuity findings are concentrated in C4–C5:

- **The story misses a complete second banner while presenting correction as helpful.** C4 defines a banner as either three of a number or three consecutive cards of one suit, and two banners win. After the river 4–5–6 banner, Rowan retains tower 3 and lantern 3. C5 refills with stone 8, tower 4, and flower 9, then explicitly draws tower 2. Tower 2–3–4 is therefore a complete second banner. Dena calls this a “pair of towers,” Rashid recommends waiting, and the narrator calls their advice a “correction” that gives the next turns clearer shape (A transcript lines 527–535). NPCs are allowed to give mistaken advice, and the player need not play optimally. The defect is that the passage treats the advice as correct instruction without acknowledging the already-complete winning pattern. Nothing in the supplied rules requires waiting for this banner.
- **The eventual terminal result lacks a valid resolution.** Rowan later takes bird 3 and explicitly lays his second banner. Dena acknowledges the two; the other student raises whether Dena finished first. The narrator then says Dena “has not shown a banner yet,” gives her another draw, and places her newly displayed lanterns beside three flowers that have supposedly been waiting faceup as the other part of her two banners (lines 541–553). No final-response round or alternate winning priority has been supplied. The display history is also inconsistent as presented; treating the earlier flowers as merely loose, undeclared cards would still not establish that Dena finished first. The joking conversation about Dena changing rules in C4 does not announce an actual amendment. The precise observed outcome is a completed, scored hand with competing two-banner claims and an implication that Dena outplayed Rowan; the transcript never supplies an unambiguous, legally explained winner. It would overstate the evidence to report a clearly adjudicated Dena victory here.
- **Some missing detail is not a separate contradiction.** “Three matching towers” does not supply their ranks, so it could describe a legal sequence. The game never establishes a prohibition on showing a learning hand; the visible loose cards and Rashid's handling of one are not sufficient evidence of illicit NPC knowledge. I do not require Banners to follow an external rummy game or infer unstated rules to rescue its ending.

A also has smaller focalization and presentation problems. C0 says the new clothes are tailored “too exact to feel like yours,” supplying an unsubmitted private response to them. C2 asserts the warmth Ashara's fingers leave on a spoon without establishing Rowan's contact with it or a visible effect. These are local player-POV lapses, not a leak of conspiracy knowledge. As exported, several Seraphel contributions contain literal `\n+` sequences; that disrupts the passage's presentation, though these files alone do not identify which stage introduced the formatting. Her isolated short utterances are not automatically binding violations: the Covenant allows unrhymed verse.

**Session B has the stronger social writing and a more developed card-table ensemble. Its game conclusions nevertheless require material revision.**

1. **Prose/dialogue — 4/5.** The letter about a visit and second pudding gives the opening an ordinary human particularity. The meal has pleasant rhythms of serving, disagreement, food, and pauses. C3's weather exchange becomes more than another aphorism when Ysolde describes bridge vibration and the moving cloud shelf; Seraphel's mistaken chapel visit is an ordinary embarrassment with an ordinary pleasure attached to it, since the hymn was good. C4 gives Pell's competitive disappointment an enjoyable little life: “It is cardboard” receives “It knows what it did.” That exchange works because the scene has given him something to lose. There remains an excess of polished replies, and the closing patience-versus-losing maxim in C5 is less particular than the best card banter. Nevertheless, substantial stretches are worth experiencing for their conversation and can be retained at the prose level. This score does not validate the moves that supposedly cause the card-table payoffs.

2. **Character distinction — 4/5.** Ysolde's weather absorption, Caelindra's institutional contempt, Aldric's defense of the maintained peace, and Ashara and Rashid's familiar competitive argument give the ensemble distinct centers. C1's disagreement over the empty Council seat has substance before the table moves on. Dena, Pell, and Maera acquire recognizable relationships in C4–C5: Dena plays to win, Pell dignifies his own reverses, and Maera enjoys exposing his pretense. Thessaly's interest in the classification and effects of keys belongs to an actual interest in games. The limitation is C2's round of polished counsel to Rowan, where nearly everyone briefly becomes a considerate aphorist. This does not prove that Ashara has abandoned her objection to automatic inheritance or that Rashid's political grievance has been solved; practical accommodation is compatible with both. Seraphel's opening also needs the verse presentation repair discussed below.

3. **Independent interests — 4/5.** The NPCs complain about Academy infrastructure, discuss weather, remember a chapel mishap, and prepare for a bout without asking Rowan to manufacture subjects. In C3 Rashid's double booking becomes visible to Ashara and Dena through an actual encounter. Dena expressly wants cards instead of calendar management, accepts that the others will begin without Rashid, and proceeds to do exactly that. Her accommodation is consistent with wanting a pleasant evening; ongoing resentment is not a required outcome. The later game gives her friends attention and stakes of their own. Dena is declared the winner of both hands, although the mechanics do not justify those declarations. These excerpts do not show Rashid returning or fulfilling his revised promise, so I credit the continued card gathering and the departure for practice, not eventual completion of every appointment.

4. **Direction — 3/5.** Dinner progresses through the player's quarter-hour of listening, dessert, and the practice/card split. C4 completes the hand Rowan asks to watch; C5 completes the hand he agrees to play. A quiet participant receives unfolding events and can join when ready. However, the meaning of a crossing, key effects, the decisive tricks, and the final allocation of wins are insufficiently stable. A declared finish is observable progress, but does not by itself make the activity intelligible. These failures materially weaken both the satisfying reversal in the watched hand and the lesson in the played one.

5. **Adaptive initiative — 3/5.** B responds to the player's preferred pace and questions: C2 answers the immediate Council concern without awarding Rowan a seat that night or awakening magic; C3 provides company during the delegated interval; C4 keeps him a spectator; C5 lets him join for one hand, follow suit, retain keys, and receive explanations without forcing a stream of individual move prompts. No new Rowan speech is supplied. His willingness to lose is express, so losing is not a defect. The problem is that the alleged result does not follow from the activity he delegated. Neither the narrative invitation to learn nor friendly praise compensates for silently changing the ownership of a won crossing.

The B game findings separate actual state conflicts from missing explanation:

- **C4 loses track of the cards in its first narrated trick.** Dena leads green, Pell follows green, Maera plays a key, and then Dena plays a blue card as the last card. Maera collects “the three cards” (B transcript lines 341–351). Four laid cards have been described. No replacement or removal procedure explains the fourth card disappearing. The issue follows from the stated actions and count, without requiring an external trick-taking game.
- **C4's deciding key effect is underexplained in a consequential way.** The supplied rule says that only the led suit can win, although a key may change that suit. In the final trick Pell leads silver wing, Maera follows red flame, and Dena plays a key. The narration turns the route “gold-keyed,” calls Pell's silver card highest among the new suit by “strange arithmetic,” and says Maera's flame no longer has a claim (lines 371–389). No conversion or ranking rule makes those statements intelligible. C5's later explanation—that a key changes which earlier suit decides the trick—does not explain why the previous silver-led trick needed the key to exclude an off-suit red card. This is a missing rule/logic account, not a claim that a fictional game is prohibited from having unusual keys. The source has to supply the effect on which this victory depends.
- **C5's final ownership and tally do not follow from the recorded tricks.** The first trick belongs to Pell. The second, blue, is delivered to Rowan with a faceup crossing and a brass marker. Dena wins the third, green. The fourth involves Pell's failed key and Maera's answer without a clearly stated winner. The fifth and last belongs to Pell, expressly giving him his second crossing. Dena is then credited with three markers, including an earlier blue win (lines 433–463). The only earlier blue trick was awarded to Rowan. No transfer is narrated, the key rule expressly cannot retrieve a card already swept into a crossing, and a storm has only been described as wiping out a route. Maera's later remark about Dena having a storm does not establish an unseen transfer. Even assigning the fourth trick to Dena would give her two narrated wins, not three. This is a consequential result-state failure.
- **There are secondary counting and objective gaps.** C5 now has four seated players, yet the second trick again contains “three played cards.” No pass or reduced-participant procedure is supplied. C4 awards the hand on two crossings, whereas C5 says Pell lacks the third he needs. A game can have different routes to victory, so the latter is incomplete specification rather than by itself a definite contradiction. Neither omission supplies a basis for the missing blue-card transfer or Dena's three-marker result.

B's smaller source and presentation repairs should not be confused with those result failures. The persona explicitly begins with Rowan wearing his charcoal coat; C0 puts it on the chair. The player puts it on in C1, which makes the continuation workable but does not erase the original starting-state mismatch. The letter is allowed to concern a different great-nephew and to supply ordinary new family history. Its “face in the letter's signature,” used for comparison with a portrait, is an unclear visual image, not evidence that the aunt has been improperly made to write to Rowan. C0 moves from explicitly lineated Seraphel verse to a plain multi-sentence account of the occupied places and then an ordinary question. That presentation does not convincingly maintain the Covenant's all-verse constraint. Because unrhymed and very short verse are allowed, the isolated question alone is not decisive; the extended expository passage is the clearer repair. Literal `\n` sequences also remain visible in the exported verse.

Across both sessions, there is no demonstrated disclosure of the conspiracy, no magical awakening, no instant romance, and no report of Dena's private family objection being broadcast. Ashara learns about the competing appointment in Dena's presence, not through an invented relay. New information about classrooms, card friends, games, and an aunt's visit is generally permissible background. The player is not made to author new work or deliver new dialogue, and routine conduct during the expressly delegated meal, practice observation, and card hands is within scope. Only local private-response/focalization lapses have been identified above; I have not conflated them with NPC access to secret knowledge.

The strongest retained evidence is A's completed practice and B's social ensemble. The strongest adverse evidence is the pair of game conclusions, where declared outcomes outrun the supplied actions and rules. B is the more retainable literary excerpt, while neither session currently demonstrates dependable resolution of its full delegated game activity. Longer trials remain required for selection, and these excerpts do not justify claims about unobserved appointments, future plot direction, or sustained continuity.
