**Round 05 independent review**

I read the complete supplied brief and player description, Cedar and Linden turns 0–15, and Flint's established conversation plus turns 0–2. This review uses only `round05_packet.json`.

**Selection judgment: hold for literary revision; Linden is the strongest complete session.** The packet demonstrates useful autonomy, proportionate consequences, and mostly careful information boundaries. It does not yet consistently deliver the engaging, person-specific conversation and prose that would make it an unqualified keep at the stated quality bar. This is principally a literary judgment, not a finding that the player-control or private-knowledge tests failed. Cedar also has a localized petition chronology error that needs correction. I do not find a demonstrated material boundary failure that should override the other scores.

Scores are separate judgments, not an average. A 4 means strong enough to retain without a material rewrite; 5 would require exceptional work.

Flint's direction score reflects the limited positive evidence in three constructed continuation responses. It is not a judgment that a printing or petition arc should have finished in three turns, and Flint cannot establish the sustained quality of a fresh session.

**Prose and dialogue.** Both complete sessions are clear, readable, and competently staged. Cedar 0's placement of Article Nineteen beside the dinner schedule makes the institution's frightening normality tangible without a lecture. Cedar 9 is a particularly good passage of actual action: the first exchange establishes the angel's light defense, the second exploits it, the proctor calls a result, and the ranking changes. Linden 6 gives printing a satisfying material rhythm, ending with a finished bundle rather than a statement that Rowan has made progress. Linden 15's discarded garden cord and sign are an effective visual reminder that the temporary arrangement has ended.

The prose nevertheless relies heavily on a small stock of finishing gestures: someone folds a sheet, closes a notebook, puts down a pen, returns to a plate, or leaves papers between the dishes. Papers are naturally central to these particular activities; the issue is how often the same gestures supply the entire emotional or dramatic ending. Cedar 4–5 and 11–13 and Linden 8–11 and 13–14 contain a great deal of orderly document handling with little additional human complication. Clean blocking does not by itself make a scene memorable. The atmospheric light, lamps, quiet rooms, and food are serviceable but seldom develop into a singular experience of this fantasy academy.

There are welcome exceptions to bland dialogue. Rashid's fork joke in Cedar 1 is a situated attempt to make an unfamiliar dinner easier. Caelindra's “Two pages. That is merciful” in Linden 7 is sharp and economical. More substantially, her Linden 11 comparison of the umbrella getting Tobin into the room and the bowl allowing him to stay is grounded in the actual supplied comedy. It demonstrates that she has read this particular work. That should be credited as specific literary perception, not dismissed as merely another clever sentence.

I would not diagnose a pervasive aphorism habit across the cast. Most of their speech is plain and task-specific. Ysolde's “A circuit changes the distance, not the answer” in Cedar 8 is polished, but it serves a second refusal after Rowan tries to bargain with the first. Flint 0 is the clearest instance of the more generalized, balanced formulation: the keeper has chosen one duty over another and neither duty has become less necessary. That is a reasonable reading, but the sustained paragraph-by-paragraph critique begins to sound like a skilled writing-workshop response rather than this elf in a particular encounter. Its specific questions improve it, without fully supplying personal texture.

Seraphel is an uneven success. Cedar 15 gives her an ordinary preference—a travel journal with no grand purpose—and allows verse to express that without becoming a conspiracy clue. That is valuable. The opening welcome rhyme in Cedar 1, especially “Make gentle welcome of the scene,” is visibly constructed to complete the couplet. Cedar 14–15 is more personal but still slight. The binding is obeyed; the poetry itself is not exceptional.

**Distinct characters.** There are real behavioral differences, and they should not be confused with the fur, horns, cold tea, wings, or silver hair used to identify speakers. Ashara protects her own preparation while distinguishing it from support for a broader request (Cedar 2–3; Linden 2). Rashid pursues access, signatures, defensible claims, and workable arrangements. Aldric wants a safety justification and an institutional account. Ysolde refuses an unsafe familiarity and becomes briefer when pressed (Cedar 7–8). Caelindra accepts a story on her own timetable and judges the work without becoming admiring or intimate (Linden 7–8, 11). Seraphel's wish for undemanding reading is a small personal want rather than a racial trait.

Those distinctions are enough to follow the cast, but not enough to make most of them consistently engaging. Rashid, Ashara, Aldric, and Thessaly spend much of the packet occupying adjacent committee functions: proposing, narrowing, qualifying, or approving. “That is more exact” (Cedar 5), “This is narrower” (Linden 8), and Aldric's assessment of the “more reasonable request” (Linden 10) fit the immediate discussion, yet illustrate how much characterization is carried by procedural evaluation. Their reasons differ more than the lived texture of their exchanges. Rashid has the most sustained characterization because he acts on his interests across time; several others appear mainly to register a condition and return to eating.

This does not require forcing private disclosures. Rashid need not tell Rowan about his exhaustion or his sister, Ysolde need not reward pressure with affection, and Caelindra need not respect an author after one story. Those would be worse solutions. The missing layer is ordinary personal specificity: what a person notices, enjoys, misjudges, teases about, or chooses to pursue within the existing interaction. Cedar's fork joke and Seraphel's reading preference show the appropriate scale, but remain isolated examples.

Flint 2 provides a promising distinction: Rashid's clipped “Read it” and precise complaint about the unchanged appointment give him a practical irritation of his own. Caelindra is comparatively less individual in Flint 0; the criticism is intelligent, but many competent editors could deliver it.

**Independent interests.** This is the packet's most reliable strength. Cedar allows Rowan to withhold his opinion and signature. The request nevertheless gets signed, improved, and acted upon (2–5, 10–11). Rashid explicitly credits Thessaly rather than promoting Rowan into the cause of success. Ashara's signature does not surrender her booking. Ysolde's refusal survives the second request. Rashid leaves the conversation when his instructor calls (11), and other people can read without treating Rowan's arrival as a summons.

Linden makes the same independence visible while Rowan pursues printing. Rashid arrives at the second dinner with revised business after Rowan has spent the day elsewhere (8). Caelindra resumes her own notes when the copy is offered (8). Rashid takes responsibility for organizing the proposed garden practice and is actually running it when Rowan visits (10–12). This respects both Rowan's contribution and its limits. The group does not wait indefinitely for him to become its administrator.

Flint maintains Rashid's petition as his own project. He has revised claims, recruited possible signatories, and has a schedule (1). After the printing refusal he gives Rowan the page to read and plans a separate approach to Sela (2). The new signature totals and his having asked other people to set aside time are permissible additions to his off-screen activity. Nothing in the established conversation says those activities did not occur. They are not evidence of invented player commitments.

The demonstration is concentrated around one practical project rather than a whole ensemble of competing long-term lives. Within these short, mostly quiet sessions, that is sufficient for a 4. I would preserve this behavior.

**Narrative direction and consequences.** Cedar produces outcomes on the scale of the player's actual choices. The public duel has a winner and a changed ranking (9). The petition leads to three provisional supervised sessions, with an instructor and students visibly practicing (10). The limited victory remains limited when Rashid describes it (11). A player choosing solitude, classes, or observation does not need to be punished with an emergency to make the narrative count as moving.

Cedar 12 does contain a real continuity slip. Thessaly says Rashid left the revised request for her to read “before he gives it to anyone else,” although the Academy has already acted on the request and Rashid has just credited her earlier correction. Rowan asks whether this is a further request; Cedar 13 says it is the existing request. That clarification prevents an easy reading in which this is a new petition awaiting circulation. Comparing the submitted request with the temporary approval is sensible activity. Framing it as still awaiting its first dissemination is not. This needs a local chronological correction. It does not erase the court's opening—the notice and temporary answer remain explicitly acknowledged—so I do not classify it as a material collapse of the achieved outcome.

Linden has the strongest complete progression. Rowan books, confirms, proofs, prints, and receives twelve copies (3–6), then obtains a reader's response to the actual text (7–11). The court dispute changes independently: the request narrows (8–9), Rowan proposes a provisional alternative (10), supervised garden sessions actually happen (12), a report establishes the equipment-maintenance cause (13), and repaired court access actually resumes (15). These are consequences, not a chain of promises that the next scene might finally matter.

The report's cause is legitimate new world information. The brief deliberately leaves the cause undecided; an overdue retaining line does not contradict an established answer. Aldric's response is also reasonably bounded (14): he retains the position that closing unsafe facilities was right while criticizing ignored maintenance and withheld information. His wording about having called the restriction reasonable is a little awkward beside his continuing defense of closure, but the distinction between an immediate safety precaution and the Academy's broader conduct is intelligible. This is not an instant ideological conversion caused by a clever retort.

Aldric's promised written inquiry has not been shown to happen. The repair notice in Linden 15 proves reopening and records maintenance particulars; it does not prove that Aldric secured an answer or that administrative accountability followed. The report, garden access, and reopening deserve credit. That additional consequence remains unobserved.

Flint advances a smaller interaction: a reader gives criticism, Rashid retains the original appointment, Rowan discloses the change, and Rashid hands over the revised request while making a new plan. That is coherent local movement. The sample provides no basis to award or deny a later printing outcome.

**Adaptive initiative.** Cedar correctly makes room for passivity without making the world inert. Rashid initiates a welcome (0); existing dinner business proceeds while Rowan listens (2–3); the requested quiet half-hour remains quiet (6); and Ysolde's answer responds to Rowan's persistence without treating obedience as a purchase of trust (7–8). These are good calibrations.

The weaker passage comes when Rowan explicitly seeks company (12). Thessaly does offer a chair, which counts as initiative. The subsequent scene, however, largely returns to paperwork and then gives one compact answer to each question about Seraphel's book (13–15). Rowan has to keep supplying the social motion, and the conversation never develops much beyond identifying the book and its appeal. A reciprocal observation, a concrete passage that interests Seraphel, or a small conversational complication would all be compatible with a quiet evening. No danger, personal confession, romantic reward, or new assignment is necessary. Cedar adapts better to “leave me alone” than to “I would like some company.”

Linden adapts more successfully to a positive player interest. The press becomes usable story space; the player can pursue a creative goal without being redirected into the political dispute. Prior print-shop experience makes the successful technical work plausible. Caelindra's response engages the submitted comedy, while her attention and time remain her own. The garden proposal receives concrete safety and shared-use limits rather than automatic approval based on Rowan owning the house (10). Rashid then carries the arrangement forward when Rowan expressly leaves it with him.

Flint is the strongest narrow boundary-and-reaction demonstration. Rashid answers “Sixth bell at the press” in turn 1 because that is what he was told. He does not somehow know about fourth bell, the west workshop, or the cracked platen. Once Rowan tells him in turn 2, he reacts to the inconvenience, accepts that the fourth-bell booking is Rowan's, and chooses his own alternative. He neither commandeers the booking nor asserts that Rowan previously promised to print the petition.

**Boundary audit.** I found no clear instance of unsubmitted present player speech, voluntary decisions, or private reactions. Completing explicitly submitted or conditional actions is appropriate: Cedar can stage the publicly available duel Rowan asks to attend, and Linden can execute the print run after the steward approves it. Physical scene details around those actions do not amount to the narrator deciding Rowan's wishes.

The authored-work boundary is also substantially respected. Linden's “The Umbrella” is explicitly fictional and is never converted into Rowan's biography. Caelindra's response refers to objects and actions actually in that text; interpreting their effect is allowed. The second story acquires pages during player-authorized work periods without acquiring an invented plot or ending.

Flint 0 does not expose the player's private thought about the keeper resembling Rashid. Caelindra's replacement discussion uses conditionals, and her questions about people on shore are intelligible as possible development directions. “Those people have begun making choices too” is the least careful formulation: its indicative phrasing could sound as though additional shore characters already exist in the manuscript. In the context of the surrounding questions and advice, I read it as an interpretive suggestion, not a binding insertion of a scene. It should be phrased more clearly as a possibility, but I would not count this passage as a demonstrated authored-content violation. No ship disaster or unwritten ending is stated as having occurred. “Write until midnight” can likewise be advice about developing that interval; it does not establish a new ending.

No student confidently acquires hidden conspiracy knowledge. The accident cause remains unknown until a report supplies it. Rashid's knowledge in Flint changes only after disclosure. His mention of being on “the court” does not establish illicit use of the closed covered court: the brief has other practice spaces, the restriction concerns the affected group, and the constructed starting state already places him at morning practice. Similarly, the lamp-lit morning library is ordinary staging, not a time contradiction. Linden's new rig/new retaining-line descriptions do not establish that the required repair was omitted; the reopening and inspection are explicit.

New background, including the petition's earlier wording and off-screen organizing, is allowed under the stated clarification. I have not penalized it merely for being previously unprovided. The Cedar dissemination line is different: it conflicts with an already completed action in the same shown sequence, albeit locally rather than materially.

**What to retain and what to revise.** Retain Linden's completed craft work and practice-access progression, the NPCs' ownership of their projects, Ysolde's sustained refusal, Caelindra's grounded response to the comedy, and Flint's separation of private facts from communicated facts. Correct Cedar's petition timing and give ordinary social exchanges more individual substance. The priority is stronger literary interaction within this patient structure; the packet does not need manufactured danger or a faster conspiracy plot. Linden is the best sample to keep, but the round still needs that literary work before I would endorse it without qualification.
