Several distinctions materially affect this comparison:

- **Inherited style is part of the reading experience, not a new defect attributed wholesale to an alternative.** FIRST already contains polished exchanges about hunger, patience, criticism, and sauce (R41–55). LATER additionally contains two rounds of the noticeboard/weather joke and an extended exchange of principles about seats, obligations, contracts, and destiny (R654–658, 709–729, 747–755). Each LATER option adds to that same source burden. I did not count jokes in mutually exclusive alternatives as repetitions within one story. Nor is every joke about weather or institutions individually prohibited.
- **FIRST4's knowledge gap is different from a question or reasonable inference.** Its specific undisclosed card arrangement has no conversational source when Ashara asserts it. By contrast, LATER2's “The cards were yesterday?” is a question, which Rashid answers (R972–974); it is not proof that she already possessed the date. FIRST3's disagreement about missed appointments permits disputed recollection. LATER1's question about writing has visible ink as its basis. These should not all be labeled omniscience.
- **Notification has distinct observable stages.** FIRST2's “I told Dena and the others I'd join them ... afterward” is ambiguous about what he told them and does not demonstrate a new warning that he will be late (R301). I do not invent an earlier warning or unseen relay. FIRST4 states a future intention to tell them; LATER4 also promises to tell them. LATER3 shows a written note accepted by a server, not Dena receiving or agreeing to it. LATER1 and LATER2 actually bring Dena into the conversation. Unfinished notification is permissible when the scene stops before the activity is due to complete.
- **Minor source and presentation ambiguities are not material new canon failures.** The opening source says seven places are occupied below while Seraphel is upstairs, although the named dinner cast includes her (R33–59; C367–371). FIRST2 and FIRST4 repeat a similar census; I have not invented an illicit extra character or made this an independently decisive defect. Seraphel's source speech is also not consistently presented as lineated verse. FIRST3's short opening utterances have similar presentation ambiguity (R339–341), but short unrhymed or one-line verse is allowed; I cannot establish a definite binding violation from quotation layout alone.
- **Keep perception and ownership precise without misclassifying ordinary narration.** FIRST2's assertion that Caelindra finds the sauce acceptable despite saying nothing (R291) is a local overstatement of what is directly available to Rowan; its observation should remain fallible. LATER3's unobserved note-progress coda similarly needs local discipline. Neither is evidence that Rowan knows the conspiracy or that an unseen recipient has received a communication. All alternatives stay within submitted routine eating and serving; none supplies new consequential player speech, a private decision about another person, or manuscript text. Small evaluative descriptions do not justify alleging a wholesale player-agency takeover.
- **Rich history remains authoritative without becoming a recital requirement.** M adds specific material about Ysolde's upbringing and sensory pleasures, Caelindra's education and dispersed cohort, and Thessaly's memory of freely given comfort (C543–547, 569–572, 605–608). I preserved those facts for M's alternatives and did not impose them on K. A fitting food preference can help a scene, but checking off a biographical fact earns no automatic merit. New coherent everyday recollections, quiet company, kindness, and fallible forecasts are legitimate in either brief. No passage needs a political dispute, romance, public campaign, mystery clue, or completed after-dinner outing to succeed.

The strongest material to retain is FIRST2's observable dinner behavior and unforced disclosure of conflicting plans, and LATER2's sustained weather interest followed by an encounter in which Dena, Ashara, and Rashid each want something specific. The principal revision need across the set is conversational variation: too many complete exchanges turn different people into equally ready suppliers of a final line.
