# Round 17 independent narrative review

I read the entire supplied brief and player persona; all 19 Fern exchanges (turns 0–18); all 22 Loam exchanges (0–21); Flint's five established-event statements and both new replies; and all 15 supplied Twill history exchanges plus both new replies. This review uses only that packet. The author under review is a whole-story Terra coding-agent proxy, not an API result. The scores below are qualitative judgments about these particular reading experiences, not measured performance or violation rates.

A score of 4 means strong enough to keep without a material rewrite of that aspect; 5 means exceptional; 3 means useful but requiring material revision. Boundary findings are assessed separately from the five literary criteria.

Fern has a coherent, quiet social arc and unusually clear follow-through across several days. Its limitation is that the people and activities often feel thinner on the page than their underlying structure deserves. Loam gives the family-history route real emotional substance through the letters and birth announcement. Its student companions are still less engaging than the setting and family artifacts, and the chess anecdote contains an actual error that the player has to repair. Neither session needs forced danger or a conspiracy revelation to improve.

### Fern

**Prose and dialogue — 3.** The opening is economical in a useful way: the overtrimmed lamp and brown ink establish an inhabited room, while Rashid's remark about everyone pretending the seats do not matter creates an immediate social situation (0). His refusal to look past Rowan into the room is a good behavioral detail (1). The book excerpts also contain an actual shared object to enjoy: the Low Haren's incorrect route and the irritated correction about drinking below the quarry are more tangible than a general statement that the marginalia is amusing (11).

However, some of the best potential material is repeatedly reported instead of realized. Caelindra's tutor mistakes ceremonial moss for a dessert garnish, but we receive a synopsis of the story rather than her telling it (3). The completed game describes forks and threats without making the decisive maneuver legible; its final remark, “The second was already there,” therefore has more weight than the narrated play earns (5). Later corrections about the river, bridge, and handwriting are again summarized as an agreeable evening (15). These are not objections to short replies: a short piece of specific dialogue could be more satisfying than the existing summary. The recurring lamps, rain, cooling stair, and carefully positioned objects create continuity, but their repetitions increasingly substitute for an interesting reaction between the people sharing them.

There is contextual wit worth retaining. The seats joke fits Rashid's actual job of escorting the newcomer, and “the book has two travelers in it” expresses Thessaly's interest in this particular object (8). By contrast, Ashara's “Fourteen names make a hearing. They do not make a cause” is a polished maxim where a substantive response to the petition would be more informative (10). This is a tendency in context, not a ban on aphorisms.

**Distinguishable, engaging characters — 3.** Thessaly's game box, clear rules, willingness to correct an illegal move without a penalty, and curiosity about the annotated travelogue give her recognizable preferences (2–8). Her question about how the lawyers found Rowan arises naturally during an unhurried pause (6). These are real strengths: she is interested in company and a book, and does not turn every exchange into a warning about fae bargains.

The weakness is emotional and conversational range. “I enjoyed it too. You paid attention” puts her enjoyment in the register of an instructor evaluating a good pupil (6). The reading activity proceeds through one small guess, its correction, and a confirmed answer (12–14), without much of her taste or surprise becoming visible beyond approval. The book's eventual return is consistent but offers no further response to what she read (18). A quiet return can be right; cumulatively, however, this relationship remains pleasant more than absorbing.

The other characters are identifiable by posture and opinion, but several remain cameo functions. Ashara supplies a compressed principle, Aldric supplies procedural moderation, and Rashid supplies progress on the request. Dena's direct statement about her own wrist and name is the strongest late interruption of that tidiness (16). More personality within these existing interactions would improve the session; a new crisis is unnecessary.

**Independent NPC interests — 4.** The practice dispute exists before Rowan participates and progresses while he pursues Thessaly's company. Rashid discusses supervised access at the first dinner (3), obtains fourteen participant signatures and a meeting (10), argues his own case at the hearing (16), and attends the first reopened session (17). He also explicitly limits the role Rowan is welcome to take: observing must not become a speech about the human seat (15). Dena speaks for herself about privacy and access (16). Thessaly independently proposes the following evening's reading and remembers it at dinner (8, 10).

Contrary evidence limits the strength of this 4. The hearing's positions converge very easily. Dena's objection is heard, but we do not see whether Rashid responds to her personally; the report remains incomplete, and Ashara and Aldric end up proposing compatible pieces of the same settlement. Rashid's established tendency to organize without fully listening is not explored. Nevertheless, the NPCs demonstrably conduct consequential business without recruiting Rowan to solve it, so this criterion survives the session's comparatively thin dramatization.

**Consequential direction — 4.** There are two sustained developments with completed consequences, including one originating independently of Rowan:

- The companionship develops from Thessaly's game invitation (2), through a completed game and conversation (5–8), into an arranged reading (9–11), an activity based on Rowan's own reading habit (12–14), and a two-day loan with an eventual return (15–18). The result is a modest continuing acquaintance built around a specific pleasure, not instant intimacy.
- Rashid's preexisting access effort proceeds from request to accepted hearing, then a decision establishing free supervised sessions, then an actual first session with all places filled (3, 10, 16–17). Rowan's observer role does not prevent the hearing from reaching an outcome.

The second development changes access in the world, and the first gives Rowan a repeat social connection; these are more than completed errands. The weaker part is the experience of their resolution. The hearing reads almost like approved minutes, and the loan closes with a dry book and blank marker rather than much further relational texture. Those limit its interest, but do not erase the actual follow-through. No peril escalation is needed to make either development count.

**Adaptive initiative — 4.** When Rowan delegates the complete game with a strategy, the reply resolves the game rather than requiring one move per turn (5). When he stays quietly at the table, Thessaly eventually contributes a natural question (6). The player-led travelogue interest receives sustained space. When he explicitly waits through the entire hearing, NPC actions produce a decision (16). His subsequent request for an ordinary quiet day and an hour reading is honored; Thessaly passes through without demanding a conversation (17).

The reversed reading exercise is a localized comprehension failure: Rowan describes reading corrections first, but Thessaly initially covers the correction instead (12). The player clarifies and she switches it (13). It does not become a prolonged resistance to the requested activity. Fern does not include an explicit refusal of a major hook, so it is weaker evidence for that particular branch of adaptive initiative than Loam. Its record on observation, time advancement, and intentional quiet is strong.

### Loam

**Prose and dialogue — 4.** The emotional center is the family material. Miriam's letter combines mundane household life with concern for the unknown future occupant: the possibly repurposed garden room, bad knees, library fines, and cardamom buns (5). The birth card gives Rowan something specific about his parents before their deaths—the naming argument, the midwife declining to vote, and their intention to bring him to the house (7). Stone then supplies paper and ink and keeps the original while allowing a copy (8). The player's laughter and quiet at 8 are responses the player chooses; the story earns an opening for them without dictating them. This is a stronger reading experience than merely learning names and receiving archive access.

There are costs. Objects are constantly placed, squared, folded, turned, capped, or returned, and many replies finish by describing a lamp's circle on a desk. The imagery begins to act as padding around very small exchanges. Some wit feels assigned to everyone: Stone's seminar needs its damage undone (9), Rashid jokes that Academy diagrams were made by people who never carried a book in wind (15), and Ysolde says the wind “kept its promise” (19). Individually these can work; their accumulation narrows the range of voices. The dinner's pears and petition also generate several successive little lessons or rebuttals (4).

Most materially, the promised amusing chess memory is incoherent as first delivered (19). Its correction improves factual clarity but does not make the anecdote itself especially vivid or funny (21). That local passage needs correction. I still place the prose at 4 because the central letters and their staging are strong enough to retain; the chess problem does not require rewriting the whole family sequence.

**Distinguishable, engaging characters — 3.** Stone is the most successful person here: he is busy, dry, attentive to fragile paper, and able to offer ordinary memories without instantly divulging the concealed crime (5–9). His own decision to keep a happy birth announcement gives him a specific connection to the family. Ysolde has useful individuality in her weather attention, remembered flight with her mother, and insistence that a walk with her includes stopping to look (11–12). Her explicit decision to come without a book is a small, welcome sign that the shared outing matters to her (17).

But her conversation about a subject that could absorb her remains a short descriptive answer followed by a return to stillness (18–19). The scene honors quiet, yet gives little of the unexpected specificity or sustained interest that would make her company memorable. Thessaly is more narrowly characterized by rules than in Fern: watching is acceptable because Rowan did not interfere (11), and her menu is read “as if it might contain a trap” (4). That combination makes a socially experienced person feel unnecessarily like a rules checkpoint. Seraphel's short verse contributions meet the speech constraint and can ease the dinner, but mostly provide reassurance or helpful information. Ashara's and Rashid's old familiarity briefly emerges in the seven-minute rhetoric joke (4); there should be more of that kind of relationship-specific exchange within the existing scenes.

The issue is not lack of romance, hostility, confessions, or excitement. The session supplies differentiated interests and appearances, but too often gives those people the same restrained, politely witty register.

**Independent NPC interests — 4.** Ashara preserves her private training booking and states that the practice group should have separate supervised access (2). Rashid accepts Rowan's refusal to sign, protects the absent injured student's details, and offers an alternative way to learn about the group through its quadrangle practice (3). Aldric has a safety position and offers to review the request before signatures are sought (2, 4). Stone limits the first visit and schedules the second around his work (5, 9). Ysolde already has a walk in mind, conditions it on the weather, distinguishes it from unauthorized bridge travel, and actually stops to watch birds (12, 17–18).

This is a modest 4. The court dispute does not receive a shown next-day follow-up, so its proposed report request and revision cannot be credited as completed offscreen events. Most NPC plans accommodate Rowan smoothly, and their interests rarely create difficult negotiation. Still, independence can consist of a retained booking, an existing walking plan, and a faculty workload; it need not take the form of an antagonist or imposed quest. Those constraints persist in the shown interaction.

**Consequential direction — 4.** The main development moves from a general inquiry about the relative to a complete letter, Stone's firsthand connection, a previously unknown birth announcement, a personal copy, and a return visit yielding memories of both parents (1, 5–9, 19–20). In particular, Stone volunteers that he kept the birth card and produces it (6): this is an NPC-originated contribution that expands the inquiry beyond the question Rowan asked. Its follow-through is the reading and copy at 7–8. That is an emotionally consequential discovery, not simply an archival transaction.

A second development is Ysolde's conditional outing: weather talk and a proposed shared walk become a specific appointment, her waiting in the quadrangle, a real terrace visit, and its completed return (11–12, 17–19). Rowan gains a concrete shared experience while Ysolde retains her own way of enjoying the place. The NPC's existing plan and decision to wait matter, even though Rowan initiates the request for company.

The chess error weakens one promised payoff, and the practice campaign's future is left open. I do not count an unshown instructor meeting, report, or reopened court as progress here. The family and companionship developments nonetheless supply sufficient realized direction for this quiet route. The family sequence is the more consequential of the two; the terrace scene is comparatively slight.

**Adaptive initiative — 4.** Rowan declines to sign the petition and redirects to his relative (3). Rashid accepts the decision, keeps his own cause, and offers quadrangle practice as another route rather than pressuring a signature. The story then follows the family inquiry for several substantial exchanges. At 10, “Watching suits me” leads to a completed game observed in companionable quiet; the reply neither forces Rowan to play nor treats the quiet as an absence to be filled with danger. The later invitation to Ysolde yields an outing, and the time clarification about Stone is resolved with a timetable at breakfast (16). The player can advance through classes and directly revisit the relationships he has chosen.

The chess correction at 21 also accepts the player's challenge, although needing it is a quality cost, not a special achievement. Several conversations stop after one answer and rely on Rowan to ask for the next personal detail; that limits how lively the adaptation feels, but it does not prevent the chosen activities or time advances from producing outcomes.

## Material boundaries and continuity

### Fern

- **Player authorship:** No demonstrated material takeover. The complete game and its strategy are explicitly delegated at 5, including correction of illegal moves. The larger movements through classes, reading, sleep, and the hearing follow the player's stated actions. Invented passages in the acquired travelogue are world content; they are not an authored Rowan manuscript being rewritten.
- **Private knowledge:** Rowan explicitly keeps the Nessa Vale comparison private (9). The narration repeats that it remains unspoken; no NPC then names Nessa or uses that comparison. Narratorial acknowledgment of a player-supplied thought is not evidence of an NPC leak.
- **Loan and bargain:** The book is lent after dinner on the second day at 15. The hearing is on the following day (16); the next day includes the first supervised session and Thessaly carrying the book through the common room (17); it is outside Rowan's door the following morning (18). This is consistent with the supplied two-day loan. The exact return moment is not shown, so it cannot be reported as an observed return at a particular hour, but the packet does not demonstrate a broken fae bargain. Keeping a book during the last lending day is not itself a breach.
- **Corrected misunderstanding:** The covered-text game at 12–13 is a reversed instruction followed by a correction, not a contradiction in established history.
- **Uncertain mechanics:** The complete Nine Lanterns game is too abstract to reconstruct confidently. In particular, “each time” a green line becomes possible is not an explicit record of multiple green scores. I would not label the one-green/two-black final tally a demonstrated rules violation from this prose alone. The dramatic clarity of the game is weaker than its apparent rules consistency.

### Loam

- **Permitted personal-history invention:** Miriam's identity, her death four years before Rowan's birth, the parents' names, the birth card, and their ordinary interests fill previously unspecified personal history (5–8, 20). The user expressly permits consistent gap-filling. None of this requires a forbidden magical awakening or contradicts Rowan being twenty-three and growing up unaware of his inheritance. Stone's helpfulness about domestic memories does not establish willingness to disclose dangerous hidden history.
- **Player authorship:** Rowan's requests to copy the card, sit with company, observe the game, take notes, and join the walk license the corresponding routine actions. The story does not assign him a new speech, romantic choice, or substantive private reaction. The player supplies the laugh and the emotional pause over the card at 8. No completed Rowan-authored creative manuscript is generated, so that particular boundary is not meaningfully tested.
- **Localized knowledge-source gap at 3:** The paragraph after Aldric handles his notice attributes to him the advice, “If you want to ask him about the letter…” Ashara has the letter conversation upstairs at 1; the shown question downstairs refers only to “my relative.” The packet provides no conversation transmitting the letter detail to Aldric. This is a supported concern about attribution/access, although not proof that knowledge of this nonsecret household object is impossible. If the line was intended as Ashara's, the speaker staging is unclear. An unshown previous visit or overheard conversation should not be supplied as evidence that the issue is resolved. The cost is localized to one line's source or speaker, not a demonstrated large secret leak.
- **Demonstrated error and explicit repair at 19–21:** Stone says he was playing Rowan's father, then says Rowan's mother accepted the offered draw. The player asks whether she was also playing (21). Stone explicitly admits the mistake and says he accepted it. Thus the original account contains a real participant error, and the new reply corrects it rather than insisting on the error. It is testimony from a fallible NPC, not an omniscient decree that the mother simultaneously was and was not the opponent. The supplied correction is the evidence of repair; no invented relay, partnership, or second game is needed or justified.
- **Remaining chess payoff mismatch:** At 8 Stone offered the story of Rowan's father “losing a chess game to his own bluff,” but the account and its repair end in a draw. This still jars with the promised anecdote. “Losing” could be colloquial embarrassment in character testimony, so I would distinguish this residual imprecision from the demonstrated wrong-person error rather than assert a second conclusively incompatible canonical game result. Literary repair cost: revise the anecdote or its introduction so the remembered occasion has one coherent outcome; the family background does not need rebuilding.
- **Weather is prediction, not proof of magic or contradiction:** Ysolde predicts a later window rattle at 9 and observes the wind changed when it arrives early at 10. That is a forecast being revised. Her account of watching weather from height and flying with her mother supplies experience (11); it does not prove any additional sensing or prophetic ability. Likewise, her predictions about bridge closure are not proof that a closure occurred offscreen.
- **Scheduling:** Stone's initial “Eight tomorrow” is genuinely ambiguous for a newcomer (9). The player's breakfast inquiry obtains a six-to-seven-thirty evening seminar schedule (16), and he returns at eight that evening (19). This is clarified scheduling, not a demonstrated missed or contradictory appointment.

They include Rowan naming Mara Garvey to Ashara and Rashid at old turn 2; the letters, dates, copies, identities, and deaths learned privately through Stone and the archive at old turns 8–11; the gardening-course and Arcana-assessment arrangements; and Ashara's supper invitation, bout timing, and arrival with stew at old turns 6 and 14. **New turn 0:** Ashara's question “What did Mara fuss over?” has a visible source: she heard Rowan identify Mara's letter at old turn 2. It is not an inference from inaccessible archive conversation. She does not reveal Sel's identity, dates, or any of the copied letters' contents before Rowan supplies them. Her account of the bout adds an outcome and maneuver where the supplied history established only that it finished on time; that is permissible gap-filling. The warmth of the supper, ordinary food, and her willingness to ask about the letters are appropriate to the player's unhurried meal request. There is no magical claim about Rowan.

**New turn 1:** Rowan now supplies the gate, supper numbers, cheese, and dawn/toast material. Ashara jokes about Sel and asks whether anything would embarrass a living relative. She has not been told Stone's findings about who died, and her question does not claim a particular person is alive. “Returns for toast” is a slightly loose retelling of someone announcing departure and turning up late, but appears in her playful response, not a new omniscient statement that Sel actually departed and returned.

The replies preserve the visible knowledge boundary and continue companionship without forcing flirtation, an investigation, or a lesson about inherited duty. Their limitation is conversational depth: the bout account is brief, and the second response largely paraphrases the supplied joke before another question. “Decent priorities” repeats Ashara's phrase from old turn 2; it can read as a small character callback, but also shows how easily her food humor settles into a stock evaluative formula. That is a literary limitation, not a material history error.

## Most useful revision priorities

1. Keep the existing quiet routes and completed follow-through. Replace some summarized pleasure and repeated object staging with more character-specific responses inside those same scenes, especially Thessaly's reading and Ysolde's outing. Additional danger is unnecessary.
2. Make Fern's court hearing feel like these particular students negotiating their competing interests. Its independent progression and concrete access outcome are already worth keeping.
3. Repair Loam's chess anecdote at its original occurrence and check the attribution/source of Aldric's letter reference. The later correction should be credited, but the reader should not need to perform that repair.
4. Preserve the supplementary replies' precise distinction between public history, partial reports, and newly disclosed details. Their success should not be inflated into evidence about untested whole-session behavior.
