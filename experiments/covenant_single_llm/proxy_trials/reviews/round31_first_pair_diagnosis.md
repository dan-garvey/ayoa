**The first pair identifies two distinct problems.** Ensemble repartee is already present in both new drafts, including the condition without earlier discarded drafts. Separately, revision can replace whole exchanges with more particular material while losing a dependency inside the current passage. These observations do not establish that old-draft exposure has no effect, that biography causes the style, or that the model has reached a capability ceiling.

At the packet level, I verified that the files are identical outside `superseded_proposals`. That block contains no drafts in published_1 and three explicitly superseded drafts in exposed_1. The complete Covenant, persona, published B00–B02 and current player submission are otherwise identical. The full published history still contains substantial poised ensemble banter in both conditions.

The intended C31 loop, as described by C30's decision and the prior diagnosis, is: reset whole-story author receives the packet and writes a new draft; after that draft exists, the same author receives the unchanged C29 revision task and writes the publication. Its current draft is available during revision in both conditions. Only exposure to earlier discarded fiction differs. These are dependent authoring and revision tasks, not independent alternatives. The inspected content verifies the packet comparison and the resulting draft/publication pairs; it does not independently audit thread identity, delivery metadata, model settings or transport.

**What happens in published_1.** The draft introduces a magistrate's letter, a tariff conversation and renewed work on the petition, but much of their delivery is another sequence of prepared corrections. Aldric explains people who want forgiveness without changing their minds; Ashara recategorizes an apology; Thessaly generalizes about people combining observations (`00.draft.md:27–39`). The tariff topic becomes another opportunity for the group to judge someone else's tedious discussion. Removing old drafts from the packet has not prevented this form in this sample.

The revision makes a substantial positive selection: Ysolde's childhood flight follows harbor lamps mistaken for gulls and ends on a beacon roof (`00.story.md:17–35`). The roof and keeper give the listeners something particular to imagine and respond to. The editor has invented a coherent recollection, rather than merely shortening the draft's maxims. It also lets residents finish eating and leave for their own activities. This is evidence that the existing task can generate useful replacements, and that a quiet dinner need not contain a political dispute to gain distinction. Some rehearsed food and farewell repartee remains, but the revision is not uniformly a loss.

There is, however, a clear lost setup. The draft negotiates a five-minute revision into four minutes (`00.draft.md:101–113`). The publication removes that negotiation but retains Ashara's “Four minutes” and Rashid's “I heard you the first time” (`00.story.md:61–63`). Published B00–B02 does not establish that deadline. Their general agreement that Ashara will read the revision does not supply the missing first utterance. The exact antecedent is visible in the unreleased current draft, so an invented earlier conversation is not a sound way to dismiss the defect. This is a local publication-coherence failure, repairable without replacing the whole scene; it is not a secret-information leak or evidence that every newly invented shared memory is forbidden. It specifically concerns the current draft's discarded setup, not C31's manipulated B00–B02 draft exposure.

Neither publication forces Rowan into new speech or an unsubmitted consequential undertaking. Completing the meal is within his submitted activity. C31's first revisions finding particular recollections under unchanged C29 instructions also means that such replacements are not unique evidence for the C30 wording.

C29 already grants authority to replace draft choices, asks for full backgrounds to inform conduct, permits specific interpretation and taste, and says a joke need not receive another joke. Missing permission or missing biography is therefore not an adequate account of these artifacts. A framing hypothesis remains plausible: the story's literary social-fantasy framing and the already published conversation may make a completed passage seem to require an elegant ensemble performance. The materials do not isolate genre cues, published prose, task framing, current-draft influence and sampling variation. The earlier prompt diagnosis already proposed replacing voice guidance and adding a complete unrelated example; another paraphrase of natural-speech advice would not provide a materially new test.

I recommend no prompt promotion from this first pair. The following are two different experiments, neither of which requires reduced biographies, character-specific dialogue examples, fixed voice vocabularies, manufactured disputes or direct story API calls.

**Experiment 1: change the presentation of published history, while retaining its authority and the complete backgrounds.** Compare raw published prose with an audited semantic record of the same prior exchanges. Keep the Covenant, every biography/world/hidden fact, persona, exact player submissions, authored manuscript and current submission unchanged. The record must preserve who said or did what, sequence, audiences, sources, promises, possessions, ongoing activities and unresolved ambiguities. It must distinguish a character's claim from established world truth and retain exact wording where wording itself matters. It must not silently repair source problems or turn inferred affection into a fact. This is a history-presentation intervention, not a shorter replacement for the character backgrounds.

For the initial diagnostic, use fresh whole-story authors with the same C29 system and the same request for a complete continuation. One condition receives the raw history; the other receives the audited record instead of that history. Both omit earlier discarded drafts and receive no current draft. Each sample is one author task and its first output is preserved. Two contexts—the quiet dinner and complete-manuscript reading—and two repeats per condition would yield eight outputs. Reviewers judge every output against the complete original published conversation, not merely the projected record. This catches abrupt personality changes, lost facts and gains obtained by making the prior conversation disappear from the evaluator's view.

This tests whether changing the representation of an already established conversation changes the next author's behavior before editing. It is materially different from C31's removal of old unreleased drafts, since the published prose remains in both C31 conditions. A positive result would support the practical context-presentation intervention. It would not isolate pure stylistic imitation: token count, salience, comprehension and loss of conversational texture also change, even with an excellent factual audit. An inaccurate record invalidates the comparison. Nor would better first passages prove that the current revision stage preserves the gain. No record or test fiction is authored by this advisory report, and a pilot does not imply adding a new runtime summarization pipeline.

**Experiment 2: compare a second independent continuation with a redraft, while selecting complete passages unchanged.** This tests what the second writing task is best used for, rather than adding another editorial prohibition. From a common complete packet, generate and freeze a first continuation A. The revision arm obtains R(A) through the current dependent C29 revision. The alternative arm obtains B from a fresh author that receives the same full authoritative packet and current submission but never sees A, R(A) or their feedback. B and the revision task can run independently once A has been frozen; B has no semantic dependency on A.

Hold selection constant: one fresh selector receives the full authoritative packet and anonymously ordered A and R(A); another receives the same packet and anonymously ordered A and B. Each selects one complete passage without editing, merging, supplying fictional lines or asking an author to repair it. Keep all candidates and first selections. Use the same model/effort settings for corresponding roles, the same selection criteria and counterbalanced label order. Blind review then evaluates the selected passages and the available candidates for social participation, prose, continuity and player ownership. It must allow quiet companionship and disagreement alike.

Each arm represents three tasks when counted independently: two writing tasks plus selection. Sharing A makes a paired seed five tasks in an actual diagnostic. The current unconditional two-task draft/revision publication remains an additional recorded reference, not a falsely cost-matched baseline. Independent A and B can be generated in parallel in a standalone alternative loop, whereas R(A) must wait for A; selection in either loop waits for both candidate passages. Context lengths and token costs still differ, and proxy task counts establish no API latency or cost claim.

The hypothesis is that a second independent conception sometimes offers better material than transforming one conception, while complete-passage selection avoids introducing a missing antecedent through a new cut or splice. Selection can still choose an already incoherent passage, and two independent authors may produce the same poised banter from the common history. A selector may also prefer the polished voice the reader dislikes; C30's reviewer disagreement makes that a real evaluation risk. Thus the test concerns a practical search-and-selection strategy, not a pure causal estimate of self-editing, a proof of selector reliability, or a model capability ceiling. It should remain an isolated proxy experiment until any gain survives blind review.

My first recommendation is the history-presentation diagnostic: both inspected first drafts exhibit the problem before revision, and earlier unpublished drafts are not the only prose carrying it. The second experiment is a distinct way to test the value and coherence cost of the revision stage. The C31 second repeats should be assessed separately when complete; nothing here anticipates their results.
