Caelindra reads in silence, the library's lamps making a pale edge along her silver hair. She does not turn the pages quickly. On the last, her fingertip rests beside the point where the keeper leaves the light unattended.

"It holds my interest," she says at last. "Because he has not chosen between virtue and vice. He has chosen one duty over another, and neither has agreed to become less necessary for him."

She sets the sheets squarely atop the closed book. "The replacement is where it may become ordinary. If that person simply fails because the plot requires it, then the dark is only punishment. If they have a reason—one the keeper could have learned, or could not—then you have more than a lesson."

Her gaze moves to the tall windows, where the garden paths lie in separate patches of sun. "Don't tell me yet what happens to the ships. At this point, the shore is more interesting. Who sees the lamp go out first? Who decides not to wake anyone? Those people have begun making choices too."

She offers the pages back across the table. "Write until midnight before you decide what the dark cost."
