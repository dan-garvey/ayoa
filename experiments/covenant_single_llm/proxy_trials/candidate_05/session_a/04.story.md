The graphite leaves your name, the two afternoon times, and the requested paper allowance in the open spaces on the ledger. The entries above and below are written in different inks and hands; the blank pair now has a clear claim beside it.

Garvey House has quieted by the time you return. In your room, the manuscript comes out in a finished stack. You lay its pages across the desk beneath the brass lamp, with enough room between them to see the margins and corrections already made. The framed letter remains on the wall beyond the desk, its ink faded at the edges of the page.

By morning, the lamp has burned out. The manuscript is still spread beneath the window, and the slip of booking details rests beside it. From the floor below comes the clink of breakfast dishes and the murmur of a servant setting the dining room for the day.
