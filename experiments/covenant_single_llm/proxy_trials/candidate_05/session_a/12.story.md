Late afternoon finds the west lawn marked off with a thin white cord set well back from the paths and flowerbeds. A card tied to the nearest post gives the terms in a gardener's neat hand: SUPERVISED CONDITIONING ONLY. NO WEAPONS. NO LIVE MAGIC. PATHS REMAIN OPEN.

Rashid stands at the center of the grass with six students spaced around him. They move through a sequence of turns and low steps, shoes pressing the lawn flat and then springing free. His sleeves are rolled to his elbows despite the cooling air.

“Again,” he calls. “Slow enough that you know where your weight is.”

A student on the outer edge clips the cord with a heel. Rashid raises one hand, and the group stops before anyone carries the motion farther.

“Reset inside the line. If you need more ground, ask for it. Don't take it.”

They shift inward, leaving the path clear as two Commerce students pass through the gardens with their books under their arms. Rashid waits until they have gone by, then lowers his hand and begins the count for the next sequence.
