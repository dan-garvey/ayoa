Two clear chimes rise from the dining hall, softened by the old wood of Garvey House. Your second-floor room is spare but finished: a made bed beneath a dark green coverlet, clothes sent ahead in a narrow wardrobe, and a desk placed to catch the garden light. Beyond the window, pale paths thread between flowering hedges toward the glow of the bridges over the storm.

The Academy primer lies open beside a small brass lamp. Its thin pages name the seven Council peoples, the welcome dinner, and Article Nineteen in the same clipped type. The inherited signet on your hand bears the Garvey device sharply enough to press a mark into the desk if you lean on it. Across the room, a framed letter in an older hand addresses a great-nephew who never arrived.

Below, chairs scrape over the dining hall floor. A voice says something too low to catch; another answers with a brief laugh. Candlelight reaches the landing through the open stairwell, carrying the smell of roast onions, pepper, and warm bread. A place has been set at the long table for the first human Conclave student in generations.
