Rashid gives a short nod and moves the folded sheet clear of the server's bowl. The soup is sharp with pepper and root vegetables. Around the table, spoons begin to move; wax gathers in small pale pools below the candles.

“You still have the covered court tomorrow,” Rashid says to the horned woman across from him.

Ashara tears a piece of bread. “I have an existing booking.”

“I need your name on the request for supervised access.”

“You need the instructor's report first. You have statements from the students who were there. That isn't the same thing.”

At the far end of the table, Aldric sets down his spoon. “A student was injured. A short closure while the dean reviews it is reasonable.”

Rashid turns toward him. “Do you know whether the equipment failed, whether the instructor ignored it, or whether someone made a mistake?”

“No.”

“Neither do I. But closing it to everyone means the students without private tutors lose their practice time first.”

Ashara takes the sheet from beside Rashid's plate and reads the heading. “Bring me the report when you have it. I will not give up tomorrow's booking.”

“I didn't ask you to.”

She folds the sheet once along its existing crease and sets it beside her glass.
