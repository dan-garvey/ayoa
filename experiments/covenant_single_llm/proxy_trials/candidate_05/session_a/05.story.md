Breakfast is nearly cleared when you leave Garvey House with the manuscript. The lower annex smells of damp stone and machine oil in the morning. Inside the steward's office, a woman in rolled ink-stained sleeves sits behind a high counter with the booking ledger open before her.

She takes the pages and checks the title, the length, and the margins without hurrying. Her thumb holds the second leaf flat while she compares it with the entry in the ledger.

“Good. Two pages, twelve copies, twenty sheets. That gives you room for proofs.” She writes CONFIRMED in red beside both of the afternoon entries. “Composing Table Two at third bell. Hand Press One from fourth until sixth. Bring the first proof here before you ink the full run.”

She returns the manuscript and tears a narrow receipt from the ledger's duplicate page. The same times and paper allowance are stamped across it in blue, with the annex seal pressed into the lower corner.
