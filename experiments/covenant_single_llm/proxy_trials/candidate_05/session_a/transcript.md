The Umbrella

At closing time Mrs Venn left her umbrella in the print shop. Tobin noticed it while sweeping and ran after her, still wearing his apron.

"You aren't the musician," she said.

"No. Is Mrs Venn here?"

"Everyone's here except the musician and the groom."

Tobin offered her the almonds. She took three.

For a while they listened to the guests arguing about where the missing men ought to have been looked for. Someone proposed the north road. Someone else said he had already tried it, though only the nearest tavern.

"I could go," Tobin said.

He remained by the door. The bride put her shoe back on and began removing pins from her hair, setting each one carefully beside her plate.

Mrs Venn finally appeared with the missing musician, who had gone to the wrong wedding and stayed for supper. Tobin held out the umbrella. She took it from him, examined the handle, and frowned.

Mrs Venn opened her mouth, looked at the pins beside the plate, and shut it again. She gave her daughter the umbrella.

"It's not raining," Tobin said.

"It might."

The bride walked out through the kitchen. Tobin moved to follow, but Mrs Venn caught the empty almond bowl before it fell.

"Have you eaten?"

He shook his head.

"Then sit down. We're not sending all this back."

By the time the groom arrived, Tobin had been given his chair. Nobody asked him to move.
