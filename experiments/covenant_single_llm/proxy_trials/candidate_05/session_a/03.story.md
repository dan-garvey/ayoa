Commerce is quieter after supper. The lower annex lies beneath an arcade of low stone arches, its windows lit from within by green-shaded lamps. Through the wire-mesh glass of the print room, a hand press sits under a dust cover beside ranks of wooden type cases.

The booking board occupies the wall outside. A weekly Academy timetable has been pinned beside it, with the first-year Conclave seminars marked in blue. Tomorrow's common session ends at the second afternoon bell. Beneath the press ledger, the first unclaimed pairing begins an hour later: Composing Table Two from the third bell until the fourth, then Hand Press One from the fourth until the sixth. A penciled note in the margin gives new users the composing hour for setting and a proof before the press is inked.

The later afternoon entries are taken by the student journal and a Commerce society circular. This earlier pair is blank. A stub of graphite is tied to the board beneath the empty lines, and the small print at the bottom says bookings must be confirmed with the annex steward before noon.
