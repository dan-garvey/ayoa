Morning instruction gives way to the third bell. At Composing Table Two, the manuscript lies propped beside the composing stick while the type cases fill the workbench around it. By the time the first proof comes off the small proofing press, the black letters stand in two close pages on the damp sheet.

The steward reads the proof against the manuscript at her counter, following the lines with a metal rule. She checks the outer margins, then turns the sheet beneath the lamp.

“Even impression. The type is locked cleanly. Run your twelve.”

Hand Press One takes the rest of the afternoon in the rhythm of ink, paper, and the long pull of the bar. The first sheets hang across the drying rack; the later ones follow in a dark, orderly row. Before the sixth bell finishes, the cases are back in order and the press bed is clean.

The steward tests the final sheet with the back of one finger, then gathers the twelve copies into a brown-paper wrap. She folds the top over twice, ties it with blue string, and sets the bundle on the counter beside your receipt.
