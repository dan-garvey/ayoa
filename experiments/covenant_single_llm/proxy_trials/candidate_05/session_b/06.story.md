Rashid gives a short nod and returns to the clean sheet. Thessaly stays at the long table while he writes; the petition remains there without your name.

The gardens are nearly empty by the time you reach them. Their high pale walls keep the wind off the paths, though the storm's slow light still washes the flowerbeds blue and white at intervals. Water moves through a narrow stone channel beneath the scent of night-blooming vines. For half an hour, no one comes through the garden door.

When you return upstairs, the house has gone quiet around its separate rooms. The framed letter beside your desk is a dark rectangle in the lamp's low light. Beyond the window, the bridges have faded into the storm.

Morning reaches the room as a thin band of gold across the floorboards. The house below has not yet begun its daytime noise.
