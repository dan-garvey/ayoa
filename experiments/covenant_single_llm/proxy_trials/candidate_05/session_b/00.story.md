Garvey House holds the evening in old timber, lamp oil, and the faint mineral scent that comes before the storm below the Nexus lights. Your carefully mended charcoal coat sits square over your shoulders; the inherited signet at your hand catches the last grey light from the window.

The room has been made ready with more order than familiarity: a bed with sharp corners, clothes folded by people who know your measurements only from papers, and a desk facing the gardens. A framed letter in an older hand hangs beside it, addressed to a great-nephew who never arrived. On the desk, the Academy primer lies open under its red ribbon, Article Nineteen's prohibition printed in the same neat type as the dinner schedule.

Beyond the glass, pale garden paths run between sheltering walls. Farther out, the six bridges rise from the plateau into the slow, soundless lightning.

Below, dishes touch porcelain in the dining hall. Several voices carry through the stairwell, then a chair scrapes across the floor.

A measured knock comes at your door.

"Rowan Garvey?" a man's voice calls from the corridor. "Rashid vel Amara. I thought I'd make sure you found the dining hall. They're setting the first course down now."
