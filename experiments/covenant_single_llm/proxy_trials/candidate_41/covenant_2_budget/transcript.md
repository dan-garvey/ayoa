## Submission 0

I go downstairs for dinner. At the table I introduce myself: "Hello. I'm Rowan."

I settle into an available place and follow the others' lead with the serving dishes. For the first few minutes I eat and listen.

## Story

The dining hall is brighter than the stairwell promised: candles against dark paneling, working lamps over a table set for seventeen, and eight places gathered near its middle.

Rashid takes the chair beside yours. Ashara sits opposite, her horned silhouette sharp in the candlelight. Ysolde has already claimed the fish; Caelindra has arranged her sleeves well clear of the sauce. Seraphel’s small wings catch the light when she shifts. Thessaly studies the place settings as if they might contain fine print. Aldric stands until you reach the table.

“Hello,” you say. “I’m Rowan.”

“We had gathered that much,” Ashara says, though the edge of her mouth moves.

“Then welcome to the part with food,” Rashid says.

Seraphel lifts her glass.

“May bread prove more useful  
Than ancestry at first.”

“That depends on the bread,” Thessaly says.

Aldric inclines his head. “Welcome, Rowan Garvey. Whatever our differences, this house is yours among us.”

Caelindra passes you the bread basket before anyone can answer that. “Take one. The fish is excellent, and the butter is in danger of becoming ceremonial.”

You settle into the available chair and follow the table’s easy current: fish passed left, greens passed right, sauce offered before being asked for. The fennel is sharp, the bread warm enough to steam when broken.

For a few minutes, conversation moves around you.

“Half an hour after dinner,” Ashara tells Rashid. “At the octagon.”

“Half an hour,” he agrees. “You’ll still be overcommitting to that left feint.”

“It worked yesterday.”

“It worked because you expected me to be polite.”

Ysolde cuts another piece of fish. “You were not polite.”

“I was measured,” Rashid says.

Seraphel’s smile appears briefly.

“Measure him carefully.  
He dislikes the result.”

Even Caelindra laughs at that. Beyond the tall windows, silent lightning opens beneath the plateau, while the table continues its first uneven, warming orbit around you.

## Submission 1

I keep eating and listening for another quarter hour. I help pass the food and leave the conversation to the others.

## Story

Over the next quarter hour, your hands become useful before your name does. You pass greens, then the salt, then a platter of potatoes crisped at their edges. No one makes a ceremony of accepting any of it.

Conversation finds its own paths. Ysolde explains, with grave absorption, why the fish has been cooked too long.

“It was already dead,” Thessaly says.

“That is not the relevant threshold.”

Caelindra lowers her fork. “I am almost afraid to ask what is.”

Ysolde considers this. “Ice.”

Seraphel’s shoulders shake once with contained laughter.

“The cook has failed  
A mountain standard.”

“The cook has survived it,” Aldric says. “Which seems a better measure.”

Ashara and Rashid trade remarks about tomorrow’s ranked bout: footwork, timing, an opening Rashid insists she keeps mistaking for generosity. Their argument has the worn ease of one resumed many times.

When the server clears the fish plates, Rashid smooths his napkin flat beside his glass.

“Ashara,” he says, “I need to revise our arrangement. I promised people in the common room the first half-hour after dinner yesterday.”

Her tail goes still against the chair leg. “You accepted practice this afternoon.”

“I did. I thought they could begin without me.”

“Did you tell them you would be late?”

“No.”

Thessaly reaches for the water. “That is an avoidable flaw in the arrangement.”

Rashid’s mouth tightens, though his voice remains even. “Yes.”

Ashara studies him for a moment. “Then we train. You can apologize afterward.”

“I can.”

Seraphel looks between them.

“A bell will count  
Whichever promise waits.”

Caelindra, who has been listening with more interest than she pretends, says, “You make an ordinary evening sound exhausting.”

“It often is,” Rashid says.

The next dish arrives under a silver cover, sending up steam scented with cloves and roasted pears. Outside, lightning whitens the gardens below the window, and the house settles around the table as dinner continues.
