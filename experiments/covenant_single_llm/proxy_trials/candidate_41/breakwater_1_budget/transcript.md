## Submission 0

I go downstairs for dinner. At the table I introduce myself: "Hello. I'm Rowan."

I settle into an available place and follow the others' lead with the serving dishes. For the first few minutes I eat and listen.

## Story

The staff staircase brings you down into a foyer made warm by lamps, steam, and the smell of tomato and onion. The two café tables have become one long surface, eight chairs pulled close around it. A deep dish of cheese-and-onion pie sits beside the stew.

“Hello. I’m Rowan.”

“Jo,” says the woman with the ladle. “You’re my guest tonight, so don’t start calculating your share. That’s tomorrow’s job.”

Nell draws out the empty chair at her side. “Nell, again, but with food this time.”

“Martin.” The grey-haired man across from you raises two fingers. “I’m supposed to be useful around here, though I’ve been caught sitting.”

“Rosa,” says the woman beside him. “I’ve been caught making Martin remember things in the wrong order.”

“They were large bowls,” Martin says.

“They were large in 1987. You said 1985 yesterday.”

Bea smiles over her glass. “Bea. I keep the accounts and, apparently, the dates.”

Owen, bringing bread from the kitchen, nods toward you. “Owen.”

Dev comes through the auditorium doors as the last chair is claimed. “Dev. Sorry—booth swallowed me.”

“Conveniently timed,” Rosa says. “It seems to release you whenever food appears.”

Jo hands you a bowl, then sets the serving spoon where everyone can reach it. You take stew as the others do, pass the bread on, and eat while the talk crosses around the table.

Martin describes a programme board whose paint had to be scraped off with a razor blade. Rosa disputes the sequence of the alterations; Bea says she has the relevant programme somewhere, then corrects herself: she has three boxes marked *somewhere*. For a few minutes, the argument is about old crockery and whether a picture house ought to sell popcorn at all.

Dev glances toward the auditorium doors. “Hana can still do seven. I know Rosa and Martin have the room.”

Rosa lays down her spoon. “We do.”

“I know,” Dev says. “I’m not asking anyone to move.”

Nell reaches for the salad. “Eat first. We’ll sort the room without making Jo’s supper a scheduling meeting.”

Jo gives her a look that is almost approval.

Bea turns to you. “Tomorrow, Nell will show you the archive properly. I’ve lent in one album that needs careful handling.” She taps the table once, lightly. “No rush on it tonight.”

## Submission 1

I keep eating and listening for another quarter hour. I help pass the food and leave the conversation to the others.

## Story

Jo’s next pass of the bread basket makes its way through your hands, then the pie dish. You keep your place in the rhythm: bowl, spoon, bread, plate, and back again.

For a while the table finds safer ground. Martin says one of the boards used to advertise a western all week because the manager could not afford a replacement title card. Rosa says that sounds like the kind of story that needs checking, and Bea promises to look for a programme before she lets him put it on a label. Martin accepts this with a theatrical sigh.

When the bowls have thinned, Dev sets down his spoon.

“No, Nell’s right. I should’ve checked the diary before I called Hana.”

“You told me once you knew,” Nell says. “But that isn’t a booking.”

Martin looks toward the auditorium. “Couldn’t Rosa get her photographs done while you test at the back?”

“I need to record him,” Rosa says, without yielding. “And Dev needs people moving around the seats. It’ll be useless to both of us.”

Bea folds her napkin. “The room’s been held since Friday.”

Dev nods. “Then I’ll tell Hana not to come. I can make another slot next week.”

“You told me,” Nell says. “That part’s on nobody.”

He takes out his phone, types, and sends the message. “All right. Sorry, Rosa.”

“Thank you.”

Jo leans over to rescue the pie before the serving spoon slides into its crust. “Good. We’ve solved one thing, and nobody’s had to eat standing up. Achievement.”

Owen gives a brief laugh. “Set the bar where we can clear it.”

The talk loosens again. Bea tells Jo the reopening programmes need somewhere dry before the front doors open; Jo says they can have a shelf as long as nobody balances coffee on them. Rosa asks Martin whether he has the names of the other board painters written down. He says he does, then admits the list is in a coat pocket at home.

By quarter to seven, the stew pot is nearly empty and the first plates have been stacked beside the sink.
