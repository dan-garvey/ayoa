The staff staircase brings you into a foyer bright with work lamps and the last blue light from Harbour Street. Two tables have been joined beneath the old poster cases. Plates, bread, and a heavy casserole dish crowd the surface.

Jo emerges from the kitchen with the cheese-and-onion pie. “There you are. Good. Sit down before Nell finds you a clipboard.”

Nell pulls out the open chair between Bea and Owen. “Rowan’s our cataloguer. Starts properly tomorrow.”

“Hello. I’m Rowan.”

Bea smiles over the rim of her glasses. “Bea. Treasurer, which sounds more solemn than it is.”

“Martin,” says the man beside Rosa. His hands are marked with paint at the knuckles. “I used to work upstairs when the films came in tins.”

Rosa lifts two fingers from around a glass of water. “Rosa. I’m making the old boards look like somebody cared for them, because they did.”

“Owen,” says the man at your other side, passing the salad bowl along. “Front of house, apparently.”

“Jo,” says Jo, setting down the pie. “And this is dinner. You’re a guest tonight, so don’t start looking guilty about it.”

Dev comes down from the stairwell with his phone still in hand and takes the remaining chair. “Dev. I’ve just cancelled an assistant and disappointed some very expensive hearing receivers.”

“You’ve postponed a test,” Nell says. “The receivers will survive.”

“They’d better. They cost enough.”

Serving begins in small, practical motions: stew ladled into bowls, bread torn, salad passed, the pie divided with a knife that turns out too blunt for its job. You follow the movement of the dishes and eat.

For a few minutes the talk stays with the building. Martin wants the names on six old programme boards checked before Rosa sends anything to print. Rosa wants daylight in the auditorium and complains that the sea makes every photograph look colder than it is. Bea says the printer will not be persuaded by nostalgia.

Jo listens while she eats, then says, “If you all finish arguing over paint before Saturday, I’ll put a pudding on the opening menu.”
