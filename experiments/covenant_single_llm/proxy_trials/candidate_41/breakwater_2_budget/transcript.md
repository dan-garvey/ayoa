## Submission 0

I go downstairs for dinner. At the table I introduce myself: "Hello. I'm Rowan."

I settle into an available place and follow the others' lead with the serving dishes. For the first few minutes I eat and listen.

## Story

The staff staircase brings you into a foyer bright with work lamps and the last blue light from Harbour Street. Two tables have been joined beneath the old poster cases. Plates, bread, and a heavy casserole dish crowd the surface.

Jo emerges from the kitchen with the cheese-and-onion pie. “There you are. Good. Sit down before Nell finds you a clipboard.”

Nell pulls out the open chair between Bea and Owen. “Rowan’s our cataloguer. Starts properly tomorrow.”

“Hello. I’m Rowan.”

Bea smiles over the rim of her glasses. “Bea. Treasurer, which sounds more solemn than it is.”

“Martin,” says the man beside Rosa. His hands are marked with paint at the knuckles. “I used to work upstairs when the films came in tins.”

Rosa lifts two fingers from around a glass of water. “Rosa. I’m making the old boards look like somebody cared for them, because they did.”

“Owen,” says the man at your other side, passing the salad bowl along. “Front of house, apparently.”

“Jo,” says Jo, setting down the pie. “And this is dinner. You’re a guest tonight, so don’t start looking guilty about it.”

Dev comes down from the stairwell with his phone still in hand and takes the remaining chair. “Dev. I’ve just cancelled an assistant and disappointed some very expensive hearing receivers.”

“You’ve postponed a test,” Nell says. “The receivers will survive.”

“They’d better. They cost enough.”

Serving begins in small, practical motions: stew ladled into bowls, bread torn, salad passed, the pie divided with a knife that turns out too blunt for its job. You follow the movement of the dishes and eat.

For a few minutes the talk stays with the building. Martin wants the names on six old programme boards checked before Rosa sends anything to print. Rosa wants daylight in the auditorium and complains that the sea makes every photograph look colder than it is. Bea says the printer will not be persuaded by nostalgia.

Jo listens while she eats, then says, “If you all finish arguing over paint before Saturday, I’ll put a pudding on the opening menu.”

## Submission 1

I keep eating and listening for another quarter hour. I help pass the food and leave the conversation to the others.

## Story

The stew settles into a companionable quiet, broken by the scrape of chairs and the soft refusal of the pie knife against its crust. You pass bread when it reaches you, then the salad, then the mustard after Martin asks for it as though it has been misplaced by hostile hands.

“Your boards are in the auditorium already,” Nell tells Rosa. “No one’s moving them.”

“Good.” Rosa breaks off a piece of crust. “I only need forty minutes of decent light and Martin not rewriting the history of every letter.”

“I don’t rewrite it,” Martin says. “I remember it.”

Bea looks over at him. “You remember it with confidence. That isn’t quite the same thing.”

Martin considers this, chewing. “Fair.”

Dev leans back, his phone face-down beside his plate. “I’ll put the receiver test in the diary before I invite anyone next time.”

“That would be a thrilling administrative breakthrough,” Nell says.

“It’s a new system. I’m adapting.”

Jo laughs once, then begins gathering empty serving spoons onto the edge of her plate. “You can all adapt after you’ve had seconds. There’s enough stew to feed a committee, which is unlucky for the stew.”

Owen takes more pie but leaves the last narrow slice in the dish. When Nell mentions finishing the opening-weekend rota by Wednesday, he says only, “Send me what you’ve got when you’ve got it. I’ll check my other shifts.”

“Of course,” Nell says, without pressing him.

The foyer darkens beyond the glass doors. Outside, a pair of cyclists pass along Harbour Street, their lights sliding briefly across the ceiling. Martin checks the wall clock and gets to his feet.

“Rosa, we ought to move the boards before seven. Better to be early than find somebody’s decided they need the room for a choir.”

“No choir,” Nell says. “I promise.”

Rosa folds her napkin, already looking toward the auditorium doors. Bea rises with them, carrying her glass.

“I’ll come down for a minute,” she says. “I want to see which ones you chose.”

Jo begins stacking bowls in the kitchen doorway. Dev reaches for the bread basket, and the conversation shifts to whether the old sign will be visible from the harbour once its lamps are repaired.
