## Submission 0

I go downstairs for dinner. At the table I introduce myself: "Hello. I'm Rowan."

I settle into an available place and follow the others' lead with the serving dishes. For the first few minutes I eat and listen.

## Story

Seraphel steps aside at the dining-hall door. Candlelight burnishes the long varnished table, though only eight places have been laid at its nearer end. The empty chair between Thessaly Morrow and Ysolde Thornmantle is unclaimed.

You cross to it. When you say, “Hello. I’m Rowan,” the room pauses, listening.

Aldric Verantus lifts his glass. “Welcome to Garvey House, then. May we make it worthy of its name.”

“A house need not become a monument,” Seraphel says. “It need only keep / a light for those within.”

Thessaly considers this. “That sounds like a promise. Is it one?”

“Not from me,” Aldric says, a little too quickly.

Rashid’s mouth bends around a smile as he reaches for the bread. “Good. We’d only just finished arguing over whether the chairs have rank.”

“They do not,” Ashara says. Her tail hooks around the leg of her chair. “The matter is settled.”

“By decree,” Caelindra says, arranging a silver cuff above her wrist. “How refreshingly unlike rank.”

Ysolde looks at the empty stretch of table. “There are seventeen chairs. It would be inefficient for them all to matter.”

A short laugh loosens the room. The serving dishes begin their circuit: fire-pepper lentils steaming hard enough to blur the air above Ashara’s plate, cold red slices under frost for Ysolde, roast squash, bread, and a dark stew scented with cloves. You follow the dishes as they come, taking a portion and passing each on.

For the first few minutes, talk drifts to tomorrow’s seminar and the dueling-board notices due at dawn. Ashara and Rashid dispute whether a ranking posted before breakfast counts as fair warning; neither seems interested in winning quickly. Caelindra complains, with genuine precision, about the Academy’s lamp oil. Seraphel turns the complaint into three dry lines, and even Aldric smiles into his cup.

Then Caelindra’s violet eyes settle on the ink at your fingers.

“You write?” she asks.

## Submission 1

I keep eating and listening for another quarter hour. I help pass the food and leave the conversation to the others.

## Story

Caelindra’s question waits beside the clink of a spoon against china. When no answer follows, she turns her attention to the printed notices tucked beneath Aldric’s elbow.

“The Academy writes as though every timetable were a heroic proclamation.”

“They are clear,” Aldric says.

“A notice may still aspire,” Seraphel offers, “to something finer than obedience.”

Ysolde examines the nearest sheet. “If it aspires, it should do so after stating the hour.”

Thessaly takes it from her. “It says attendance is required. That is clear enough.”

Rashid reaches across before the bread stalls at the end of the table, passing it on with the same neat efficiency he brings to everything else. “The dueling board is worse. It puts your rank where you can see it before breakfast.”

Ashara tears a piece from the loaf. “If you have a bout, your rank is useful information.”

“If you have a bout, you already know it.”

“You might have forgotten.”

“I don’t forget appointments.”

Her gold eyes narrow by a degree. “The octagon after dinner.”

“I said I’d be there.”

“Good.”

The exchange is brief, but it has the polished edge of an old argument neither of them needs to explain. Caelindra hides a smile behind her glass. Aldric notices it and looks back at his plate.

The dishes make two more circuits. You help the servers’ work along whenever a bowl or platter reaches your place, and the table gradually settles into smaller currents of talk: the cold on the public terraces, an Arcana lecturer’s fondness for impossible assignments, whether the gardens can truly flower through a winter. On that point Ysolde becomes unexpectedly firm, describing a wind that freezes rain in the air before it falls. Seraphel listens with her chin resting on one hand.

By the time the lamps have burned lower and the stew has cooled in its tureen, another quarter hour has passed. The dinner continues around you, warmer now than when you entered.
