Caelindra’s question waits beside the clink of a spoon against china. When no answer follows, she turns her attention to the printed notices tucked beneath Aldric’s elbow.

“The Academy writes as though every timetable were a heroic proclamation.”

“They are clear,” Aldric says.

“A notice may still aspire,” Seraphel offers, “to something finer than obedience.”

Ysolde examines the nearest sheet. “If it aspires, it should do so after stating the hour.”

Thessaly takes it from her. “It says attendance is required. That is clear enough.”

Rashid reaches across before the bread stalls at the end of the table, passing it on with the same neat efficiency he brings to everything else. “The dueling board is worse. It puts your rank where you can see it before breakfast.”

Ashara tears a piece from the loaf. “If you have a bout, your rank is useful information.”

“If you have a bout, you already know it.”

“You might have forgotten.”

“I don’t forget appointments.”

Her gold eyes narrow by a degree. “The octagon after dinner.”

“I said I’d be there.”

“Good.”

The exchange is brief, but it has the polished edge of an old argument neither of them needs to explain. Caelindra hides a smile behind her glass. Aldric notices it and looks back at his plate.

The dishes make two more circuits. You help the servers’ work along whenever a bowl or platter reaches your place, and the table gradually settles into smaller currents of talk: the cold on the public terraces, an Arcana lecturer’s fondness for impossible assignments, whether the gardens can truly flower through a winter. On that point Ysolde becomes unexpectedly firm, describing a wind that freezes rain in the air before it falls. Seraphel listens with her chin resting on one hand.

By the time the lamps have burned lower and the stew has cooled in its tureen, another quarter hour has passed. The dinner continues around you, warmer now than when you entered.
