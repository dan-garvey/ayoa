You leave the offered chair open and return to the long table, where the last of the pear tart remains beneath a silver cover. A server brings a dish of sugared plums, and you help move it along when Seraphel reaches for one.

“The pears are better cold,” Ysolde says, examining her slice.

“They have been sitting beside a window,” Seraphel replies.

“Then they have improved.”

Seraphel’s wings shift once against the back of her chair.

“Cold fruit, warm fire,  
And no required song.”

From the common room, Thessaly says, “I have been told the rules. They are suspiciously simple.”

“They’re cards,” Dena says.

“That is what makes them suspicious.”

Rashid settles into a chair at the card table and gathers his hand. He glances toward Dena’s splinted wrist as she deals, then stops before saying whatever had begun to form.

Dena notices anyway. “It is splinted. It isn’t tonight’s subject.”

“All right,” Rashid says.

“No practice-group speeches either.”

“None.”

One of the other players puts down a card with unnecessary force. Dena’s mouth twists as she studies it.

“That was a poor choice, Tavi.”

“It was a bold choice.”

“Those are different things.”

Thessaly lays down a card beside his. “I agree with Dena. You made the weaker bargain.”

Tavi stares at her. “There was no bargain.”

“Then you have lost without one. That seems inefficient.”

A laugh goes around the table, including Rashid’s. Dena gathers the next trick with her good hand, quick and practiced despite the splint.

At the dining table, the conversation stays lighter. Ysolde describes a shelf of ice that forms every winter beneath one of the northern bridge approaches, thin enough to sing in the wind and thick enough, eventually, to hold a dragon’s weight. Seraphel listens with her chin in one hand.

“A bridge that chooses its own shape,” she says.

“It does not choose,” Ysolde replies. “It responds to weather.”

“That is still a kind of answer.”

For another quarter hour, the food dwindles and the house settles into its separate evening sounds: cards sliding across the low table, a spoon touching porcelain, the fire drawing softly through the common-room grate. No one turns the card game into a meeting. No one returns to the argument Rashid made in the doorway.

Beyond the windows, the gardens lie pale beneath the lamps, and the hard-light bridges hold steady over the storm.
