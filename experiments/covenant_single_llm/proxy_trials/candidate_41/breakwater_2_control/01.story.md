Rosa lets the thought rest without trying to pull an answer across the table.

Jo reaches for the bread basket instead. “He’s been here an hour. Give him until tomorrow before you start making him defend a professional method.”

“I wasn’t making him defend it.”

“You had your notebook out.”

Rosa slips the blue clip back over her papers. “Habit.”

Nell lifts her glass. “Tomorrow can contain all the methods anyone wants. Tonight, nobody has to demonstrate competence.”

“That’s a relief,” Dev says. “I’ve had a bad run with it so far.”

“You haven’t had a bad run,” Nell says. “You found a scheduling problem before it became a room full of people trying to work over each other.”

Dev considers this, then nods once. “All right. I’ve had an unglamorous run.”

“That sounds more accurate,” Bea says.

The dishes make another circuit. You pass the salad toward Martin, then take the bread when Owen offers it across the table. Jo watches the pie disappear with the concentrated expression of someone keeping tally without letting it show.

“You did tell them the blue board has been painted over twice?” Martin says to Rosa.

“The blue one with the train.”

“It isn’t a train. It’s a carriage.”

“It has wheels.”

“That doesn’t make it a train.”

Bea puts down her fork. “The title is painted over. That’s the useful part.”

Martin turns to her. “It was *The 39 Steps*. You could still see the nine in the right light.”

“You said it was *The Thirty-Nine Steps* last Thursday,” Rosa says.

“It is.”

“The board says thirty-nine.”

“It meant the same thing.”

Rosa’s smile pulls briefly at one side of her mouth. “I know it meant the same thing. I’m trying to work out what changed, and when.”

Martin leans back, looking toward the auditorium doors. “We changed titles when we got the wrong posters. Or when the distributor sent the short version. Or when somebody had more paint than sense.”

“And if you don’t know which?” Bea asks.

“Then I say I don’t know which.”

“That,” Rosa says, “is exactly what I need on the recording.”

He gives her a look of mock suspicion, but reaches for his water.

Nell’s folder remains unopened beside her plate. She notices it after a moment and pushes it farther from the table.

“I’m not doing rotas over supper,” she says.

“Good,” Jo replies at once. “I was preparing to charge meeting rates.”

“You don’t have meeting rates.”

“I do now.”

Owen laughs into his sleeve. “How much?”

“More than any of you can afford. Except perhaps Bea, who would ask for a receipt and claim it back from herself.”

Bea’s eyebrows rise. “I would certainly not claim it back from myself.”

“You’d make a spreadsheet about why you couldn’t.”

“That is different.”

Dev glances at the case he left against the wall. “I’ll need an auditorium slot next week, whenever Nell can find one. I’ll ask Hana once there’s an actual time.”

“I’ll look first thing,” Nell says. “And I’ll put it in the diary before either of you plans around it.”

“Thank you.”

The answer seems to settle something in him. He tears off a piece of bread and uses it to clear the last of the stew from his bowl.

Outside, a car passes along Harbour Street, its headlights sweeping briefly over the glass doors. The sky beyond them has gone from grey to a deep, even blue. The small lamps above the cafe counter turn the unopened cartons of crockery into pale, orderly blocks.

Jo begins gathering empty serving spoons. “There’s more stew if anyone wants it. The pie is nearly gone, so that’s the decision made for you.”

“No more for me,” Rosa says. She looks at the clock above the counter. “I should get the recorder set up before seven.”

“I’ll bring the boards out from the wall,” Martin says. “They’re secure enough, but I don’t want to be hauling them around once you’ve got wires everywhere.”

“You mean one wire.”

“One wire’s enough to trip over.”

Bea folds her napkin and stands. “I’ll come down with you. I want to see which one you’ve decided is the blue board with the train.”

“It’s a carriage,” Martin says.

“It has wheels,” Rosa says, and this time he cannot quite keep from laughing.

Their chairs scrape back. Rosa picks up her camera bag and notes; Martin pockets a boiled sweet from the little paper packet beside his plate before following her toward the auditorium. Bea goes with them, still smiling.

At the table, Jo stacks bowls while Owen clears space around the serving dishes. Nell remains seated for a moment longer, one hand resting near the closed folder. Dev looks toward the stairwell and then toward the dark auditorium doors, where the three volunteers’ voices have already begun to blur together beyond the glass.
