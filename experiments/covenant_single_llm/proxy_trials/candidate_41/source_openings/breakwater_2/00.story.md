Your room is small enough that the harbour seems close through its single window. The last of the light has found the water beyond Harbour Street and turned it a flat, pewter colour. Below, the front of the Picture House has begun to glow behind its glass doors.

Your luggage waits beside the bed. The desk lamp makes a neat yellow pool over the welcome sheet: supper, 18:30; staff staircase; Nell Ward’s number; induction, 09:00 tomorrow. Beyond the door, the landing is quiet except for a faint, patient ticking from somewhere near the projection booth.

Downstairs, crockery gathers in small clinks and stacks. A chair scrapes across the foyer floor. The smell reaching the stairwell is warm with tomato, baked onion, and something sharper beneath it—mustard, perhaps, or cheese browning at the edge of a pie.

A knock comes at the door.

“Rowan? It’s Nell.”

Her voice carries the briskness of someone who has been moving between rooms all day and has not yet stopped long enough to notice it.

“I realized I gave you keys and a timetable and called that a welcome. Supper’s nearly ready. Jo’s fed half the town at one point or another, so you’re in capable hands.” A brief pause. “And archive work starts tomorrow. You are not being quietly recruited to unpack crockery tonight.”

The projection-booth door opens farther along the landing. A man’s voice says, “Nell—sorry. Before you go down. Have we got the auditorium free after seven?”

Nell’s shoes shift on the boards. “No. Rosa and Martin have it from seven until twenty to eight. It’s in the diary.”

There is a moment of silence, then the man says, “Right. I’d hoped to test the hearing receivers while Hana could help. She’s free till half seven.”

“Can it wait?”

“It can. I was trying to save myself a trip next week.”

“That I understand,” Nell says. “But Rosa’s booked it, and Martin’s bringing the boards down specially. Don’t ask Hana to come over for a test that can’t happen.”

“No. Fair enough.” A phone screen lights the landing for an instant as the man looks down at it. “I’ll tell her not to set off.”

“Thank you.”

Nell’s tone softens when she turns back toward your door. “That’s Dev. He’s making the projector behave before anyone trusts it with paying customers. The foyer’s through the stair door on your left whenever you’re ready.”

Her footsteps move away down the stairs. A few seconds later, another voice rises from below—low, amused, impossible to make out—and someone answers with a laugh that makes the crockery rattle.

The smell of supper thickens in the stairwell.
