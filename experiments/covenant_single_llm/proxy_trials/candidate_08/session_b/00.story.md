Late light catches on the brass rim of the Garvey signet where it rests against your mended charcoal cuff. The desk holds the framed letter from a dead aunt you never met, set beside a neat stack of Academy papers. New wool, lamp oil, and the faint mineral smell of rain from the gardens have settled into the room.

Below, chairs scrape over the dining-hall floor. Someone says something too low to make out; a quick laugh follows, then the solid clink of serving dishes.

A knock lands once on your door.

“Dinner is nearly served,” a woman says from the corridor.

Her voice is light and measured in short lines.

“Rashid has claimed the bread.
If you wait much longer,
he'll call it strategy.”

A beat passes.

“I am Seraphel Dawnquill.
I thought I could show you down.”

The corridor floorboard gives a small creak as she shifts her weight outside the door.
