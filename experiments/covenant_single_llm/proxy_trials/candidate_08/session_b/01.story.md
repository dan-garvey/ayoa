Seraphel steps aside as the door opens. Her small white wings are folded close enough that only their tips show against her gold-and-white sleeves. At your introduction, her expression warms.

“Then welcome, Rowan Garvey.
I know the shortest route—
which is useful here.”

She takes you down a stair where old wood changes beneath the newer black stone of the lower hall. The common room opens past the landing: dead Garveys watch from their frames over a fire laid but unlit, and unfamiliar voices drift from the library doorway. Seraphel doesn't pause.

At the dining-hall threshold, six faces turn toward your greeting.

Rashid, with the promised bread basket within reach, lifts two fingers in answer. “You beat the soup. That's better than most first evenings.”

“Barely,” Ashara says from beside him. Heat gives the air around her a dry edge; her tail hooks around the back of her chair. “Sit before he starts counting it as a favor.”

Aldric rises long enough to incline his head. “Welcome to Garvey House.” Across from him, Ysolde studies you with a stillness that seems to leave room for the serving plates and nothing else.

You take the open place as a server sets a bowl of clear broth before you. Seraphel sits near Thessaly, who says, “It is a handsome house. It has too many stairs.”

Caelindra smooths one silver sleeve and looks from the signet to your place at the table. “The lawyers gave you an inventory, I assume. Did they explain which of the Garvey obligations are yours?”
