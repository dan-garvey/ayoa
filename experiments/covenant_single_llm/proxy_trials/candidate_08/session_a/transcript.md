My character: Rowan Garvey. A twenty-three-year-old human man with brown skin, unruly dark hair, and ink-stained fingers, wearing a carefully mended charcoal coat and the inherited Garvey signet. Newly arrived and unsure of Conclave customs. He grew up unaware of his heritage, lost his parents young, and does not know whether he has any magic.

Begin the story shortly before the welcome dinner, in my room at Garvey House. I play Rowan Garvey.

**Story**

The Garvey signet catches on the worn cuff of your charcoal coat when you straighten it. On the desk, beside an untouched blotter, a framed letter faces the door: dark, cramped handwriting beneath the glass, addressed to a great-nephew who never came.

Below, the house has begun to fill itself. Chairs draw over wood. Crockery touches down in careful intervals. Something warm and peppered rises through the stairwell, followed by the brief flare of laughter from a room you have not yet seen.

Your window looks over the gardens, where the pale paths hold the last of the daylight. Beyond their walls, one of the hard-light bridges cuts a clean line into cloud, and lightning moves soundlessly far below it.

A knock comes at the door—not hurried, but too deliberate to be accidental.

“Mr. Garvey?” says a voice from the corridor. “Dinner is being laid. The first-years are taking their places in the dining hall.”

When the door opens, a server in Academy livery stands with one hand resting on a silver tray. A small card on it bears your name in black ink.

“Would you like an announcement?”

## Turn 1

**Player**

"No announcement, thank you." I go downstairs to dinner and introduce myself to the table. "I'm Rowan. Please tell me one of you is also pretending to understand how this place works." I take the place on my seating card.

**Story**

The server accepts that with a small bow and takes the tray away. Downstairs, the dining hall opens in lamplight and candle flame: a long varnished table, seventeen places, and a cluster of students already breaking the formality of it by talking over one another.

Your card waits halfway down, between a place set with black stone cutlery and another with a napkin folded into an elaborate green knot. You give your introduction as you take the chair.

The wolf-blooded man across from you smiles first. Fine dark fur shows at the edge of his cuffs as he reaches for his water glass. “Rashid vel Amara. I know where things are, which is different from understanding them. The maps leave out the parts people argue about.”

“The maps are perfectly clear,” says the white-haired woman beside him. Her grey eyes settle on you. “The people giving directions are not. Ysolde Thornmantle.”

“Caelindra Vaeyn,” says the silver-haired elf at the far end of the table, with a slight inclination of her head. “If anyone directs you through the west arcade, ask whether they mean the old west arcade or the newer one. They will assume you know.”

“Seraphel Dawnquill,” says the warm-gold woman nearby.

“For dinner, there is one simple rule:

“It isn't dangerous,” says the small, black-eyed woman arranging her spoon. “It is merely determined. Thessaly Morrow.”

The horned woman at Rashid's side rests an elbow on the table. “Ashara vel Kothren. Don't let them frighten you with seasoning.” Her gold eyes remain on you a moment longer. “What did you expect this place to be?”

## Turn 2

**Player**

"A school where I would be painfully behind everyone else." I taste the broth. "I did not expect people to be treating me like their landlord. The lawyers say this is my house. Is that going to be a problem?"

**Story**

The broth is dark and clear, carrying pepper, roasted root vegetables, and a bitter mineral bite from the black salt. It has cooled enough not to punish the first mouthful.

Rashid sets his spoon down. “Nobody's room is yours to grant. The Academy assigns us here.” He glances along the table, then back to you. “But people will make assumptions from the name on the door. Some will expect you to use it.”

“Aldric Verantus,” says the gold-haired man at the other end, as if the introduction has been waiting its turn. “The residential rules remain Academy rules. Garvey House is a residence before it is anyone's inheritance.”

Caelindra breaks a piece from her roll. “A house and a household are not the same thing. Lawyers are often careless about the distinction when they are not the ones sleeping under the roof.”

“I have not agreed to be anyone's tenant,” Thessaly says. “I would recall agreeing.”

Ashara's tail shifts once against the chair leg. “It will be a problem if you start acting like a landlord. Asking the question doesn't settle it.”

“It does make the answer easier,” Rashid says, without looking at her.

The server returns with a platter of sliced bread and pauses at your shoulder. Candlelight catches the silver edge of the Garvey signet as you reach for it.

Rashid folds his hands beside his bowl. “Did they give you the residence charter, or only the deed?”
