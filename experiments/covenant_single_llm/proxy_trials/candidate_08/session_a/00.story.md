The Garvey signet catches on the worn cuff of your charcoal coat when you straighten it. On the desk, beside an untouched blotter, a framed letter faces the door: dark, cramped handwriting beneath the glass, addressed to a great-nephew who never came.

Below, the house has begun to fill itself. Chairs draw over wood. Crockery touches down in careful intervals. Something warm and peppered rises through the stairwell, followed by the brief flare of laughter from a room you have not yet seen.

Your window looks over the gardens, where the pale paths hold the last of the daylight. Beyond their walls, one of the hard-light bridges cuts a clean line into cloud, and lightning moves soundlessly far below it.

A knock comes at the door—not hurried, but too deliberate to be accidental.

“Mr. Garvey?” says a voice from the corridor. “Dinner is being laid. The first-years are taking their places in the dining hall.”

When the door opens, a server in Academy livery stands with one hand resting on a silver tray. A small card on it bears your name in black ink.

“Would you like an announcement?”
