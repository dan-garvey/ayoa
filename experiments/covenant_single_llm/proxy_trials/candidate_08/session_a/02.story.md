The broth is dark and clear, carrying pepper, roasted root vegetables, and a bitter mineral bite from the black salt. It has cooled enough not to punish the first mouthful.

Rashid sets his spoon down. “Nobody's room is yours to grant. The Academy assigns us here.” He glances along the table, then back to you. “But people will make assumptions from the name on the door. Some will expect you to use it.”

“Aldric Verantus,” says the gold-haired man at the other end, as if the introduction has been waiting its turn. “The residential rules remain Academy rules. Garvey House is a residence before it is anyone's inheritance.”

Caelindra breaks a piece from her roll. “A house and a household are not the same thing. Lawyers are often careless about the distinction when they are not the ones sleeping under the roof.”

“I have not agreed to be anyone's tenant,” Thessaly says. “I would recall agreeing.”

Ashara's tail shifts once against the chair leg. “It will be a problem if you start acting like a landlord. Asking the question doesn't settle it.”

“It does make the answer easier,” Rashid says, without looking at her.

The server returns with a platter of sliced bread and pauses at your shoulder. Candlelight catches the silver edge of the Garvey signet as you reach for it.

Rashid folds his hands beside his bowl. “Did they give you the residence charter, or only the deed?”
