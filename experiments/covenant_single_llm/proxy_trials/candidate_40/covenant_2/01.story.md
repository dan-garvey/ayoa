Rashid turns toward the stairs, and the common room opens beneath you in firelight and old varnished wood. Portraits watch from the walls above the oversized hearth; their gilt nameplates catch the glow, then vanish again as the flames move. Beyond the open dining-room doors, servers set the last covered dishes along a table large enough to make eight people seem provisional.

A place card marked **GARVEY** waits midway down the right side. Rashid takes the chair on one side of it. On the other sits the snow-pale woman from below, white hair gathered at her nape and grey eyes fixed on the platter nearest her.

When you reach the table, the conversation gives you a space.

“Hello. I’m Rowan.”

“Ashara vel Kothren,” says the woman at the far end. Her swept horns clear the high back of her chair by an inch. “Welcome.”

“Ysolde Thornmantle,” says the woman beside you. “The fish has survived your arrival.”

Across from her, Caelindra inclines her silver head with careful economy. “Caelindra Vaeyn.”

“Seraphel Dawnquill,” says the angel beside her.

“Sit. Eat.  
The names will keep.”

Thessaly Morrow’s black eyes settle on you for a beat, then on the basket between you. “She is right. The bread is getting cold.”

Aldric straightens slightly in his white and gold. “Aldric Verantus. Welcome to the Conclave, Rowan Garvey.”

Rashid reaches for the nearest serving spoon. “And you’ve met everyone who intends to be civil before supper. It’s a better beginning than most first nights get.”

Ashara lifts one dark brow. “Most first nights?”

“Most things,” Rashid says.

The dishes begin their slow circuit around the table. There is a shallow bowl of smoked river fish under a crust of herbs, glazed roots bright with pepper, a green bean dish swimming in butter, and a copper pot of broth fragrant with fennel. You follow the movement of hands and spoons, taking portions as the others do. Rashid passes the bread without letting the basket catch on the curve of Ashara’s tail.

“I could have managed that,” she says.

“I know.”

“Then why did you move it?”

“Because I could manage it too.”

Caelindra cuts into a piece of fish. “A moving demonstration of shared competence. We are fortunate to witness it.”

Ysolde tastes the broth, pauses, and reaches for the salt.

“It needs it,” she says.

Aldric looks toward the server standing discreetly by the wall. “Could we have a little more?”

“We have some,” Ysolde says, already passing the cellar.

Thessaly studies the small silver vessel as though it has been introduced under suspicious terms. “Is there a custom concerning how much salt one may take?”

“No,” says Ashara.

“Yes,” says Caelindra at the same moment. “One takes enough not to ruin the dish.”

“That is advice, not a custom.”

“It becomes a custom when enough people survive it.”

Seraphel breaks a corner from her bread.

“Salt, bread, and fish.  
A treaty has begun.”

A short laugh escapes Rashid. Even Aldric’s expression gives way for a moment.

The first minutes settle into the small sounds of dinner: knife against plate, the low hiss from a lamp wick, a server replacing an empty bowl before anyone asks. The storm’s light comes and goes through the tall windows, whitening the paneled walls without reaching the candles.

Ashara eats with the concentration she might bring to a bout. Rashid, after finishing his first helping, asks the server whether the kitchens can spare the recipe for the pepper glaze and receives a flat refusal that appears to please him. Caelindra makes an annotation in the margin of a slim book propped beside her plate, then closes it when Ysolde looks at the ink.

“You cannot possibly be reading through dinner,” Ysolde says.

“I can read at any reasonable hour.”

“This is not an hour. It is a meal.”

“That is a distinction humans make because their hours are so short,” Caelindra says, then glances across the table as if the sentence has arrived somewhere unintended. “No offense intended.”

Thessaly takes another piece of bread. “I cannot tell whether that is better or worse than offense.”

“It is worse,” Ashara says.

“It is more honest,” Rashid adds.

Aldric folds his napkin with exactness. “It is also an unfortunate way to welcome someone.”

Caelindra’s ears move sharply, then still. “Then I withdraw the phrasing. Not the observation.”

“Fair,” Rashid says.

For a few moments, no one presses it further.

Ashara sets down her fork. “Amara. The octagon after dinner.”

“I remember.”

“Half an hour.”

“At least.”

“Tomorrow’s ranking does not care whether you have been charming at supper.”

Rashid’s courtesy becomes almost formal. “I have no intention of losing because I ate well.”

“Good,” Ashara says, and returns to her plate.

At the mention of tomorrow’s ranking, Aldric’s attention goes briefly to the posted schedule tucked beneath the sideboard clock. “The Chancellor will have the full first-year Conclave in seminar before then. I imagine he will make something of it.”

“He makes something of most arrangements involving witnesses,” Thessaly says.

“He is welcoming a restored signatory presence,” Aldric replies.

“The human seat has been empty,” Rashid says. His tone remains even. “That is different from restoring what was there.”

Ashara looks toward the dark crest on your hand, then up at you. Her gold eyes do not leave room for a polite evasion.

“Did you choose this, Garvey?”
