The last line leaves a clean little silence behind it. A log settles in the fireplace; somewhere beyond the open dining-room door, china clicks softly as the staff clear the table.

Seraphel looks at the page for a moment rather than at you. One hand rests on the dark wood of her harp.

“Nell counts to ten.

Ada counts to noon.

They are standing in the same room,

but they are not living

in the same amount of time.”

She plucks one low string. The note barely forms before her palm quiets it.

“I liked the coat becoming

a third person in their argument.

And I liked that Ada gets

the last line.

It is funny,

but she is not made foolish.

Would you read the last four lines

again?

This time, leave a breath

before Ada answers.”
