Ysolde keeps her eyes on the window until the last pale trace of the bridge has gone.

“You are welcome,” she says. “It is better from the gardens. The lamps are lower there, and the storm has more room around them.”

She returns to her plate without adding an invitation to the observation. Across from her, Caelindra has discovered that the tarragon sauce has reached the edge of her bread and is considering this as a personal affront.

“If the kitchen puts that on dessert,” she says, “I will formally complain.”

“To whom?” Thessaly asks.

“Whoever is responsible for dessert.”

“That is not a person you know.”

“Then the complaint will be a surprise.”

Rashid smiles into his cup. Ashara does not, but the tip of her tail shifts once beneath the table. A server removes the empty meat dish and replaces it with a plate of small pear tarts, their edges dark with sugar.

“No tarragon,” Seraphel says.

“A mercy,” Caelindra says.

“A pear, a crust,
and no argument
except the proper amount of sugar.”

“There is no proper amount,” Ashara says. “There is only enough.”

“That is a position you would take,” Rashid says.

“It is a correct one.”

He takes a tart before she can take the last. Ashara looks at the empty place on the plate, then at him.

“You did that deliberately.”

“Of course.”

“Then enjoy it while you can.”

Rashid does, evidently without concern.

The table’s attention settles into smaller conversations. Aldric has opened a book whose spine is stamped with the Academy seal, though he seems to be reading the same two paragraphs without advancing. Caelindra notices.

“Is that Windwhisper’s assignment?” she asks.

“The preliminary commentary.”

“There is nothing preliminary about two hundred pages.”

“It is a survey of the founding arguments.”

“It is six writers making the same objection to a treaty they could no longer prevent.”

Aldric closes the book over one finger. “That is an interpretation.”

“It is an accurate one.”

“The commentary also explains why the treaty held.”

“For whom?”

The words are not loud, but they put a small pause in the table. Rashid rests his cup down. Aldric looks across at Caelindra.

“For more people than a war would have,” he says.

“That is not the same answer.”

Before either can take the thought further, Thessaly reaches for the book. “May I see the page?”

Aldric passes it to her.

She reads in silence, turning the book slightly toward the candles. “The sentence is very long.”

Caelindra gives a short laugh that breaks the pause cleanly.

“It is,” Aldric admits.

“A sentence with seven doors,
and each one locked
with a different key,” Seraphel says.

“That is precisely the problem,” Caelindra says.

“It is also why Professor Windwhisper assigned it,” Aldric says, not quite defending the prose and not quite conceding.

Thessaly hands the book back. “If it has seven doors, it should say which key is for which.”

“You would make an excellent editor,” Rashid says.

“That sounds like labor.”

“It is.”

“Then I would be terrible at it.”

The candle nearest the window gutters when a draft slips through the old frame. Ysolde reaches past it and turns the wick down with a practiced pinch of metal between two fingers. The room smells briefly of warm wax.

Dinner eases toward its end without any formal signal. Plates are cleared. The petition remains folded under Rashid’s plate; Aldric’s book stays closed; Caelindra finishes her pear tart after all. Around you, the talk has become lower and less pointed, crossing the table in fragments while the storm flashes beneath the gardens.
