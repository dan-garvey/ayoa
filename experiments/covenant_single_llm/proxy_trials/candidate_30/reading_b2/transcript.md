**Player**

I fetch the page from my room and return to my chair. "Nell's the one with the interview." I smooth the paper on my knee and read the twelve lines, trying a quicker voice for Nell and a lower one for Ada:

Nell: Hold still. You've caught the lining.
Ada: Let go of the sleeve.
Nell: My interview's at ten.
Ada: And Voss comes at noon.
Nell: If I get this job, we can pay him next Friday.
Ada: He came last Friday. You were out.
Nell: I was looking for work.
Ada: I know. I was the one who answered.
Nell: Give me until eleven.
Ada: The pawnbroker won't take it if you spill anything on it.
Nell: Then come with me. You can guard the coat.
Ada: In what?

I lower the page. "That's all I've got down."

**Story**

The paper makes a soft dry sound as it comes down to your knee. For a few breaths, Seraphel studies the names on it. One hand lies across the harp strings, keeping them mute.

“‘In what?’
is a good place to leave them.

Nell has made a future
out of ten o’clock.
Ada has a man at the door
at noon.

They are both speaking
about the coat,
but not only the coat.”

Her thumb brushes a single low string. The note swells once against the fire’s ticking iron and dies under her palm.

“Your quick voice for Nell
was right.

At first I thought
she meant to win.
Then Ada says
she answered Voss.

Ada has been carrying
the trouble already.”

Seraphel looks back at the page.

“I like that she says,
‘I know.’

People make a sister
cruel or foolish
when they want an easy song.
Yours is neither.

The coat must be respectable,
saleable,
clean,
and in two places at once.
It has a difficult evening.”

The corner of her mouth lifts. She shifts the harp from her knees to the hearthrug beside her rather than wrapping it in its blue cloth.

“When you have more,
I would like to hear it.”
