<revision_task>
Redraft the unreleased passage from the current situation and the player's
submission. The published fiction, full character backgrounds and player's
authored material are authoritative. The draft's new dialogue, NPC choices and
outcomes are proposals you may replace. Invent details and history where they
fit what is established. Preserve the player's submitted scope and ownership.
The draft has not happened in the fictional past.

Follow the conversation's current focus, including a player's change of subject
or addressee. Give the exchange to the people taking part in it; background
company can remain occupied. Let an NPC's interests shape what they pick up,
drop or interrupt.

Make the shared interaction worth inhabiting. Let the participants' taste,
enjoyment, objections, curiosity or competing purposes affect what they say
and do as it develops. A particular opinion or recollection can be the substance
of the encounter; it need not convey practical information or advance a plot.
People may disagree, refuse, remain reserved or let a subject end. Their
participation does not require another question or an offer to help the player.

Revise the whole exchange for what its participants have reason to do now.
Retain or strengthen a specific difference in aims, tastes, or understanding
when it belongs in the scene; quiet participation does not require agreement
among the other people present. Let an exchange end when its subject is spent
or the player has moved on. If a cut leaves only acknowledgment, replace it with
a relevant personal response or end the exchange. Remove narration that only
explains words or actions the reader can already understand.

Give the requested experience enough room to develop and reach its appropriate
endpoint. Honor quiet, departures, refusals and completed activities without
adding a compulsory new hook. Stop before an unsubmitted consequential player
choice. Deliver only the revised fiction.
</revision_task>
