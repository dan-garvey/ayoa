# C43: one sentence can preserve both benefits, with mixed repeatability

One of two augmented edits is a shared success. Both reviewers find that it
reduces irritating rhetorical display and gives Seraphel a personal, engaging
response to the reading. The other augmented edit removes the aphorisms but
mostly explains the text. A fresh short-only edit also succeeds, so the extra
sentence is compatible with the desired result without yet being a demonstrated
reliable improvement over the shorter task. Preserve the successful edit as a
positive result; the unfinished broader narrative goal does not negate it.

The complete 40-word revision task is:

> Revise the previous draft to remove aphorisms from character dialogue. Preserve each character’s individual voice and make the dialogue natural for the situation. Make the exchange engaging through the characters’ particular tastes, feelings, interests, and reactions to what is happening.

Only the final sentence is new: 17 words. The [entire supplied prefix](contexts/reading.txt)
and [original draft](drafts/reading.md) are byte-identical to C42. Full rich
backgrounds, persona, published conversation and current player submission remain
present. No character example, fixed voice recipe, word ceiling or extra editorial
wrapper was added. The user authorized this focused follow-up after C42; that
earlier screen's advancement gate did not block the new request.

The selected prefix is Rowan reading his twelve-line scene to Seraphel after
hearing her songs. C42's two short edits reduced rhetorical display, while both
of that round's reviewers preferred the second long edit for its reaction to
the performed voice and changing feelings. C42's disputed first long edit is
also retained, not omitted to make that choice seem uniformly successful.

In the successful [augmented edit 1](cases/02/00.story.md), Seraphel asks for the
last four lines again because:

> I want to hear
> whether she is angry
> or only tired.

That gives the rereading a particular purpose: she wants to hear a distinction
she has not settled. Her tentative view that Ada has heard the Friday promise
before remains marked as an impression, not a new fact imposed on the manuscript.
The coat carrying Nell through the door is a small image of her hope, not an
unrelated maxim. The response stays plain while providing opinion, uncertainty
and interest in the performed voice. It is 82 words, compared with the original's
138; shorter length itself is not the quality judgment.

The [second augmented edit](cases/05/00.story.md) explains that Nell's invitation
needs another coat and repeats what Ada knows about last Friday. Its rereading
request is legitimate, but the preceding speech feels like an account of the
text rather than a particular person's reaction. Both reviewers mark the rhetoric
as reduced and the personal participation below retainable. This is a partial
success on cleanup, not a second success on both goals.

The strongest fresh long-prompt edit for the independent reviewer is [long 2](cases/04/00.story.md).
Seraphel compares Ada's humor with her own previously performed goat song. The
reviewer sees reciprocal enjoyment and a little pride in her song, giving
distinction and participation 5. Root gives it 4/4/4 and finds "That is praise"
an unnecessary explanation of the joke. Both nevertheless retain it and find
less rhetorical burden than the original. That is additional evidence, on this
same prefix, of the long task producing the kind of fuller interaction the user
wants the short task to preserve.

[Short-only 2](cases/06/00.story.md) is the important control: an expectation,
surprise at Ada's last line, and appreciation of Rowan's actual lower voice.
Both reviewers give it 4/4/4 with reduced rhetoric. The added sentence therefore
cannot be credited as necessary for a successful personal response. Short-only
1 is weaker: its claim that Ada has nowhere to go in the coat appears to misread
her predicament. That is a literary weakness in a listener's response, not an
authored change to the manuscript.
