The separate original draft has useful attention and performance curiosity, but
its paired time/coat interpretations make the response feel prepared. These are
relevant artistic observations, not forbidden knowledge or inherently bad verse.
Its main weakness is how completely the character packages what the scene means.

01 replaces those images with straightforward commentary on Nell's planning,
Ada's worry and the coat's physical use in the argument. That is legitimate
attention to staging. Its "She has nowhere to go / in the coat" is a muddled
account of Ada's predicament, which concerns what she could wear if Nell takes
the only decent coat. A character may misread a play, so this is not a new
canonical fact or authorship violation. Here it weakens an otherwise plain
response, and the generic praise/pause instruction does little to distinguish
the listener. The short instruction still removes the targeted rhetorical display.

02 retains something to hear. Seraphel singles out the last line, offers the
tentative reading that Ada has heard the Friday promise before, and wants to
hear whether the reply is angry or tired. The "I think" makes the prior-promise
idea a fallible impression, not invented manuscript canon. Nell speaking as if
the coat will carry her through the door is a local image of her hope, not a
general lesson. That small figure does not undo the more ordinary conversation.
The request grows from her reaction and her interest in hearing a performance.
It need not copy the selected long reference's anger/sympathy arc to match its
quality. This is the clearest augmented success on both requested objectives.

03 supplies a changing allegiance: initially wanting Nell to take the coat,
then reacting to Ada having answered the door. The account of Voss being in the
room all week is a more dramatic interpretive image than the short edits supply;
the manuscript establishes his visit, not his remaining there literally. Root
reads it as Seraphel's expression of the pressure of that visit, not a new
narrator-established event. The line is conspicuously composed and can distract.
Nevertheless, not knowing whose side she favors and wanting the next page
provide an actual listener's response. Its engagement is stronger than its
success at reducing the original's rhetorical display.

04 notices the same turning point, then compares Ada's humor with Seraphel's own
goat song, which was performed in the published conversation. This is grounded
personal enjoyment and a welcome connection between the activities. "That is
praise" unnecessarily explains the joke, and "I would leave her there" sounds
like workshop advice, but neither imposes an ending on Rowan's manuscript. The
whole response is retainable. The long task can also produce both less rhetoric
and a more engaging encounter in this sample; it is not a uniformly bad control.

05 gives concrete attention to the joke and Ada's prior encounter with Voss.
Its repetition of what Ada knows and its explanation that the plan requires a
second coat remain closer to analysis than to a person's unfolding response.
Wanting to hear the pause is a real listening interest, and the response is
plainer and useful. It does not recover as much personality as 02 or the strong
long reference. The addition therefore has mixed repeatability in this screen.
