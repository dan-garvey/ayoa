The book settles back into its place among the public volumes. From the library, the garden door opens onto pale paths and flowering beds held warm behind their low walls.

A stone bench beneath a spreading silver-leafed tree faces away from the house. The storm lies beyond the garden walls as a blue-white brightness, distant enough here that the air carries only the damp scent of earth and crushed herbs. The tea gives off a thin curl of steam at first, then cools in the cup.

For half an hour, the garden remains empty around the sheltered bench. Lamp-globes brighten one by one along the paths. A small moth circles the nearest globe, vanishes into the leaves, and returns.

When the tea is finished, the paths lead back through the common room, where the fire has burned lower and no one is seated before it. Upstairs, your room is as it was before dinner: the desk lamp lit, the primer waiting beneath the window, and the framed letter facing the made bed.
