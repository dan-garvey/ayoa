The library smells of lamp oil and old bindings. Its eastern study tables stand beneath the iron spiral stair, where the light falls cleanly across their scarred wood. The open shelves nearest them run from annotated Covenant law to student histories of the Sundering, their titles stamped in several scripts.

Rashid arrives with his packet and three students from the practice group. He keeps his voice low enough for the reading room.

“This asks for supervised access while the report is pending,” he says, setting the sheet flat between them. “It does not say who caused the injury.”

A Military-track student with a split lip still healing looks down at the page. “If I sign, they will say I am blaming the instructor.”

“They may say it. The request does not.” Rashid points to a line near the bottom. “Read it before you put your name there.”

The student reads. So does a woman with scaled hands in an Arcana coat, who takes the pen first. Another name follows after it. The student with the split lip folds his arms instead of reaching for the sheet.

At the western tables, Caelindra has spread her notes into a precise half-circle and does not look up. Rashid gathers the page once the two signatures are dry, thanking each signer with the same brief formality.

The sheet gains two names without coming toward the shelves where you stand. Above the legal commentaries, a worn brass marker reads HUMAN COVENANT RECORDS; beyond it, the public volumes are few.
