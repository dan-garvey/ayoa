The house holds the day’s warmth strangely: old masonry at your back, garden air slipping in around the lifted sash. Below the window, pale paths thread between flowerbeds toward walls that blunt the storm wind. Beyond them, one bridge burns white above the cloudbank, where lightning travels without thunder.

The room has been made ready for a Garvey heir. An Academy primer waits on the desk beneath the window, its ribbon markers dividing public histories from the dinner’s formalities and the dense printed clauses of Article Nineteen. Clothes sent ahead by the lawyers hang in the wardrobe, untouched. Across from the bed, a framed handwritten letter addresses a great-nephew who never came.

The carefully mended charcoal of your coat is a darker shape against the room’s orderly furnishings. The Garvey signet catches the lamplight at your hand.

On the ground floor, chairs scrape over the dining hall’s stone threshold. Voices drift up through the stairwell: one low and measured, another brisk enough to cut across it, then a line delivered in rhyme. Candlelight moves along the landing below.

A server in blue Academy livery appears at the open doorway, carrying a small brass lamp. “Mr. Garvey. Dinner is being set.”

They remain in the hall, leaving the stair and the gathering below in view.
