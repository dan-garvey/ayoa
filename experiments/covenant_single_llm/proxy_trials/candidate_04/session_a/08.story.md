When the ink has set enough to travel without marking the pages, you gather the copies and leave the Commerce building. Garvey House is quieter at this hour. No one is in the common room beyond the portraits and the fire's low movement in the grate.

The library lamps are lit. Caelindra sits at a ground-floor study table beneath the iron stair, alone with two open volumes and a narrow sheet of notes. Her silver hair has been pinned back from her face; one pointed ear shifts when you approach, though she finishes the line she is writing before looking up.

At the offer, her gaze moves to the copy in your hand. “A comedy,” she says, as if testing the category.

She closes neither book. “I am occupied at present. Leave one.”

When a copy is placed beside her notes, Caelindra squares its edge with the table rather than opening it. “You printed it yourself?”

The question remains between the lamplight and the stacked pages. Beyond the library windows, the gardens have gone dark except for their paths.
