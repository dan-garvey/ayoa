Rashid answers with a brief nod and returns to his papers.

The quadrangle is nearly empty by the time you cross it. Lamp-globes hold steady above the mixed stone and timber of the paths, while the storm moves far below the terrace walls. The Commerce building's green-glass awning casts a muted color over its open west door.

Upstairs, the north corridor smells of paper dust, lamp oil, and the sharper trace of metal polish. Three press-room doors stand shut behind wired glass. Outside the middle one, a brass box holds the booking ledger beneath a small notice:

FIRST USE REQUIRES PRESSKEEPER APPROVAL. ALLOW TWO HOURS FOR TYPESETTING, PROOF, AND RUN.

The ledger is ruled by hour and machine. Tomorrow's afternoon page has been filled heavily around the student-journal slots. The first unclaimed block long enough for a two-page job runs from the fourth bell to the sixth; the next begins after evening meal. Each line asks for a name, a brief description of the work, the number of copies, and the amount of paper requested.

In red ink at the foot of the page, the presskeeper has added: FIRST-TIME BOOKINGS HELD UNTIL MORNING CONFIRMATION.

The fourth-bell line remains blank beneath the corridor's quiet lamps.
