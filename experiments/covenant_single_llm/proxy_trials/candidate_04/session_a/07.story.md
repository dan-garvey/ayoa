Morning instruction gives way to the afternoon without delaying the approved slot. At the fourth bell, the middle pressroom is unlocked and waiting.

Its hand press stands beneath a high window, dark iron against the pale wall. The cabinets of type line one side of the room; a proofing table, ink slab, and drying rack occupy the other. You set the two pages, letter by letter, and lock the forms into place. The first pulls come out crisp enough to read under the work lamp.

Both proofs pass through your hands before the final run. The corrections you mark are made at the case, then checked again against the manuscript. When the press is inked for the twelve copies, its platen comes down evenly and releases without a hitch.

By the end of the sixth bell, twelve fresh copies rest in order on the drying rack. You wash the ink from the slab and rollers, return the used type to its labeled drawers, and leave the proofing table clear. The middle pressroom has its former smell again: paper, oil, and metal cooling in the afternoon quiet.
