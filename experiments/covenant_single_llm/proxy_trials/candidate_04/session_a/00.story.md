The evening bell has not yet sounded, but Garvey House is beginning to gather itself below. Chairs draw against the dining-hall floor; a burst of conversation carries up the stairwell and folds into the older hush of the house.

Your room is orderly in the way a place prepared for a stranger can be. The bed has been made with too-tight corners. Clothes bearing unfamiliar tailoring hang in the wardrobe. On the desk, the Academy primer lies open beside the framed letter addressed to a great-nephew who never arrived. Its last marked page gives the welcome dinner's hour, then Article Nineteen in neat, impersonal type.

Beyond the window, garden lamps have begun to wake among pale paths. Farther out, one of the hard-light bridges runs from the plateau into the dark, its edge steady above the storm.

Two brisk taps sound at the door.

“Garvey?” calls a man's voice from the corridor. “Rashid vel Amara. I thought I should make sure you knew the route. Dining hall is downstairs, through the common room.”

There is a brief pause on the other side of the door. From below comes the faint clink of plates being set at the long table.
