By the time the servers clear the last bowls, the court dispute has not returned. Rashid has spoken with Aldric only about the morning's seminar; Ashara leaves her coat draped over the back of her chair until the room has thinned around it.

At your question, Rashid pulls his attention from the notes he has begun sorting beside his plate.

“Commerce stays open until the tenth bell,” he says. “Cross the quadrangle and take the building with the green-glass awning. The press rooms are on the second floor, at the end of the north corridor.”

He considers the rest of it before adding, “The booking ledger is mounted outside the door. You can put your name down for an available session, but the presskeeper has to approve a first use. Her office is beside the rooms; she will be gone tonight.”

Rashid tucks his papers away. “If you want to see the arrangements, you have time. If you want the machine running, come back after morning instruction.”

Beyond the dining-hall doors, the common room is quieter now. Firelight catches along the portraits and the dark banister leading toward the house entrance; through its tall front windows, the lamp-globes of the quadrangle have fully kindled.
