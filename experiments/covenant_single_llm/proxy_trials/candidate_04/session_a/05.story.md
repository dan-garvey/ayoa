Your entry takes its place in the ledger beneath the fourth-bell heading: Rowan Garvey, twelve copies, two pages, twenty sheets including proofs. The red-ink notice about morning confirmation remains at the bottom of the page.

By the time you return to Garvey House, the quadrangle lamps have dimmed to small moons over the paths. The common room is empty except for the fire settling in the grate. Upstairs, your room holds the same prepared quiet it had before dinner.

The finished manuscript goes on the desk beside the Academy primer. Its pages contain the short comic account of an apprentice, a lost umbrella, a sequence of mistaken identities, and a wedding reached by accident. You set out what will be needed for the morning, leaving the pages flat beneath the lamp.

Night passes without interruption.

Morning light comes pale through the garden-facing window. Somewhere below, a door closes and voices begin to carry through the house as its residents wake. The manuscript waits on the desk; across the quadrangle, the presskeeper's office will be opening before the afternoon booking.
