After breakfast, the quadrangle has filled with students moving between the residence halls and the morning buildings. The Commerce corridor is quieter. A painted sign beside the press rooms reads MERA DELL, PRESSKEEPER.

Her office door stands open over a scatter of paper samples and brass type held in shallow trays. Mera is bent over a ledger when you arrive, sleeves rolled past her wrists. She takes the manuscript after hearing the details of the booking, then checks the page count and the entry copied into her own book.

“Two pages, twelve copies,” she says. “Twenty sheets will cover the run and leave room for two proofs, provided you print both sides. That is what this press is for.”

She turns one leaf just far enough to inspect its margins, then sets the pages square on her desk. “Fourth to sixth is enough time if you have worked a hand press before. You will use the middle room. The type cabinets are labeled; return every sort to its case when you finish.”

Mera reaches for a small stamp, presses it beside the booking in her ledger, and writes a short approval beneath it.

“Bring the manuscript back at fourth bell. If the proof shows a problem, you will have time to correct it before the run.”

The approved booking sits open on the desk between the trays of type, while the first bell for morning instruction begins to sound across the quadrangle.
