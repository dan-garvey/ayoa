Seraphel comes through the doorway after a moment, carrying her tea in one hand. Her small wings settle close against the white-and-gold fabric at her back as she crosses to the cabinet. From it she chooses a narrow-bodied lute, tests two strings, and sits in a leather chair opposite the fire.

Ysolde takes the chair near the window. Thessaly remains by the dining-hall threshold, her cup held between both hands. Rashid stays long enough to lower the lamps nearest the study tables, leaving the fire and the nearer wall lamps to light the room.

Seraphel plucks a quiet opening pattern. Then she sings:

“Bread on the board and boots by the door,
The road may wait for morning's shore;
Keep one lamp against the rain,
And welcome home the lost again.”

The tune is plain and unhurried, carried by the lute more than by force of voice. She repeats it once, changing the last chord into something softer before letting the strings fall still.

For a few moments, only the fire answers. Thessaly sets her cup on a side table. Ysolde looks from the dark window back to Seraphel.

“That is a good song for a house,” Ysolde says.

Seraphel lowers her gaze to the lute and gives its strings one last, gentle touch.
