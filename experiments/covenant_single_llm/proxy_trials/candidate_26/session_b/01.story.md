The argument breaks cleanly on your name.

Firelight takes up most of the common room, catching in the varnish of the long tables and leaving the Garvey portraits high on the walls in a dim procession of collars, brooches, and serious mouths. The horned woman nearest the hearth turns first. Her black-red horns sweep back from severely tied hair; one hand rests on the mantle, and the tip of her tail has curled around a chair rung.

“Not at all,” she says. “Ashara vel Kothren. You have arrived during an argument with limited culinary value.”

The man across from her closes a leather folder over his notes. Fine dark fur shows along his knuckles against the pale paper. “Rashid vel Amara. I contend it has a great deal of culinary value, because I was promised supper before it began.”

“You were promised supper whether you began it or not,” Ashara says.

“Then I was right to begin it.”

The gold-haired young man standing near the fire gives you a measured inclination of his head. “Aldric Verantus. Welcome to Garvey House, Rowan. We have had a few days to make the place untidy before you saw it.”

Beside him, a woman in white and gold folds her small wings close against her back. Her smile reaches her eyes before she speaks.

“Seraphel Dawnquill.
The house has waited;
we have merely arrived first.”

At the window, a white-haired woman looks from the storm to the armchairs. Her skin has the cool, pale cast of moonlit stone. “Ysolde Thornmantle. The chair by the window is sound. The one beside it is not.”

“Thessaly Morrow,” says the small woman seated cross-legged on the carpet near a stack of game pieces. Her blue-black hair drifts slowly about her shoulders though the room is still. “I am not responsible for the court, or for Rashid's timing.”

The last woman has a book open across one knee, its gilt-edged pages reflecting the fire. She closes it with a fingertip tucked between the leaves. Silver hair falls over one shoulder as she looks up.

“Caelindra Vaeyn. I fear you have joined at an inauspicious moment, but supper remains salvageable.”

“The court is closed because someone was injured,” Aldric says, with the careful tone of a point resumed rather than introduced.

“The covered court is closed,” Rashid replies. “A group of students has lost its place to practice, and the people who made the decision have not asked them what happened.”

Ashara takes her hand from the mantle. “He is collecting signatures for a request for supervised access. He has not yet collected enough information to make it a good request.”

“I have enough to begin.”

“Tomorrow, then,” she says. “After you have eaten.”

Seraphel glances toward the dining hall doors as a server passes behind them with a covered silver dish.

“The soup will not wait
for our agreement.”

That settles the room more effectively than another objection. Rashid slips the folder beneath his arm. Aldric gives a small, conceding nod, and Ashara steps away from the hearth.

Beyond the open doors, the dining hall is bright with lamps and candles. A long varnished table runs beneath dark paneling, set with seventeen places though only a handful of chairs have been drawn out. At one place, a white card bears your name in black ink: **ROWAN GARVEY**. The servers have put fresh bread in a shallow bowl beside it, still steaming when torn.

Caelindra pauses at the threshold and looks back from the table. “What precisely did the primer tell you about the Conclave?”
