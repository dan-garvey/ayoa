Thessaly looks at the bird beside your crown, then at the closed gate.

“Most people do,” she says. “The first gate feels like progress because it is open. The third feels like progress because it is nearly enough. The return is where the board becomes rude.”

At your invitation, something in her expression shifts into a small, pleased smile. “Another evening is possible. After sleep is sensible. I will tell you if I have already promised someone a game.”

She opens a cedar box with fitted compartments and begins indicating where the pieces belong. The black towers go into a felt-lined row. The pale crowns fit into narrow slots at the far end. Each red bird has a separate hollow carved to its shape.

“Vessa taught me,” Thessaly says as the last tower is put away. “She kept accounts in my grandmother's house. When I was small, I interrupted her until she put this board in front of me and said I could speak again after I found a route home.”

She closes the lid over the pieces. “I found one. Not a good one.”

The fire has burned down to a red, steady bed beneath the logs. For a while, the common room asks nothing more than a chair and its warmth. Rashid's two visitors sit opposite him with their heads bent over the petition; once, one of them objects to a phrase, and Rashid crosses it out without argument. Beyond the garden windows, rain begins against the glass in a fine, even pattern.

Thessaly stays at the small table, turning the closed game box once beneath her hand before setting it on the shelf beside the other games. She does not fill the quiet.

When you wish her goodnight, she looks up.

“Goodnight, Rowan. Keep the stairs with lamps on your first evening. The other ones are less useful.”

The main stair has been left lit, each brass wall lamp throwing a warm oval across the runner. From the second-floor landing, the common room is only a muted glow below and the rain is softer behind the garden-facing windows. Your room waits at the end of the corridor with its green desk lamp still burning over the supper card and the unopened lawyers' envelopes.
