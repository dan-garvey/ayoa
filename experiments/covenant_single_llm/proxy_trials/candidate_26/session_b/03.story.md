Caelindra taps the cover of *The Mapmaker's Widow* twice with one lacquered nail.

“Then it may suit you. The author tells the reader where the map is in the second chapter. Not plainly, of course, but fairly. The pleasure is watching everyone become distracted by a more theatrical possibility.”

“A tiger,” Rashid says.

“A tiger is a theatrical possibility.”

“And an excellent one,” Ashara says.

Caelindra looks at you again. “You may borrow it when I have finished. Do not lend it onward, do not bend the spine, and do not write in the margins. I want it back.”

“Those seem like modest terms,” Thessaly says. “You should state the last one twice.”

“I will remember it,” Caelindra says.

Rashid sets down his spoon. “After supper, two students from the practice group are coming to the common room. I promised I would read the request with them before I ask anyone else to sign it. Then I intend to find out whether the instructor's report has been filed.”

“That is more sensible than beginning with a petition,” Aldric says.

“It is what I said I was doing.”

“You said several things,” Ashara replies. “Some were more useful than others.”

Rashid gives her a look that does not quite dislodge the corner of his mouth. “You have a ranked bout tomorrow. Do you not have a blade to worry over?”

“I have three.” Ashara tears a piece of bread, then considers it before eating. “I will spend an hour at the open ground after dinner, while it is empty. My opponent favors a high cut he thinks no one notices. Then I will sleep.”

“He notices,” Rashid says.

“He notices that I notice. It has not helped him.”

Ysolde lifts her gaze from the windows, where a thread of lightning travels soundlessly behind the garden walls. “I will be on the west terrace. The storm has shifted north twice since sunset.”

“Does that affect anything here?” Aldric asks.

“The bridge lights alter when it reaches the ridge. Also, the terrace is less crowded when rain begins.”

“A compelling itinerary,” Caelindra says.

“It is not an itinerary. It is where I will be.”

The small bell beside Aldric's setting gives a soft chime as a server sets down the next dish. Aldric glances toward the chapel windows visible beyond the far doors.

“Dusk prayers begin shortly,” he says. “Afterward, I have reading to finish.”

Seraphel rests both hands beside her bowl.

“It has at least one,” Aldric says.

“It needs two.”

Thessaly begins arranging the abandoned game pieces from the common room in a neat row beside her plate: little black towers, pale wooden crowns, and a pair of red glass birds. “I planned to play *Seven Gates* with someone who can bear losing a round without revising the rules. That leaves me with a smaller field than I had hoped.”

“You enjoy announcing a challenge after the game has begun,” Rashid says.

“I enjoy the rules being present.”

“I intend to read,” Caelindra says, sliding her book farther from the edge of the table. “Possibly in the library, if the lamps have been filled. The last person there left enough soot on the spiral stair to make the handrail unpleasant.”

“The library is easy to find from here,” Ysolde says. “The stairs beyond the common room go up to it. The other stairs go to rooms and eventually nowhere useful.”

“There are portraits upstairs,” Thessaly says.

“That is not useful.”

“It may be to him.”

Caelindra folds her hands over the blue cover and looks down the table at you. “Which of those counts as an achievement you want tonight?”
