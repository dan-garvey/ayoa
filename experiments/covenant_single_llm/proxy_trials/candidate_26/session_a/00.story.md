The inherited signet catches the last of the garden light whenever you turn your hand. It is too ornate for the carefully mended charcoal at your shoulders: a heavy ring, a formal crest, and a name that has only recently become yours to carry. On the desk beneath the window, a framed letter takes up more room than it ought to. Its close, slanting hand was written for a great-nephew's visit long ago; the glass over it has a haze of dust at the corners.

A serving bell sounds somewhere below, one clean note through the house. Then voices rise through the open stairwell.

“I am asking for supervised access,” a young man says, each word placed with care. “Not a key to the court.”

“You asked after I told you my booking was not available,” a woman answers. Her voice is low and precise. “That makes it a request for my time, Rashid.”

“It makes it the only time the group has while the Academy considers its report.”

Another man's voice enters, measured enough to make the interruption conspicuous. “The student who was hurt deserves caution, at least until someone establishes what happened.”

“And the rest of them deserve an answer,” Rashid says.

For a moment there is only the soft clink of tableware. Then a woman's voice, light and plainly pleased with itself, says in short lines,

“The petition can wait.
The broth, I fear,
cannot.”

Chairs shift below. The corridor outside your room runs toward the stair, where firelight from the common room flickers across the banister. Past it, the dining hall stands open and bright, with the welcome dinner already beginning to gather its guests.
