“Nine, then. Thank you.”

Ashara gives a short nod and returns to her plate. Dinner continues through pear tart and a second pot of tea. Rashid keeps his pencil tucked beside his napkin; when the plates are cleared, he folds the notes into his inner pocket. Around the table, the first easy silences begin to take hold between the talk.

For half an hour afterward, you sit in a leather chair in the common room with the primer open beneath a lamp. Its clipped sections cover formal introductions, bridge permissions, the public history of the Covenant, and Article Nineteen's wording. The passages on Academy life leave room for a private recollection of delivering parcels to Jory Hale's fencing school, and for wondering whether lessons like that could ever be within your means. No one hears the name or the thought.

By the time you go upstairs, the house has quieted. In your room, you bring the framed letter close to the lamp and read it through. The salutation is to another named great-nephew. The letter is ordinary and precise: a planned visit, a room to be aired, a borrowed book to be returned, a reminder to bring warmer boots. It closes with the affectionate impatience of someone expecting a young relative who never arrived.

You set the frame back on the desk, put your coat aside, and get ready for bed. The lamp is turned low when the room finally goes dark.
