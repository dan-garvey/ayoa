The primer remains on the desk beneath the framed letter. Downstairs, the common room smells of old leather warmed by the fireplace, and the dining hall beyond it is bright with lamps and candles. Seven people occupy a small stretch of the long table; the rest of its places wait under polished plates.

At the doorway, you pause long enough to find an empty chair. “Good evening. I'm Rowan. Am I late?”

“No,” says the horned woman nearest the door. Ember-red skin catches the candlelight along her forearm as she draws back the chair between her own and that of a slender, white-haired woman. “We were merely finding ways to postpone soup. Sit.”

You take the offered chair. The horned woman settles back into hers. “Ashara vel Kothren.”

Across from her, a wolf-blooded young man rises just far enough to make the greeting formal. Fine dark fur shows at his cuffs. “Rashid vel Amara. Welcome, Rowan.”

“Rashid has a petition,” Ashara says.

“I have a practice group without a court,” he replies. “The petition is a consequence.”

“Aldric Verantus,” says the golden-haired man beside him. His white-and-gold sleeve has been kept clear of the candlewax. “I hope Garvey House has given you what you need.”

The white-haired woman at your other side regards the bowl set before her, then the window latches along the far wall. “Ysolde Thornmantle. The upper rooms are cold after midnight. Lift the latch before you turn it, or it sticks.”

“Caelindra Vaeyn.” The silver-haired elf has arranged a napkin with exact corners beside her plate. “The house has survived several generations of students, despite its efforts.”

The small woman with blue-black hair tilts her head toward a basket at the table's center. “Thessaly Morrow. The dark bread is better than it looks.”

Last, the angel with honey-blonde hair folds her hands beside her untouched place setting. Her voice comes in soft, unrhymed lines.

“Seraphel Dawnquill.
Welcome, Rowan Garvey.
The bread is sound.”

A server in Academy livery sets a bowl of onion broth before you, steam rising around sprigs of thyme. Rashid reaches for the bread basket without looking away from Ashara.

“Tomorrow, then. I will ask the people who train.”

“You should have begun there,” Ashara says.
