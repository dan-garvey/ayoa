The tile you choose carries a straight painted road with a small turn at its far edge. It fits against the central square without forcing either road to stop short. You set a blue lantern on the left entry circle and move it two marks along the new stretch, toward a gate painted with a silver key.

Thessaly studies the board before selecting her next tile. She gives her amber lantern a curve toward the moon gate, then moves it the full two spaces and lifts it away when it reaches the painted arch.

“One lantern home,” she says, setting it beside the box. “It is usually best to return them before the roads begin behaving badly.”

The clear routes narrow as the map spreads over the tablecloth. Your first blue lantern reaches the key gate on its next turn. A second follows a branch you open toward the gate marked with a green tower. Thessaly sends another amber lantern through a longer road that winds around the center rather than competing for the nearest crossing.

When you try to carry a blue lantern past one of hers at that crossing, she puts a fingertip beside the occupied mark.

“Not through that one. You may stop one space short, but you cannot pass it.”

You leave the lantern where the road allows and alter the next move. Thessaly watches the correction settle into the board, then places a tile whose fork gives both colors a way outward.

“The card makes that rule sound obvious because the person who wrote it had already lost an argument about it,” she says. “They revised the wording, but not enough.”

The game goes on beneath the common room lamp while the apricots steadily disappear. Your blue lanterns take the shortest open roads when they can; when a route closes, you begin another at the entry circle or turn a tile to keep the map from folding back on itself. Thessaly is less direct. Twice she places a road that lengthens her own way in order to leave a crossing empty for a later lantern.

“You prefer a road that goes somewhere at once,” she observes as you return your third blue lantern. “That makes a clean map. It also makes you easy to predict.”

By the last few tiles, three blue lanterns and three amber ones stand returned beside the board. Your remaining lantern is one marked space from the green tower, but Thessaly's last amber lantern has a clear line to the moon gate. She moves it two spaces, lifts it through the painted arch, and sets it with the others.

“Four to three,” she says. “Your last one would have arrived next turn.”

She begins sorting the road tiles back into the lacquered box, leaving the board in place until the final blue lantern's route is clear again.
