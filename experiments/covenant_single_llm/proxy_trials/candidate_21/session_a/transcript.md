## Opening

**Player**

My character: Rowan Garvey. A twenty-three-year-old human man with brown skin, unruly dark hair, and ink-stained fingers, wearing a carefully mended charcoal coat and the inherited Garvey signet. Newly arrived and unsure of Conclave customs. He grew up unaware of his heritage, lost his parents young, and does not know whether he has any magic.

Begin the story shortly before the welcome dinner, in my room at Garvey House. I play Rowan Garvey.

**Story**

The green-shaded lamp makes a hard circle on the desk, catching the edges of the Academy primer: welcome dinner at dusk, seven signatories, Article Nineteen set in heavier type than the rest. Below, Garvey House is discovering its evening voice—china touches china, a chair protests across the floorboards, someone laughs without restraint.

A single knock sounds at your door.

“Rashid vel Amara,” says the voice beyond it. Warm, controlled, and near enough to the wood that it carries clearly. “I thought I would spare you an entrance into a room that has already decided seating is a constitutional matter. We are about to sit. Would you like company going down?”

Candlelight reaches under the door from the landing.

## Turn 1

**Player**

I open the door. “Yes, please. Rowan. I know almost no one here, so I'd be glad of the company.” I leave the primer on the desk and join Rashid on the landing.

**Story**

Rashid’s mouth bends at one corner. “Then we have a practical arrangement. I know their names; you know which parts of the Academy brochure are nonsense.”

He falls into step beside you, his dark-furred hand loose on the carved banister. The dining hall below is bright with candles and the scent of charred citrus. At the long table, a horned woman is disputing the wording of a faculty notice with a gold-clad young man; a silver-haired woman watches the candle flames rather than either of them. Somewhere among the places set for dinner, a spoon taps once against a glass.

Rashid stops beside an empty chair halfway down the table. “This is neutral territory, as far as I can tell. May I introduce you from here?”

## Turn 2

**Player**

“Please do.” I take the offered chair and greet the people he introduces. Once food is served, I eat and listen to the conversation around the table for a while.

**Story**

Rashid makes the introductions with an efficiency that saves everyone an awkward speech. Ashara vel Kothren answers your greeting with a short nod; Aldric Verantus stands properly for it. Ysolde Thornmantle says only, “Ysolde.” Caelindra Vaeyn’s violet eyes travel from the inherited signet to your face before she returns a formal greeting.

“Seraphel Dawnquill,” says the angel with the small white wings.

“Welcome to the table.
May the soup be worth the ceremony.”

“Thessaly Morrow,” says the woman beside her. “It usually is.”

Servers bring bowls of saffron broth and crusted bread. As you eat, the argument from the far end resumes.

“A temporary closure is hardly tyranny,” Aldric says.

“No,” Ashara replies. “It is merely easier than finding out what happened.”

Rashid sets down his spoon. “I am asking for supervised hours for the group, not Ashara’s private booking.”

Ysolde looks up. “What caused the injury?”

“Dena’s wrist was hurt. We have accounts from the students. The instructor’s report has not been released.”

Ashara tears bread cleanly in half. “Then get the report before you collect signatures.”

## Turn 3

**Player**

“I don't know enough about the court to put my name to anything tonight. I hope you get an answer.” I finish dinner, listening rather than joining the argument. Afterward I go into the common room and take an unoccupied chair near the fire. I have no particular plan for the evening; I'm happy to see what the others do.

**Story**

Rashid inclines his head. “Fair enough. I’ll have something worth reading tomorrow.”

The disagreement survives the rest of dinner without becoming louder. Aldric calls caution a duty; Ashara calls it an excuse only when no one has bothered to investigate. By the time the plates are cleared, neither has yielded an inch.

Footsteps scatter through Garvey House afterward. Rashid takes the faculty notice and a sheaf of paper to a study table. Ashara carries a narrow practice case upstairs. Aldric leaves by the garden door, white-and-gold sleeves bright in the dark beyond it.

The fire in the common room gives the portraits above it a shifting, disapproving light. Thessaly arrives with a black-backed deck of cards and settles at the low table opposite your chair. She deals two neat hands, then looks up.

“Seven Stones,” she says. “No stakes. Do you know it?”

## Turn 4

**Player**

“No, but I'd like to try.” I lean forward to see the cards. I listen to the rules, then play a complete game with her, trying to win with the cards I'm dealt. If I misunderstand a rule, I accept her correction and continue.

**Story**

Thessaly slides seven smooth black counters into a line between you and deals the cards.

“Seven tricks,” she says. “If a suit is led and you hold that suit, you must follow it. Highest card of the led suit takes the trick. Four counters wins. There is no trump.”

The cards carry small painted marks—leaf, wave, feather, flame—and numbers one through seven. She leads a blue wave card. On the third trick, you reach for a flame while a low wave remains in your hand.

“That one first,” Thessaly says, tapping the wave with one pale finger. “The rule does not become inconvenient merely because the flame is larger.”

She waits while you exchange the cards, then takes the trick with a six. The next four go back and forth. By the sixth, three counters sit before each of you. You keep a feathered seven until the last hand, but Thessaly leads the suit you have already spent; her flame five takes the fourth counter.

She gathers the cards into a precise stack. “Four to three. You learn quickly enough. Most people lose the first game by trying to win every hand.”

## Turn 5

**Player**

“Thank you for the game.” I set my cards back with the deck and look over toward Rashid's study table. “Do you want a break? There's room over here.”

**Story**

Rashid looks up from the study table, then at the fire. Ink has smudged the side of one furred hand.

“A break would rescue this draft from me,” he says.

He brings the papers over and takes the chair nearest the hearth. The top page has been rewritten twice; the current heading reads *Request for Supervised Court Access* in a hand almost too precise to be relaxed.

“And thank you for saying so plainly that you would not sign,” he adds. “Decorative support would make a worse petition.”

Thessaly pushes one black counter toward the papers. “It has already lost twice.”

“Only twice?” Rashid says, and the corner of his mouth lifts. He taps the page with his pen. “Could I read this aloud? I need to know whether it says what I mean before I put it in front of anyone.”
