Rashid waits until the second course has thinned, then sets a folded sheet beside the salt.

“The covered court is closed until further notice. Tomorrow I am taking a request for supervised evening access to the provost. It will be in the common room if you wish to read it.”

“I will read it,” Aldric says. “A temporary restriction is not automatically unjust.”

Ashara sets down her fork. “Nor is it automatically sensible. I will sign a request for supervised access. My booking tomorrow remains mine.”

“I did not ask for it,” Rashid says.

The sheet stays between the salt and the wine while dinner moves on to bridge weather and a story Rashid tells against himself about arriving at a duel in mismatched boots. “I still won,” he says, “but only because my opponent laughed long enough for me to recover.”

Later, your good-evenings carry you upstairs with the house settling around you. Under the lamp, the letter yields its small particulars: it is dated 14 Leafturn, 641, and addressed to Elias. Mara Garvey signs herself his great-aunt. She has aired the blue room, ordered lemon cakes because he liked them as a child, and asks him to bring the notes he mentioned so that his uncle may pretend not to care before keeping him talking past midnight.

The primer's forms of address are followed by a stiff little timetable. Tomorrow's first Conclave seminar begins at the ninth bell in the oval seminar room: Covenant Law, with Professor Elara Windwhisper.
