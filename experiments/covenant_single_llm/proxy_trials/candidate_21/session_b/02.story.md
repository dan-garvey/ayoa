“Rashid vel Amara,” he says, giving the chair a small push toward the table. “And I will remember: garden.”

Ysolde chooses the place opposite you, where the window and the door remain equally visible. A liveried server begins setting down covered dishes; when the bread basket reaches you, you pass it along before it can settle in anyone’s hands.

“The gardens tolerate visitors,” Ysolde says. “The herb plots on the west wall tolerate help. The roses do not. They have made that clear to several people.”

A woman with silver hair looks up from the far end of the table. “They are roses, not legislators,” she says, with the weary precision of a correction. Her violet eyes settle on you. “Caelindra Vaeyn. Have you actually read the primer they gave you, Garvey, or only its title?”
