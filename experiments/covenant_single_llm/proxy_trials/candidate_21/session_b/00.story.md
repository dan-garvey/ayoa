The dinner bell is not a bell at all: three clear notes from somewhere below, bright enough to set the water glass on your desk singing in reply. Beside it, the framed letter catches the lamp-globe's light. The handwriting begins with a name that is not yours and continues, in a warm, unhurried hand, with plans for a visit that never happened.

Someone knocks at the half-open door. Ysolde Thornmantle stands in the hall in dark blue, bare hands clasped behind her back; the warmth from the lamp seems to stop just short of her.

"They have begun deciding where everyone ought to sit," she says. "It may be useful for you to arrive before they reach agreement."

From the stairwell, a man's voice carries up. "Rashid, stop arranging the chairs as if the furniture has offended you."

Ysolde tips her head toward the stairs. "The dining room is below. I was going there."
