The paper gives a soft crackle as you lower it. Seraphel does not look away at once. The blue cloth from the harp has slipped loose across the hearthrug, and one bright string still catches the fire through the narrow space beneath her hand.

From the dining hall comes the muted work of the servers: porcelain meeting porcelain, a chair leg eased rather than dragged across the floor. No one comes through.

Then Seraphel says,

“‘In what?’ is a good end.

It leaves Ada
with only that question.

She knows Nell looked for work.
She knows Voss is coming.
She is not saying
that Nell did nothing.”

Her thumb touches the lowest harp string. The note is small and dark under the crackle of the fire.

“Ada has been answering doors,
I think.

That is why she is angry.
Not only because of the coat.”

She glances at the page again, not reaching for it. The corner of her mouth lifts briefly at the last line.

“And Nell says ‘Give me until eleven’
as if an hour were a thing
she could put in Ada’s hand.

I like that.
It is hopeful.
It is also unfair.”

The fire settles inward. Outside the tall window, pale lightning moves somewhere below the gardens, too distant to make a sound in the house.

Seraphel shifts the harp from her knees and lays it carefully in its blue cloth. Her wings make a faint dry whisper against her sleeves.

“Do not make them agree too soon.

Ada may be right
and still be frightened.

Nell may be trying her best
and still ask too much.”

She looks toward the dining-hall door as another dish is lifted away, then back to you.

“Bring the next page
if you want a listener.

I want to know
whether Ada goes with her.”
