Seraphel comes down the stairs beside you, her folded wings clearing the banister by an inch. The dining hall opens below in candlelight and working lamps: a long varnished table, seventeen places set beneath dark paneling, only eight of them occupied. The high-backed chair at its head remains empty. Your place waits halfway down the garden side, marked with a small card in black ink.

Conversation breaks only long enough to make room for your greeting.

“Thank you for having me.”

The gold-haired young man nearest the empty chair rises neatly from his seat. “Aldric Verantus. Welcome, Rowan Garvey.”

“You are welcome,” says the small woman with black eyes and blue-black hair. Her smile arrives a moment after the words. “We had an empty chair, not a quarrel.”

“Thessaly Morrow,” she adds.

“Rashid vel Amara,” says the man beside her, drawing your chair clear with one furred hand. His cuffs are immaculate despite it. “And the chair is less dangerous than that sounded.”

At the far end, the horned woman with ember-red skin gives you a brief nod. “Ashara vel Kothren.”

The pale woman across from her sets down her glass. “Ysolde Thornmantle.”

Silver hair catches the lamp-light as the elf beside Ysolde inclines her head, no more deeply than politeness requires. “Caelindra Vaeyn.”

Seraphel takes her place again.

“The table is named.
The meal may now
stop performing grief.”

Ashara’s mouth moves at one corner. “A sound rule.”

The serving dishes make their way along the table without ceremony: peppered lamb dark at the edges, glazed squash with its sweet char still steaming, lentils in brown butter, black bread torn warm from a covered basket. You take what the others take and pass what they pass. The first few mouthfuls give your hands something to do besides rest near the unfamiliar weight of the signet.

Rashid reaches for the sauce.

“A dangerous confidence,” Ashara says.

“It has garlic.”

“It has a military future.”

Ysolde studies the copper dish for a moment. “It has separated.”

“That settles it, then,” Thessaly says.

“It does not settle anything,” Caelindra replies. “It produces factions.”

Rashid tastes the sauce anyway. “I stand by it.”

“You would,” Ashara says.

The conversation runs on around the food, not quite avoiding the fact of you, but not pinning you beneath it either. Names begin to acquire their places: Rashid’s deliberate courtesy, Ashara’s heat across the table, Aldric’s sleeves without a wrinkle, Ysolde’s stillness, Caelindra’s attention that seems to settle only where it chooses. Seraphel folds a corner of bread between her fingers while Thessaly arranges lentils into a small, precise line at the edge of her plate.

“Tomorrow begins with Covenant Law,” Aldric says. “Professor Windwhisper will expect us to have read the first Accord.”

“He arrived today,” Caelindra says.

“The primer includes it.”

“The primer includes summaries.”

Ashara tears bread in half. “He will need the text by nine.”

Rashid glances from her to Aldric. “He will also need dinner.”

Seraphel lifts her eyes.

“That is cruel,” Aldric says.

“It is only cruel if you prefer being wrong vaguely,” Thessaly tells him.

For a moment, even Caelindra looks amused.

Ashara sets down her knife. “Rashid. The octagon after dinner. Half an hour.”

Rashid laughs once, helplessly enough that Ashara’s tail makes a quiet turn beneath the table.

Caelindra has been listening without much sign of it. Now she looks across the breadboard at you, violet eyes steady.

“Did the primer say anything useful about us?”
