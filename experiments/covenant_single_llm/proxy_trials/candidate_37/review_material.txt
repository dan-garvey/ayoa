The Garvey signet on your hand catches the desk lamp whenever the house shifts its light across the room. It is an unfamiliar sort of announcement: not loud, exactly, but difficult to put away.

Below, Garvey House is gathering itself around supper. A chair leg scrapes over old wood. Someone laughs once, abruptly, and another voice answers too low to separate from the murmur of the dining hall. Seraphel's mouth turns at one corner.

"Rashid vel Amara,\nwho has arrived hungry.\nAshara vel Kothren,\nwho has arrived armed with criticism."

The downstairs conversation continues with the scrape of a serving dish. Seraphel does not call further introductions down the hall. The sentence sits between the soft motion of the curtains and the storm-light at the window. Downstairs, someone begins describing a sauce with great indignation; a second person says, with patient precision, that salt is not an insult to the cook. Seraphel steps back from the doorway and leaves the stair clear.

"Would you like company going down?"
