The cloud closes over the bridge before the last of its sideways light has faded. Beyond the garden wall, the storm is dark again.

“I've never seen anything like that,” you say quietly to Ysolde. “Thank you.”

Ysolde looks from the window to you. “You are welcome. The west current should last until morning.”

“How fortunate,” Caelindra says. “A whole night for the bridge to appear improperly assembled.”

“It is assembled properly,” Ysolde says. “You are standing in the wrong place to judge it.”

Caelindra gives the window another look, as though considering whether to take this personally.

“From above the cloud, it is better,” Ysolde continues. “The light is on the rain then.”

Rashid has paused with his fork halfway to his mouth. “Have you flown over it?”

“Not here.” Ysolde settles her spoon beside her plate. “When I was twelve, I followed harbor lamps through sleet because I thought they were gulls. They bent in the wind much the same way.”

Ashara looks up. “You mistook lamps for birds?”

“From far enough away.”

“And then?” Rashid asks.

“I landed on the beacon roof.”

For the first time since you sat down, Ysolde's stillness gives way to a small movement of one shoulder.

“It held. The keeper did not want me to test it again.”

Rashid laughs, the sound brief and warm. Ashara's tail gives a slow sweep against the side of her chair.

Seraphel rests her chin lightly on one hand.

“A wise keeper,
with one good roof
and one young dragon.”

Ysolde inclines her head as if this is a fair accounting. The bridge remains hidden, and the conversation loosens around the little story instead of trying to pull anything more from it.

You finish the rest of your meal at an unhurried pace. The green sauce keeps its sharpness against the browned meat, and candlelight shines in the varnish of the long table. A server replaces the emptied dishes with flat pear tarts, their sugared tops breaking under the first touch of a fork, and a bowl of thick cream.

Thessaly watches the server set down the bowl. “May I have another tart?”

“Of course,” the server says.

“Then I will.”

The answer arrives so promptly that Caelindra glances at her. Thessaly takes the second plate without embarrassment and begins on it with the same attention she had given the first.

“You dislike the tarragon,” Ysolde says to Caelindra. “You do not dislike pears.”

“I dislike poor pears,” Caelindra says. She tastes a bite and looks briefly less guarded. “These are acceptable.”

“High praise,” Rashid says.

“Do not make it ceremonial.”

At the far end of the table, the folded request stays beneath Rashid's water glass until the dessert plates are nearly clear. He moves it into his folio, then checks the small clock set into the dining-room paneling.

“Four minutes,” Ashara says.

“I heard you the first time.”

“Then you have no excuse.”

He stands, folio in hand. Ashara rises after him and follows through the open doors into the common room, where firelight throws the Garvey portraits into uneven gold. Their voices lower as they take the nearer study table; a pen scratches, stops, and scratches again.

Aldric lays down his napkin and pushes back his chair. “I should be at the chapel.”

Seraphel lifts her cup toward him.

“May the lamps burn straight,
and the prayer be short.”

“The lamps will,” Aldric says. “The prayer may disappoint you.”

He goes out into the hall, white and gold passing beneath the lamps one by one. Caelindra finishes her tart, retrieves a slim book from the chair beside her, and makes for the library without waiting for anyone to accompany her.

Ysolde remains by the window with one hand resting on the back of her chair. A sheet of lightning opens beneath the plateau. For a few seconds the western bridge returns above the storm, its lamps casting long pale strokes eastward through the dark.
