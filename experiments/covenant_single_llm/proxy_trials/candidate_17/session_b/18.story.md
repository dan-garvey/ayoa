Ysolde nods once and sets off along the path at an unhurried pace. The garden wall holds back most of the wind, leaving only a cool current that stirs the flower heads against the stone. She stops once beneath a dragon-carved arch to watch a pair of dark-winged birds circle above the far end of the bridge, then continues when they disappear into the clouds.

The north terrace opens beyond the last arch: black stone underfoot, a low rail, and the storm spread below in slow sheets of white light. From here the bridges are not routes so much as bright strokes drawn from the Academy into distance. The gardens lie behind you in orderly color, their paths threading between walls and glass lamps.

Ysolde rests two fingers on the rail.

“I look for currents first. Birds cross them differently than walkers, and the clouds reveal paths that streets do not. I also look at the eastern ridge; from above it has a fold that never shows from the ground, and from here the bridges make it easier to remember.”

The birds reappear beyond the nearest span, banking together where the cloudbank thins.
