“No. I know the house, not the people who belonged to it. I have seen every portrait downstairs often enough to recognize the faces, but I have never heard anyone say what a Garvey was like when there was no audience. If the letter carries a name, I would ask Stone before I trusted a lawyer.”

She takes the stairs at an unhurried pace. At the ground floor, the common room opens around the fireplace in pools of lamplight, its leather chairs occupied by coats, books, and no one. The portraits along the far wall have been given small brass plaques, but their faces are too distant to make much of from the stair. Beyond the archway, the dining hall shines with candles and polished wood; the long table is set for more people than have yet found their seats.

Near one end, a brown-skinned young man in an impeccably cut dark jacket turns from a conversation with a gold-haired student in white and gold. Fine dark fur catches the lamplight over the first man’s knuckles. The second keeps a folded faculty notice flat beneath one hand.

“Rowan Garvey, I presume,” the first says. “Rashid vel Amara. Before dinner, I’m collecting names for supervised use of the covered court; you are welcome to hear why before deciding whether it deserves yours.”

He extends a single sheet just far enough for its heading to show beneath his thumb: **Request for Supervised Covered-Court Access.** Ashara stops beside the nearest empty chair, her attention moving from the page to the faculty notice and back again.
