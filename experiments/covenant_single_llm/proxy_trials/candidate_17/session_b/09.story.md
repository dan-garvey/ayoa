“Eight tomorrow. My seminar ends at half past seven, and I need the half hour afterward to undo its damage.”

Stone closes the drawer on the sleeved card and returns to the essays. By the time the blue ink has lost its shine, the library has settled into its later quiet. You thank him, carry the copy and Miriam’s frame upstairs, and set them together on the desk: the older letter leaning against the lamp, the fresh page beside it.

The common room is dimmer than it was before dinner. The fire has burned down to a red, steady bed beneath the logs, with one leather chair left open close enough for warmth. Ysolde occupies the chair nearest the window, white hair silvered by the stormlight, a closed book resting on one knee. At a low table nearby, Thessaly has arranged small black and ivory tiles into a careful grid.

You take the open chair. Ysolde shifts the book farther from the edge of the hearth.

“That one is less likely to scorch your coat. The storm is moving east, so the window will not rattle for another hour.”

Thessaly turns one of the ivory tiles between two fingers. “I am playing Nine Terms. The rules are printed on the box, and they mean what they say. You may watch without taking a turn.”

The tiles click softly against the wood. Beyond the firelight, the portraits of the Garveys remain watchful along the wall, while rainless lightning opens and closes over the gardens.
