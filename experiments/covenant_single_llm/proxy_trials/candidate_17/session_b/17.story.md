At eight-forty, Rashid folds away the map and stands. The small group leaves Garvey House together: Rashid and Seraphel beside you, with Ashara and Aldric joining at the garden path. The mist has lifted enough to show the pale route across the quadrangle, though the storm still makes a moving ceiling beyond the Academy roofs.

The gold circle on the schedule proves useful. Beneath the white-marble colonnade, the Conclave seminar room holds its oval table and its fifteen chairs beneath framed Accords in seven scripts. Chancellor Ashworth begins with names, schedules, and the practical fact that every student will need to learn where the faculty offices are before needing them. He leaves the history of the Covenant to the courses that follow and dismisses the group with a stack of timetables, access cards, and a reminder about the evening’s introductory reception.

The day gives the Academy shape in pieces. Professor Kira vel Shaan’s Practical Rhetoric class begins with a motion about meal allowances and asks each student to separate a claim from its evidence and its request. In Covenant Law, Professor Windwhisper takes a trade clause apart word by word until an apparently harmless phrase about seasonal passage proves to govern three different obligations. Between rooms, the corridors acquire names: chapel stair, east arcade, low dragon arch, garden gate. Your notes fill with arrows, room numbers, and the names of people who speak quickly enough to make a second line necessary.

By the end of the afternoon seminar, the wind has changed from a hard pressure against the windows to a cool current moving through the quadrangle. Students filter out beneath the black demon-stone arches and toward the gardens. The nearest bridge is clear enough to see from one end to the other, its light unbroken over the storm below.

Ysolde waits near the north-side path with her hands in the pockets of a pale coat. She is looking up at the clouds rather than toward the seminar door. When you emerge, she turns.

“The wind is down. The north terrace will be clear for perhaps an hour, and I have brought no book. I thought we could look at the view.”

The path beyond her curves between the garden wall and a row of low dragon-carved arches.
