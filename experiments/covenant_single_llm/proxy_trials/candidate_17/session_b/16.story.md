Rashid pulls a narrow timetable card from inside the folded map and scans it between sips of tea.

“Evening. Stone’s seminar begins at six and ends at half past seven; he holds it in the lower library. Eight is correct, and he will have time to get upstairs and make himself disagreeable before you arrive.”

Seraphel breaks off a piece of toast.

“His morning belongs to shelves,
his evening to questions,
and eight is what he meant.”

The porridge has been kept warm beneath a silver lid, with stewed plums and a small dish of honey set beside it. Around the table, morning noises gather without much ceremony: cups set down, pages turned, a server asking whether anyone wants more tea.
