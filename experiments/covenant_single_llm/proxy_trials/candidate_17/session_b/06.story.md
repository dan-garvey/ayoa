Stone’s expression settles as you speak. He takes off his spectacles, polishes one lens with the corner of a handkerchief, and puts them back on before answering.

“No. Miriam died four years before you were born. Your father wrote me once, the winter after your birth, with a small card saying that you had arrived and that he had named you Rowan; I kept it because he sounded happy, and because I had forgotten how much I missed good news.”

He opens the shallow drawer beneath the desk’s writing surface and moves aside a stack of marked essays. From underneath them he takes a cream card inside a clear paper sleeve, its corners worn soft with age. He lays it beside the open book rather than reaching for the frame in your lap.

On the front, in a careful hand unlike Miriam’s, are four words: **For Gareth Stone, personally.**
