Stone moves the ivory queen one square and studies the board as if the answer might have changed since yesterday.

“By the end, she could correct him move for move. Your mother, Lysa, collected bad maps—not merely old ones, but maps made by people with theories about places they had never seen. One spring she spent an afternoon comparing three maps of the same bridgehead, laughed hard enough at the third to spill tea across it, and repaired the page before letting anyone else see what she had done.”

He sets the queen back where it stood. The lamp lays a clean circle over the board, the marked essays, and the quiet length of the desk between you.
