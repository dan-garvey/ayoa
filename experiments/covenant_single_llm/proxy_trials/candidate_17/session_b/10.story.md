Thessaly gives the nod back, then squares the box beside the board.

“Then watch. I will not explain each move unless you ask.”

The rules are bluntly printed beneath the lid: build a square of nine ivory tiles; after every third placement, set a black limit against an exposed edge. Completing a row returns one limit to the box. Three blocked rows end the game.

Thessaly begins with an ivory tile marked by a small key, then adds a wave and a lantern without comment. On her third move she places a black tile along the bottom edge, its lacquer catching the firelight. The room stays quiet around the soft clicks of wood. Ysolde opens her book but looks up whenever lightning spreads white across the window.

For a while, the game supplies its own small progression. Thessaly builds two rows, gives one back to the box, and pauses over the seventh ivory tile with her thumb resting on its corner. She chooses the lantern again rather than the crown. Two more turns complete the final row before the third black limit can close it.

“A win,” Thessaly says, studying the neat square. “Not an interesting one, but a win.”

The window gives a faint tremor in its frame. Ysolde looks toward it.

“Earlier than I expected. The wind has changed.”

Thessaly gathers the tiles into the box one by one, leaving the warmth of the fire and the weather’s distant light to fill the common room.
