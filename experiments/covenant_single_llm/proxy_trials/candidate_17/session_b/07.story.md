Stone slides the card across the desk and turns it so its opening faces you.

“Of course. Take it from the sleeve if you prefer; the paper has survived worse than a pair of hands. I kept it because I thought someone should be able to say your parents were happy.”

Inside, the card carries only a few lines in the same careful hand as the address.

*Gareth—*

*Good news first: Lysa and I have a son. His name is Rowan, settled after an argument in which both of us behaved badly and the midwife wisely refused to vote. He is loud, healthy, and has a fist strong enough to make a promise feel binding.*

*When he is old enough to travel, we will bring him to the house. If the garden room is still there, I want him to see it. If it is not, we will find another room and make trouble in it.*

*I hope you are well.*

*Elias Garvey*

The lamp makes the ink gleam faintly against the cream card. Stone sits back from the desk, leaving the small circle of light and the quiet around it undisturbed.
