Stone sets aside the essays nearest the lamp and draws a sheet of heavy cream paper from a desk drawer. He uncaps a blue-ink pen, tests it once in the margin of an old marking sheet, then places both beside the card.

“Of course. The ink at your elbow will hold, and I would rather you copied it than carried the original through a storm. Come tomorrow after my seminar, if you like; I can tell you about your father losing a chess game to his own bluff, and your mother declining to rescue him.”

The card stays between you and the lamp while you copy. Its brief lines take longer by hand than they did to read, each stroke of Elias Garvey’s signature settling into blue ink on the fresh page. When you finish, Stone waits until the copy is clear of the card before he eases the original back into its sleeve.

He caps the pen and returns it to its place among the papers. Your copy remains on the desk in the warm circle of light, the last line drying beneath the name you have only just learned.
