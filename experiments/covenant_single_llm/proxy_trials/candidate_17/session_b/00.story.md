The framed letter leans against the lamp on the desk, its narrow hand faded to brown except for the salutation: *To the great-nephew I never expected.* The desk itself has been polished too recently; beneath it, one leg wears a furred cuff of dust where the cloth missed. A blue primer on the Covenant lies among the lawyers’ papers, its red ribbon caught halfway out. Beyond the window, garden lamps blink on under the early dusk, making pale paths through the flowerbeds. Farther out, one of the hard-light bridges stretches into the violet weather like a held breath.

Below, a chair scrapes in the dining hall. Someone says something too low to make out, followed by a quick laugh. The house carries the sound without disguising it. A knock lands against your half-open door, once, then again.

The woman in the hall is tall enough that the doorframe makes an awkward border for her swept black-red horns. Heat comes off her in a small dry shimmer despite the cool stone corridor. Her black hair is tied severe; a long tail lies still against the line of her dark coat. She looks past the threshold only long enough to find you, then gives a brief nod.

“Rowan Garvey. Ashara vel Kothren. Dinner is downstairs, and I am on my way there. Would you prefer to go in with someone who knows which door is which?”

Beyond her, the corridor turns toward the stairs. Candlelight moves over the banister; beneath it the common room smells of woodsmoke, rosemary, and a butter-rich sauce. A pair of voices reaches the landing from below, one low and clipped, the other answered by a soft clink of glass. Ashara waits in the hall, one hand resting near the stair rail.
