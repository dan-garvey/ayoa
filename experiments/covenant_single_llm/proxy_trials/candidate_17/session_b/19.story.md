The birds keep to the thinning edge of the cloudbank, banking sharply where the current catches them and flattening their wings when it lets them go. You remain at the rail with Ysolde until the light below the terrace begins to turn from white to a softer grey.

When you thank her on the return path, she glances once toward the terrace behind you.

“So am I. The wind kept its promise.”

Later, the upper gallery is lit by the same single lamp as the night before. Stone’s desk is clear of visitors; a small chessboard sits beside the stack of essays, its pieces arranged in the middle of an unfinished game. He looks up when you reach the edge of the light.

Stone turns a black bishop between two fingers before setting it back on the board.

“Your father had his queen trapped in a game against me and announced that he had arranged it to force mate in four; I asked him to demonstrate. He made three moves beautifully, then found he had counted the bishop as white rather than black. Your mother accepted the draw he offered and let him explain his plan for the rest of the week.”

The bishop stands at the corner of the board, its dark polished head turned toward an ivory queen.
