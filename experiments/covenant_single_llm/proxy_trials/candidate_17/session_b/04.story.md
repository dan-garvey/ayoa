“Then take it while it is warm.
A useful rule is improved
by pear and thyme.”

Seraphel lifts her spoon with a small, approving tilt of her head. You thank the server and try the soup. It is pale gold beneath a spill of green oil, sweet at first and then sharp with thyme; steam fogs the lower edge of the glass lamp above it.

The rest of the table settles by degrees. Name cards give the white-haired woman near the window as **Ysolde Thornmantle**, and the silver-haired woman examining the bread basket as **Caelindra Vaeyn**. A small figure with blue-black hair and entirely black eyes takes the place beside Seraphel, reads the menu card as if it might contain a trap, and sets it down again. Her card says **Thessaly Morrow**.

Rashid does not reopen the petition during soup. When the bowls are cleared, he tucks it beneath the faculty notice and looks across at Aldric.

“Tomorrow I will ask the instructor for the report and write the request around supervised sessions. If it turns out we were careless, I will put that in it. If the court or its rules caused the injury, the Academy should say so.”

“Then I will read it before you ask anyone to sign,” Aldric says. “It is easier to object to a thing before it has become everyone’s proof of bad faith.”

Ashara reaches for the bread basket. “Do it after dinner.”

The main course arrives on platters: saffron rice, roasted roots glazed dark at their edges, and thin slices of fish laid over fennel. When a dish comes within reach, you help pass it on. The motion travels around the long table in small exchanges of hands and thanks; for a while there is nothing asked of you except whether the rice has reached the next person.

Ysolde looks from her plate to the storm-dark window. “Those pears came over the southern bridge this morning. It will be closed by midnight; the pressure is already moving at the lower anchors.”

Caelindra considers a slice of fennel. “Then the kitchen has earned its drama for once. If the next delivery is delayed, perhaps it will learn restraint.”

“The bridge will reopen,” Thessaly says. “The pears will not care whether we call it drama.”

Seraphel laughs softly into her napkin. Further down the table, Rashid describes an entire rhetoric seminar spent finding polite names for a refusal. Ashara says he made it seven minutes before abandoning the exercise; Rashid says that seven minutes was generosity. Even Aldric’s mouth shifts at that, though he turns back to his plate before it becomes a smile.

By the time the servers bring small dishes of candied ginger and cream, the petition has disappeared into Rashid’s jacket and the faculty notice into Aldric’s. You leave the letter’s name unspoken. The welcome dinner loses its formal shape one emptied plate at a time, until the last tureen is carried away and chairs begin to scrape back from the table.

Ashara glances through the archway toward the library. “Stone’s upper-gallery lamp is lit. He usually keeps that desk after dinner; whether he feels like company is another matter.”

Beyond the common room, the iron spiral stair catches the remaining candlelight.
