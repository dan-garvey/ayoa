My character: Rowan Garvey. A twenty-three-year-old human man with brown skin, unruly dark hair, and ink-stained fingers, wearing a carefully mended charcoal coat and the inherited Garvey signet. **Story**

The lamp on the desk has been trimmed too high. Its flame makes the framed letter beside it look newer than it is, though the ink in its salutation has gone brown: a dead aunt addressing a great-nephew who was never meant to arrive. The Academy primer lies open nearby, its tidy print stopped at Article Nineteen. Across the room, the made bed holds its untouched shape. Rain stipples the window, and beyond the garden walls a bridge of hard light hangs like a white stroke over the black storm.

From downstairs comes the uneven music of preparations: china set on wood, a server's low question, then laughter from the dining hall. It breaks off when a door swings shut.

Three quick knocks sound in the corridor. After a moment, a man's voice says, "Rowan Garvey? Rashid vel Amara. They've begun pretending the seats downstairs do not matter, which means they matter very much. May I show you down?"

Rashid heads for the chair nearest the open transom, where cool air slips into the warm room. Aldric is standing at the middle of the table with a small stack of place cards. In white and gold, he lifts the card beside his own setting and leaves the chair clear.

"I put you here, Garvey," he says. "You should not have to lean across half the table while everyone asks after your family. Does that suit you?"

## Turn 3

**Player**

**Story**

He says he will circulate a request tomorrow for supervised access, not an unattended reopening; Ashara says the request should demand the instructor's report before anyone decides what the injury proves. "Nine Lanterns is three counters each," Thessaly says. "You place one on an empty lantern each turn until they are all down; after that, move one along a line to an empty neighboring lantern. A straight line of three lights a lantern, and two lights win."

She sets the black counters before herself and pushes the green and ivory sets toward you, then turns the empty board squarely between you.

## Turn 4

**Player**

I take the green counters. "How do we keep score once someone has made a line—can they break it and make the same line again?"

**Story**

The green counters are cool and heavier than they look, each one polished into a low round dome. Thessaly gathers the unused ivory set back into its fitted groove and draws a small dish of brass score-pins from the far side of the board.

"No. When a line is made, its player sets a pin in the line's notch, and that line cannot score again even after the counters move away. You may still use its lanterns to make a different line."

She places one pin across the empty middle row to show where it would go, then returns it to the dish. Her black counters wait at her side of the board; the nine lanterns between you are still unclaimed.

## Turn 5

**Player**

"You go first." I play one complete game with her. Whenever I can make a line that has not scored, I try to do that; otherwise I try to block her next scoring line, and if neither is available I move toward an open line. I keep to that strategy through the end of the game, asking her to correct me if I try an illegal move.

**Story**

Thessaly begins at the center lantern. During the placing rounds, your green counters take two corners of the upper row, and when the third can make an unpinned line, it goes between them. The first brass pin lands across that row with a small, satisfying click.

After that, the board grows less forgiving. Thessaly moves her black counters patiently through the open lanterns, never crowding the green row that has already scored. Each time an unpinned green line becomes possible, you take it; when she leaves two black counters poised for a line, you turn one aside. Once, reaching for a better opening, you begin to carry a green counter past an occupied lantern. Thessaly puts one finger on the board.

"One step," she says.

The correction costs no turn. You make the adjacent move instead, and the game continues beneath the library lamps. A few exchanges later, Thessaly creates two black threats from the same shift. You block one; her next move completes the other and brings the score level. The pattern returns later in a different part of the board. With no green line available and two black ones opening at once, you can close only one of them.

Her final counter settles into place. She sets the second black score-pin in its notch, then begins collecting the pieces without hurrying. Two black pins and one green remain across the dark wood.

"You saw the first threat," Thessaly says. "The second was already there."
