After dinner, the library table beneath the iron stair is waiting as Thessaly promised. The travelogue lies between you, its cracked leather cover bowed from use. She opens it near the middle, where a page has been crowded from edge to edge by a second hand in brown ink. For a moment she reads with one fingertip holding the place.

"This is a good disagreement," she says. Then she reads, "We followed the Low Haren west for three days, passing orchards and a slate quarry before the river gave out among the hills."

Her finger moves into the margin. "The river runs north from the sluice gate. The quarry is east, and no one with sense drinks below it in spring."

The correction continues in a narrow column beside the printed chapter, less concerned with being polite than with making the route usable.
