By the time the stew bowls are empty and the bread basket holds only crumbs, Rashid has taken a folded sheet from inside his jacket and set it beside his plate. The talk turns to the covered court. He says he will circulate a request tomorrow for supervised access, not an unattended reopening; Ashara says the request should demand the instructor's report before anyone decides what the injury proves. Aldric agrees to that much, adding that a temporary restriction need not become a permanent one.

Rashid folds the sheet again. The conversation moves on before it can settle anywhere useful: which bridge has the worst evening wind, whether the kitchen puts saffron in everything it serves, and a story from Caelindra about a court tutor who mistook ceremonial moss for a dessert garnish. She tells it with great irritation and enough detail that Rashid laughs anyway.

When the servers begin clearing the last plates, Thessaly closes the lacquered box. You follow her through the common room into the library. The lower floor is quiet except for the tick of cooling lamps and the soft scrape of her chair at a study table beneath the iron stair. She lays out a square board of dark wood, nine small lanterns inlaid across its surface, then sorts the counters into their colors.

"Nine Lanterns is three counters each," Thessaly says. "You place one on an empty lantern each turn until they are all down; after that, move one along a line to an empty neighboring lantern. A straight line of three lights a lantern, and two lights win."

She sets the black counters before herself and pushes the green and ivory sets toward you, then turns the empty board squarely between you.
