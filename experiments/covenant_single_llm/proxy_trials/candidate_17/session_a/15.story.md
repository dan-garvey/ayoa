Thessaly closes the travelogue carefully over the disputed road. "I would like that. I will keep it with the game box, where I will not misplace it."

The next pages make a quieter back-and-forth: a correction that saves a village from being placed on the wrong side of a river, another that objects to an author's description of a bridge, one that seems to have been written purely because its owner disliked the author's handwriting. At last the lamps are low enough that the words blur at their edges. Thessaly keeps the book when you say good night. Upstairs, rain and the dark window carry the house through the rest of the night.

Dressed, you go downstairs to a dining hall that smells of coffee and toasted oats. Rashid is already at the table in a fresh jacket, his folded request tucked beneath one hand. He listens to your question without interrupting.

"The clerk allows observers. The group is comfortable with listeners, provided no one turns it into a speech about the human seat. Come to the covered court at three, if that works for you."
