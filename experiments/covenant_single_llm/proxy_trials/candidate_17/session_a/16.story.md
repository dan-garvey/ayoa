Classes carry the morning into afternoon: argument forms in Practical Rhetoric, treaty exceptions in Covenant Law, a lunch taken quickly enough that the last bell finds you crossing the quadrangle. Shortly before three, the covered court is already open to a narrow line of observers. Its packed-earth floor lies empty beneath the high canvas roof; the practice group gathers along one side, with Ashara and Aldric among the students who have come to hear the decision.

The court warden, in plain grey Academy livery, stands beside a clipboard at the edge of the ring. "The instructor's report is incomplete, and the equipment inspection is not finished. I will not reopen an unsupervised court on guesses."

Rashid speaks from the group without turning toward the benches. "We are not asking for unsupervised time. We are asking for an instructor schedule that people without private tutors can use."

A demon student with a splinted wrist rises beside him. "I do not want my name printed on a petition. I do want people to stop treating my wrist as an excuse to close everything." Her hand stays braced against the bench as she sits again.

"The current restriction has made a private booking the only option left," Ashara says. "That is not a safety policy; it is a price."

Aldric waits until she is done. "Trained supervision is reasonable until the report arrives. Put an end date on it, so temporary does not become permanent."

The warden makes a note, then looks over the signed request. "Beginning tomorrow, the court will offer two supervised open sessions each week without a private-instruction fee. Outside those sessions, the closure remains in place while the report is completed. We will review the arrangement ten days after that report is filed."

No one calls it a victory. Rashid folds the request into his jacket; the splinted student takes a place near the sign-up sheet, and the observers begin to leave the benches. The court remains empty for the afternoon, but the first supervised session is posted for the next day.
