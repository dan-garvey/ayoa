The rest of the hearing day passes without another claim on your time. Garvey House settles into its evening traffic of doors, stairs, and distant voices; outside, the storm keeps its slow light beneath the windows.

The following day brings its classes, then open hours spent following the Academy's routes past the low dragon-carved arches of the quadrangle, the chapel's white stone, and the sheltered gardens. By evening, the lamp-globes have come on again over the paths when you return for the shared meal.

The first supervised court session has already taken place by dinner. Rashid, with earth still caught at the edge of one cuff, tells Aldric that the warden brought two assistants and that every available place filled. The conversation stays with practical details: which hours students can reach after lessons, whether the posted list is readable, who will ask for another session if the demand holds.

Later, the common room gives you an hour of quiet reading. The fire has been built low; chairs and study tables gather separate pools of lamplight beneath the Garvey portraits. Thessaly crosses through once with the battered travelogue under her arm and a book-marker tucked between its pages, then leaves the room without interrupting. When the hour is done, the house has thinned to creaks and the weather beyond the glass. Upstairs, sleep comes without ceremony.
