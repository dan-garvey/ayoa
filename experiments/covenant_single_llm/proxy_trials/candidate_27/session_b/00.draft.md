Evening turns the garden paths below your window the color of old bone. Beyond them, a bridge of hard light holds steady over the storm, its far end lost in cloud. The careful repair at the cuff of your charcoal coat catches the lamplight in a dull silver line. Your inherited signet gleams against the ink on your fingers. Across the room, a framed letter in a gilt frame has been hung beside the desk: close, sloping handwriting addressed to a Garvey great-nephew with another name.

Below, a dinner bell gives one soft note. A chair leg scrapes against the dining hall floor.

“Your booking is tomorrow, Ashara,” says a man's voice through the open stairwell.

“Precisely. I need it.”

“Everyone needs it.”

“Then don't speak of my hours as though they're yours.”

“I haven't.”

“Not yet.”

For a moment there is only the clink of serving spoons and the low rush of the storm outside. Then two deliberate knocks sound at your door.

“Rowan Garvey?” says a young woman's voice. “I'm Thessaly Morrow. I was told you had arrived, and I am going down to dinner. The table is long enough to be a small problem. Would you like to enter it with company?”
