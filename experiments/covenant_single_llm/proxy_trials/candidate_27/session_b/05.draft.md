You set your glass beside the chair, clear of the hearthrug, and settle in to listen. Seraphel shifts one hand higher on the harp. The next tune comes quicker than the first, with a light, skipping figure beneath the melody. When you catch it, your finger begins to mark the beat against your knee.

“A courier came to Bellford Gate

with letters sealed in blue;

a piebald goat came down the hill

and followed after too.

At Fordside Inn it ate his bread,

at market, ate his twine;

the courier saved the letters, but

the goat drank half his wine.

The mayor rang a brass bell twice

and named the goat his clerk;

the courier took his double fare

and went back to his work.”

Seraphel lets the final chord tremble through the room, then quiet beneath her palm. The fire gives a low pop behind its screen. She looks down at the harp for a moment without speaking.
