Evening has blued the garden paths below your window. Beyond their pale walls, a bridge of hard light reaches over the storm and vanishes into cloud. The careful repair at the cuff of your charcoal coat catches the lamp in a dull silver line. Your inherited signet has caught a stripe of light against the ink on your fingers. Beside the desk, a framed letter hangs in its gilt frame, close handwriting addressed to a Garvey great-nephew with another name.

The dinner bell sounds once below. A chair shifts across the dining hall floor.

“Will you sign the request, Ashara?”

“I agree the restriction is too broad. I won't sign a draft that uses my booking tomorrow.”

“It shows the court can be supervised.”

“It shows I have a ranked bout.”

“I have statements from six students.”

“You do not have the instructor's report.”

“They won't release it.”

“Then ask them again.”

Two taps sound at your door.

“Rowan Garvey?” says a young woman's voice. “I'm Thessaly Morrow. The dining hall is downstairs, left at the stair. Would you like me to show you?”
