Ashworth lays seven brass counters along the centre of the table: angel, demon, elf, dragon, fae, beastkin, and human. The human counter is unmarked.

“A Council proposal begins with a sitting representative or a standing committee. It is registered, copied to the other seats, and sent to the committee that holds the subject: trade, borders, law, or military affairs. The committee returns it with a recommendation. Only then does a representative move it at the Council table.”

He turns one counter on its edge.

“Ordinary measures pass on four assents. Treaty articles and declarations of war have a separate threshold: every original signatory must ratify. The beastkin seat may introduce a measure, debate it, and vote on ordinary business. On those reserved matters, its assent is recorded but does not ratify.”

Rashid's pencil stops above his timetable. Across the table, Aldric writes without looking up.

“Those are the present rules,” Ashworth says.

He opens the folio to a page headed COUNCIL COMMITTEES and begins listing the routes by which a trade petition, a border complaint, and a treaty amendment reach the agenda.
