The blue-glass lamp throws a small, steady circle across the desk. Your Academy primer lies open beside it, its stamped cover already curling at the corners. Across the room, the framed letter left by a dead Garvey aunt stands beside the made bed: ordinary paper, careful handwriting, addressed to a great-nephew who never arrived.

Beyond the window, the sheltered gardens are pale beneath their lamps. Farther out, one of the hard-light bridges fades into the storm, its white span appearing and disappearing behind sheets of rain that never quite reach the glass. From below comes the clink of laid silver, then a burst of laughter cut short by someone saying, "That is not what happened."

The primer's final section begins with Article Nineteen in severe black type. A place card rests on top of the page, folded tentwise: ROWAN GARVEY. The inherited signet at your hand catches the lamp flame in its dark red stone.

A knock sounds at the door: one measured rap, then another. "Rowan Garvey?" calls a young man's voice, warm and exact. "Rashid vel Amara. I thought you might prefer to meet one person before being introduced to seven at a table. May I come in?"
