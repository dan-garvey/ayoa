"Ten, then. Thank you."

Ashara gives a short nod and returns to her broth. Rashid leaves the petition beside the salt until the bowls are cleared, then folds it into his coat without asking again. The courses that follow arrive in measured intervals: roast roots with dark gravy, thin slices of peppered lamb, a pear tart cut small enough that the servers make two passes around the table.

The court does not vanish from the evening entirely. At one point Ashara says, "Ask the instructor before you take that paper anywhere else."

"I said I would."

"You said you would ask for the report."

Rashid wipes his fingers on his napkin. "Which is why I am asking the instructor."

Aldric lets out a quiet breath through his nose and reaches for the water. Ysolde watches the storm beyond the windows for a while, grey eyes following the white flare of a bridge in the rain. Caelindra leans close to Seraphel, their words too soft to distinguish at the far end of the table; Thessaly listens to neither of them, intent on separating the tart crust from its filling without breaking either. By the time the final plates are carried away, the candles have burned lower and the storm has turned the windows black between flashes.

Afterward, you spend half an hour in the common room with the primer open on your knee. The fireplace has been banked to red coals. The book gives the public shape of the Academy in tidy pages: the five tracks, the hours of the chapel and dueling ground, the permissions required to cross a bridge beyond the Nexus. A chart of the Conclave repeats the seven Council seats and leaves the human column blank except for the Garvey name. The house settles around you with the creak of timber and the distant close of doors overhead.

Upstairs, the framed letter waits beside the bed. The ink has browned beneath its glass, but every line remains clear.

> My dear Tomas,
>
> Your father says you mean to arrive on Friday, so I have had the blue room aired and told Mrs. Pell not to give away the last of the quince jelly. Do bring the bird sketches if you can find them. The gardeners say the kingfishers are back at the lower pond, and I should like to see whether your blue is better than theirs.
>
> Your affectionate great-aunt,
>
> Elspeth Garvey

You read it from salutation to signature. As you set the primer aside and get ready for bed, you remember delivering parcels to Jory Hale's fencing school and wonder whether staying to watch might have taught you anything. You do not mention Jory or the thought to anyone.
