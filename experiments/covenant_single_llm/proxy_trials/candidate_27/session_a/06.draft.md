"Half an hour would be very welcome. I'll be there."

The rest of the morning gives the quadrangle its bearings. The gardens lie beyond the pale paths to one side; the chapel's white doors stand open on another. Low dragon-carved arches lead toward seminar rooms, while a passage behind the dueling ground runs toward faculty offices. At lunch, the storm still presses against the high windows of the dining hall.

Before you return to the ground, you put the Garvey signet in the desk drawer and change into boots with soles that turn against the floor instead of catching. At the south end of the octagon, Ashara is already waiting beside the practice rack. She has exchanged her bout clothes for a dark sleeveless tunic, and two wooden blades rest against the rail.

She looks at the blade you take from the rack. "That one will do. Hold it."

When you do, she circles close enough to tap the grip with one blunt fingernail. "Your hand is too high. Two fingers below the guard. Keep your thumb loose."

She takes the second wooden blade and stands a few paces away. "Left foot forward. Bend the knee. Put the point at my collarbone."

Ashara lifts her own blade into the same line. Her elbow stays soft; the wood points steadily toward you. "Not straight out. If you lock the arm, I can move it."

She presses the flat of her blade against yours. The point wavers. "Again."

For the next stretch of the lesson, she gives you one thing at a time. A turn of the front foot. A hand-width less distance between your boots. The point kept between you and her as she moves right, then left. When the blade follows too far toward her shoulder, she touches the wooden flat to your free arm.

"You are following the blade," she says. "Watch where I can step."

She moves again. You practice the guard, reset your feet, and keep working through the same short sequence while the next bouts rise and fall on the far side of the rope. Ashara corrects the angle of the wooden blade, then makes you take it again without her hand near it.

At the half-hour, she lowers her sword. "Enough. Return that before the rain reaches the rack. Before you take it again, make the grip without looking at your hand."

The practice blade is still warm from your palm as the storm gathers darker beyond the quadrangle.
