"Ten, then. Thank you."

Ashara inclines her head. The petition stays beside the salt through the clearing of the broth, then Rashid folds it into the inside pocket of his coat. Roast roots come next, followed by peppered lamb and a small slice of pear tart.

When the lamb plates are nearly cleared, Ashara says, "Speak to the instructor before you carry that paper anywhere else."

"I will."

"Ask whether the court was examined."

Rashid looks across the table at her. "I intend to."

"And ask for the report," Aldric says. "If he gives it to you, bring it with the request."

"Fine."

The petition remains in Rashid's coat for the rest of dinner. Ysolde watches a bridge flare white beyond the windows; Caelindra turns from a low exchange with Seraphel and asks a server for more water. Thessaly separates the tart crust from its filling without breaking either. By the time the final plates are carried away, the candles have burned low and the windows show only black glass between flashes.

Afterward, you spend half an hour in the common room with the primer open on your knee. The fire has been banked to red coals. Its tidy pages give the public shape of the Academy: the five tracks, the hours of the chapel and dueling ground, the permissions required to cross a bridge beyond the Nexus. A chart of the Conclave repeats the seven Council seats and leaves the human column blank except for the Garvey name. Timber creaks overhead; a door closes somewhere on the upper floor.

Upstairs, the framed letter waits beside the bed. The ink has browned beneath its glass, but every line remains clear.

> My dear Tomas,
>
> Your father says you mean to arrive on Friday, so I have had the blue room aired and told Mrs. Pell not to give away the last of the quince jelly. Do bring the bird sketches if you can find them. The gardeners say the kingfishers are back at the lower pond, and I should like to see whether your blue is better than theirs.
>
> Your affectionate great-aunt,
>
> Elspeth Garvey

You read it from salutation to signature. As you set the primer aside and get ready for bed, you remember delivering parcels to Jory Hale's fencing school and wonder whether staying to watch might have taught you anything. You do not mention Jory or the thought to anyone.
