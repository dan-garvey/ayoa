Rashid squares the petition against the salt cellar as you leave it unsigned. "All right," he says. "I'll ask the instructor for the report."

"Give it to me with the request," Aldric says. "I want to read both."

"You will still need a supervisor," Ashara says.

"I know."

"And it will not be me tomorrow."

Rashid's hand remains on the edge of the paper. "I heard you."

The servers finish their circuit with the broth. Caelindra accepts pepper from the nearer one; Thessaly tears her bread into even pieces beside her bowl. For a few minutes there is only the soft click of spoons against china, the rustle of Seraphel's tucked wings, and conversation too low to make out from the far end of the table.

When there is a pause, you turn to Ashara. "Is tomorrow's bout open to spectators? I'd like to watch."

Ashara sets down her spoon. "Yes. Ten bells at the octagon. Use the north rail."

"The east side catches glare in the morning," Ysolde says.

"Stay behind the rope," Ashara says. "If the referee calls a hold, don't speak to either fighter."

Rashid breaks off a piece of bread. "You'll have a clear view from the north."
