## Opening

**Player**

My character: Rowan Garvey. A twenty-three-year-old human man with brown skin, unruly dark hair, and ink-stained fingers, wearing a carefully mended charcoal coat and the inherited Garvey signet. Newly arrived and unsure of Conclave customs. He grew up unaware of his heritage, lost his parents young, and does not know whether he has any magic.

Begin the story shortly before the welcome dinner, in my room at Garvey House. I play Rowan Garvey.

**Story**

The blue-glass lamp makes a small island of light across the desk. Your Academy primer lies open beside it, its stamped cover already curling at the corners. Across the room, the framed letter left by a dead Garvey aunt stands beside the made bed: ordinary paper, careful handwriting, addressed to a great-nephew who never arrived.

Beyond the window, the sheltered gardens lie pale beneath their lamps. Farther out, a hard-light bridge vanishes and returns through the storm, its white span blurred by rain that never quite reaches the glass. From below comes the clink of laid silver, then a burst of laughter cut short by someone saying, "That is not what happened."

The primer's final section begins with Article Nineteen in severe black type. A place card rests on top of the page, folded tentwise: ROWAN GARVEY. The inherited signet at your hand catches the lamp flame in its dark red stone.

A knock comes at the door, unhurried and precise. "Rowan Garvey? Rashid vel Amara. The dining hall's filling up. I thought I might spare you the grand entrance and introduce myself here first. May I come in?"

## Turn 1

**Player**

**Story**

Rashid comes in after your invitation, keeping clear of the desk and bed. His dinner coat is dark and sharply fitted; fine fur darkens the backs of his hands. He returns your introduction with a brief clasp.

"Thank you," he says. "The bowls aren't out yet. We haven't missed anything."

He takes the narrow stair at an easy pace. Firelight reaches up from the common room below, catching on the gilt frames of dead Garveys above the hearth. Study tables stand between leather chairs, and the library door across the hall breathes lamp-oil air into the passage. As Rashid turns toward the dining room, a woman's voice carries through the open door.

"Rashid, it is one hour. The booking is mine."

The dining hall is bright with candles and working lamps. Six students occupy the places nearest the center of the long varnished table. A server in Academy livery waits beside the setting marked for you, while a second empty chair faces a tall horned woman with one hand on a folded sheet of paper. The golden-haired man opposite her has paused with his spoon halfway to the bowl. White hair, silver hair, tucked white wings, and blue-black hair frame the rest of the table.

Rashid says your name. The horned woman folds the paper once and looks up. "Ashara vel Kothren. Your seat is there."

The white-haired woman gives a small nod. "Ysolde Thornmantle."

"Caelindra Vaeyn," says the woman with silver hair. "I was informed Garvey House would have a human resident again."

The angel beside her smiles. "Seraphel Dawnquill.
Welcome to this table,
Rowan Garvey."

"Thessaly Morrow," says the small woman with black eyes. "I checked. No one has claimed your chair."

The man in white and gold acknowledges you from his seat. "Aldric Verantus. Welcome to the Conclave."

The server draws out the offered chair between Ysolde and Seraphel, and you take it as clear broth scented with herbs arrives from the kitchen. Rashid settles into the chair across from Ashara and opens the folded sheet beside his place.

"One of the evening group was injured during practice," he says. "The Academy closed the covered court. I'm asking for supervised access while they sort out what happened."

Aldric lowers his spoon. "The announcement said the instructor's report is pending. We don't know whether the court caused the injury."

"I'm not saying it did. I'm saying a supervisor could be there while the report is pending."

Ashara takes up the earlier thread. "And I said no to using my hour. I have a ranked bout tomorrow."

"I asked whether you would take one session."

"You did. My answer remains no."

Ysolde studies the petition. "Has anyone inspected the floor?"

"Not as far as the group knows," Rashid says. "I've spoken to everyone who was there."

"Then have it inspected," Ashara says. "The people in his group don't have private instruction to fall back on."

Rashid taps the paper once. "The request is for a supervisor. It doesn't claim the court is safe."

"I'll read the report before I put my name to it," Aldric says.

Rashid leaves the petition beside the salt cellar. Beyond the high windows, slow lightning passes through the storm without a sound.

## Turn 4

**Player**

I go to bed. In the morning I wash, dress, eat breakfast and make my way to the octagon in time for the ten-bell bout. I take a place at the north rail behind the rope and watch the whole match, keeping quiet while they fight.

**Story**

You go to bed with the primer closed on the desk and wake to stormlight thinning across the window. After washing and dressing, you eat breakfast in the dining hall while the lamps still burn against the grey morning. The quadrangle's black stone is wet with mist by the time you leave Garvey House, and the bridges beyond the walls hang white in the storm.

The dueling ground occupies the far side of the quadrangle: packed earth within an open octagon, a rope between iron posts, and a small crowd already gathered at the rails. You take a place at the north side behind the rope, clear of the eastern glare.

At ten bells, a referee in plain grey enters with Ashara and her opponent, Mera Voss. Mera carries a long ash staff and wears padded black at the ribs and throat. Ashara has a blunted saber in one hand, its guard bound in dark leather; her black hair is tied high, leaving the sweep of her horns bare in the damp air.

The referee looks from one fighter to the other. "Two clean marks. Break on my call. Ready."

The first bell rings.

Mera takes the first mark. Her staff comes low toward Ashara's knee, changes line, and touches the padded strip at Ashara's left ribs before Ashara can close. The referee's hand drops.

"Mark, Voss."

Mera steps back with the staff lowered. Ashara waits through the next bell rather than meeting the first swing. When Mera sends the staff across her shoulder line, Ashara lets its end pass, closes under the return, and catches the shaft on her guard. Heat wavers along the leather binding. Mera breaks contact a moment too late; Ashara's blunt point rests against the white patch at Mera's collarbone.

"Mark, Kothren."

The third exchange keeps them moving around the octagon. Mera holds the staff low, drawing Ashara nearer to the rope and changing direction whenever Ashara tests the space inside her reach. Ashara's tail brushes the packed earth as she shifts her weight. When Mera finally commits to a high strike, Ashara takes the blow on the reinforced guard, turns the shaft aside, and puts the saber to Mera's side beneath the arm.

"Mark, Kothren. Bout."

Mera salutes with the staff. Ashara returns it with her saber, then takes a towel from an attendant at the edge of the ground. Applause rises from the south rail and carries around the rope. You remain quiet at the north rail while the referee writes Ashara's two-to-one result on a slate.

**Story**

You applaud once the referee has finished with the slate. Mera has already gone toward the equipment shed, her staff over one shoulder, when Ashara comes through the opening in the rope with a towel around her neck. Ember heat still rises from her skin into the damp air.

You offer your congratulations and ask whether she would show you the basics of using a sword.

Ashara looks toward the octagon. "Mera kept leaving the same lane because I kept taking it. I gave her the first mark before I stopped." She turns back to you. "You noticed the change."

"I can spare half an hour after lunch," she says. "South end of the ground. Wear boots you can turn in. Leave the signet in your room; it will catch on the grip. Take a wooden blade from the rack, and I will show you the first guard."

Inside the rope, the referee calls for the next pair. Ashara takes the towel from her neck. "After lunch, then."
