You spend the intervening hours back at Garvey House. At fourth bell, you return to the covered court.

The rope has been taken from the entrance. Inside, the new canvas strip lies flat among the other mats, and an instructor in a grey Academy coat checks names against a small board. Rashid stands with six other students near the far wall, a bundle of padded practice batons at his feet.

"May I watch this first session?"

The instructor glances toward the west rail. "Yes. Behind the blue line. Do not cross the court while the session is active."

You take the indicated place. From there, you watch the instructor walk the group along the repaired mat, press each seam with the heel of a boot, and explain where the practice area has been narrowed. The students begin with footwork and short turns, then pair off with the padded batons.

Rashid works with a taller student in a green Commerce sash. Their first passes are slow and deliberate while the instructor moves between pairs, correcting distance and stopping each exchange before it gathers speed. A student with a splinted wrist remains at the west wall with the instructor's board, watching the drills and taking down the times when each pair changes.

The session stays within the marked space. No one crosses the repaired mat at speed. When one baton slips from a student's hand, the instructor calls a halt, has it picked up, and restarts the drill from the beginning.

At the end of the hour, the instructor calls time. The group returns the padded batons to their bundle, and Rashid helps carry them toward the equipment cupboard. The new canvas strip remains smooth beneath the court lamps as the supervised practice comes to its end.
