The tile with the long, gently curving road gives the blue lantern a direct line toward the gate marked with a painted star. You fit it against the central square, set a lantern on the nearer entry circle, and move it two marks along the new road. Thessaly watches the join before she watches the lantern.

"That meets cleanly," she says. "A promising beginning."

Her next tile carries an amber road away from the moon gate and toward the crowded middle instead. She advances her lantern only one space. The board begins to spread between you: one clear route toward the star, another folding back on itself, and a narrow branch whose usefulness is not yet apparent.

On your following turn, you turn a tile so that its painted road appears to touch the one already on the board. Thessaly puts a finger beside the gap at the corner.

"Not that way. Corners are decoration. Roads have to meet at their edges."

You choose another placement, extend the star road, and carry your first lantern to its gate on the next move. Thessaly returns it to your side of the board without comment, then places a tile that gives her amber lantern an efficient route through the center.

The later turns go faster. You start a second blue lantern at the other entry circle and take the shortest open road whenever the tiles allow it. When one would pass through an amber lantern, Thessaly stops you before it crosses the first mark.

"You may not go through mine," she says. "You may make me move around you, though. That is part of the game."

So you do. A blue lantern takes the branch she had left toward the west gate; another reaches the star gate by a road that briefly looked like a dead end. Thessaly blocks neither route with an easy gift. She lays her tiles carefully, once closing a straight run you had been watching, once opening a turn that lets her own third lantern slip toward the moon. The painted map grows into a tangle of crossings, little cul-de-sacs, and four gates bright under the lamp.

Near the end, both of you have three lanterns returned. Your last blue piece waits two marks from the west gate. Thessaly's last amber lantern has a longer path, but one she has kept clear since the middle of the game. She lays a final curved tile, moves it twice, and the amber glass passes through the moon gate before your turn comes around.

Thessaly gathers the amber lanterns into their compartment, then the blue ones. "I win by one turn," she says. "Enough to be irritating."

She slides the dish of dried apricots nearer the center of the table and studies the finished board for a moment. "You chose the short roads. By the end, you were also looking at the roads you could make me use. That was better."
