Thessaly leaves the curved tile on the table. She traces the amber lantern's last route with one fingertip: from its old position to a mark at the bend, then to the second mark beside the painted moon.

“Those are the two spaces,” she says. “The gate is after them. It does not count as a space.”

She taps the moon itself. “You counted this as a third. The card says a lantern reaches a gate; it never occupies one.”

The curved tile goes into its fitted row in the box. “Your blue lantern had the same two marks left to the west gate. If I had not closed that road, you would have gone through on your next turn.”

She finishes sorting the remaining tiles, fitting the last one into place before closing the lacquered lid.
