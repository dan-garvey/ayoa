The tile with the long curved road gives the blue lantern a clear way toward the star gate. You set it against the center square, place a lantern on the nearer entry circle, and move it two marks along the road.

Thessaly checks the edges where the paint meets. “That fits.”

She answers with an amber tile that bends away from the moon gate and back toward the middle of the board. Her lantern moves only one space. Between the two of you, the map begins to gather a starward road, a loop, and a short branch with no gate at its end.

On your next turn, you set a tile corner to corner with an existing road. Thessaly touches the small gap between them.

“No. It only touches here. Turn it, or take another.”

You choose another placement and extend the road to the star. On the following move, the first blue lantern reaches its gate. Thessaly lifts it from the board and places it beside your hand before laying a tile for her own route through the center.

The game settles into a quicker rhythm. You bring in another blue lantern, then another, taking the shortest open routes as the board permits. When you try to move one blue piece through an amber lantern, Thessaly raises a hand before it reaches the first mark.

“You cannot cross that one. Stop before it, or use the branch and make me go round.”

You take the branch. A blue lantern reaches the west gate; another returns through the star gate after a route that had seemed to end at the board's edge. Thessaly closes one long run with a forked tile and sends her third amber lantern around the moonward loop. The roads spread across the tablecloth in crossings and blind turns, the glass pieces catching the lamp whenever one is moved.

Near the end, three lanterns of each color have passed through gates. Your last blue lantern stands two marks from the west gate. Thessaly's last amber lantern has farther to travel, but its road remains open. She adds a curved tile, moves the amber piece twice, and sends it through the moon gate before the turn returns to you.

She gathers the amber lanterns into their compartment, then the blue ones. “By one turn.” She taps the forked tile near the center. “I closed this when I did because your last lantern was coming west.”

The dried apricots remain between the open box and the finished board as Thessaly begins sorting the road tiles into their fitted rows.
