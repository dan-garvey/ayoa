“Not through that one,” she says. “You can stop beside it if the road reaches there.”

You take the open branch instead. It costs a turn, but it leads toward the gate marked with a gold fish. Thessaly adds a curved road that continues her own route without closing yours.

“The tile before this could have turned my lantern south,” she says, looking at the board. Then she moves hers.

The next blue lantern goes out through the fish gate. An amber one reaches a narrow black arch along the western edge. Apricots disappear from the dish between turns. The common room quiets around the lamp; somewhere farther into the house, a door closes, and ash settles in the fireplace.

As the board fills, Thessaly points to the moon gate. “This set is older than the Academy's. Their copy has a plain crescent. This one has three points.” Her finger moves to the gold fish. “And that is supposed to be a river serpent. The Academy's has no teeth.”

You choose the branching turn, send your lantern around the southern side, and leave it a turn from another gate.

She looks at the four amber lanterns in front of her, then gathers them into their section of the box.

“That was close,” she says. “You asked when you weren't certain. I liked playing with you.”
