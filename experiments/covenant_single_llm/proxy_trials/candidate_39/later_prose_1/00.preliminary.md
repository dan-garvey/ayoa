"A sensible distinction," Rashid says. "Though you may find a Council meeting can last four years as well. It merely has better chairs."

"And worse bread," Ashara says.

At that, Seraphel looks down into her water glass to hide a smile that reaches her eyes anyway.

"Let the first evening hold\nno office beyond supper,\nno oath beyond passing salt."

"That is a modest programme," Aldric says. "I believe even this house can meet it."

"The house can," Caelindra says. "The Academy has not yet demonstrated the same restraint."

The server arrives with the next course before anyone can decide whether that deserves defending. A broad platter of glazed roots settles in the center of the table, followed by saffron rice in a lidded bowl and dark bread whose crust crackles where the knife has gone through it. The candles bend in a faint draft from the corridor and then straighten again. Outside, storm-light comes up from below in a pale pulse, too distant to cast a sound into the room.

You take a piece of the bread while the dishes begin their second circuit. Its steam fogs the edge of the silver knife. Rashid reaches past his own plate to make sure the rice comes your way before he serves himself; Ashara catches the sauce boat before it can follow the same route and sends it down the other side of the table with a flick of her wrist.

"The sauce is hotter than it looks," Rashid says.

"It is sauce," Ysolde says. "It is supposed to be hot."

Rashid glances across at her. "I mean in the manner of a warning."

"Then it is poorly designed."

Caelindra's careful expression breaks for a moment. "There. A useful observation at last. We ought to have warnings carved into the silver."

"I will volunteer to word them," Thessaly says. "'Take this at your own risk' is clear."

"It is also needlessly dramatic," Aldric says.

Thessaly considers the little silver boat as if it has personally offered evidence. "It contains red peppers. It has earned drama."

Seraphel's laughter is soft and unguarded this time. You smile with the table, and the laughter carries on long enough for the server to look pleased as she retreats toward the sideboard.

The talk finds its way to tomorrow's first seminar, which nobody has yet attended and everyone appears already prepared to dislike in a different fashion. Caelindra has seen the reading list.

"Professor Thorn assigned six competing accounts of the Sundering War," she says, lifting a small forkful of rice. "Three of them disagree on the date it began. One manages to disagree with itself halfway through."

"That one is his favorite," Ashara says.

"He told us history is what survives the people who wish it had gone differently," Rashid says. "Then he assigned us a merchant's diary that calls the entire war a dispute over grain tariffs."

"Was it not also a dispute over grain tariffs?" Ysolde asks.

"Among other things," Aldric says.

"Among nearly all things," Rashid says. "Armies eat."

Ashara leans back in her chair. The banked heat of her skin rises faintly through the sleeve of her black dinner coat when she reaches for her glass. "Thorn likes a fight with enough footnotes that no one can tell when it started."

"That is not a criticism in your mouth," Caelindra says.

"No. It is a description."

"Then I agree with it," Caelindra says, with the composure of someone granting an unusual concession to the evening rather than to Ashara.

Rashid looks at the open door to the common room, where the fire has begun to take hold behind its grate. "At least the first session is indoors. Last year, someone decided the orientation walk ought to include the western terrace."

"It was clear," Ysolde says. "There was a good view."

"There was sleet moving sideways."

"That did not obstruct the view."

Her answer is so exact that even Aldric's mouth gives way at one corner. Caelindra turns toward Ysolde with genuine attention now.

"You attended an orientation walk in sleet?"

"I had arrived that morning. It seemed likely the school would continue doing things outdoors. I wished to establish the terms."

"And did it?"

Ysolde pauses with her fingers resting beside her plate. "No. It still does things outdoors without asking."

The next laugh comes more easily. It leaves room afterward for the quiet business of eating: bread torn and shared, a spoon touching porcelain, the silk hush of an Academy servant replacing an emptied water carafe. You listen while the others trade particulars of the grounds: a shortcut behind the chapel that is only shorter if the doors have not been locked, a lecture room whose windows whistle in every storm, the library tables that rock no matter how precisely they are wedged. None of it arrives as instruction. It is simply the sort of accumulated grievance people collect when they have been obliged to live in the same place.

When the conversation reaches the octagon, Ashara's attention sharpens. She sets her fork down, the small sound lost beneath a mutter of rain on the glass.

"The western edge was slick this afternoon," she says to Rashid. "If it rains through the night, the ranking board will be the least of your problems."

"It will not rain through the night," Ysolde says.

Rashid turns to her. "You know that?"

"I have been watching it."

There is no flourish in the answer. She looks toward the blackened windows, following some change none of the rest of the table has noticed.

"The lower clouds are moving east. The wind will turn before midnight. The ground will be damp at dawn, not sodden."

Ashara's tail gives one slow, approving movement behind her chair. "Then half an hour after dinner. We check the footing before the lamps are out."

"Half an hour," Rashid agrees. "You will object if I ask for forty."

"Correct."

"Then I will spare us both the ritual."

"That would be a first," Thessaly says.

Rashid puts one hand briefly to his chest. "Morrow, I have been wounded at a welcome dinner."

"You are not bleeding," she says. "I have looked."

Seraphel covers another laugh with the back of two fingers. Ashara does not smile this time, but the sternness in her face has no particular target. Her eyes have gone back to the plate in front of her, already occupied with the next day's bout.

Aldric reaches for the bread just as Rashid does. Both stop; Aldric withdraws his hand with a courteous gesture for Rashid to take it first. Rashid does, then tears the loaf in half and sets the larger piece within Aldric's reach without comment. The exchange passes quickly, but it alters the table's shape less than the earlier argument had seemed likely to do.

Caelindra notices the dark crumbs gathering on the varnished wood. "Will the restoration include teaching us how to dine without leaving evidence of it?"

"No," Ashara says. "That sounds like an angelic course."

Aldric looks up, not offended so much as wary of the opening. "Courtesy is not concealment."

"Often it is," Thessaly says.

For a breath the room could narrow around that. Then Seraphel tilts her head toward the half loaf and speaks before anyone chooses a side.

"Bread leaves its proof\nwhere hands have been.\nThat seems a mercy."

The line does not settle an argument; it gives it nowhere urgent to go. Rashid brushes the crumbs into his palm and drops them onto his plate. Aldric takes the offered bread. Caelindra resumes eating with an expression that suggests she has filed away the exchange for a less crowded occasion.

By the time the clock in the common room gives its soft quarter-hour chime, the first tightness of the welcome dinner has thinned into something more usable. The long table is still far too large for eight people, but plates, lamplight, and interrupted conversations have claimed part of it. Beyond the open doors, the fire throws a steadier orange over the leather chairs. Ashara and Rashid have an appointment waiting at the octagon; the rest of the evening has not declared itself.
