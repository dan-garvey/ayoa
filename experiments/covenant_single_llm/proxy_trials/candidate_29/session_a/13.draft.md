You take some squash.

“It is good,” you say after trying it. “Watching today made me want to learn something I can actually do. I've read the primer twice and still don't know whether I can make a spark, let alone stop a spear.” You eat for a moment. “I suppose I need to find a first lesson somewhere.”

Ashara takes another piece of flatbread. “A spear is not a first lesson. It is what people pick when they want to look as though they have one.”

“That is unkind to spears,” Rashid says.

“It is kind to beginners.”

Ysolde sets down her cup. “The Arcana intake office does aptitude assessments. It is in the north corridor of the Arcana wing, past the copying room. They will tell you whether you can channel anything. A spark is not useful evidence for every kind of magic.”

Caelindra looks up from her greens. “And they will not be impressed if you ask them to make one for you. Say you need an assessment. They have a form for it.”

Ashara reaches for the water carafe. “Do that first. If you still want a lesson that does not involve setting yourself on fire, come to the octagon tomorrow after second bell.”

Rashid glances at her.

“One lesson,” Ashara says. “A practice blade. Stance, balance, and how not to put your thumb where it does not belong.”

She fills her glass and takes a drink. “You will not need magic for that.”

The serving dishes make another circuit of the table. Outside the tall dining-hall windows, the lamps in the quadrangle come on one by one.
