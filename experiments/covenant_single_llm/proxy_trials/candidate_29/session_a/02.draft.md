You take a piece of bread from the basket. Its crust gives with a quiet crack beneath your fingers.

“Fair enough, Rashid. I mistook a loud conversation for an invitation.”

Rashid sets his knife beside his bowl. “I made it loud. That part is mine.” He lifts his cup, takes a drink, and sets it down again with care. “But I do not need a new resident's signature on a request for the people already using the court.”

The stew is thick with lentils, onion, and fennel, the rosemary arriving after the pepper. You try it, then turn to Ashara.

“Do you compete for the Academy, or is the dawn booking for your own training?”

Ashara has already folded one sleeve above her wrist to keep it clear of the bowl. She looks at you across the candle flame.

“There is no Academy side,” she says. “The Academy posts the rankings and runs the bouts. My name is first on the board this week.” Her tail makes one slow movement along the floor behind her chair. “The booking is mine. I have a ranked bout coming, and I need the covered court for footwork.”

“First,” Rashid says, reaching for the bread, “this week.”

“That is what I said.”

“You make it sound dull on purpose.”

“I make it sound accurate.”

At the far end of the table, Caelindra spoons the fennel from her broth with careful disapproval. Ysolde has added a slice of lemon to hers and watches it turn slowly in the steam. Seraphel breaks bread into small pieces beside her bowl; Thessaly has arranged three crusts in a neat row along the edge of her plate.

Ashara takes a piece of bread for herself. “Have you ever watched a ranked bout?”
