“Never,” you tell Ashara. “I'd like to see one.” You set your spoon down. “If the covered court is closed, how does your dawn booking work?”

“The covered court is not closed to everything.” Ashara rests her forearms on the edge of the table. “The group hour was cancelled. My individual slot was entered separately, before the injury, and it remains.”

Rashid breaks his bread in two. “That is the distinction.”

“Yes,” Ashara says. “It is a bad one. It is still my session.”

She reaches for her spoon, then looks back to you.

“Ranked bouts are outside, in the octagon. The board posts the time and the names. Come to one of those if you want to watch. Do not come to dawn; practice is work, not a performance.”

“The south rail is better,” Ysolde says from beside you. “At noon, the north side catches the light. You will miss the footwork.”

Ashara gives her a brief nod. “She is right about that.”

Seraphel folds a scrap of bread between her fingers.

“Then the south rail,
and clear light.”

Rashid takes the smaller half of his bread for himself. The court is left for morning, and the stew begins to cool.
