You look from the card to the glass rods.

“Could we check once more? I don't know whether I'm doing it wrong.”

Orr studies you over the rim of her spectacles, then nods. “We can repeat the broader resonance. Put your hands back on the plate.”

You place your palms where she showed you. Orr wipes the dark glass square with a cloth, settles it over the copper again, and returns the white stone to its center.

“You are not meant to make anything happen,” she says. “The response, if present, meets the instrument.”

The tuning fork sounds. Its note steadies in the table and fades. The stone remains where it is. No color gathers beneath your hands.

Orr waits until the last vibration has gone, then lifts the stone away.

“Same result,” she says. “Your hands are in the right place. You are not doing it wrong.”

She makes a second, smaller mark beside the first entry on the assessment card and slides it across the table again.
