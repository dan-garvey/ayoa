“The south rail, then. Thank you.”

Ashara gives a short nod and returns to her stew. The talk at the table moves away from the covered court. You eat and listen while the servers clear the bread plates and bring a final course of poached pears in dark syrup.

Caelindra studies hers as though it has presented credentials.

“Clove,” she says.

“You dislike clove?” Rashid asks.

“I dislike its insistence on being noticed.”

Ysolde cuts a neat slice from her pear. “It has been noticed.”

Caelindra tastes the syrup, then puts down her spoon. “This is why your people make terrible diplomats. You call a thing successful the moment it has become unavoidable.”

“That is not why,” Ysolde says.

Seraphel's wings rustle once against her chair.

“The pear is blameless.
The clove has chosen its life.”

Thessaly turns her spoon over in the syrup. “It did not choose. It was put there.”

For the first time that evening, Caelindra smiles without seeming to notice it. She takes another bite.

The plates are being cleared when you say, “I'm going to have a look at the gardens before bed. I'd welcome company, if anyone fancies a walk.”

Rashid folds his napkin once and leaves it beside his cup. “I have wording to revise before morning. I hope the gardens are better company than I have been.”

“They are,” Ashara says. “I am sleeping. Dawn is early.”

Aldric rises, smoothing the front of his white-and-gold coat. “I have evening prayer. Good night, Rowan.”

Caelindra looks toward the dark windows, then at her shoes. “I will admire the gardens more efficiently by daylight.”

Ysolde pushes her chair back. “I will come. The south path has the clearest view of the bridges.”

Thessaly sets her spoon down. “So will I. I have not walked there at night.”

Seraphel tucks her hands together over her lap.

“A garden walk is kind.
I will stay with the last of the tea.”

Ysolde waits beside her chair, white hair bright in the candlelight. Thessaly has already turned toward the doorway that leads through the common room and out to the pale garden paths.
