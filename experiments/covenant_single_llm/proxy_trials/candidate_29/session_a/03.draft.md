“Never,” you tell Ashara. “I'd like to see one.” You set your spoon down. “If the covered court is closed, how does your dawn booking work?”

“The covered court is not closed to everything.” Ashara rests her forearms on the edge of the table. “The notice suspended the group's hours. My individual booking was already on the slate, so it remained. That is part of the problem.”

Rashid breaks his bread in two. “A person with a booking keeps the court. Six people who share an hour lose it.”

“Yes,” Ashara says. “I think that is a bad distinction. I do not think handing over a session I arranged is a remedy for it.”

“No one said it was.”

“You asked three times.”

“Twice.”

Ashara's mouth twitches once. “Then I am improving your record.”

Rashid takes the smaller piece of bread for himself. The point does not return to the table.

“Ranked bouts are outside,” Ashara says to you. “The open octagon. The board announces the time, the two names, and the terms. My next one will be posted there.” She reaches for her spoon. “Watch that. Practice is for correcting errors, and I would rather do it without an audience.”

Ysolde looks up from her bowl. “The octagon is better in clear weather. The wind comes across the north edge and makes the second marker difficult to judge.”

“You say that as though the weather has opinions,” Caelindra says.

“It has patterns.”

“Patterns are simply opinions with better publicity.”

Seraphel's smile stays near the rim of her cup.

“Then may the wind be quiet
when the board calls your name.”

Ashara gives the angel a small nod and returns to her stew.
