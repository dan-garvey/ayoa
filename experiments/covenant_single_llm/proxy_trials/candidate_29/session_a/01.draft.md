The primer closes beneath your hand with a soft papery clap. The server accepts your thanks with a practiced nod, then leads the way down the stairwell, where the firelight from the common room warms the old wood underfoot.

The formal dining hall opens off it in a spill of candlelight. Seventeen places run the length of the varnished table, most of them empty tonight; the occupied settings cluster near the middle among water carafes, bread baskets, and low bowls of citrus. Paneled walls hold the lamplight in amber squares. Servers move quietly between the sideboard and the table with covered dishes.

The conversation stops when you reach the doorway.

“Rowan Garvey,” you say. “Thank you for having me.”

The tall woman nearest the open place reaches past a candle to draw a water carafe out of its way. Her swept-back horns are black at the base and dark red at their tips; a long tail rests in a deliberate curve beside her chair. “Ashara vel Kothren,” she says. “Welcome.”

Across from her, the well-dressed young man with dark fur along his hands lifts his cup. “Rashid vel Amara.” His amber eyes flick briefly toward the stairs, then back to you. “You arrived at the noisy part.”

“Aldric Verantus,” says the golden-haired man beside him. He has the upright stillness of someone accustomed to chapel benches and formal portraits.

At the far side of the table, a pale woman with white hair gives you an exact nod. “Ysolde Thornmantle.”

“Caelindra Vaeyn.” The silver-haired woman says it without rising. Her violet eyes remain on you for a moment longer than courtesy strictly requires.

The smallest person at the table has blue-black hair drifting slowly around her shoulders and eyes so black the candlelight finds no color in them. “Thessaly Morrow,” she says. “The stew has no terms.”

Beside her, the warm-gold angel's small white wings are folded close against the back of her chair. She smiles.

“Seraphel Dawnquill.
Welcome to dinner.”

The server indicates the vacant setting between Ashara and Ysolde. You take it and settle your napkin across your lap. A bowl is placed before you, steam carrying rosemary, pepper, and the sharp citrus scent from the stairwell.

“I've heard enough about the court to know I shouldn't sign anything on my first evening,” you say. “I'd be glad of some stew, though.”

Rashid's mouth shifts around the edge of a smile. “No one expected your name tonight. The request can survive one dinner.”

“It had better,” Ashara says. “I have no intention of having tomorrow's argument served with breakfast.”

“You have an argument scheduled for breakfast?” Caelindra asks.

“Only if Rashid brings paper.”

“I will bring paper,” Rashid says. “After I see the report.”

“Then it is a much better argument,” Aldric says.

Thessaly tears a piece of bread in half. “It remains an argument. Better is not the same as good.”

Seraphel dips her spoon into her bowl.

“The bread has no opinion.
It is already winning.”

Caelindra's mouth almost changes shape before she takes a measured sip of water. Across the table, Ysolde examines the rising steam above her stew as if it has offered information worth considering.

“The fennel is stronger tonight,” she says. “That is an improvement.”

Ashara reaches for the bread basket. “At last, a matter on which this house can reach a decision.”

The servers begin setting the remaining bowls down, and for a few moments the room fills with the small, useful sounds of dinner instead of debate.
