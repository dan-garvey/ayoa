## Opening

**Player**

My character: Rowan Garvey. A twenty-three-year-old human man with brown skin, unruly dark hair, and ink-stained fingers, wearing a carefully mended charcoal coat and the inherited Garvey signet. Newly arrived and unsure of Conclave customs. He grew up unaware of his heritage, lost his parents young, and does not know whether he has any magic.

Begin the story shortly before the welcome dinner, in my room at Garvey House. I play Rowan Garvey.

**Story**

The window's latch is cold, though the storm beneath the Nexus never makes a sound.

Three hundred feet below Garvey House, lightning opens and closes inside a violet-black country of cloud. The gardens lie between the house and that drop: pale paths, flowering hedges held close by low walls, lamps beginning to pearl in the dusk. Beyond them, one hard-light bridge runs straight into the weather, white and unwavering until the storm takes it.

Your room occupies a quiet corner of the second floor. The bed is tightly made. The clothes sent ahead by the lawyers wait in the cedar wardrobe in careful folded ranks. A washbasin gleams on its stand. Your charcoal coat carries its neat repairs at cuff and elbow; the Garvey signet at your hand catches a pulse of distant lightning. Ink darkens the creases of your fingers.

An Academy primer rests beside the green-shaded desk lamp. Its contents promise introductions to the Conclave, dinner customs, bridge permissions, and Article Nineteen in the same dry typeface. Beside it stands a framed handwritten letter. The visible lines are addressed to a great-nephew whose name is not yours and concern an upstairs room, fresh sheets, and a visit expected before winter. Whatever became of that visit, the letter has been kept behind glass.

The door is ajar onto a corridor of dark floorboards. At its far end, warm light from the stairwell falls across the runner. From below come the sounds of dinner being arranged: a serving spoon against china, the low rush of a fire in the common room, a chair dragged an inch and set straight again.

Then voices rise through the house.

“Rashid, if you want to ask for access, ask for access,” says a young man with a clear, measured voice. “Do not turn an injury into a referendum before anyone knows what caused it.”

“The gate has been shut since this afternoon,” Rashid answers. His restraint gives each word a hard edge. “No report has to be complete for that to be true.”

“You have a point about the court,” a woman says. “You do not therefore have a petition.”

“That is an arbitrary distinction.”

“No.” The answer arrives without heat. “I can tell when a blade will take off a thumb. I cannot tell why someone was hurt when I was not there.”

“Ashara is right,” the first man says. “We should know whether the restriction is sensible before we demand it be lifted.”

“I did not say lifted.” Rashid lets out a breath through his nose. “I said supervised access for the group. A time. An instructor. Something other than telling people to acquire private tutors.”

Another woman joins them, her voice light and unhurried. “I will sign a specific request. I will not sign a mood.”

“Fine,” Rashid says after a beat. “Supervised access. Tomorrow, after the report.”

“Bring the wording,” Ashara says. “And leave my dawn booking out of it.”

“I was not going to steal your booking.”

“You have asked me three times.”

“Twice.”

“Then I was charitable.”

The chair legs downstairs scrape again. A different woman speaks in brief, plain lines.

“Arguments improve in daylight.
Stew improves before it dies.”

The silence that follows is interrupted by a short laugh from Rashid.

“Tomorrow,” he says.

“Tomorrow,” says Ashara.

The conversation loosens. Someone asks whether the bread has arrived. Someone else replies that it has, and that it is being guarded from desperate hands. The scent reaching the stairwell is roasted herbs, warm bread, and something sharp with citrus.

Footsteps climb to the landing. An Academy server in blue-grey livery pauses at your open door with a covered dish balanced on one hand. She knocks once against the jamb and inclines her head.

“Mr. Garvey,” she says, “dinner is served.”
