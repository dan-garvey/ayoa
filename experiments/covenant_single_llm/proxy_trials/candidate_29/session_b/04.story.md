Ysolde looks up at the mention of the garden.

“Take the south path,” she says. “Past the white cedars, there is an arch in the wall. The western bridge lines up with it.”

Thessaly cradles her cup between both hands. “Good night, Rowan.”

“Good night,” Ysolde says.

Rashid lifts a hand from farther down the table. Ashara is still saying something low to him over the empty tart plate, and neither of them looks inclined to postpone it. The server takes your cleared place in stride, accepting your thanks with an inclination of her head before turning toward the sideboard.

The dining hall’s warmth follows only as far as the garden door. Beyond it, the air is damp and cool, smelling of wet stone and turned soil. The storm is far below the plateau, yet occasional gusts find their way along the outer walls and stir the climbing vines.

The garden has gone dark without becoming colorless. White flowers crowd one trellis beside the door, heavy with scent; farther along, small blue bells nod beneath clipped hedges. The path lamps are round glass globes on low posts, their light falling in quiet circles over the pale gravel.

The south path bends between a wall of yew and the white cedars Ysolde named. Their branches whisper high above the garden. Beyond them, a broad stone arch cuts through the outer wall. A recessed bench stands well inside the curve of the path, with solid paving underfoot and an open view through the arch.

The western bridge lies beyond it.

Its hard-light span remains a straight, pale thread over the cloudbank. The lamps along it throw no round pools now. Each bright spill stretches eastward, fine at the ends and broad at the posts, until the bridge seems furred with slanting strokes of light. The line of the bridge holds steady beneath them.

Lightning stirs inside the storm without a sound. For a moment, the clouds turn the color of tarnished silver, and the lamps sharpen against them. Then the light below fades again, leaving the bridge and its leaning glow suspended over the dark.
