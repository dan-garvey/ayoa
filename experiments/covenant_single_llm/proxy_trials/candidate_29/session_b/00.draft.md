The storm keeps its distance below the window, a slow white bruising of cloud and lightning under the plateau. In the glass, the room has settled around you with the cautious completeness of a place prepared by people who knew a name better than its owner: the made bed, the fresh clothes in the wardrobe, the walnut desk with its lamp burning low.

Your signet catches the lamplight whenever your hand passes over the desk. Beside it, the framed letter that came with the room leans slightly crooked. Its thin, brown script begins *My dear Edrin* and goes on about a summer visit, plum cake, and a bedroom that would be made ready. The great-nephew never came. The letter did.

Somewhere below, a chair leg protests across a floorboard. Then comes the more persuasive evidence of dinner: a lift of voices, the bright clink of serving spoons, something fragrant with pepper and butter.

“That is not what I said.” Rashid’s voice carries cleanly up the stairwell. “I said the court should be available under supervision.”

“And I said,” another voice answers, low and hot enough to seem nearer than it is, “that one faculty chaperone with a whistle is supervision only in the most decorative sense.”

“No one has proposed a whistle,” Rashid says.

“You have no imagination.”

A brief laugh escapes him before he can quite turn it into a sigh. “Ashara, you’re booked there tomorrow morning. You could spare an hour.”

“I could. I will not.”

The response lands without cruelty, merely a hard edge set on the table. A plate is put down in the dining hall.

“The restriction is temporary,” Aldric says. His voice has the careful projection of someone accustomed to rooms falling silent for it. “If a student was hurt, surely a day or two of caution is not oppression.”

“No,” says Ashara. “But neither is it an answer. Was the warding faulty? Was the instructor inattentive? Did someone ignore a call? You don’t know.”

“Neither do you.”

“Correct.”

For a moment there is only the small hiss of something meeting a warm platter.

Thessaly speaks next, conversationally. “That appears to be the disagreement. It saves time when people name one.”

“It does not save dinner,” says a woman with a cool, measured voice. “The soup has formed a skin.”

“Ysolde,” Rashid says, “I promise to address the soup before I mount a rebellion.”

“That is sensible.”

There is a little burst of laughter from several people, including—after a beat—Ashara. It makes the voices below easier to distinguish: a quick silver remark from Caelindra about the Academy’s talent for producing both regulations and overcooked roots; Seraphel’s gentler reply, each word shaped into its own small line.

“A bowl gone cool,
an argument warmed—
both may yet be mended.”

“The bowl has better prospects,” Caelindra says.

The door at the end of the corridor opens. A server in dark Academy livery appears on the landing carrying a covered dish in both hands, pauses when she sees your door, and inclines her head toward it.

“Mr. Garvey,” she calls, not loudly. “Dinner is laid. I’ve been asked to say that the first-years are at table.”

Below, a spoon rings once against a bowl. The dining hall’s open doorway spills amber light across the foot of the stairs, and the disputants have turned, for the moment, to serving one another food.
