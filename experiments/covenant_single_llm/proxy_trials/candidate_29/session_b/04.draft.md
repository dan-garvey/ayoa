Ysolde looks up at the mention of the garden.

“The south path,” she says. “Follow it past the white cedars. There is an arch in the wall where the western bridge lines up.”

Thessaly shifts her cup between both hands. “Good night, Rowan.”

“Good night,” says Ysolde.

From farther down the table, Rashid lifts one hand in farewell without interrupting whatever low argument Ashara is making over the remains of the pear tarts. The server takes your plate and inclines her head at your thanks before carrying it toward the sideboard.

The dining hall is warmer than the corridor beyond it. Once the garden door closes behind you, the air changes again: cool and damp at the edges, smelling of wet stone and leaf-mold. The storm lies below the plateau, but its wind still works along the outer walls in brief, searching gusts.

Pale paths run through flowerbeds bright even at night. Small blue blossoms crowd beneath a trellis; something white and heavy-scented climbs the sheltered wall beside the door. The lamps along the path are ordinary glass globes at first glance, their light falling in calm circles over the gravel.

The south path curves between a clipped hedge and a row of white cedars whose branches stir high overhead. Beyond them, the garden wall opens into a broad stone arch. A bench sits well back from it, set into a recess where the path turns. From there, the ground is firm underfoot and the view clears over the garden’s outer wall.

The western bridge hangs across the storm.

Its hard-light span holds straight and pale above the cloudbank, but the lamps along it do not. Their bright pools reach eastward in long strokes, each one thinning into the next. The effect is stranger from this distance: a row of fixed posts casting light that seems to have caught a current of its own, all of it leaning through the dark while the bridge itself remains unmoved.

Below, lightning flickers soundlessly through the clouds. For a little while, the bridge and its slanting lamps are the only things visible beyond the garden.
