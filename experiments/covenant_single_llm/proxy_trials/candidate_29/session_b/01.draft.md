The server leads the way down the stairs, the covered dish sending up rosemary and browned butter at each turn of the landing. At the dining-room doorway, the long table gives you a brief, collective pause.

Seven faces turn toward you through candlelight.

The woman nearest the fire has ember-red skin and black-red horns swept back from a severely tied braid. Her gold eyes rest on you without either welcome or challenge. Beside her, the tall young man with amber eyes and dark fur along his hands rises halfway from his chair before seeming to decide that standing would make too much of it.

“You aren’t late,” he says. “The soup was early. I’m Rashid vel Amara.”

“Ashara vel Kothren,” says the horned woman.

Across from them, a pale woman with silver-white hair has set her spoon down with exact care. “Ysolde Thornmantle.”

The introductions continue around the table without ceremony. The gold-lit angel in white and gold inclines his head. “Aldric Verantus.” The woman beside him, all silver hair, violet eyes, and immaculate cuffs, gives a narrower nod. “Caelindra Vaeyn.”

“Thessaly Morrow,” says the small woman with black eyes. “There is a chair for you. That seems useful.”

The angel with honey-blonde hair smiles from the far side of the table. Small white wings show against the back of her chair as she speaks.

“Seraphel Dawnquill,
and I am glad you came
before the bread was gone.”

“It is not gone,” Ysolde says.

“Then I am glad you came before it had an opportunity.”

The empty setting waits between Thessaly and Ysolde. The server draws back the chair, and when you take it, she lifts the lid from the dish she has carried down: roasted roots glossed with butter, a small joint of dark meat, and a spoonful of sharp green sauce. Bread, still warm, stands in a basket within reach. The first mouthful has enough salt and pepper to make the room’s scents resolve into themselves.

Rashid sits again and pulls a folded sheet of paper from beside his plate. The top of it is covered in close, disciplined handwriting.

“We were arguing about the covered court,” he says. “Or, more accurately, we had begun a petition and then improved it by disagreeing over every line.”

“You improved it,” Ashara says. “I prevented it from becoming melodrama.”

“There was no melodrama.”

Caelindra lifts one brow. “You were both louder than the serving bell.”

Rashid lays a finger on the page. “A student in the practice group was injured two days ago. The Academy withdrew access until faculty has reviewed it. I want the students who attend to ask for the findings and for supervised time once they are available.”

“He first wanted to demand that the court reopen tomorrow,” Aldric says.

“I wanted a timetable.”

“You wrote *immediately* twice.”

“It was a draft.”

Thessaly reaches across to take the bread basket, then pauses with it balanced between both hands. “Will the people who sign have read the report?”

“No. It is not being released to us.”

“Then they should not sign a conclusion.”

“They won’t.” Rashid’s eyes flick once to the page. “The request will be for the conclusion, not an invented explanation.”

Ashara leans back. “And no names from the injured student’s account unless the student agrees to them.”

“Of course.”

The answer comes quickly enough to have been decided before the correction. Ashara studies him for a beat, then turns to the food.

“That is better,” she says.

It is not quite praise, but Rashid’s mouth shifts as if he has heard it anyway.

“May I see the wording when it is done?” Aldric asks.

“You may see it before anyone signs.”

“Then I may sign it.”

“A conditional endorsement,” Caelindra says. “How thrillingly angelic.”

“You may refrain,” Aldric tells her.

“I had intended to. The court has never affected me.”

“That is an unusually efficient reason,” Thessaly says.

Caelindra looks at her. “It is also true.”

“Those qualities do coexist,” Thessaly says, passing the basket toward you.

For a while the petition gives way to the practical work of dinner. A server refills water glasses. The gravy boat makes its way down the table under Ysolde’s watchful attention. Seraphel eats the roasted roots with unguarded pleasure; Caelindra, after declaring them beyond rescue, takes another spoonful when she thinks no one is watching. Rashid scratches out a sentence, considers it, and folds the paper away rather than bringing it between the plates again.

Through the tall windows, a line of lightning flares beneath the plateau. Ysolde turns her head toward it.

“The wind has shifted west,” she says.

Ashara glances at the glass. “Can you tell that from one flash?”

“Not from the flash. From the cloud above it.”

“Will it matter?” Rashid asks.

“The bridge lamps will lean in the morning.”

“Lean?” Caelindra repeats.

“Their light does. You will see.”

The certainty in her voice closes the matter more neatly than an argument. Conversation moves on to the first seminar of the term, to a professor who has assigned a hundred pages before meeting the class, to whether the Academy kitchens have always used so much pepper. Names begin to settle into their faces amid the exchange: Ashara’s clipped certainty, Rashid’s careful warmth, Ysolde’s spare observations; Caelindra’s disdainful precision, Aldric’s polished gravity, Thessaly’s untimely literalness, Seraphel’s bright voice making even a complaint sound like a small song.

The candles burn lower by degrees. Outside, the storm continues its silent work beneath the Academy, while the table fills and empties its dishes around you.
