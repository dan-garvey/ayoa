Ysolde waits until the bridge has vanished behind the cloud before she answers.

“You are welcome,” she says. “It is easier to see from the gardens. Here, the window reflects too much of the room.”

She turns back to her meal. The conversation does not press itself into the space after her words. Cutlery touches porcelain; a server collects the dish of roasted roots and brings a plate of small pear tarts, their pastry dark at the edges.

Caelindra inspects one as if it has arrived for examination. “No tarragon.”

“No tarragon,” Seraphel agrees.

“A pear, a crust,
and no green ambush
at the center.”

“That is an unreasonable description of a herb,” Aldric says.

“It is an accurate description of dinner,” Caelindra replies.

Ashara takes the last tart from the plate before Rashid can reach it. He looks at her hand, then at the empty plate.

“You did that deliberately.”

“Of course.”

“You had already had one.”

“Then I have verified their quality.”

Rashid reaches for the bread instead. “And?”

“Acceptable.”

“A moving endorsement.”

The petition remains folded beneath his plate. When his sleeve catches one corner, Ashara puts two fingers on the paper and slides it farther from the spill of gravy.

“Not during dessert,” she says.

“I was not opening it.”

“Good.”

At the other end of the table, Aldric has opened the heavy book he brought to dinner. Caelindra spots the title and makes a small sound of dismay.

“You are reading Windwhisper’s commentary while eating?”

“I was looking at the first chapter.”

“Why?”

“Because it is assigned.”

“So is attendance at chapel. That does not require you to bring it to supper.”

Aldric marks his page with one finger. “The prose is difficult.”

“The prose is a corridor with no windows.”

Thessaly looks toward the book. “Does it eventually arrive somewhere?”

“Yes,” Aldric says. “It explains the terms used in the Accords.”

“After how long?”

“Two hundred pages.”

Thessaly considers this. “Then it has no corridor. It has a tunnel.”

Caelindra’s laughter is brief and unguarded. Aldric closes the book, perhaps to spare it further inspection.

Seraphel takes a piece of pear tart and says,

“A page without air
may still contain a door.
Bring a lamp.”

“There,” Ysolde says. “A use for the bridge lamps.”

The tarts disappear at different speeds. Ysolde eats hers in neat, even portions; Ashara finishes the one she claimed without further comment; Caelindra makes an exception for the crust while continuing to deny that the dessert has any merit. The servers refresh the water glasses and clear away plates as the talk moves from the reading list to the merits of the Academy’s library chairs, then to whether the kitchens have always put pepper in every sauce.

Outside, the storm continues to flicker below the garden wall. The warm light of the dining room holds steady in the glass, and no one turns to wait for another question before returning to their own conversation.
