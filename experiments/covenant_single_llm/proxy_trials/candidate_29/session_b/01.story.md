The server accepts your thanks with a small nod and leads you downstairs. At the dining-room doorway, the conversation thins into silence just long enough for your introduction to reach everyone at the table.

“You’re not late,” says the young man with amber eyes and dark fur along the backs of his hands. He has started to rise, then settles back with one hand on the chair. “The soup was early. Rashid vel Amara.”

The horned woman beside him gives you a level look. Her swept-back black-red horns frame hair tied so severely that not a strand has escaped it. “Ashara vel Kothren.”

“Aldric Verantus,” says the angel in white and gold.

The pale, white-haired woman across the table sets down her spoon. “Ysolde Thornmantle.”

“Caelindra Vaeyn.” Silver hair catches the candlelight as its owner inclines her head. Her violet eyes move briefly to your signet, then back to your face.

“Thessaly Morrow,” says the small woman beside the empty setting. “That chair is yours. It has been empty for some time, which is its only qualification.”

The honey-haired angel at the far side smiles. The small white wings visible behind her shoulders lift slightly before she speaks.

“Seraphel Dawnquill.
Sit while the sauce is warm;
your name will keep.”

“It is still warm,” Ysolde says.

“An excellent reason to sit,” Seraphel replies.

The unused place has been laid between Thessaly and Ysolde. The server holds the chair as you take it, then sets down the dish she carried from upstairs: roasted roots glossed with butter, dark slices of meat beneath their browned crust, and a green sauce sharp with herbs. A basket of warm bread stands near Thessaly’s elbow. When she shifts it toward you, the crust gives softly under your fingers.

For a few moments, the table attends to food. The servers move in and out with the quiet precision of people who know how to cross a crowded room without becoming part of its conversation. Ashara takes the gravy boat before it can pass her. Rashid watches the motion, then reaches for the folded sheet beside his plate instead of another helping.

“Since we have a new audience,” he says, “I should make clear that I was not trying to overthrow the Academy before supper.”

“Your draft contained the phrase *immediate redress*,” Aldric says.

“Once.”

“Twice,” Ashara says.

Rashid glances at the page. “Twice, apparently.” He turns it facedown. “The covered court was closed after someone in our evening practice group was injured. I want the Academy to tell us what happened and give the group supervised time when it can be used safely. Ashara thinks the first version sounded as though I already knew what the report would say.”

“You did not,” Ashara says.

“I did not.”

“Then do not write as though you do.”

The paper stays under his hand. “I’ve changed it.”

Thessaly reaches for a piece of bread. “Does it name the injured person?”

“No.”

“Good.” She tears the bread in half. “Names have a habit of acquiring jobs they did not volunteer for.”

Rashid nods once. “The people who attend can decide whether they want to sign it. No one will be put on it because I know their name.”

That earns him a brief glance from Ashara, less guarded than the one she gave the paper.

“I will read the revised version,” she says.

“That is all I asked.”

“It is not all you asked.”

“It is all I am asking now.”

Caelindra turns a roasted root over with the end of her fork. “A distinction brought to you by the presence of dinner.”

“Dinner improves many arguments,” Rashid says.

“Only the ones that survive it.”

Aldric reaches for the water pitcher. “Show me the wording before you collect signatures. I will decide then.”

“Fair.”

The matter is set aside without being settled. Rashid tucks the folded sheet beneath his napkin. Ashara takes a bite of the roasted roots, considers them, and passes the serving dish toward Ysolde.

Ysolde studies the green sauce at the edge of her plate. “Tarragon,” she says.

Caelindra looks at her. “I had hoped it was an accident.”

“It is deliberate.”

“That makes it worse.”

Seraphel’s smile returns.

“A herb with intent
is still a herb,
however fiercely applied.”

“You are too forgiving of plants,” Caelindra says.

“I have never been forgiven by one.”

The line sits for a moment before Thessaly laughs, quick and unselfconscious. Even Caelindra’s mouth threatens a smile before she returns to her plate.

Outside the tall windows, lightning runs through the storm below the Academy. Ysolde watches the next pale flare.

“The wind has gone west,” she says.

Ashara follows her gaze. “Can you see that from here?”

“From the cloud above the lightning.”

“What changes?” Rashid asks.

“The bridge lamps will lean by morning.”

Caelindra turns toward the window. “Lamps do not lean.”

“Their light does.”

Ysolde returns to her meal. Nobody immediately finds a reason to dispute her, and the conversation drifts instead to the term’s first seminar, a professor’s punishing reading list, and the Academy kitchens’ longstanding affection for pepper. Around the table, the names begin to attach themselves to voices: Rashid and Ashara’s unfinished argument, Aldric’s deliberate replies, Caelindra’s dry objections, Thessaly’s sudden observations, Seraphel’s verse, Ysolde’s few precise words.

The plates continue to empty beneath the candlelight while the storm flashes below the windows.
