The stone bench is cold through the wool of your coat. With your hands in its pockets, you watch the western bridge hold its straight line above the storm.

The lamps do not settle into stillness. Their light draws east in the storm’s pull, thins, then reaches again. Beneath the bridge, silver stirs inside the clouds whenever lightning moves through them—not a flash from above, but pale brightness opening in the weather itself. Behind you, the garden remains quiet: cedar branches shifting, a globe lamp making its small circle on the gravel, water ticking from a stone lip after the damp evening.

By the time the cold has worked through your sleeves, cloud has swallowed the bridge and released it twice. The last clear view holds long enough to keep: the fixed span, the lamps slanting over it, the silver gathered below.

The south path is darker on the way back. The garden door opens easily, and Garvey House sends warmth out around you with the smells of wax, old wood, and dinner’s fading spices. Voices still carry from the dining hall, softened by the closed door until they are only the shape of conversation. Upstairs, the corridor is empty.

Your lamp is still burning low. At the washstand, a blue-and-white basin, folded towel, and square of harsh soap have been set out. The water turns gray at once. Ink comes away from the pads of your fingers and leaves faint shadows at the drain; a little remains in the creases around your nails.

You hang the coat carefully over the chair beside the desk, its sleeves still cool from the garden. The framed letter keeps its place above it, faded handwriting visible in the lamplight. The bed has been turned down, and the room is quiet except for the house settling beyond the walls.

When the lamp is lowered and the covers drawn up, the storm is only a muted brightness behind the window. The bridge and its leaning lamps are left for morning.
