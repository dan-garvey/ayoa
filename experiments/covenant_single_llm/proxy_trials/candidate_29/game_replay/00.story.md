Thessaly rests two fingers on the edge of the board while she considers the question.

"Then it is a draw," she says. "After the seventh white and seventh black, we leave the stones where they are. A near line is only a near line."

She slides the white pile a little closer to you. The fire gives a low pop behind her. At the study table, Rashid turns a sheet over beneath the lamp and resumes his careful writing; Ysolde has her grey-covered book open by the rain-streaked window.

You set your first white stone at the middle fork, where the pale routes divide toward both notches.

"Middle," Thessaly says. "I would have taken an edge."

Her first black stone goes below it, near enough to make a darkward run without touching your white. You extend your own line toward the left pale notch. Thessaly answers across the board, beginning a black route toward the far side instead of cutting off yours.

The stones make small, solid clicks on the carved crossings. On your third turn, you take the open place that would have joined her lower black stones. Thessaly looks at the board, then at the white stone you have just set down.

"That was inconvenient," she says.

She finds another way around it, farther from the dark notch and closer to the center. You bring white toward the opposite pale edge. Her fourth black stone takes the crossing that would have made your two newest stones a straight run; your fourth turns the route aside rather than abandoning it.

For a while the game is quiet except for rain on the windows and Rashid's pen moving over paper. The black line reaches toward both sides of the board in a shape that does not quite declare itself. Your fifth white stone closes one of its openings. Thessaly's next move reaches across the board and puts black beside the route you have been nursing toward the pale notch.

She leans back a fraction. "I dislike this board now."

You use your sixth stone to close the black opening nearest the lower dark notch. Thessaly studies the remaining crossings longer than she has before, then places black at the central turn. It does not connect either notch. It does leave two routes open.

Your seventh white stone blocks one of them.

Thessaly picks up her last black stone and holds it over the other. "I had meant to use the lower side," she says. "You made it unpleasant."

She sets the stone down.

The black path now runs, uneven but unbroken, from one dark notch to the other. Thessaly follows it with her eyes and nods once.

"Black."

Your white stones have come close to joining the pale notches twice, but a single black stone interrupts each route. Thessaly looks over the final arrangement.

"The upper line was prettier," she says. "This one merely works."

She begins sorting the pieces back into their separate piles, white on your side of the box and black on hers. When the board is clear, she fits it beneath the lid and presses the clasp shut. The rain continues against the glass, and Rashid's pen scratches on at the study table.
