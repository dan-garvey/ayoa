The paper is folded now, its ink hidden between the creases. At your question, Seraphel looks toward the high window, where pale lightning moves silently inside the clouds.

“There was.

A road song

about a ferryman

and a duck

that stole one of his shoes.

I knew only the chorus

at first.

Then I made up verses

until my mother forbade it

at breakfast.

That made it much better.”

She pulls the blue cloth back from the harp and turns a tuning peg. When the string answers, her fingers find a skipping measure, two quick notes chased by a lower one.

“Boatman, boatman,

one shoe gone;

the duck wears leather

and waddles on.

Boatman, boatman,

do not shout;

the duck knows rivers

in and out.”

She lets the last chord fade instead of stopping it.

“Apparently I have always liked

an animal upsetting

a serious man.”
