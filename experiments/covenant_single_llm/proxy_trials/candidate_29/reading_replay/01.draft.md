The folded page makes a neat, small square in your hands. Seraphel's gaze follows it, then drops to the blue cloth over her harp. A small smile appears.

“There was.

It was called

The Miller's Blue Hen.

The hen laid silver eggs

whenever the miller said

she could not.

My grandmother said

it encouraged insolence.

When I was seven,

I thought that was

the point.”

She lifts the cloth from the harp again and finds a bright, skittering pattern near the upper strings.

“Blue hen, blue hen,

one egg, two;

the miller says never,

the blue hen says blue.”

The final note gives a quick shine to the room before Seraphel quiets it beneath her palm.

“By winter,

the kitchen staff

sang it at me

when I was late for breakfast.”
