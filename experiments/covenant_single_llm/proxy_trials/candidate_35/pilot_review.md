## What the new situation accomplishes

A02 brings Dena to the house at the end of the delegated dinner. She challenges
Rashid's intention to arrive late, and Ashara offers to find someone else. Rashid
keeps the drill appointment and Dena accepts starting without him. A03 completes
the requested half-hour of drills with repeated attacks, changing counters, and
modest improvement. It does not invent a ranked victory. A04 brings Rashid back
to the game and lets Dena acknowledge his lateness. Those are actual activities
and an accommodation, not just announced plans. The two unnamed card friends
receive much less individual development than the duelists.

B03 similarly brings the card players into the house while Rashid and Ashara
leave for practice. B04 lets Rowan watch a hand, then B05 lets him join one. The
friends have their own rivalry; Thessaly comes to watch because games interest
her. Rashid's return and the actual offstage drills are not observed in B. This
is a scope limit, not permission to provide an omniscient completion report.
The game's unsupported winning state weakens the consequential finish, hence
the lower direction judgment here.

Both routes respect the player's choice of company. A declines cards initially,
watches training, then changes his mind and asks to join. B receives ordinary
company while eating and watching, without having to arrange the NPCs' business.
The player need not solve the overlapping commitments. Dena's accommodation is
plausible; a harsher refusal, lasting resentment, or confrontation was never a
required outcome. Her existing friendship can include forgiving an inconvenient
choice. The records should not turn that possibility into a general requirement
that NPCs always accept an apology.

The knowledge division works in the tested encounter: Ashara learns about the
earlier card arrangement through Dena and Rashid's public exchange. Dena learns
about drills when Rashid tells her. Neither demonstrates access to his private
expectation beforehand. Dena's confidential family concern is not disclosed, and
no conspiracy secret is used by an uninformed character. The new friends and
ordinary recollections are permissible invented background.

## Why the writing still needs material revision

The dinner repeatedly assigns everyone a finished contribution to an extended
verbal contest. A01's seating chart, bandages and travel lead into another
sequence; A02 turns plays, silence, contracts, weather and rhetoric into compact
positions. B02 answers a reasonable question about inheritance with multiple
lessons about chairs, contracts, favors, ceremony and institutions. These are
not isolated witty lines. The recurring arrangement makes different biographies
produce too much of the same social voice.

A03 is stronger when it follows the actual drills. Rashid and Ashara have
different tasks, physical habits and responses, and the repeated maneuver changes.
Its frequent correction-and-answer dialogue still adds commentary about habits,
accidents, kindness, weaknesses and coats. A04 and A05 transfer the same rhythm
to Dena and the card table: lateness becomes a different kind of failure; cards
acquire duties; looking is declared costly; matching cards become a lesson about
plans. The spoken analogies accumulate beyond the instruction actually needed.

B04's friends have a particular game and rivalry, but also trade prepared replies
about prediction, fragile words, cardboard, collateral pleasure and narrow wins.
B05 explicitly announces that card advice should not become general advice while
continuing to furnish conclusions about confidence, danger, courage and patience.
Neither ordinary joking nor formal speech is disallowed. The concern is the
cumulative similarity and the way the passages explain their own subtext.

## Logical state failures and narrower uncertainties

**A05 contains a definite material contradiction in the game's decisive state.**
The narration says Dena has not shown a banner yet, then immediately supplies
three green flowers already waiting faceup beside her token, to which she adds
three red lanterns. A05 has just clarified that completed banners are displayed
on the table. Both descriptions cannot be true as written. A previously unseen
history is not the issue: the same ending both denies and relies on that display.
The supplied two-banner win condition also does not explain why Dena gets another
draw after Rowan displays his second banner. A rule granting a final chance might
have been possible, but none was supplied; it cannot be retroactively assumed as
evidence that the published ending is coherent.

Earlier in A05, Rowan draws the tower two while retaining tower three and tower
four, which already form a valid sequence under the stated rules. Dena calls
these a pair of towers and the narration treats the ensuing advice as clarifying
the game. An NPC can give poor advice, but the passage does not establish that
as an intentional feature or acknowledge the valid banner. This is an additional
rules-handling concern; it need not be treated as a proven secret motive. A04's
initial explanation of a five-card hand was incomplete rather than necessarily
contradictory. A05's refill rule resolves that initial omission consistently.

**B05 supplies an unsupported winning total, including an apparent transfer of
the player's trick.** The second trick's high blue card wins for Rowan, and Dena
slides that crossing to him. The last-trick ending gives Dena three markers,
including the earlier blue win, with no described transfer. The narration numbers
the first and second tricks, the next two, and the last. Pell wins the first and
last, Rowan the second, and Dena one of the middle tricks; even awarding her the
other middle trick does not explain three separate winning crossings. An unstated
conversion or extra award is not observed support for this outcome. The new key
rule explains a way to change the winning suit, not a way to acquire an already
collected crossing; B05 explicitly says a key cannot retrieve one. This is a
material unresolved game-state failure, not evidence that a fictional game must
copy a known game.

B04's first trick describes a green lead from Dena, Pell's green, Maera's key,
then Dena's blue, but gathers three cards. B05 also gathers three played cards
after adding Rowan as a fourth player. The first passage's final key conversion
depends on unexplained arithmetic to make a silver-wing card win the changed
suit. These need concrete clarification. Some individual game mechanics are
underspecified, so not every unclear line proves a separate contradiction.
No invented exception should be used to silently clear the actual problems.

A03 refers to dinner waiting for Rashid, although the disclosed appointment is
cards after dinner. A04's claim of exactly half an hour late is also imprecise
against the practice and the walks. These are local continuity/clarity concerns,
distinct from the decisive displayed-banner contradiction.

## Player authority, POV and delivery

The played hands resolve explicitly delegated activities. B05 also expressly
delegates asking for needed rules. No new manuscript text or consequential
player speech is invented in these turns. The opening nevertheless has local
errors: A00 assigns a feeling about the prepared clothes and ends with an unseen
downstairs arrangement; B00 puts Rowan's coat on a chair despite the persona and
opening explicitly saying he is wearing it. B01's player-authored decision to
put it on does not erase the original mismatch. B00's comparison to a face in a
letter's signature is an unclear image, not a supported revelation. Other brief
narrator glosses interpret what people mean rather than leaving it perceptible.
These should not be conflated with an NPC accessing a conspiracy secret.

Opening files contain literal escaped line breaks, with stray plus signs in
A00's verse. They remain in the preserved first outputs. The input packets and
all later submissions were read in full; model/effort and unchanged output hashes
were checked from public tool records and artifacts, not private reasoning.

Provisional decision: reject C35 as tested after five turns per policy. The
changed situation produces practical follow-through but does not remove the
dialogue pattern, and both games add material state concerns. There are twelve
published passages and twelve author requests, not two completed long trials.
No story is rerolled or repaired, no prompt is promoted, and no runtime change is
made. An independent review will retain its own judgments and any disagreements.
