Seraphel keeps pace beside you down the stairs, leaving the center of each broad tread clear. The old wood has been rubbed pale along its middle by generations of feet. Portraits watch from the walls in the unhurried way portraits do, their dark coats, pale collars, and jeweled brooches made stranger by the blue-white weather beyond the tall windows.

At the bottom, the common room opens away in lamplight: leather chairs drawn toward an oversized fireplace, several study tables abandoned beneath their books, and the wide doors of the dining hall standing open beyond. The warmth there has a different source. Candle flames make small double images in the varnished table, and a row of working lamps throws clear light over bowls and silver. Seventeen places have been laid. Only eight are in use.

Your thanks meet no answering chorus. Aldric Verantus, seated near the middle in white and gold, gives a formal inclination of his head. Across from him, a woman whose red skin carries its own ember glow lifts two fingers from the stem of her glass; black-red horns sweep back from her tightly bound hair. The man beside her smiles, canines briefly visible, and moves the nearest serving dish toward the empty place. Fine dark fur catches against the cuff of his immaculate sleeve.

"The chair is yours," he says. "We have only been using it to keep the table symmetrical."

"We have not," says the horned woman.

"Then I have been doing it alone. An important distinction."

Seraphel's wings shift once as she takes the chair at your other side.

"Rashid's distinctions\nusually multiply\nwhen he is hungry."

"A cruel welcome," Rashid says, though he is still smiling.

The name-and-face business proceeds without any printed ceremony. The pale woman at the far side of the table has white hair gathered at her nape and a stillness that makes the candlelight seem restless around her.

"Ysolde Thornmantle," she says. Her grey eyes rest on the bowl before her. "The kitchen has used fennel."

Beside Ysolde, silver-haired Caelindra Vaeyn looks from the soup to Ysolde with measured skepticism. Her pointed ears are pinned with tiny amber leaves.

"Is that praise?" she asks.

"It is fennel."

"A precise and therefore deeply unhelpful answer," Rashid says.

The small woman near the window sets down her spoon. Her blue-black hair moves slowly over one shoulder though the dining hall has no breeze. Her eyes are entirely black.

"Thessaly Morrow," she says. "The soup is ordinary. That is useful to know." 

"Aldric Verantus," says Aldric, with the same composed gravity he brought to your arrival. "Welcome to Garvey House, Rowan. I hope it proves a good home while you are here."

Ashara vel Kothren does not embellish her introduction. "Ashara."

"And you know me," Rashid says, drawing the platter of roast vegetables closer so you can follow its passage around the table. "At least well enough to blame me for the opening claim about furniture."

The serving dishes move in a rhythm that becomes plain after a turn or two: soup first, then the saffron rice, then the vegetables, then a narrow silver boat of the peppery sauce that had scented the stairwell. An Academy server in dark livery refills water glasses with the quiet efficiency of someone who has heard every version of an important dinner and decided none of them requires notice.

For the first few minutes, talk travels around you rather than toward you. Rashid complains that the new rankings board outside the octagon has been hung low enough for rain to reach it. Ashara says the rain has better judgment than the person who posted it. He tells her that this is a poor attitude to bring to tomorrow's bout. She says it is precisely the attitude she intends to bring.

Caelindra, who has been separating the peel from a piece of roasted carrot with exacting care, remarks that a school able to build bridges out of light ought to manage a noticeboard. Ysolde says the bridge makers and the noticeboard makers are not the same people. Thessaly observes that this has not stopped either party from claiming credit for the other. Seraphel's laugh comes out in three soft, surprised notes before she catches it.

"The board survives,\nwhich is more than can be said\nfor several arguments about it."

Even Ashara's mouth bends at that. The moment passes into the ordinary work of dinner: bowls emptied, bread broken, a candle guttering and righting itself. Outside the high windows, lightning moves soundlessly through the storm beneath the plateau, turning the garden walls into hard black cuts for an instant at a time.

It is Aldric who brings the conversation back to the reason for the extra candles and the full table. He folds his hands beside his plate.

"The Academy's notice called today a restoration. That is a large word for one person's arrival."

"The Academy enjoys large words," Caelindra says. "They fill the spaces where it has not done the work."

Rashid's expression settles into something more deliberate. "It has had sixty years to explain why a Council can call itself complete with an empty seat."

"It has had sixty years to keep a peace that was not guaranteed," Aldric replies. There is no heat in his voice, but his thumb presses once against the rim of his glass. "Those are not identical failures."

"No," Ashara says. "They are merely related ones."

No one reaches for a grander argument. The sauce boat makes another circuit. Seraphel looks down at the soup she has nearly finished, and Thessaly studies the rain stippling the dark window as though it has offered terms too small to read.

Ashara sets her spoon beside her bowl. Her gold eyes settle briefly on the Garvey signet before returning to your face. When she speaks, the question is level enough to leave room for any answer.

"How much did the people who brought you here actually tell you before they decided this was yours?"
