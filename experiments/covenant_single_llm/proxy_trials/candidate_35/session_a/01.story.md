The latch gives without ceremony.

Aldric Verantus stands on the other side in white and gold, the lamplight gathering warmly on his skin and in the embroidery at his cuffs. He is tall enough that the corridor seems to have been built with a smaller person in mind, but he has taken care to leave the doorway clear. At your introduction, he inclines his head.

“Aldric,” he says. “Welcome to Garvey House, Rowan.”

The woman beside the stair has no need to make room for herself. She stands half a head taller than Aldric’s shoulder before the swept black-red horns, with a tailored dark coat over a strong frame and a black tail curved close along the landing’s narrow rail. Her gold eyes settle on you when you ask about tomorrow’s bout.

“You may watch,” she says. “The octagon is open during ranked practice. Stay beyond the white stones unless one of the fighters calls you in. They are there for a reason.”

“Ashara vel Kothren,” Aldric says, making the introduction before she appears interested in doing it herself.

“Ashara is enough.” Her gaze returns briefly toward the stair. “Rashid and I are scheduled after dinner. The bout is tomorrow afternoon.”

The man below her comes up one step, enough for the lamp to find the fine dark fur at his wrists where it meets his immaculate sleeve. He is dressed for supper with the same care someone else might give an examination, and his amber eyes take in the open door, Aldric, and the fact that Ashara has already answered.

“Rashid vel Amara,” he says. “She has made the boundary sound more perilous than it is. You need only avoid becoming a third opponent.”

“It is perilous if he steps across it at the wrong time.”

“Which is why he has been told.” Rashid’s mouth turns at one corner. “You see, we are exceptionally thorough.”

Aldric steps aside as the three of you start down. The house is warmer on the stairs, the fire in the common room sending a slow pulse of heat up through the open center. Portraits in dark frames follow the landing wall: severe men and women in old-fashioned collars, their painted eyes fixed on a household none of them could have imagined. At the bottom, the common room opens around the oversized hearth. Its fire has burned down to red-gold logs, and conversation has gathered in its light.

A pale woman stands at the garden-facing window with one hand resting on the sill. White hair falls straight down her back. She has the stillness of something waiting through weather rather than merely looking at it.

“The storm has shifted east,” she says, without turning. “The western bridge will be clear by morning.”

“Ysolde Thornmantle,” Aldric says.

Ysolde turns then, grey-eyed and cool-skinned in the firelight. “I am Ysolde.” Her attention rests on you for the length of a breath, then moves to the coat rack near the door as if checking whether it is likely to topple under the weight of formal outerwear.

Near one of the leather chairs, a small woman with blue-black hair is arranging a hand of lacquered cards into a precise fan. Her entirely black eyes lift from them.

“Thessaly Morrow,” she says. “I was told there would be dinner. That was true.”

She places the cards face down on the side table and rises. No one asks what game she had been playing.

The woman by the fire has small white wings visible against a white-and-gold coat, folded carefully enough that their feathers do not catch the chair behind her. Her bright blue eyes move between the gathered faces. When she speaks, the words come in a plain, soft measure.

“A new name at the table,\n+and soup already cooling.\n+We have done worse things.”

“Seraphel Dawnquill,” Aldric says, with the faintest pause before her family name.

Seraphel’s smile is quick. “Seraphel.”

The dining hall beyond the common room has been made bright against the storm. Candles run down the long varnished table between low bowls of late-summer flowers, while working lamps keep the paneled walls from swallowing the corners. Servers in Academy livery move between the chairs with covered dishes. At one place setting, an ivory card bears your name in dark ink.

Caelindra Vaeyn is already there, standing beside her chair with one hand on its carved back. Her silver hair has been arranged into a shape too elaborate to have survived an ordinary walk across campus by accident. She examines the new card, then you, violet eyes steady.

“Caelindra,” she says. “The Academy has placed you between Seraphel and Ysolde. It seems to have decided proximity will make us civilized.”

“It has high hopes,” Thessaly says.

“It has a seating chart,” Ysolde replies. “Those are separate things.”

For the first time since the door opened, Ashara laughs outright. It is short, but it changes the room’s balance more than the candles do.

The servers uncover the first course: broth fragrant with pepper and citrus, dark bread still steaming where it has been broken, small dishes of greens glossed in butter. Aldric takes the chair across from yours rather than the one at the table’s head. Rashid sits beside Ashara, though the space between their settings is no smaller than anywhere else around the table.

“To an uneventful first dinner,” Aldric says, lifting his spoon a fraction before using it. “And to making Rowan’s arrival less of an occasion tomorrow than it is tonight.”

“You may be disappointed,” Ashara says. “The first-ranked bout tends to draw an audience.”

“A qualified audience,” Rashid says. “We should be grateful for the distinction.”

“I am grateful when it is useful.”

Seraphel lowers her gaze to the steam rising from her bowl.

“No oaths before bread,\n+no wounds before dessert.”

“That seems sensible,” Ysolde says. “Dessert is difficult to clean from bandages.”

Caelindra looks from Ysolde to Seraphel, then takes a measured spoonful of broth. “It is remarkable that this is the least alarming table I have dined at this month.”

“You should travel less,” Thessaly says.

“I have traveled only as much as was required.”

“Then your requirements are alarming.”

Rashid reaches for the bread basket, then pauses with his hand above it. His attention has settled on the dark stains at your fingers, made more apparent against the pale bowl and the silver setting.

“What do you write, Rowan?”
