The room has been made ready for someone staying a little while, and no longer. The narrow bed is freshly made, its striped blanket tucked beneath the mattress. One wardrobe door sits slightly higher than the other. On the desk, the lamp throws a small circle of light over the welcome sheet and the keys Nell handed you: supper at half past six; the staff staircase beside the office; induction at nine tomorrow.

Your luggage takes up most of the clear floor between the bed and desk. Beyond the window, Harbour Street slopes towards the harbour. The evening light catches a parked van's windscreen and the upper panes across the road; farther down, the water has begun to turn the same grey as the sky.

From below comes the clink of plates, then a woman's voice from the kitchen.

“Owen, thinner than that. We need the bread to go round.”

“I am cutting it thinner.”

“The last one was nearly a doorstep.”

“It was a generous slice.”

“Put it on the plate before you start defending it.”

A drawer shuts. Someone laughs softly, close to the kitchen, and the sound of a knife resumes against a board.

At the foyer tables, another woman says, “Martin, when we go in, can you tell me again about the blue board? Slowly this time.”

“I told you. I painted the lettering. Pete changed the showtime.”

“Pete who?”

“Hargreaves. He did late shifts.”

“And you're sure it was him?”

“I didn't say I was sure.”

“You said it as though you were.”

A second woman joins in, amused but firm. “Write down what Martin remembers, Rosa. We can check the programmes before anything goes to print.”

“That's what I'm doing,” the first woman says. “I'm asking before he gets ahead of himself.”

“I don't get ahead of myself,” Martin replies. “I get interrupted.”

Across the landing, a door clicks open. A man calls down the stairwell. “Nell? Can I use the auditorium just after seven?”

The office door opens below. “For what?”

“The hearing receivers arrived early. Hana can help until half seven, and I need somebody moving round the seats while I check the signal.”

“Rosa and Martin have the auditorium from seven to twenty to eight,” Nell says. Her voice is even, but it carries clearly up the stairwell. “It was booked on Friday.”

There is a short pause.

“I didn't know,” the man says. “The cases only came in this afternoon.”

“I need the room quiet,” Rosa calls from the foyer. “The test audio will be on the recordings.”

“I know. I'm not asking you to move.”

“Don't take the receivers down yet,” Nell says. “Call Hana. If she can come earlier, we'll make it work before seven. If she can't, we'll find another evening.”

“All right. I'll ask.”

From the kitchen, Jo calls, “And nobody starts a test while the pie's on the table. It's coming out now.”

The welcome sheet remains beneath the desk lamp. Beyond the room door, the staircase leads down into the warm foyer and the voices of people whose names you have only just begun to place. What does Rowan do?
