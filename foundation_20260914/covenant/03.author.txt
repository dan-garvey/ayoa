The last dessert dishes disappear beneath the attendants' trays as you thank the table and make your plans plain. Ashara has already pushed back her chair; the low light catches red along her horns when she looks up.

“Then have your quiet evening,” she says. “The octagon will still be there tomorrow.”

Rashid's gaze goes briefly to the doorway and then back. “The card table is hardly a solemn obligation. Good night, Rowan.”

“Good night,” Ysolde says. “The wind is louder against the upper windows after dark. It does not mean anything is wrong.”

Caelindra gathers the fall of one silver sleeve clear of the candlewax near her plate. “That is perhaps the most useful thing anyone has said all evening.”

Seraphel's small wings settle close behind her shoulders.

“May the old house keep
a gentle watch tonight.”

Thessaly glances from Seraphel to the high, shadowed ceiling. “Does it do that?”

“No,” Caelindra says.

“I did not think so.”

The exchange has already loosened as the first-years rise and sort themselves toward the common room and its separate plans. You leave the dining hall's candlelight behind and take the stairs while the sound of chairs and conversation recedes below.

Your luggage waits beside the bed where it was left. Over the next several minutes, you put the contents away: folded clothes into the wardrobe, smaller travel things into the drawers and washstand, loose papers squared on the desk. The room grows less like a place prepared for a stranger and more like a room with its surfaces in use.

At the desk, you take a clean page and write the names you learned at dinner in a plain vertical list:

Ashara vel Kothren
Rashid vel Amara
Ysolde Thornmantle
Caelindra Vaeyn
Seraphel Dawnquill
Thessaly Morrow
Aldric Verantus

By the time you set the pen down, twenty minutes have passed. The sounds from the ground floor have thinned to an occasional footstep and the muted closing of a door; beyond the window, the garden lamps hold steady against the dark.
