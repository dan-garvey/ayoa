The last dessert dishes disappear beneath the attendants’ trays as you thank the table and say that you are going upstairs for a quiet evening, declining both the cards and the practice.

Ashara has one hand on the back of her chair. She glances at Rashid before looking back to you. “Fine. He and I have enough to work through.”

Rashid folds his napkin beside the untouched pear. “You said you wanted a partner.”

“I said I wanted practice.”

His mouth moves as if he is deciding whether to answer that. Then he turns to you instead. “Good night, Rowan. Dena has enough people at the table. We will manage without another player.”

Caelindra is disentangling the edge of one silver cuff from a loose thread in her napkin. “If you want something quieter tomorrow, the fiction is upstairs in the library, beside the north windows. Avoid the yellow volumes. They are Professor Windwhisper’s lectures, and every one of them is longer than it needs to be.”

“That is useful,” Rashid says.

“I know.” Caelindra frees the cuff and lets the napkin fall.

Ysolde looks toward the stairwell. “The garden-facing windows use a brass catch. Turn it twice before you leave it for the night. The first turn does not hold.”

“The primer should have included that,” Caelindra says.

“The primer is not about windows,” Aldric replies. He has risen from his chair, straightening one sleeve. “Our first seminar begins at eight. The garden path is less crowded than the front steps at that hour.”

Seraphel’s small wings settle close behind her shoulders.

“Good night, Rowan.
The lamp at the second landing
takes a moment to wake.”

Thessaly looks toward the stairs. “I thought it was refusing to light for me.”

“It needs a firmer touch,” Ysolde says.

“Then it was refusing,” Thessaly says, and takes the last bite of her almond cake.

Ashara gives Rashid a pointed look. “Common room first. Tell them you will be late.”

“I was going to.”

“Then go.”

The table breaks apart around that exchange. Aldric moves toward the common room with a nod in your direction; Caelindra gathers the sleeve of her coat clear of a drop of candlewax before following at an unhurried pace. Ysolde waits for an attendant carrying a tray to pass, then leaves by the garden doors. Rashid and Ashara cross toward the common room together, already talking over the left-side turn in lower voices.

You take the stairs to your room. Your luggage waits beside the bed where it was left. Over the next several minutes, you put folded clothes into the wardrobe, smaller travel things into the drawers and washstand, and loose papers into a squared stack on the desk. You hang the charcoal coat over the chair.

At the desk, you take a clean page and write the names you learned at dinner in a plain vertical list:

Ashara vel Kothren
Rashid vel Amara
Ysolde Thornmantle
Caelindra Vaeyn
Seraphel Dawnquill
Thessaly Morrow
Aldric Verantus

By the time the ink has dried on the last name, twenty minutes have passed. A door closes somewhere on the ground floor. Beyond the window, the garden lamps remain lit along the pale paths.
