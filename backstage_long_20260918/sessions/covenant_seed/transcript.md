# covenant: published story

## Turn 1

**Player**

Begin the story.

**Story**

The ink on your right forefinger has dried into a dark crescent beside the Garvey signet. It looks incongruous against the mended cuff of your charcoal coat: an heirloom heavy enough to leave its mark, worn by someone who still has not learned the proper order of forks.

Your room is warm, though rainless lightning keeps whitening the gardens beyond the window. Far below the plateau, the storm turns slowly beneath the Academy’s bridges. A trunk sent by the lawyers stands open near the made bed, half-full of clothes chosen by people who knew your measurements but not, apparently, your taste.

On the desk, a framed letter leans against a lamp. The handwriting is firm and old-fashioned.

*My dear Tomas,* it begins. *When you come to Garvey House—*

A dinner bell sounds downstairs: three clear notes.

Then, through the floorboards, a woman’s voice says, “If the lamps are meant to be decorative, they are doing a poor job of it.”

Another voice—male, precise—answers, “They are functional lamps, Caelindra.”

“Then their failure is worse.”

A brief laugh follows, low and unrestrained. Someone else says something too softly to catch.

A knock comes at your door. When you open it, an Academy server in dark blue livery stands waiting with a silver-covered tray balanced on one hand.

“Welcome dinner is served, Mr. Garvey,” she says. “The dining hall is downstairs, through the common room. They’ve begun without you, but only just.”

Beyond her shoulder, the staircase descends toward firelight and the murmur of seven strangers who have inherited, in one fashion or another, the futures of their people.

## Turn 2

**Player**

I go down to dinner, introduce myself as Rowan, and take an available place at the table.

**Story**

The common room gives way to the dining hall in a wash of candlelight and polished wood. Silver lids have been lifted from the serving dishes; steam carries pepper, browned butter, and something sharp with citrus along the length of the table.

Conversation thins as you enter.

“I’m Rowan Garvey,” you say.

A server pulls back the nearest vacant chair. It stands midway down the table, beside a pale, white-haired woman whose glass is packed with melting ice.

“Ysolde Thornmantle,” she says. “That chair’s leg is loose. Don’t lean back.”

Across from you, the small woman with blue-black hair and entirely black eyes glances at the place setting. “Thessaly Morrow. It was unclaimed.”

A young man in white and gold rises from farther down the table, exact even in the small courtesy. “Aldric Verantus. Welcome, Rowan.”

“Rashid vel Amara.” The impeccably dressed beastkin gestures toward the bowl a server is bringing your way. “Take the soup while it’s hot. It’s the best thing on the table.”

A laugh comes from the tall horned woman seated beside him. Her tail has hooked neatly around the back leg of her chair. “Ashara vel Kothren. Ignore him; he says that about any food he hasn’t had to cook.”

“I say it about food that deserves defending.”

At the other end, the silver-haired elf tilts her head toward one of the working lamps. “Caelindra Vaeyn. You’ve arrived in time to see Aldric defend these atrocities.”

“They are lamps,” Aldric says.

“They are an argument against sight.”

The winged woman beside him smiles, her warm gold light catching along the rim of her cup.

“I’m Seraphel Dawnquill. I hope your journey here was fair.  
Take the chair beside Ysolde—there’s space enough to spare.”

You sit. The loose leg gives a small, honest creak beneath you.

A server fills your bowl with thick soup, pale beans and ribbons of roasted greens beneath a crust of peppered oil. Rashid is right about the heat. Ashara reaches past him for a dish of charred red peppers without asking; he shifts the bread basket out of the way before her sleeve can catch it, the motion practiced enough not to interrupt his conversation.

Caelindra has abandoned the lamps for the moment. Her violet eyes settle on the ink at your fingers, then on the mended cuff beneath the heavy Garvey signet.

“You write?” she asks.

## Turn 3

**Player**

I say, "Most recently I earned my keep copying books. I haven't had much practice at being an heir." I help myself to dinner.

**Story**

You help yourself from the dishes within reach: roast squash glazed dark at the edges, sliced fowl beneath citrus and herbs, a bowl of sharp green beans. If there are rules for the several forks beside your plate, no one interrupts you to demonstrate them.

Caelindra considers your answer over the rim of her glass.

“Then I asked the wrong question,” she says. “Copying requires attention. Most people who claim to write have very little of it.”

Ashara takes a charred pepper from the dish between her and Rashid. “Knowing you haven’t been trained is not the same as being trained. But it’s better than arriving convinced the signet did it for you.”

“Let him have dinner before you begin grading him,” Rashid says.

“I’m not grading him.”

“You have the exact voice you use when grading people.”

Ashara looks at him. “You’ve never submitted work to me.”

“I’ve survived enough conversations.”

Aldric sets down his cup. “The Conclave exists to prepare its students. No one expects Rowan to have mastered a role he learned about only recently.”

“The Academy has had a very long time to prepare for a human heir,” Ashara says. “It apparently chose not to.”

Thessaly cuts a piece of squash with careful attention. “The primer is poorly organized. It explains seating precedence before it explains where to find breakfast.”

“That is a genuine defect,” Ysolde says. She slides the pepper dish a little nearer your place. “Those are hotter than they smell.”

Seraphel’s smile catches the candlelight.

“Let papers, titles, and old blood wait awhile;  
A first good meal may make the evening mild.”

Rashid lifts his spoon toward her in agreement. “A policy I can support.”

Caelindra’s attention returns to you, more direct now than dismissive.

“What sort of books did you copy?”

## Turn 4

**Player**

I eat and listen to the conversation around me, without trying to take charge of it.

**Story**

The question hangs for only a moment before a server reaches across with the potatoes, and the table’s attention drifts on without insisting on an answer.

One of the lamps over the far paneling flickers twice, then steadies.

Caelindra looks up at it. “There. Proof.”

“It’s still working,” Aldric says.

“It is intermittently producing light. That is not the same thing.”

“The wiring runs near the southern terrace,” Ysolde says. “Storm charge affects it.”

Ashara glances at the lamp. “So it’s a cowardly lamp.”

Rashid laughs into his cup. Even Caelindra’s mouth moves at one corner before she returns to her dinner.

You eat while the talk shifts around you: tomorrow’s first seminar, a lecturer Ashara calls intolerably fond of diagrams, the quality of the Academy bread compared with bread in each of their homes. Thessaly says the rolls are better when stolen from the kitchen at midnight; when Aldric asks how she knows, she looks at him steadily.

“I stole one.”

“That isn’t—”

“It was bread, Aldric. It recovered.”

Seraphel covers a smile with her napkin.

“A roll survives a midnight raid with grace;  
Its murderer leaves crumbs to mark the place.”

Rashid points his spoon at Thessaly. “A careless criminal.”

“I wasn’t concealing it.”

“No,” Caelindra says. “That part is becoming clear.”

The meal lengthens pleasantly despite itself. A server replaces emptied dishes with a tart glazed in dark berries, and the rainless lightning beyond the tall windows makes the silverware flash white.

When the last plates are being cleared, Ashara sets down her napkin and looks to Rashid.

“Octagon. Five minutes.”

“For half an hour,” he says.

“That was the arrangement.”

“After that, I’ve cards in the common room. Dena and the others said they’d begin without me.”

Ashara’s gold eyes narrow a fraction. “You did not mention cards.”

“I’m mentioning them now.”

“You put two plans in the same half-hour.”

“I put drills in the first half-hour and cards after it.”

“You’ll be late.”

“I know.”

For a beat, she studies him. Then her tail gives the chair leg one brisk tap.

“Fine. Half an hour. If you complain about your footwork tomorrow, I’ll remind you that cards were apparently urgent.”

“I won’t complain.”

“You always complain.”

“Only when you’re right.”

That earns him a short laugh from her. They rise almost together, Ashara already turning toward the garden doors that lead out toward the dueling grounds.

Ysolde takes the remaining ice from her glass in one quiet swallow. “The weather is clearing above the western bridge,” she says, mostly to herself, and leaves by the opposite door.

Aldric gathers his papers with the look of someone who intends to prepare for tomorrow’s seminar before anyone else can make him regret it. Caelindra lingers long enough to inspect the berry tart as if it might have disappointed her personally, then follows him out toward the library. Thessaly remains at the table, turning a silver spoon between two fingers while the servants clear the last settings.

The formal dinner disperses into the ordinary sounds of Garvey House: chairs shifted back, low voices beyond the dining hall, the crackle of the common-room fire.
