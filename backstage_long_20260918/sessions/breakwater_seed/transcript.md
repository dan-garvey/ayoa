# breakwater: published story

## Turn 1

**Player**

Begin the story.

**Story**

The upstairs room has the clean, temporary look of a place made ready for someone whose name was not known until recently: narrow bed, small wardrobe, desk beneath the window. Your luggage waits where it was brought in. Beyond the glass, Harbour Street slopes toward a strip of pale water, fishing boats reduced by distance to dark marks against the evening.

On the desk, the welcome sheet sits beside the lamp.

**Supper, 18:30 — foyer tables**  
**Induction, 09:00 tomorrow — office**  
**Staff staircase beside office**

Nell’s mobile number is printed underneath in careful block type.

Below, crockery touches crockery. A door swings shut, followed by a man’s voice carrying up from the foyer—too indistinct to make out—then a brief burst of laughter. From farther overhead comes a softer mechanical clatter, as if someone is working with a case of tools.

The coat’s mended cuff catches faintly against the desk edge when you stand near it. Downstairs, someone says, “Not that stack, those are programmes,” and another voice answers, “I know what programmes look like.”

At the bottom of the stairs, warm light reaches across the landing floorboards.

## Turn 2

**Player**

I go down to the staff supper, introduce myself as Rowan, and take an available place.

**Story**

You follow the staircase down into the foyer, where the last of the daylight still reaches through the glazed doors.

Two square café tables have been pushed together beneath the old film posters. A woman with a dark braid and a pencil tucked behind one ear is straightening a stack of folded opening programmes; beside her, an older man in a navy work shirt is examining the leg of a chair as if it has personally offended him. Bea Finch sits with both hands around a mug, watching the room with an alert, patient expression.

Nell comes out of the office with a clipboard under one arm. “Rowan. Good—you found us.”

You introduce yourself, and she gestures around the tables.

“Rosa Chen, who’s making the reopening exhibition. Martin Vale, who knows where every loose screw in this building has been since 1982. Bea Finch, our treasurer.”

“Former usher,” Bea adds. “Welcome.”

Martin rises enough to offer a hand. “Archive tomorrow, is it? You’ll want a decent pencil. Half the ones people leave lying about here are fit only for stirring paint.”

Rosa smiles. “Ignore him about the pencil. He’s right about most other things, unfortunately.”

From the kitchen doorway, a tall man in a rolled-sleeve shirt carries out a bowl of green salad. “Owen,” he says. “There’s a clear chair there, if you want it.”

A woman behind him sets a pie dish on the counter with a pair of oven gloves. “Jo. You’re not contributing tonight,” she says. “First supper’s on me. After that, you’ll find we all become extremely interested in receipts.”

Nell gives Jo a look that is more fond than reproachful. “Rowan’s the cataloguer for six weeks, not a new source of free labour.”

“Good. We’re already overstocked.”

You take the open chair between Owen and Bea. The food begins arriving: tomato-and-chickpea stew in a broad blue pot, the cheese-and-onion pie with its browned crust cut into uneven wedges, bread wrapped in a tea towel. For a few minutes, the table is occupied by plates, serving spoons, and the small practical negotiations of people finding room for one another.

Footsteps sound on the staff stairs. A man with a compact black equipment case comes down, pauses at the sight of the laid table, and puts it carefully beside the office door.

“Sorry,” he says. “The booth decided it wanted to be mysterious.”

“This is Dev,” Nell says. “Dev Malik, projection and sound.”

Dev pulls out the remaining chair. “And you’re Rowan. Welcome to the glamorous end of cinema.”

Martin points his fork toward the ceiling. “He means cables.”

“I mean exactly cables.”

Jo passes Dev a plate before he can reach for one. “Eat first. Be mysterious later.”

Conversation settles around the meal. Rosa tells Bea that the printer wants the exhibition layout by Wednesday, and Bea says she will bring the old usher photographs another day, “once I’ve worked out what belongs in public and what doesn’t.” Martin begins to object, then thinks better of it and tears off a piece of bread instead.

Rosa turns toward you, her pencil still tucked behind her ear.

“So—have you worked with old paper archives before?”

## Turn 3

**Player**

I ask Nell what my first day of archive work will involve.

**Story**

Rosa lets her question rest as you turn to Nell and ask about the morning.

Nell sets down her fork. “At nine, we’ll go through the archive workroom and the records system. The first job is knowing what we have, where it came from, and what we’re allowed to do with it.”

She gestures lightly toward the stairwell. “You’ll start with the trust’s business papers and programme boxes. They’re comparatively straightforward. For each item or group: description, date if we can establish it, condition, provenance, and whether it belongs to the trust or is on loan. If something’s fragile, unclear, or has restrictions attached, you flag it rather than trying to solve it alone.”

“So,” Bea says, “don’t let Martin persuade you that a photograph is definitely from 1987 because he remembers the weather.”

“I do remember the weather,” Martin says.

Rosa breaks a piece of bread in half. “You give all your dates weather. That’s not the same thing.”

Nell’s mouth shifts briefly toward a smile. “There are loan records to read before you handle any of those boxes. Some people are happy for material to be described but not copied or displayed. The paperwork matters as much as the object.”

She lifts her water glass. “By the end of tomorrow, I’d like you comfortable enough to make a proper start. Not to have conquered the whole building.”

Dev’s phone vibrates against the table. He glances at the screen, then looks up. “Nell—before I forget. Hana can help with the hearing-assistance test from seven. Could I have the auditorium from five past?”

Nell’s expression changes as she reaches for the clipboard beside her chair. “Rosa and Martin have it from seven until twenty to eight.”

Dev looks toward the auditorium doors. “For what?”

“The programme boards,” Rosa says. “Photographs and Martin’s notes on who painted them. It was booked on Friday.”

“I didn’t know,” Dev says at once. “I should’ve checked.”

“I could do it later,” Rosa says, though she does not sound pleased by the idea.

“No,” Nell says. “Your booking stands. Dev, don’t bring Hana in for a test that can’t happen. Ask when she’s next free and send it to me; I’ll put it in the diary properly.”

For a moment, Dev studies his phone. Then he nods. “All right.”

He steps away from the table to make the call, leaving his plate untouched beside the stew.

## Turn 4

**Player**

I help myself to food and listen to the conversation around the table.

**Story**

You serve yourself stew, pie, bread, and a spoonful of salad. The stew is thick with chickpeas and softened onion; steam fogs the inside of the blue pot’s lid where Jo has set it aside.

At the office doorway, Dev speaks quietly into his phone.

“No—sorry. I should’ve checked before I called you. Don’t make the trip for this. I’ll send you a proper time when we’ve got one.”

He listens for a moment, thanks Hana, and comes back to the table, slipping the phone into his pocket.

“She’s not coming in,” he says. “She’s at a hotel after half seven. I’ve asked her to send over her next free evenings.”

Nell nods. “Thank you. I’ll put an actual slot in the diary.”

“I should’ve looked in it first,” Dev says. “That’s mine.”

Rosa gives a short nod across the table. “Thanks.”

Jo points her serving spoon at Dev’s plate. “Then you can eat before the pie turns into building material.”

The conversation shifts back to the exhibition. Rosa unfolds a sheet of notes beside her glass.

“The boards are all ready where we left them,” she says to Martin. “I’ll take the photographs first, then I need you talking me through the changes. Names where you know them, dates only where you’re sure.”

Martin chews, considering this as seriously as if it were a mechanical specification. “The blue one was definitely Rafi Bell’s lettering. He used that little brush with the split ferrule. Could get a line narrow as thread out of it.”

“And the dates?” Rosa asks.

“I’ll tell you when I’m guessing.”

“That’ll do.”

Bea looks from one to the other. “It matters. People did that work here for years, and most of it went up for a week, then got painted over.”

Nell says, “That’s part of why Rowan’s records need sources as well as descriptions. A note can say Martin remembers it; it doesn’t have to pretend it came stamped with a date.”

Martin glances toward you. “Fair system, that.”

Owen has been cutting his pie into neat pieces. “Do you want me nearby when you’re moving the boards back afterward?”

“We’re only taking pictures,” Rosa says. “But maybe at twenty to eight, if you’re still about.”

“Fine by me.”

Jo shakes her head, though there is no real objection in it. “You lot are incapable of eating without arranging furniture around one another.”

“Cinema work,” Martin says. “It gets in your blood.”

“Most things get in your blood if you spend enough years not washing properly,” Bea says.

That earns a laugh from Owen and Rosa. Martin accepts it with a look of grave injury and reaches for more bread.

As the clock above the café counter approaches seven, the meal loosens into smaller exchanges: Jo asking Bea whether the new crockery has been counted; Nell making a note against the opening-weekend rota; Dev describing, in less technical language than before, the small receivers he needs to test. He leaves the equipment case by the office door for now.

Rosa gathers her camera bag and notebook. Martin takes up a pencil and a folded list of board titles. Together they cross the foyer to the auditorium doors, with Bea following far enough to hold one open while they take the first board inside.

“No sound through there for forty minutes,” Rosa calls back lightly.

“None,” Nell says.

The doors close behind them. In the foyer, Jo begins stacking plates, Owen carries the empty stew pot toward the kitchen, and Dev picks up his case for the stairs.
