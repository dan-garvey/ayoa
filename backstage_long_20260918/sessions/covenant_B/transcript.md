**Story**

The ink on your right forefinger has dried into a dark crescent beside the Garvey signet. It looks incongruous against the mended cuff of your charcoal coat: an heirloom heavy enough to leave its mark, worn by someone who still has not learned the proper order of forks.

A trunk sent by the lawyers stands open near the made bed, half-full of clothes chosen by people who knew your measurements but not, apparently, your taste.

On the desk, a framed letter leans against a lamp. The handwriting is firm and old-fashioned.

*My dear Tomas,* it begins. *When you come to Garvey House—*

A dinner bell sounds downstairs: three clear notes.

Then, through the floorboards, a woman’s voice says, “If the lamps are meant to be decorative, they are doing a poor job of it.”

Another voice—male, precise—answers, “They are functional lamps, Caelindra.”

“Then their failure is worse.”

A brief laugh follows, low and unrestrained. Someone else says something too softly to catch.

A knock comes at your door. When you open it, an Academy server in dark blue livery stands waiting with a silver-covered tray balanced on one hand.

“Welcome dinner is served, Mr. Garvey,” she says. “The dining hall is downstairs, through the common room. They’ve begun without you, but only just.”

Beyond her shoulder, the staircase descends toward firelight and the murmur of seven strangers who have inherited, in one fashion or another, the futures of their people.
