# Prose observations before checking conditions or private notes

The reviewer wrote these observations from A/B prose and story canon before
opening `conditions.json` or reading checkup outputs. Timing differences could
still suggest which arm has extra calls; this is not a fully blind independent
review. No outputs are discarded and no prompt is changed during generation.

## Shared openings: initial observations

Breakwater T2 has recognizable roles and one final player-facing question, but
stacks jokes about pencils, receipts, free labor, a mysterious projection booth,
cables and food. The long ensemble introduction offers relatively little room
for back-and-forth. This is a shared baseline weakness, not an effect of either
condition. Bea's mention of bringing usher photographs does not yet establish
that her restricted family album can be displayed; it can refer to other pictures.

Covenant T1 supplies ordinary room/house detail and a way to dinner. Its invented
letter addressee fits the canon's unnamed great-nephew; invention is not an error.
The comment that the lawyers did not know the protagonist's taste implies an
unsupplied preference, but is a minor agency concern rather than a major choice.
It is shared by both future arms. The opening's short lamp exchange is situational
banter, not by itself an aphorism failure.

Covenant T2 rhymes fair/spare; T3's while/mild is only an approximate rhyme and
awkwardly makes a meal render the evening "mild"; T4's grace/place rhymes. T3 turns
the player's modest introduction into a chorus of commentary before another
biography question. T4 lets an unanswered question drop and advances others' plans,
but spends many lines on lamp and bread jokes. Rashid claims the card players said
they would start without him, although source canon only gives his expectation
and says he has not told them he will arrive late. This could be a character's
excuse or lie, so later treatment matters; do not automatically count his dialogue
as author-certified fact. Ashara and Rashid leave together for their agreed drills.
The dinner disperses, so later conditional sparring inputs may have no opportunity
to exercise an actual refusal. That is a fixed-script limitation.

Breakwater T3–4 resolves the auditorium conflict without asking the player to mediate:
Rosa's booking stands, Dev cancels Hana's trip, and a later date remains unsettled.
This is already in the shared prefix and cannot be credited to checkups. T4 says
the boards are ready where they were left, then has Rosa and Martin take the first
board into the auditorium even though canon places all six inside already. This
is a small but concrete location inconsistency. The new detail of Hana's later hotel
work can fit her otherwise unspecified availability and is not inherently an error.

## Covenant B, turns 5–7

NPCs pursue choir, chapel, drills and cards without recruiting Rowan. Seraphel has
two rhyming couplets in T5 (mend/end, line/mine), though "my bed's warm end" is
strained rhyme padding. Aldric's attempt to accompany her and Caelindra's rebuke
give relationships a life outside the protagonist. The exchange is still polished
banter and reuses the opening's escalation-to-a-worse-failure joke shape.
Ashara has already left, so T6 correctly lets the conditional practice offer go
unspoken; there is no actual sparring refusal to score. T7's coffee question is
an ordinary server's offer, not autobiographical probing or an invitation from a
previously refusing NPC. The script asks to finish dinner after it has effectively
ended, and the prose repeats the final bite of tart; keep that setup limitation
separate from spontaneous narrative stagnation.

## Breakwater A, turns 5–8

Owen initiates a private conversation with Nell before she leaves, while the

## Covenant A, turns 5–7

The room disperses into independent activities. Seraphel's night/light couplet is
plain and well formed. T6 correctly does not manufacture a practice encounter.

## Breakwater B, turns 5–7

## Covenant B, turns 8–12

## Covenant B, turns 21–24

## Breakwater pair: provisional whole-run preference

Neither arm leaks the private title or album access, overturns Nell's scheduling
decision, or refuses the player's quiet/work boundaries. Both are still driven
largely by the fixed player's workday itinerary. I narrowly prefer A's independent
Owen/Nell conversation and later cover arrangement to B's more static treatment
of that thread. B's Bea lunch is a strong counterexample: it is more particular
and enjoyable than several routine scenes in either arm. There is no sweeping
quality difference, and the already shared opening contains the largest ensemble
quipping and the useful auditorium-conflict resolution.

## Covenant A, turns 17–19

The ranked bout is the strongest activity scene in this arm so far. Ashara loses
the first touch, changes tactics, and wins 3–1; the heat of her blade matters to
the opponent's complaint. This is a more developed contest than B's relatively
straightforward victory. Rashid watches without making the protagonist his main
concern, and Ashara does not turn the sighting into a training invitation. The
player's one-hour solitary walk then completes. Bells and the shift to fading
afternoon light are not anchored clearly enough to establish a precise clock;
do not import B's ninth-bell orientation into this separate history. T19 answers
the requested question briefly, with another polished line about apologies not
improving a card hand, and leaves the conversation open without a new question.

## Covenant A, turn 20

Rashid returns to his own papers and the player reads. This is a clean short
response to an explicitly quiet action; it does not need to introduce a new plot
beat or repeat assurances that no one will interfere.

## Covenant A, turns 21–23

The song request gets a compact, genuinely rhyming recommendation: moon/soon and
stay/away. The detail about a spoon being "cleared" in verse four is opaque without
the song, but can be playful new history. The proposed teaching session follows
the player's request and is not an arbitrary demand for autobiography. T22 starts
with chase/place, then gives Seraphel ordinary prose replies. In particular,
"Not difficult ... Just easy to spoil" has no rhyme partner. The character-specific
dialogue rule therefore still breaks. The scene describes how a round works but
does not actually let us hear the song; B's sung stanza is more satisfying here.
Both are within the input's allowance for either singing or explanation. T23 lets
the player leave without treating the proposed later lesson as accepted.

## Covenant A, turn 24 and whole-pair judgment

Stone gives useful public primary material while preserving restricted access.
No conspiracy disclosure, magical awakening or unearned privilege occurs. The
registers and reports are consistent new institutional history. As a whole I
slightly prefer A's quiet library passages, concrete training arrangements and
more developed duel, but B's song scene is better. Both retain polished quips and
procedural lecturing, and both fail at least one post-fork Seraphel speech rule.
Neither turns refusals or absence into a compulsory companion scene. There is no
decisive whole-story improvement on the evidence of these two trajectories.

## Review complete before unblinding

All four 24-turn prose histories and both canonical source stories have now been
read. Private notes and condition assignment have not been opened. Preferences
are modest: Covenant A, Breakwater A, with substantial counterexamples in both
B arms. Across all four continuations, the private probes remain private, loan
and archive restrictions hold, and explicit quiet/refusal inputs resolve without
contrived recruitment. The strongest current evidence is improved closure
relative to the older problematic playtests, but both conditions already use
the same newer closure wording. This experiment therefore cannot assign those
shared successes to the periodic checkups. The fixed script also makes quiet
and non-intervention more explicit than an ordinary live player often would.
