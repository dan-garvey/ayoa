Bea’s phone alarm sounds at five to one. She seals the secondhand DVD inside its padded envelope, says goodbye to the table and heads back towards the boatyard. Martin carries his toolbox to the loose trim by the counter, and the lunch conversation breaks up without anyone drawing you into another subject.

At one, you return to the archive workroom.

The afternoon settles into a steady sequence. You finish the programme-box description, then catalogue two more files from the transfer papers. One receipt can be matched to the storage cartons now on the shelves. The typed inventory marked **assorted cinema papers** remains too vague to establish ownership of anything by itself, so its status stays unresolved in the yellow tray.

Nell comes up once to review the flagged material. She finds a reference in the acquisition correspondence that settles one item, but leaves the broad inventory as you recorded it.

“That one stays unconfirmed,” she says. “The paperwork may turn up later. We don’t improve the evidence by wanting it to be complete.”

She returns downstairs, and you continue working.

By the agreed end of your shift at four, the first transfer box has a complete set of file-level records and the programme box has a usable date range, extent and condition note. You return every sheet to its original order, close both boxes and put them back in their marked positions. The yellow tray holds only the unresolved inventory. The loan cupboard has remained locked all afternoon.

You save the catalogue, shut down the laptop and lock the workroom.

As you reach the bottom of the stairs with the archive key, Owen is standing in the open office doorway. His voice carries into the foyer.

“The Thursday appointment is an interview,” he tells Nell. “Permanent exhibition technician at a museum. I replied this afternoon and accepted the interview. Nothing else—I haven’t been offered a job.”

Nell sets down the rota. “All right. Your Friday shift is confirmed.”

“I haven’t told Dad yet.”

“I won’t mention it to him.”

“Thanks. I will.”

Owen steps away from the office as you arrive. He gives you a brief nod but doesn’t turn the disclosure into a conversation. Nell passes you the hours sheet, and you record the end of your shift.
