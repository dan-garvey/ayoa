At your morning break, the drilling at the front of the building has stopped. Martin and Owen sit at one of the cafe tables with two mugs between them. A lamp fitting rests on a folded cloth beside Martin’s screwdriver.

You ask Owen how his week is going.

“More complicated than it was on Monday,” he says. “I’ve got an interview tomorrow. Exhibition technician at a museum inland. I replied this morning.”

Martin’s hand stops halfway towards his mug.

“Permanent?” he asks.

“That’s what they’re advertising.”

“When were you going to tell me?”

“Now.”

Owen doesn’t dress the answer up. Martin sits back, looking first at him and then at the dismantled fitting.

“I thought you were staying here after the reopening.”

“I said I liked it here. My contract still ends next month.”

“They’ll need people.”

“Nobody’s offered me anything permanent. And this is only an interview. I haven’t got a decision to make unless they offer me the job.”

Martin rubs his thumb along the handle of the screwdriver. “Does Nell know?”

“I told her this morning. She’s moved my Thursday shift to Friday.”

For several seconds the only sound in the foyer is the refrigerator behind the cafe counter.

“What time’s the interview?” Martin asks.

“Eleven.”

“I can drive you.”

“I’ve arranged the train.”

“If it’s cancelled, ring me.”

Owen nods. “I will.”

Martin takes up the faulty fitting and turns it over to expose the wiring. “Permanent’s worth going for.”

“Yes,” Owen says. “It is.”

Their break ends when Martin carries the fitting back towards the front doors. Owen follows with the tools, leaving his empty mug on the table to collect when the job is finished.
