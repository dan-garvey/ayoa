On Wednesday morning, Owen is in the office when you arrive. A tape measure hangs from his belt, and a small box of wall fixings rests on the desk.

Nell looks up from her computer. “I’ve got your email. Thursday is off, Friday ten until four instead. It’s on the rota now.”

“Thanks,” Owen says.

He takes the fixings and heads into the foyer without explaining the inland appointment.

You ask Nell what she wants you to catalogue next and whether there are any practical changes you need to know about.

“Carry on with the programme run—1987 through 1989. Use separate entries for things like that duty sheet, but the duplicate weekly programmes can stay at box level. If you finish those, start business correspondence box B2.”

She pulls a location list from the archive folder and marks the two shelf references.

“Nothing changes for you today. One thing further ahead: I’m away from the ninth to the eleventh of October. Gina’s booked us a walking weekend, and I’m keeping it. We’ll arrange your hours so you’re not waiting on me for decisions while I’m gone.”

Nell gives you the location list.

“The first programme box is on shelf three. I’ll be downstairs until four.”
