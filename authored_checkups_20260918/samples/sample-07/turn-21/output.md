On Wednesday morning, Nell is in the office with the staff rota spread across her desk. The paper diary lies open beside it.

You ask what she wants you to catalogue next and whether there are any practical changes you need to know about.

“Same hours as yesterday,” she says. “Finish the programme box you started, then take the box labelled **PROGRAMMES — 1987–1990**. Make the box-level record first. If you find letters, invoices or photographs mixed into the programmes, leave them where they are and flag them rather than creating a new arrangement.”

The unsigned inventory remains unresolved. Nell has checked the office files but has not found the missing attachment.

“Keep its ownership marked unconfirmed. Sam may have another copy with the grant records, but he’s away this week. We wait for evidence.”

She turns the diary so you can see today’s building note.

“An engineer is testing the fire alarm at eleven. I’ll warn you immediately beforehand, then the sounders should run twice. If the alarm goes on beyond the test or sounds at any other time, come straight out.”

Nothing has changed with the archive access: your workroom key remains with you, while the loan-cupboard key stays in the office.

“Dev and Hana completed the receiver test last night,” Nell adds. “They’ve signed off the auditorium coverage, so there’s no follow-up in your workspace.”

She closes the diary and returns to the rota. Your next programme box is waiting on its marked shelf upstairs.
