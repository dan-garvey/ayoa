You spend the first part of the morning finishing the earlier programme box and beginning the box marked **1987–1990**. Shortly before eleven, Nell calls up the stairs to warn you that the alarm test is about to begin.

The sounders run twice, each burst loud enough to vibrate through the metal shelving. After the engineer confirms the system has reset, you take your morning break.

Owen is in the foyer. He has been fitting a wooden display rail beneath the opening programmes; his tools are closed in their case while he sits at the cafe table with a mug. With the work paused, you ask how his week is going.

“Busy,” he says. He turns the mug slowly between his hands. “I confirmed an interview this morning. Permanent exhibition-technician job at a museum inland. It’s tomorrow at eleven.”

He keeps his voice low, though the foyer is otherwise empty.

“Nell knows now. She’s moved my Thursday shift to Friday. Jo knew already—I told her when we were unloading on Monday.”

Owen glances towards the auditorium doors.

“My dad doesn’t know yet. I want to tell him myself.” He pauses. “And it’s only an interview. Nobody’s offered me a job.”

His tool case remains shut beside the chair. For the moment, he has nothing else demanding his attention.
